RUNNING NOTES — Tyndall, SOUND
The shared consistency ledger. Every translation agent reads this; the
orchestrator updates it between batches from the agents' returned notes.

=========================================================================
LOCKED VOCABULARY — caps spent, no re-gloss
=========================================================================

Tyndall's own terms, kept because he reasons with them:

  sonorous motion     what sound IS: motion of the particles of a body,
                      communicated to the air. Defined in chapter one,
                      used to the last page. Not "sound energy".
  pulse / wave        a pulse is one condensation-plus-rarefaction; a
                      wave is the pair together. He is careful about
                      this in chapter one; stay careful.
  node                a point of rest on a vibrating string, rod or
                      plate. "Ventral segment" is the belly between two
                      nodes; keep both, he uses both.
  fundamental,        the lowest tone and the tones above it. "Harmonic"
  overtone            and "overtone" are both his; keep whichever he
                      uses in the sentence.
  clang-tint          his English for Klangfarbe, alongside "timbre".
                      He gives all three together once; keep all three
                      there and use "timbre" thereafter.
  acoustic cloud      a mass of air that reflects sound though it is
  acoustic opacity    optically transparent. HIS COINAGE and the thesis
                      of chapter seven.
  sensitive flame     a flame that responds to sound. "Singing flame" is
  singing flame       a different thing — one that sounds in a tube.
                      Never interchange them.
  liquid vein         Plateau's term for a falling jet, kept where he is
                      naming Plateau's subject; plain "jet" elsewhere.

Modernized silently, first use onward, no gloss:

  vapor -> vapour        color -> colour        labor -> labour
  center -> centre       meter -> metre         fiber -> fibre
  reflexion -> reflection    inflexion -> inflection
  connexion -> connection    caoutchouc -> rubber
  india-rubber -> indiarubber

THE SPELLING RULE: the Gutenberg source is an AMERICAN reprint (Collier,
New York) of an English book. Every -or/-er spelling in it is the New
York printer's, not Tyndall's. Use British spelling throughout.

NOT modernized — these are his claims, not his vocabulary:

  the ether             real, and the medium of light and radiant heat
  acoustic clouds       his explanation of fog-signal failure, argued
                        against Joseph Henry in print. HIS argument.
  Corti's fibres        as a bank of resonators in the ear
  1,090 feet a second   the velocity of sound in air at freezing point
  Cp / Cv = 1.42        Laplace's ratio, as printed

=========================================================================
TONE CALIBRATION
=========================================================================

Tyndall is THE WRITER of the seven Royal Institution volumes, and the
only one reporting his own unfinished research. Two consequences:

  - Do not flatten his prose. He builds a paragraph and lands a cadence
    at the end of a chapter. The shape of the sentences is part of why
    these lectures were famous; unstack the syntax without levelling
    the rhythm.
  - The audience is adults, not children — the one volume of the seven
    where the room is not full of young people. A shade more formal
    than Faraday or Ball, never dry.

The demonstrator's present tense stays: "I now bring the flame under
the tube", "you hear the sound revive". Never convert a demonstration
into a statement of fact.

Register varies and should be allowed to:

  - CHAPTERS I-VI, VIII, IX: the lecture voice.
  - CHAPTER VII: a research report written in the field — cooler, more
    documentary, dated, first person, full of quoted letters and a
    running argument with Joseph Henry. Let it be different.
  - THE SUMMARIES: Tyndall's own recap of each chapter, written for
    students. Short, declarative, one point at a time.
  - THE FOOTNOTES: the same man, compressed.
  - THE APPENDICES: Le Conte's paper and the note on acoustic
    reversibility are other people's writing, or Tyndall's own more
    technical writing. Plainer.

=========================================================================
CONVENTIONS
=========================================================================

FIGURE MARKERS. `[Figure 62]` in, `[Figure 62: caption]` out. Same set,
same order, no additions, no omissions. Captions are new writing: one
descriptive line drawn from the adjacent prose, naming the lettered
points the text refers to (a, b, m, n) because the reader has to find
them in the picture. Compound ids (`[Figure 134-135]`) are one image
carrying several figures; cover each in order and say which is which.
`[Figure front]` is the Fog-Siren frontispiece and takes a caption with
no "Figure N" label.

CHAPTER SYNOPSES. Each chapter opens with a dash-separated list of its
contents. Keep it as its own paragraph in the same dash-separated form.

SUBHEADINGS. Tyndall's section headings arrive as plain title-case lines
with no terminal punctuation, which is what makes them render as
headings. Do not add a full stop to one, and do not shout a line of
prose in capitals.

FOOTNOTES arrive as their own paragraph beginning `Footnote: `, already
in the right place. Keep the form and the position; translate in full.

INDENTED BLOCKS are tables and the two displayed equations. Keep the
indentation and keep every figure inside. Column HEADINGS may be put
into plain modern English. TWO THINGS TO FIX AS YOU GO:
  - a cell that is a ditto mark followed by text (`" carbonate of soda`)
    was left as it stands by prep; write out what the row means.
  - one table's heading was a printed brace spanning two columns; the
    brace is gone, so make the heading say what the brace said.

CHAPTER HEADINGS come from the orchestrator's prompt, exactly as given.
Multi-part files: heading line, then `(Part n of k)`, then the
translation. Parts 2+ never re-introduce the chapter; non-final parts
never conclude it.

AFTER 000 (the voice-setting pass, ratio 0.99):

  - QUOTED MATTER IS LEFT ALONE. The prefaces and chapter seven quote
    other people at length — the Washington Lighthouse Board, General
    Duane, Mr. Fryer of Shanghai, Tyndall's own 1873 report to the
    Elder Brethren. Those quotations keep their Victorian wording
    verbatim, including the American spellings inside them; only
    Tyndall's own prose is modernized. A quotation is evidence, and he
    is arguing from it.
  - British spelling everywhere outside quotation marks: honour,
    favour, vapour, colour, neighbour, behaviour, centre, metre.
  - "sonorous motion" kept. "acoustic opacity" and "acoustic clouds"
    kept, in quotation marks where he puts them there.
  - Names and titles as printed: the Elder Brethren of the Trinity
    House, the South Foreland, Daboll's trumpet, the Mer de Glace,
    Professor Joseph Henry, Major Elliott, General Duane, Mr. Holmes.
  - "Hsü-chung-hu" — the source prints "Hsii-chung-hu", which is the
    printer's substitution for the u-umlaut. Restored.
  - Roman chapter numbers inside the text stay Roman ("Chapter VII"),
    because that is how the book refers to itself and how the
    cross-references read.
  - The frontispiece caption calls the instrument "the fog-siren", and
    chapter seven must use the same name.

AFTER 007-010 (Chapter Two, ratio 0.98):

  - HELMHOLTZ'S OCTAVE NOTATION (footnote in 009) prints superscript

AFTER 011-013 (Chapter Three parts 1-3, ratio 0.98):

  - SOURCE MISPRINT CORRECTED (013, the third law). The printed text
    reads: "One of them is attached to the fork a, the other to the
    fork b, which vibrates with twice the rapidity of a. Stretched by
    a weight of 20 grains, the string attached to B vibrates as a
    whole. Substituting b for a, a weight of 80 grains causes the
    string to vibrate as a whole." The first "b" must be "a": b is
    the faster fork, so it is b that needs the FOUR TIMES heavier
    weight, and "substituting b for a" is meaningless if b is already
    in place. Corrected to "the string attached to a". Verified
    against Fig. 50, which shows the same 80:20:9:5 grain series.
    The numeric check is unaffected (both numbers survive).
  - TYNDALL REUSES a AND b WITH OPPOSITE ROLES in two consecutive
    paragraphs of 013 (first a is the faster fork, then b is). Each
    paragraph defines its own letters, so both were left as printed --
    do NOT "harmonize" them, it would break the arithmetic.
  - LETTER LABELS from the plates are kept exactly (B B', s s', t t',
    m n, A R, a', o'), because the captions written for the figures
    name them and the reader needs the two to agree.

FIGURE-ID COLLISION FOUND AND FIXED (found while translating 019)

  TWO SEPARATE COLLISIONS, both of which silently swapped plates and
  which NO mechanical check in this repo could see -- every marker
  existed, every marker was placed, every ratio was in range:

  1. "Fig. 94a", the Helmholtz resonator, sits on the page after
     "Fig. 94", the sonorous bell. figure_id() read digits only, so the
     resonator claimed the id 94, overwrote the bell's file, and the
     resonator was then printed TWICE -- once above the paragraph about
     the bell. Fixed: a trailing letter is now part of the number.
  2. APPENDIX II RESTARTS THE NUMBERING at Fig. 1, and chapter one
     already has Figs. 1-4. The appendix's four sensitive-flame
     diagrams had overwritten the row of solitaire balls, the row of
     boys, Cottrell's spring model and the bell in the air pump. So
     chapter one -- the first thing a reader sees -- was illustrated
     with four plates from the back of the book. Fixed with a
     NAMESPACED id: plates found after an APPENDIX heading become
     "app_1".., and assemble.figure_label now strips a "_"-terminated
     prefix so the reader still reads "Figure 1".
  prep.py now RAISES if any id is ever claimed by two different files.
  assemble.py, verify.py: "_" added to the marker character class.
  LESSON, worth carrying to the next plate book: ALWAYS LOOK AT THE
  PLATE BEFORE CAPTIONING IT. The captions for figures 1-4 had been
  written from the prose and happened to be right, which is exactly why
  nothing looked wrong. A caption written from the image would have
  caught this on the first file.

NESTED-CONTAINER DUPLICATION FOUND AND FIXED (found while translating 026)

  walk() visited every <div> and <blockquote>, INCLUDING the ones that
  merely wrap other blocks -- so a wrapper emitted its whole contents
  once per level of nesting, on top of the children's own emission:
    - the Spenser stanza in chapter six sits three deep
      (poetry-container > poetry > stanza > line) and appeared FOUR
      times: three times run together as prose, then once properly as
      nine lines;
    - the Gaines's Farm letter (chapter seven) and the "Acoustic
      Reversibility" note at the end of chapter seven sit in
      <blockquote><div> and appeared twice.
  1,281 duplicated words in all. NOTHING MECHANICAL CATCHES THIS: the
  text is all present and in order, and the word ratio silently gets
  WORSE the more faithfully the translation refuses to repeat itself.
  Fixed: a div or blockquote containing a block-level descendant is a
  wrapper and is skipped.
  BOUNDARY DRIFT: chapter seven drops from 8 parts to 7 (so the book
  is 44 files, not 45) and file 025 loses its last three blocks to
  026. Everything up to and including 024 is byte-identical, and the
  three blocks were simply moved between the two finished modern
  files. A rule to stop a cut falling after a SECTION HEADING was
  tried and reverted: it would have moved the boundaries of six
  already-translated files to fix something invisible in the output,
  since assemble.py stitches the parts of a chapter back together
  before rendering.

  SOURCE MISPRINT (026): the section heading reads "Mr. Philip Harry's
