Canto Thirty-Four

	"Vexilla regis prodeunt inferni —
	the banners of the king of Hell, towards us; so look ahead,"
	said my master, "and see whether you can make him out."

	As, when a thick fog breathes out,
	or when our hemisphere grows dark,
	a mill turned by the wind seems to stand far off,

	so it seemed to me I saw such a structure then;
	then, because of the wind, I drew back
	behind my guide, since there was no other shelter.

	I was already — and I put it into verse with fear —
	where the shades were all covered over,
	and showed through like a straw in glass.

	Some are lying down; some stand upright,
	one on his head and one on his soles;
	another, like a bow, bends his face round to his feet.

	When we had gone so far forward
	that it pleased my master to show me
	the creature that once had the beautiful appearance,

	he moved out from in front of me and made me stop,
	saying: "Look, Dis; and look at the place
	where you will have to arm yourself with strength."

	How I became frozen and faint then,
	do not ask, reader, because I do not write it,
	since all speech would be too little.

	I did not die, and I did not stay alive;
	think for yourself now, if you have any wit at all,
	what I became, deprived of both.

	The emperor of the sorrowful kingdom
	came out of the ice from the middle of his chest;
	and I am closer in size to a giant

	than the giants are to his arms:
	see now how great that whole must be
	that fits a part made like that.

	If he was as beautiful as he is ugly now,
	and lifted his brows against his maker,
	then every mourning must indeed come from him.

	Oh, how great a wonder it seemed to me
	when I saw three faces on his head!
	One in front, and that one was crimson;

	the other two joined onto this one
	over the middle of each shoulder,
	and joined each other at the place of the crest:

	and the right one seemed between white and yellow;
	the left one was, to look at, like those
	who come from where the Nile flows down.

	Under each face two great wings came out,
	as big as suited such a bird:
	I never saw sails at sea like them.

	They had no feathers, but were made
	in the manner of a bat's; and he was flapping them,
	so that three winds went out from him:

	that is what freezes the whole of Cocytus.
	He wept with six eyes, and down three chins
	dripped the tears and a bloody slaver.

	In each mouth he was breaking a sinner
	with his teeth, like a brake for flax,
	so that he kept three of them in that suffering.

	To the one in front, the biting was nothing
	beside the clawing, which at times
	left the back entirely stripped of skin.

	"That soul up there who has the greatest punishment,"
	said my master, "is Judas Iscariot,
	who has his head inside and works his legs outside.

	Of the other two, who have their heads down,
	the one hanging from the black muzzle is Brutus:
	see how he writhes, and does not say a word!

	And the other is Cassius, who looks so heavy-limbed.
	But the night is rising again, and by now
	it is time to go, because we have seen everything."

	As he wished, I clasped his neck;
	and he took his chance of time and place,
	and when the wings were wide enough open

	he took hold of the shaggy flanks;
	then he went down from tuft to tuft,
	between the thick hair and the frozen crusts.

	When we had come to where the thigh
	turns, right on the swell of the hip,
	my guide, with effort and with anguish,

	turned his head where his legs had been,
	and gripped the hair like a man climbing,
	so that I thought we were going back into Hell.

	"Hold on tight, because by stairs like these,"
	said my master, panting like a tired man,
	"we have to get away from so much evil."

	Then he came out through the hole of a rock
	and set me down to sit on its edge;
	then he brought his careful step across to me.

	I lifted my eyes and thought I would see
	Lucifer as I had left him,
	and I saw him holding his legs upward;

	and if I became confused then,
	let the dull people think of it, who do not see
	what that point was that I had passed.

	"Get up on your feet," said my master;
	"the way is long and the road is bad,
	and the sun is already back at half of terce."

	It was no palace corridor
	where we were, but a natural dungeon
	with a bad floor and a lack of light.

	"Before I tear myself out of the abyss,
	my master," I said when I was standing,
	"talk to me a little to get me out of my error:

	where is the ice? And how is this one fixed
	so upside down? And how, in so short a time,
	has the sun made the crossing from evening to morning?"

	And he to me: "You are still imagining
	that you are on the other side of the centre, where I took hold
	of the hair of the evil worm that bores through the world.

	You were on that side as long as I was going down;
	when I turned, you passed the point
	to which all weights are drawn from every side.

	And now you have arrived under the hemisphere
	opposite the one that roofs the great dry land,
	and under whose highest point the man was consumed

	who was born and lived without sin;
	your feet are on a little sphere
	that makes the other face of the Giudecca.

	Here it is morning when it is evening there;
	and this one, who made a ladder for us with his hair,
	is still fixed as he was before.

	It was on this side that he fell down from heaven;
	and the land, which used to spread out on this side,
	made a veil of the sea for fear of him,

	and came to our hemisphere; and perhaps
	to escape him, the part that appears on this side
	left the space here empty, and ran back up."

	There is a place down there as far from Beelzebub
	as his tomb extends,
	which is known not by sight but by the sound

	of a little stream that comes down there
	through the hole of a rock it has gnawed
	with its winding course, which falls only slightly.

	My guide and I went in by that hidden road
	to go back to the bright world;
	and, without caring about any rest,

	we climbed up, he first and I second,
	until through a round opening I saw
	some of the beautiful things heaven carries.

	And from there we came out to see the stars again.
