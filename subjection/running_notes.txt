FILE / PART DISCIPLINE — THE quixote TRAP, AND IT IS AT MAXIMUM HERE
---------------------------------------------------------------------
ALL EIGHT FILES ARE CHAPTER PARTS. Every single one. The shape is
exactly:

    Chapter Two: What the Law Makes of a Wife
    (Part 2 of 2)

    <body>

Parts 2 and later NEVER re-introduce the chapter; non-final parts NEVER
conclude it. assemble.strip_front drops the first non-blank line of
EVERY file in a group and takes the heading from part 1 only, so a wrong
heading in a later part vanishes silently and a missing part marker is
invisible to verify.py. check.py is the only thing that sees either.

Headings, exactly, and never anything else:
    Chapter One: An Opinion That Has Never Been Put on Trial
    Chapter Two: What the Law Makes of a Wife
    Chapter Three: Whether Women Should Be Shut Out of Anything
    Chapter Four: What Would Be Gained
WORD-FORM numbers, as in mill/: assemble.CHAP_LINE matches only
"Chapter <digits>:" and would nest these under a Part divider that does
not exist. THE TITLES ARE NEW WRITING — the 1869 printing heads each
chapter with a bare Roman numeral — so they are also the only place in
the book where a wrong word is invisible to every check. Cross-refer to
them in word form ("as I argued in Chapter Two").

MARKUP
------
Markup-free with ONE exception: *x* and _x_ render as <em>.
  - NO asterisks except emphasis. The three "* * *" separators ride
    through as they are; they are horizontal rules, not emphasis.
  - NEVER write an all-caps line: assemble.py reads one as a heading.
  - The opening asterisk may not follow a word character, so write
    "can *not*", never "can*not*" -- the latter ships as literal
    asterisks with the marker count still balanced.

SENSITIVE PASSAGES — DECIDED IN ADVANCE
---------------------------------------
The Verne rule here mostly protects Mill's RADICALISM. Kept entire and
unhedged, with no note anywhere in the volume: the sustained
wife-and-slave legal comparison and both halves of its qualification;
the husband's legal claim on his wife's body; "no legal slaves, except
the mistress of every house"; and, on the other side, his Victorian
confidence about savages, barbarians and the stages of civilisation,
and his assumption that most wives will still choose the household.
A DATED WORD IS NOT A DATED CLAIM, and the mill/ ruling governs: where
a racial noun carries no part of the argument it is replaced by the
relationship (On Liberty's "planters and the people they enslaved");
where Mill is arguing ABOUT the institution of slavery, it is named
plainly.

RATIO
-----
Target 0.90-1.05 per file, as mill/ (which landed 0.99). Verify with
--min-ratio 0.85 --max-ratio 1.15. Under 0.85 is summarising, and Mill's
qualifications are not padding -- they are the argument being careful.
