EULER, "LETTRES A UNE PRINCESSE D'ALLEMAGNE" -- STANDING BRIEF
Read this whole file, then running_notes.txt, before translating.

WHAT THIS WORK IS
A scholarly modernization of a canonical text in the history of science.
Leonhard Euler (1707-1783), the greatest mathematician of his century,
wrote these 234 letters in French between 1760 and 1762 from Berlin, to
teach science and philosophy to a fifteen-year-old princess, Friederike
Charlotte of Brandenburg-Schwedt, a niece of Frederick the Great, who was
away at Magdeburg during the Seven Years' War. They were published at St
Petersburg in 1768-72 and became the most read popular-science book of
the century. Our source is Cournot's 1842 Paris reprint of the original
text (OCR of a scan). There is an old English version (Hunter, 1795); do
NOT use it or imitate it. You translate from the French, and your English
is what readers will have.

THE VOICE
Patient, clear, courteous, delighted by his subject. Euler builds every
idea from something the princess already knows (a foot, the road to
Magdeburg, a clavichord, a candle) and never shows off. He addresses her
as "V. A." (Votre Altesse): render "Your Highness", used as often as he
uses it -- it is the texture of the book. Letter 1 opens "Madame," --
render "Madam,". Keep his closing courtesies when he writes them, in
plain modern form ("I have the honour to be..." may stay; it is his
manners, not archaism). Keep his arguments as HIS arguments: the ether,
his theory of light as vibration, his attacks on Leibniz's monads and on
the Wolffians, his religious reasoning on prayer, the soul, providence
and free will. Do not correct his science or add modern notes; render
the eighteenth-century view faithfully and in measured register.

THE STANDARD
Faithful but not literal: every sentence, every example, every number, in
his order. Modern, natural English at an eighth-grade-to-high-school
level: split long French sentences, contractions where natural, no
archaisms, no French word order. Complete: never summarise, never skip,
never merge letters. English runs about the same length as the French.
Units: keep his units (feet, inches, lines, German miles, toises, degrees)
-- they are how he thinks; the running notes give the glosses already
spent.

THE SOURCE FILE
chapters/NNN.txt holds several letters, each after a marker line
"=== LETTER n (figures: first|second volume) ===". Each letter opens with
a date line "(19 avril 1760.)" (OCR-mangled: "I76I" = 1761, "17C0" = 1760)
and a title line ("De l'Étendue."), then the text.
OCR damage: page breaks split paragraphs mid-sentence -- rejoin them; read
through garbage characters to the obvious word; a passage genuinely
unreadable: translate what is clear and mark [?]. Never invent.
DROP Cournot's 1842 footnotes: paragraphs beginning "(1)", "(i)", "(x)",
"(r)" etc. that give metric conversions, later discoveries, corrections
("Le pied de Berlin vaut...", "La pensée d'Euler est inexacte...") --
they are the editor's, not Euler's -- and drop their markers "(1)" in the
text. Stray running heads that slipped through ("DE LA VITESSE. 7") also go.
Rows of dots in the printed text ("......") are usually Euler's own
ellipsis or a figure set in type -- not damage. Euler's own numbered paragraphs (I., II., ...), where they exist, stay.
FIGURES: keep every figure reference, as "(Figure 12)" or "in Figure 12",
using the number he gives. Letters to "(figures: second volume)" cite the
second volume's plates; keep the number exactly -- do not renumber. Keep
his letters on diagrams (A, B, C; line AB) exactly.
TABLES and lists he sets out (musical ratios, the tones of the
clavichord): one item per line, each line starting with a TAB, as one
paragraph.

FORMAT (exact)
Line 1: the file heading given in your prompt, e.g. "Letters 1–5"
Blank line. Then for each letter, in order:
  Letter N: Title
  (blank line)
  _19 April 1760_.
  (blank line)
  the letter's paragraphs, separated by blank lines.
N is the number in the marker line. Title: his title, translated and SHORTENED if
needed: at most 8 words, Title Case, no final punctuation, under 60
characters. MORE THAN HALF the words of the whole line "Letter N: Title"
must be capitalized, or the page will not set it as a heading -- so
cut "of the ... of the" chains ("On the Influence of the Freedom of Minds
on the Events of the World" -> "How Free Minds Shape Events") ("On Extension",
"On Speed", "The Twelve Tones of the Clavichord"). Keep "On ..." when
he writes "De ..."/"Sur ...". Date: day Month year, in underscores, then a
full stop after the closing underscore, exactly as shown; Include a
place only when the source gives one (Letter 1: "_Berlin, 19 April
1760_."). If the date is lost, write "_[date lost]_.".
No other headings, no part lines, no notes of your own.
Write euler/modern_chapters/NNN.txt (same number as the source file).

SENSITIVE CONTENT: render religion, war (the Seven Years' War is on) and
his polemics plainly as his. If you meet a remark about a people or race
that is an aside and carries only contempt, translate it anyway but REPORT
it in your notes with its letter number, so the editor can rule.

RETURN: (a) one line per letter: number and what it teaches; (b)
consistency notes for running_notes.txt: technical terms you settled
(e.g. how you rendered "étendue", "force", "éther", "ton"), units glossed,
forward references ("as I will show in a later letter"), anything flagged.
