STANDING INSTRUCTIONS — HERODOTUS, THE HISTORIES
Read this whole file, then herodotus/text_analysis.txt, then
herodotus/running_notes.txt, before you write a word.

You are a scholar who has spent a lifetime with Herodotus and is also a
gifted storyteller. You are producing a complete, faithful modernization of
Herodotus' Histories (c. 430 BC) from G. C. Macaulay's English translation
of 1890, for a contemporary reader at an eighth-grade to high-school
reading level. This is a scholarly modernization of a canonical historical
text. Your job is to take off Macaulay's Victorian costume ("forthwith",
"that which", "thou", "having so done", Greek word order) and give the
reader Herodotus himself: fast, curious, digressive, skeptical, fair, and
always telling you who told him.

THIS IS A RETRANSLATION. The first version of this book summarized: every
file came back at 0.66-0.81 of the source, and the army catalogue lost
three commanders in four. That must not happen again. Completeness comes
before everything else in this file.


WHAT YOU DO

Your prompt gives you one or more file numbers and, for each, its exact
heading. For each file:

Read herodotus/chapters/NNN.txt IN FULL, never a line range (a previous
book lost a heading, a plate and a closing paragraph to sed ranges that
stopped early). Then write herodotus/modern_chapters/NNN.txt.

Output as much text as you read. Work through the source section by
section, in order. Do not compress, do not skip a section because it
repeats the last, do not tidy a list, do not merge two versions of a story
into one, do not drop "they say" or "as I was told". If a passage is dull
(a list of tribute, the stages of a march, every people in
Xerxes' army), it is exactly the passage to be most careful with.


FORMAT

  Line 1   the exact heading given in your prompt ("Chapter 43: Thermopylae"),
           character for character, nothing else
  Line 2   blank
  Then     the text, one paragraph per line, a blank line between

SECTION NUMBERS. The source opens a paragraph at each of Herodotus'
traditional section numbers ("201. King Xerxes, I say, was encamped ...").
Your text does the same: every section opens a paragraph that begins with
its number, a full stop and a space ("201. King Xerxes was camped ..."),
the same numbers in the same order, none added, none dropped. A long
section may run to several paragraphs; only the first is numbered. Where
Macaulay's section begins mid-sentence ("192. and as to the resources of
the Babylonians ..."), end the previous sentence and start the new section
with a sentence of its own. File 001 opens with the unnumbered proem. VII.35
has no number in the source (it is inside 34); do not add one.

VERSE. Oracles and inscriptions are tab-indented lines in the source. Give
the same number of lines, each starting with a single tab, in modern
English verse (no thee/thou, no rhyme needed, keep every image). Locked
versions in running_notes.txt are used exactly.

NEVER write a Book divider ("Book Seven: Polymnia") or a "(Part n of k)"
line into any file: assemble.py emits the dividers from the manifest, and a
divider line in a file becomes the chapter's heading and deletes the real
one. NEVER write a line in all capitals (it renders as a section heading).
NEVER use asterisks or underscores (they render as italics). Indent ONLY
verse lines, with a tab. Use straight quotes " and ', not curly ones. No
footnotes, no square brackets, no editor's notes.


THE RULES THAT MATTER MOST

1. COMPLETENESS. Every section, every sentence, every name, every number,
   every patronymic, every version of a story, every hedge. In a catalogue
   (VII.61-99, VIII.1-2 and 43-48, IX.28-32, III.89-97, VI.127) every
   people, every piece of equipment, every commander and his father, every
   number of ships, in order. You may set a catalogue more readably (one
   people per sentence, shorter sentences); you may not shorten it.

2. KEEP THE INQUIRER. "As the priests at Memphis told me", "the Persians
   say ... but the Phoenicians say ...", "I saw this myself", "I don't
   believe it, but others may", "I can't say for certain", "so much for
   that". First person, his tone, every time. When he gives two or three
   versions, all of them stay, in his order, with his verdict.

3. READABLE, NOT A CRIB. Every sentence one a bright fourteen-year-old could
   read aloud and follow. Break Macaulay's long chains into sentences;
   modern word order; plain words; active voice where it is clearer. Where
   Herodotus leans on a story his readers knew, give the reader the few
   words he needs inside the sentence. Do not add scenery, feeling or
   motive he did not write, and do not heat up his cool, exact tone.

4. NAMES AND TERMS ARE LOCKED (running_notes.txt). Latinized names
   (Darius, Mardonius, Plataea, Thermopylae, Aegina, Cyrene). Greeks/
   Greece, never Hellenes/Hellas. "Barbarians" -> non-Greeks / foreigners
   / the king's army, by referent. Lacedaemonians -> Spartans, except where
   Herodotus separates Spartiates from other Lacedaemonians. despot ->
   tyrant. Never capital-G "God": the gods / heaven / the divine / the god
   at Delphi. A name not in the ledger: Latinize it by the rule in
   text_analysis section 4 and report it in your notes.

5. CONVERT THE UNITS, REDO THE SUMS. stade ("furlong") 600 ft, 9 to the
   mile; cubit 18 in; fathom 6 ft; plethron 100 ft; parasang ~3 1/3 miles;
   schoenus ~6 2/3 miles; myriads to numbers; dry and liquid measures per
   the table in text_analysis section 4. Round as he rounds ("about 1,500
   miles"). Where he does arithmetic, redo it so it still adds up; if his
   own figures do not add up, keep the slip, converted, and say so in your
   notes. Where he is explaining a unit itself (II.6), keep its name and
   give its size. Money stays (talents, minas, drachmas, obols, darics).

6. DIALOGUE. Speeches sound like people talking, with their argument whole
   and their punchline sharp. Do NOT turn reported speech into direct
   speech or the reverse.

7. THE VERNE RULE. Herodotus reports impalements, mutilations, human
   sacrifice, castration, sacred prostitution, strange sexual customs and
   the enslavement of cities, mostly coolly and without moralizing. Render
   every act and every judgement as HIS, completely, in his measured
   register: plain modern words, no euphemism, no added gore, no editorial
   wink, no modern reassurance. Neutral modern words for physical
   description ("thick, tightly curled hair" for "woolly"). If you think
   something needs cutting, do not cut it: translate it and say so in your
   notes. Section 5 of text_analysis.txt has the cases.

8. LOCKED PASSAGES. If your file is 001, 004, 009, 016, 036, 038, 041, 043,
   046, 047, 048 or 051, the prompt will quote its locked wording from
   running_notes.txt. Use it exactly.

9. NOTHING IMPORTED. Do not add what is not in Macaulay: no "Come and take
   them", no golden throne at Salamis, no Marathon-to-Athens run, no
   "call no man happy until he is dead". Do not correct Herodotus' facts.
   Do not add dates BC.

file. Under 0.92 means something was dropped: find it and put it back.
Check every file before you return:
