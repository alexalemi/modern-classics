METHOD (settled before transcription)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
- READ FROM pages_nijl/ (1817), CHECK AGAINST pages/ (1806). Both are
  119 openings, page for page, and both are split into half-leaves:
  n007r.jpg is the right half of opening 7 in the 1817 copy, v1_007r
  the same leaf in the 1806. Right comes before left: a Japanese book
  opens the other way.
- WHERE THE TWO COPIES DISAGREE, OR WHERE A CHARACTER IS UNCERTAIN IN
  BOTH, MARK IT. A guessed character that reads plausibly is exactly
  the defect this edition cannot otherwise catch. Uncertainties go in
  source_notes.txt with the leaf and the reading taken, never silently
  resolved.
- The picture captions are an independent outline of the narrative.
  Transcribe the caption for a scene BEFORE the prose around it, and
  use it as a check on the prose: a reading that contradicts the plate
  beside it is wrong somewhere.

  woodcut, 5 blank; the distribution is bimodal with nothing between

- 2026-09-02 MENZAN'S APPARATUS IS NOT TRANSLATED, and this is the
  bunyan/Offor precedent applied. Each text page carries Kenzei's
  narrative in large type and, beneath it, Menzan's annotation in small
  type: source-criticism arguing dates and paternity out of Edo
  reference works cited by abbreviation (山城名勝志洛陽部, 本朝通紀,
  東鑑). Three reasons it goes to reference/ rather than into the book.
    1. It is not Kenzei and not the life. It is an eighteenth-century
       scholar arguing with his sources, and modern scholarship holds
       his corrections unreliable -- so translating them in full would
       give the greatest weight to the least trustworthy layer.
    2. It is where the reading is worst. The annotation is set small,
       is dense with abbreviated titles and numerals, and my confidence
       there is materially below the main text -- and a wrong numeral
       in a citation is invisible.
    3. bunyan/ settled the same question the same way: Offor's 1,010
       commentator notes went to reference/ as a crib, drawn on only
       where a modern reader genuinely cannot follow, with exactly one
       bracketed note in the whole volume.
  WHAT IS TRANSLATED: Kenzei's narrative entire, and every plate
  caption, which is the source's own. Menzan's presence is stated in
  the front matter, not hidden, and where his annotation supplies
  something a reader cannot do without it may be used sparingly as a
  bracketed note -- the soap-bubbles annotation rule.
- 2026-09-02 ONE PASS, NOT TWO. From n018l the transcript and the
  English are written together, leaf by leaf: transcript/ and english/
  side by side. Reading a woodblock leaf carefully is the expensive
  step and doing it once for the Japanese and again for the English
  would double the only part of this book that cannot be hurried.
  chapters/ is assembled from transcript/ afterwards, so the
  character-based ratio still has a source to measure against.
- 2026-09-02 THE TENZO KYOKUN SURFACES INSIDE A 補 NOTE (n025r): Menzan
  quotes the whole encounter with the old cook of Ayuwang who came down
  to the ship for mushrooms in 1223. It is dropped here, because the
  rule is about the LAYER and not about how good the passage is -- but
  note what it implies. The Tenzo Kyokun is a short, complete, separate
  work of Dogen's own, and it is exactly the sort of thing the dogen/
  volume should take next: practical instruction in the same register
  as the Fukanzazengi, and the National Archives holds Edo printings.
- 2026-09-02 ORDER CHANGED, DELIBERATELY. The Kenzeiki is a 157-leaf
  transcription from woodblock with no crib, running at about a leaf
  per reading, and it is a multi-session job however it is approached.
  The Dogen extension is not: the Tenzo Kyokun is one short work. So
  the extension is done FIRST and finished, rather than leaving two
  books half-built. The Kenzeiki state is fully resumable -- fetch.py,
  survey.py, pagemap, readorder, layers, transcript/, english/,
  source_notes -- and picks up at the next unclassified leaf.
