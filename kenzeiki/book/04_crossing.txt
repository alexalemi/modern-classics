Chapter Four: The Crossing

The Founder went with Myozen. They left Higashiyama on the twenty-second
day of the second month of 1223 and made for the port of Hakata in
Chikuzen, on the western sea. In the last ten days of the third month
they boarded a merchant ship and cast off.

Two travel permits for the journey were kept in the treasury at
Eiheiji until they were destroyed in a fire in 1714. This was the
first:

	Myozen, resident monk of Kenninji, with two or three of his
	disciples, is going to the port of Hakata in order to cross to
	China. On the roads of the western circuit, at the harbours and
	the barriers and everywhere else, he is to be let through
	without hindrance. Issued by order of the retired emperor's
	office.

	The twenty-first day of the second month, the second year of
	Joo. Sealed by the lieutenant of the Left Guard.

And the second, from the military government at Kamakura:

	The monks of Kenninji, Myozen, Dogen, Kakunen, Ryobi and the
	rest, are going down to the western sea to cross over. Let them
	pass the barriers on the way and put in at the harbours without
	hindrance. So ordered.

	The twenty-first day of the second month, the second year of
	Joo. Sealed, the Governor of Musashi. Sealed, the Governor of
	Sagami.

The monk Dogen went to China at twenty-four. He had in mind a poem that
the great master Enchin of Miidera had made four hundred years earlier,
when he set out for China himself:

	I am one who goes out in the boat of the dharma.
	Let all the gods and all the buddhas look on me.

And he was of the same mind.

There was a great merchant family at Tsu, in the province of Ise,
called Shibahara. Their own family record says that an ancestor of
theirs, some twenty generations back, was trading overseas by ship at
the time, and that it was his ship that carried the founder of Eiheiji
across to China.

They made land at the beginning of the fourth month, at Mingzhou on the
Chinese coast. By Chinese reckoning it was the sixteenth year of the
Jiading era. For some weeks the Founder stayed on board the ship, and
from there he went about looking at the monasteries on the nearby
mountains and asking about the customs of the country.

In the middle of the fifth month, while he was still living on the
ship, the cook of the great monastery of Ayuwang came aboard to buy
mushrooms. They met, and talked, and the cook went away. The Founder
told Myozen about it afterwards, and Myozen was delighted. That meeting
is the one the Founder describes in his Instructions for the Cook, and
it changed how he thought about everything.
