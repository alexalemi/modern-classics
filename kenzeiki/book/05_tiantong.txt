Chapter Five: Tiantong

In the autumn, in the seventh month of 1223, the Founder went up Mount
Tiantong for the first time and was given a place in the monks' hall.
The abbot then was Wuji Liaopai, a dharma-grandson of the great master
Dahui.

But because the Founder and his companions were foreigners from what
the Chinese considered a barbarous country, they were seated in the
row of the newly ordained, at the very bottom. Myozen was forty years
old. The Founder spoke up. In the house of the Buddha, he said, a
monk's place goes by the years since he was ordained, not by his age
in the world and not by the country he comes from. How is it that in
the great Zen monasteries of China the seating should be turned upside
down like this?

The whole mountain heard him, and nothing was done. The Founder pressed
the point and would not let it drop. The senior monks answered: it is
handed down that when monks came from Japan under the last dynasty,
Kukai and Saicho and Eisai and the rest, every one of them was seated
with the newly ordained. That is the old precedent, and it is not going
to be altered now.

At that the Founder sighed and said to himself: that the standard set
by a thousand buddhas should sink to the level of ordinary human
feeling is a thing to grieve over. Shall I not put it right?

So he drew up a petition and laid his case before the Emperor of China.
When nothing came of it, he sent a second. The heart of it ran:

	I say it again. The Buddha's teaching reaches through worlds as
	many as the grains of sand, and the light of the precepts shines
	in every direction. The sutra says: "This whole threefold world
	is mine, and the beings in it are all my children." If it is all
	his, then this world of ours is the country of Shakyamuni
	Buddha. The country is a buddha-country already and the people
	in it are all the children of the Buddha. Brothers are brothers,
	and the natural order between them is not to be confused. As I
	see it, the law of the Buddha and the law of the world both
	follow from this. The gods of heaven and the spirits of the
	earth do not put up with what is against principle. How should
	the judgment of heaven take sides? I respectfully ask that the
	order of seating by the precepts be set right.

The emperor was Ningzong. He read the petition himself and was moved by
it more than once, and in the end he sent an order down to Tiantong
that the seating was to be fixed by the years of ordination, according
to the Buddha's rule. That Japanese monks entering Chinese monasteries
were never again put out of their proper place is the Founder's own
doing, and it was from this that his name went out through the Five
Mountains, the five great Zen monasteries of China.

Some years before, a senior Japanese monk named Ryuzen had nursed the
librarian of Tiantong through a long illness, and taken great trouble
over him. To thank him, the librarian had brought out his own
transmission document, the secret certificate that passes from a Zen
master to his heir, and let him bow before it. It was a thing hardly
ever seen. Now, when the Founder arrived, Ryuzen quietly asked the
librarian on his behalf, and the document was shown to the Founder too.
Through another senior monk, Weiyi, he was allowed to see and bow to a
transmission document of the Fayan line, and through the elder Zongyue
one of the Yunmen line. He thanked each of them with incense. Wuji, the
abbot, said to him: this is a thing few men ever get to see. Now,
elder brother, you have seen it, and that is the true resort of a man
who studies the way. The Founder's gladness was more than he could
hold.

In the monks' hall he saw the monk in the next seat lift his kesa, the
patched robe that is the sign of a monk, and set it on his head each
morning before he put it on. He wept at the sight, and made a vow that
he would carry that custom home. And when a monk arrived from Korea who
did not even know what a monk's robe and bowl were for, and looked like
a layman, he was more ashamed than ever, and took himself in hand.

In 1225, the first year of Baoqing, the Founder was still at Tiantong
and coming up to twenty-six. The abbot Wuji had told him several times
that he approved of him, but the Founder would not accept it of
himself.
