Chapter Six: Wandering, and the Plum Blossom

He left Tiantong and went to see the master Zheweng Ruyan on Mount
Jing.

Ruyan asked him: when did you get here?
The Founder said: the fourth month of last year.
Ruyan said: you came along with the crowd, just so.
The Founder said: since I came along with the crowd, just so, then
what is it that does not come along with the crowd?
Ruyan said: sit down a while and have some tea.

He went on to Cuiyan in Taizhou, met the master Zhuo there, and asked
him straight out: what is the Buddha?
Zhuo said: the one inside the hall.
The Founder said: if he is the one inside the hall, how is it that he
fills the sands of the Ganges?
Zhuo said: he fills the sands of the Ganges.
The Founder said: you have fallen into your own words.

He went back to Ayuwang. On the wall of the gallery there was a painted
circle, the full moon of the philosopher Nagarjuna, and pointing at it
he put the guest-prefect, a monk named Chenggui, to the test.

He went on to the Wannian monastery at Pingtian, and there met the
abbot Yuanzi, and asked whether he might be shown the transmission
document. Yuanzi rose himself, held it up, and said: this is a thing
not shown even to a man's own kin, nor to an attendant monk of many
years' standing. That is the rule of the buddhas and ancestors. But
yesterday, when I had come out of the city and was staying in the
prefectural town, I had a dream. There was an eminent monk in it who
seemed to be Fachang of Damei, the old master of Plum Mountain, and he
held up a branch of plum blossom and said: if there should come a real
man who has already crossed the ship's rail, do not begrudge him the
flower. And he gave me the plum blossom. Without knowing what I did, I
recited in the dream: thirty good blows for the man who has not yet
crossed the ship's rail. And this document, besides, has plum blossom
woven into its damask. Now, meeting you, elder brother, who have
crossed the ship's rail, this must be what Damei was teaching. It
agrees with the dream.

So he let the Founder look at it and bow to it. Afterwards he had a
second dream about him, and thought a great deal of him as a vessel
for the teaching. The Founder was deeply moved by the connection
between the two dreams.

He went round from place to place and met the teachers there. But he
found nobody of surpassing capacity fit to be his master, and so he
decided to go back to Tiantong and see Wuji again. On the road he heard
that Wuji had died. He grieved over it greatly, and from then on the
thought of going home began to come up in him. He decided to call on
Myozen, who was still lodging at Tiantong, and climb the mountain a
second time.

On the way he met a monk called Laogui, who said to him: of all the
masters under heaven today there is none beyond Rujing. He has just
taken up residence at Tiantong at the emperor's request. You should go
up the mountain at once and see him.

The Founder took this for good fortune, and hurried up to Tiantong.
