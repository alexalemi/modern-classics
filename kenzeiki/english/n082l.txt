The fourth year of Kangen, the year of the horse.

From the discourse in the hall on the fifteenth day of the sixth month,
the name Daibutsuji was changed and the temple was called Eiheiji. The
name is taken from the Yongping era of the Later Han, the reign-period
in which the Buddha's teaching first came east.

From that same day the Pure Rules for the Officers of the Monastery
were put into practice.

A sheet in the Founder's hand is at Eiheiji from about this time,
forty-six characters in eight lines of uneven length, with his cipher
under the dharma-name:

	Concerning the monks who attend the offering of gruel before
	the Buddha at Eiheiji. First, the monk Ejo. Second, the monk
	Kakubutsu. And so on round again in turn, making the offering
	reverently.

	The tenth day of the seventh month, the fourth year of Kangen
	— Dogen, monk resident on this mountain.
