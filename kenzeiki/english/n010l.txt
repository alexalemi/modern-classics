His imperial name was Tamehito, the first son of the retired emperor
Go-Toba. His mother was Shomeimon-in, the daughter of the Minister of
the Interior, Lord Michichika — in fact the daughter of the Dharma-seal
NOen. It is also said that the Lady of the West, wife of Munetaka, the
Kamakura shogun, was a daughter of the same Lord Michichika of
Tsuchimikado. The Founder had many branches to his family, and later
ages may judge from that how flourishing the house was.

In the third year of Kennin, the year of the boar, at four years old,
he read Li Qiao's hundred poems. An old and famous scholar saw it and
praised him, saying: this is not the ordinary run of children. He will
be a great vessel in time to come. He may properly be called a prodigy.

In the first year of Kenei, the year of the tiger, at seven, he read
the Book of Songs and the Zuo Zhuan.
