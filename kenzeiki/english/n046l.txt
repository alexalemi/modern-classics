	There is no lack of men to weed and plough, but what is to be
	done when there is no seed? After the grass has grown thick the
	confusion begins, and so, as I come to the end, my thoughts run
	on past me. You are a patched-robe monk of long standing and a
	descendant of this line. What I now entrust to you alone is the
	young hawk lately taken in. He is only a fledgling, but has he
	not merit to spare? He is the fortunate son of my dharma. If I
	were to give him the seal now, he might find it hard to bear,
	and so I entrust it to you a second time over. Take it. With
	this verse, then, and the leather sandals and the one robe and
	the eighteen articles, I hand my word over to you; and hereafter
	Yiqing is certainly to receive it. Keep it in charge and wait
	for Yiqing. The verse says:

	Grass on the ridge at Yangguang —
	I leave it to you to wait until its price is known.
	Where the strange shoot grows thick,
	the sacred root holds fast in the dark.

	He who gets the dharma must be ten years with the Linji house
	before he may open it out.

The above was copied with reverence from the late master's own scroll,
by the old man Yunxi. The late master here means Yiyun. What the
Founder of Eihei received face to face in the abbot's room at Tiantong
was handed down in the direct line for six generations and set down by
Yunxi. It is the pure secret of the Soto abbot's room, and a thing the
other houses do not know.

At that time the Founder came down from Mount Taibai and went into
Jiangxi. At nightfall a fierce tiger came at him, and when he threw his
staff at it the tiger took fright and went off. To this day there is a
