On the fifth day of the eighth month of that year the Founder went up
to the capital. Because of his illness the patron, the governor of
Izumo, had pressed him again and again to come up, and the second abbot
went with him; the object was that he should be seen by physicians.

The record of his last words says: on the sixth day of the eighth month
Gikai, at the lodging at Wakimoto, was given leave to go, and took the
occasion to ask him: hard as it is that I should not attend you this
time, what I really wish is to follow you back to the temple; and if
there is any delay, may I come up to the capital to see you? Will you
permit it?

The reverend master said: yes, certainly, that is quite right; there is
nothing to discuss. But it is because I am thinking of the temple that
I am leaving you behind. See to it, and look after the temple well. You
are a man of this province, and many in it know you, and there are
particular reasons, within the temple and outside it, why you should
stay.

Gikai received this with awe. It was his last sight of his face and his
last instruction, and he never forgot it.

There is a verse he made on going up to the capital:

	Ten years eating my rice on Eihei's ground;
	seven months now lying on a sickbed.
	To look for medicine I go out among men a while, over the
	ridge —
