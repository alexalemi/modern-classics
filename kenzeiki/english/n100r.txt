	The ancestral way came from the west, and I have handed it on
	in the east:
	fishing the moon, ploughing the clouds, longing for the old
	manner.
	The red dust of the world does not blow as far as this —
	deep in the mountains, a snowy night, inside a grass hut.

	Sitting at night, the watches nearly gone, sleep not yet come:
	I know now in my bones that the way is to be worked out in
	mountains and woods.
	The sound of the valley stream in the ears, the moon reaching
	the eyes,
	and beyond that, what use is there for the mind at all?

The retired emperor Go-Saga, hearing of the Founder's reputation for
the way, granted him the purple robe. He declined it two and three
times over, and was not permitted to; and he accepted it but never
wore it. He made a verse and sent it up in thanks:

	Eihei's valley may be shallow,
	but the imperial commands come again and again.
	I am afraid the monkeys and the cranes
	will laugh at this one old man.
