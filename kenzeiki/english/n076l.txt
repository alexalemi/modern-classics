On the nineteenth day of the second month of that year the ground for
the dharma hall of the Great Buddha Temple was levelled. On the
twenty-first day of the fourth month the foundation stones were laid
and the pillars raised, and the ridgepole went up the day after. The
hour for the ceremony was calculated by Abe no Harumune, junior
secretary of the Bureau of Yin and Yang.

On the eighteenth day of the seventh month of that year the hall was
opened and he preached, and so on. From this day, the Founder said, let
this mountain be called Kichijozan, Mount Good Fortune, and the temple
be called Daibutsuji, the Temple of the Great Buddha. And he gave a
verse:

	The great merit of all the buddhas and tathagatas,
	highest of all good fortunes, above which is nothing.
	All the buddhas come together and enter this place;
	therefore this ground is the most fortunate of all.

That day, while the sutras were being chanted, the dragon gods raised
clouds and brought down rain.
