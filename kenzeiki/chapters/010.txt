Chapter Ten: Into Echizen

His residence at the temple in Uji lasted eleven years, from the first
year of Tenpuku, the year of the snake, to the first year of Kangen,
the year of the hare.

That temple at Fukakusa was close to the imperial capital, and the
carriages and horses of court nobles and great families came and went
without a break. He taught more than a hundred households as occasion
gave him the chance, and the disciples who received the bodhisattva
precepts from him were more than two thousand.

Saving living beings by skilful means is the old way of the buddhas and
ancestors. But what I want, he said, is quiet and no business. So he
was always looking out for somewhere convenient among mountains and
woods, springs and rocks. Patrons who had a connection with him offered
him twelve places, mountain and garden land, as quiet spots to settle
in; but some were in distant provinces and some in the home provinces,
and none of them suited him.

Then Hatano Yoshishige, governor of Izumo, said to him: within the
district of Yoshida in Echizen there is an old temple, deep in the
mountains and quiet, and it lies inside my own holding. If you would go
down there and teach and save beings, it would be the good fortune of
the whole province and of my house.

The Founder answered: my late master Rujing, the old buddha, was a man
of Yuezhou in Great Song. So even to hear the name of Etsu is dear to
me.

That is what I want, he said, and answered that he would go down there.

The first year of Kangen, the year of the hare.

On the sixteenth day of the seventh month he set out from Fukakusa and
arrived in Echizen. At first he stayed at Yoshimine, and began teaching
there on the first day of the intercalary seventh month. He lodged at
Yoshimine until the sixteenth day of the eleventh month, and after that
moved to Zenjigamine, where he remained for the rest of that year.
Twelve fascicles were set out.

On the nineteenth day of the eleventh month, Seeing the Buddha; on the
twenty-seventh, Thorough Practice; on the seventeenth day of the
twelfth month, The Eye, and Everyday Life; on the twenty-fifth, The
Dragon's Song.

The governor of Izumo, together with Kakunen, the lay monk of the guard
in what is now the south-east district, built a temple —

Wishing to found a temple, he searched about the estate for a
convenient place among the mountains and woods, and east of Ichinoyama
and west of Sansho he found ground that suited a temple, and was glad
of it. On the seventeenth day of the intercalary seventh month of that
year the governor of Izumo levelled the hillside and cleared the ground
with his own hands, and the Founder moved from the thatched hut at
Yoshimine to this place.

The second year of Kangen, the year of the dragon.

In that year the building of the Great Buddha Temple was going on, so
perhaps his own lodging was not yet settled.

Until about the second month, it seems, he went back and forth between
Zenjigamine and Yoshimine.

On the fourth day of the second month of that year, the
fifty-second fascicle of the Treasury of the True Dharma Eye records
that he addressed the assembly "in the damp mountains of Echizen."
Whether that means on the mountain of Eiheiji is not clear.

On the twenty-fifth day of the second month of the same year he went on
retreat at the shrine of Tenjin, and seeing there a poem of Tenjin's on
the theme of looking at plum blossom on a moonlit night, he was moved
to answer it in the same rhymes. Tenjin had written it at eleven years
old, looking at plum blossom by moonlight and setting down what was in
his mind. It runs:

	The moon shines like snow after clear weather;
	the plum blossom is like stars shining out.
	How lovely — the golden mirror turns,
	and above the garden the jade chambers are fragrant.

The Founder answered:

	The green pine — what has it to fear from winter snow?
	On the old tree the plum blossom flies like stars.
