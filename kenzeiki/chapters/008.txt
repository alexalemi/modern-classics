Chapter Eight: The Road Home

In the winter of the third year of Baoqing, the year of the boar, he
took his leave of Tiantong. That very night, at midnight, he was called
into the abbot's room, and Rujing gave him the dharma robe of the
ancestor Furong Daokai, the Precious Mirror Samadhi, the Secret
Teaching of the Five Ranks, and a portrait of himself with his own
inscription on it. And he said:

I give these to you, a man from a barbarous country, as a token of
trust. Go back to your own country and spread the teaching there, and
do good widely to gods and men. Do not live in cities and towns. Do not
go near kings and ministers. Live in the deep mountains and the quiet
valleys, and take in one man or half a man, and do not let my school be
cut off.

— I have taken in one man, or half a one, in the deep mountains and the
hidden valleys. Do not let our school be cut off, and so on.

That same evening he got hold of Foguo's Blue Cliff Collection. He
opened it out and began copying it by hand, and grieved in his heart
that he would never get it all down. All at once a god in white robes
came in, took up a brush and helped him, and it was finished. This was
Hakusan Gongen of Japan.

That winter he sailed from China, and when the ship met a black wind he
was on deck

He sat in silence, and all at once the great being Kannon appeared on
the surface of the sea, riding on a lotus leaf, and the wind and the
waves grew quiet.

So it was that in the first year of Antei by Japanese reckoning he came
ashore at Kawajiri in Higo. The Kannon on a single leaf is a picture
made of that appearance.
