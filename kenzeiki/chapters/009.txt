In the Kangi years he was living quietly at Fukakusa.

Second. On the old site of Gokurakuji at Fukakusa in the district of
Uji, a vow was made to found Koshoji. It was finished in the spring of
the first year of Tenpuku, the year of the snake, and named Kosho
Horinji. The dharma hall was built by the nun Shogaku, and the dharma
seat was put up by the lord of Guseiin.

He built the monks' hall himself, by an appeal for subscriptions in
every quarter. The preamble of it reads:

	An appeal for subscriptions towards the monks' hall of
	Kannon-dori-in at Uji.

	Bowing my head, I respectfully address all the buddhas and
	bodhisattvas of the ten directions, the sages and the assembly
	of monks, gods and men, dragons and the eight kinds of beings,
	and all good men and good women: concerning the wish to found
	one place of practice out of the pure faith that gives a single
	coin.

	The Sutra of the Bodhisattva Precepts says: a child of the
	Buddha should always be teaching all living beings, and should
	found monks' quarters, mountains and woods and gardens and
	fields, and raise stupas, and set up places for the winter and
	summer retreats and for sitting zazen, and every place where
	the way is practised; and if he does not, he commits an offence
	of light defilement.

	So a temple is a place of practice for all the buddhas. The
	Buddhist temples of China copied the monasteries of India, and
	the training halls of Japan should learn from them in the same
	way.

The merit of it is great and deep, and it is handed on from
	country to country and given out to one person after another.

	Since I came back from Song I have carried the wish to found a
	temple, and the days have been long and the months deep with
	it. It was a thing my robe and bowl should have been laid down
	for. Now I have got one excellent site, out beyond Fukakusa, on
	the old ground of Gokurakuji, and it is called Kannon-dori-in.
	It is rank grass and no monastery yet. Here I mean to raise a
	temple of the first rank.

	The most necessary buildings of a temple are the buddha hall,
	the dharma hall and the monks' hall. Of these the buddha hall
	is here already; the dharma hall is not yet; and the monks'
	hall is the most needful of all. It is this I now mean to
	build. Its form is this: a hall of seven bays, with no
	partitions inside it, long platforms set along the walls, the
	assembly of monks living there together, and the practice of
	the way not interrupted for a moment day or night. In the
	middle the sacred monk is enshrined and the assembly sits round
	him. The custom of doing homage to the three treasures in one
	hall has been kept up a long while. The merit of it is great,
	and the work of the Buddha will spread by it.

	Though one might look for a single donor to finish the whole
	thing, I appeal widely in all ten directions, so that many may
	form a good connection with it. This is the excellent way of
	India and of China, and the manner of the monks' life in the
	age of the true dharma and the age of the semblance dharma.

I shall keep the donors' names in the heart of the sacred monk,
	as seed of the wisdom of the ten thousand characters, and as an
	adornment for myself and for others. If among them there should
	be one who goes ahead of the rest and attains the way, I shall
	make him the guide of this assembly. Would he not be a good
	friend indeed?

	And this does not urge on men alone: it should convert the
	heavens and the dragon palace too, and be heard of in the world
	of the immortals and in the halls of the dead. It is simply the
	wheel of the dharma that Shakyamuni turned, and it will reach
	within the dharma realm and beyond it. Respectfully set out.

	A day in the twelfth month of the first year of Katei. The
	chief solicitor, resident of Kannon-dori-in, a monk of the
	Shakya clan.

From that summer the retreat was formally opened.

A board survives with fifteen lines cut into it, and it is now at
Myokakuji in Echizen. The brush strokes are unmistakably the Founder's
own. It reads:

	Respectfully inviting the deities of ten places as protectors of
	Myokakuji.

	First, Kumano. Second, Nissho. Third, Kinpusen. Fourth, Kamo.
	Fifth, Hachiman. Sixth, Gion. Seventh, Sekizan. Eighth, Hiyoshi.

Ninth, Kifune. Tenth, Hakusan.

	The above are invited as set out. A day in the fourth month of
	the first year of Tenpuku.

The first year of Katei, the year of the sheep.

The second year of Katei, the year of the monkey. At the beginning of
the winter retreat he opened the hall at Koshoji.

In the winter of that second year of Katei, the priest Ejo was made
head monk and took up the whisk.

Do not be troubled that the assembly is small. Do not look back at the
fact that you are a beginner. Fenyang had barely six or seven men;
Yaoshan's assembly did not come to ten. Yet all of them practised the
way of the buddhas and ancestors, and that is what was called a
flourishing monastery.

Have you not seen it? One man came to the way at the sound of bamboo;
another cleared his mind at the peach blossom. Is bamboo sharp or dull?
Is a flower deep or shallow, wise or foolish? Flowers open year after
year, and not everyone who sees them awakens. Bamboo rings out again
and again, and not everyone who hears it realises the way. It is only
by the merit of long practice kept up, and by getting the conditions
that come of hard work at the way, that a man awakens and his mind
becomes clear.

It is not that the bamboo's sound alone is sharp; it is not that its
ring is especially deep. Call it wonderful if you like — it does not
sound of itself. And the flower's colour is not especially beautiful
either; it does not open by itself. The one waits on the tile that
strikes it before it makes any sound; the other must get the spring
wind before it opens.

Do not sneer that the head monk is not fit for it. Take up Dongshan's

The third year of Katei, the year of the cock. On the day the summer
retreat was closed he compiled the Rite for Leaving Home and Conferring
the Precepts.

The third year of Ninji, the year of the tiger. On the twelfth day of
the fourth month he gave a dharma talk before Lord Konoe. Afterwards
there was a question: had this school ever been transmitted to our
country in earlier ages?

He answered: since the buddha-dharma of names and forms came to our
country, barely four hundred years and more have passed, and the
spreading of the Buddha-mind school falls exactly in this present time.
In China the buddha-dharma of names and forms was first transmitted in
the Yongping years of Emperor Ming of the Later Han —

On the first day of the fifth month of that same year, he handed on the
great matter to the priest Gi'in.

In the course of that same year the founder of Yura stayed a while in
that province, changed his robe, and received the bodhisattva precepts.
This is to be seen in the Genko Shakusho as well.

On the fifth day of the eighth month of that same year, the recorded
sayings of Rujing of Tiantong reached him for the first time. On the
sixth he gave a discourse in the hall.

On the seventeenth day of the twelfth month of that year he preached at
the private house of the patron, the governor of Izumo. The evidence
for it is the colophon of the fascicle Total Working, which stands
twentieth in the Treasury of the True Dharma Eye: at that time, the
seventeenth day of the twelfth month of the year of the tiger, Ninji,
addressed to the assembly beside Rokuharamitsuji in Yamashiro, in the
quarters of the former governor of Izumo.
