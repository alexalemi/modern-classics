# THE WOODCUTS AND THEIR CAPTIONS.
# 76 of the 238 half-leaves are Daiken's woodcuts, and unlike
# soap-bubbles/ THE CAPTIONS ARE THE SOURCE'S OWN -- so they are
# modernised, not invented (the ball/ rule). They are also the most
# legible text in the book and an independent outline of the story: a
# reading of the prose that contradicts the plate beside it is wrong
# somewhere. Collected here as [leaf] caption / English.
#
# KEY THE ENTRY TO THE LEAF YOU ACTUALLY OPENED. The Kamakura plate was
# first entered here against n028r, which is not a plate at all -- it is
# a page of Menzan's apparatus quoting the Tenzo Kyokun, and readorder
# and layers both said so. Nothing in the toolchain compares this file
# against readorder, so a wrong leaf id here is invisible: the caption
# is right, the plate exists, and only opening n028r finds it. The plate
# is n090r, and it sits exactly where the Kamakura narrative does.
#

       Interior. (A lady behind blinds among piled robes, two
       attendants on the veranda, bamboo and a plum tree in the garden.)

n012r  四歳ニシテ李嶠カ百詠ヲ讀玉フ
       At four years old he reads Li Qiao's hundred poems. (The child
       at a book on the matting, a scholar seated opposite, women
       behind screens, courtiers waiting on the veranda below.)
n017r  洛ヲシノビ出テ木幡ノ山莊ヲ經テ叡山ヘ赴セラル
       Slipping out of the capital in secret, he passes the mountain
       villa at Kohata and makes for Mount Hiei. (The boy alone on a
       path under a crescent moon, pines and blossom on the slopes.)
n020r  山門ノ耆宿ニ法文ヲ質ス
       He puts his question on the dharma-texts to the elders of the
       mountain gate. (The boy kneeling at the edge of the matting;
       four senior monks with books open between them, none of them
       answering.)
n090r  最明寺殿ノ請ニ赴キ鎌倉ニテ菩薩戒ヲ授ケ玉フ
       He goes to Kamakura at Lord Saimyoji's request and confers the
       bodhisattva precepts. (Dogen at a low table with flowers and a
       standing candle, three monks behind him and one seated with his
       back to us; Tokiyori kneeling on a patterned mat below the
       table with a paper before him; a retainer waiting inside the
       shutters and two courtiers on the veranda beyond the pine.)
n029r  師天童山ノ僧臘ノ次第ナキヲ責ル
       The Founder objects that Tiantong keeps no proper order of
       seniority by dharma-age. (Dogen in the courtyard with his arm
       raised, arguing; monks standing about in twos, none of them
       moving.)
n040r  老珪師ヲ天童ノ淨祖ヘ指揮ス
       Old Laogui directs the Founder to Rujing of Tiantong. (Three
       figures on a path low in the picture, one pointing; ridge on

       far hill.)
n078r  大佛寺法堂上梁ノ儀式。コレ今ノ永平寺ナリ
       The ceremony of raising the ridgepole for the dharma hall of
       Daibutsuji. This is the present Eiheiji.
n089l  師自筆ニテ立春大吉ヲ書セラル
       The Founder writes "Great good fortune at the beginning of
       spring" in his own hand. (Dogen at the brush on the matting,
       four monks kneeling round him, an incense burner smoking
       between them, a rose in a vase at the open shoji.)
n093l  筑前博多ノ圓覺寺ヨリ蘭溪禪師ノ書束ヲ送リ来ル
       A packet of letters from Chan master Lanxi is sent up from
       Enkakuji in Hakata, Chikuzen. (Dogen on a round mat with the
       roll open in his hands, a book-table beside him, four monks
       waiting on the boards below.)
n107l  八大人覺ノ卷御示教
       He expounds the fascicle on the Eight Awakenings of Great
       Beings. (The hall open to the garden, monks ranked on the
       matting behind him and lay people seated in rows on the boards
       below; two more coming up the path with a staff.) This was the
       last thing he wrote, in his final illness, and the assembly the
       block shows is the largest in the book.
n110r  室内ヲ經行シテ面前ノ柱ニ法華經ノ文ヲ書付タマフ
       Pacing the room in walking meditation, he writes a passage of
       the Lotus Sutra on the pillar in front of him. (Dogen at the
       post with the brush raised, one attendant standing by; to the
       right, monks about the bedding, a staff and a bowl set down; a
       lay figure keeping watch at the foot of the picture.) This is
       the sickroom in Kyoto, and among the last things the book shows
       him doing.
n111r  東山赤辻ノ寺ニテ火葬シ奉ル
       The cremation, at the temple at Akatsuji in Higashiyama. (Three
       registers: temple roofs among trees on the hillside above; the
       cremation ground in the middle, ringed with standing lanterns
       and banners, smoke going up in a broad wavering column; and
       below, people crossing a plank bridge over the river towards
       it.) A place-cartouche in the middle register names Akatsuji.
n101l  中秋翫月ノ圖幷ニ翫月ノ眞影ニ述贊シタマフ
       Viewing the mid-autumn moon, with his inscription written on
       the moon-viewing portrait. (Dogen upstairs on the open gallery
       with brush and paper, the full moon in a break of cloud; below,
       one monk in a chair with his hand raised and another seated on
       the boards, both looking up.)
n086l  結夏上堂ノ日天花降ル
       Heavenly flowers fall on the day of the discourse opening the
       summer retreat. (Petals drifting across the whole picture;
       monks on the steps and in the yard with their heads back and
       their hands up, one sitting down in astonishment, two lay
       children among them.)
n096l  香氣僧堂ノ内外ニ薫ズ
       A fragrance perfumes the monks' hall, inside and out. (Smoke or
       scent rolling in great scrolled banks across the whole picture;
       three monks on the stone terrace, one with his hand raised, and
       three lay children at the stream below looking up.)
n077l  越前松岡ノ溪ノ奧吉峰ニ分入リ玉フ
       He makes his way in to Yoshimine, deep in the valley at Matsuoka
       in Echizen. (Dogen on the path with his staff and one hand
       raised, a bare-headed monk above him pointing the way on; behind
       them a porter under a hat, a second with a box on his back, and
       four more bearers coming up the slope; ridge on ridge of scrub
       hill and a river winding out to the left.) [The plate sits
       between the Kangen 2 leaves, but the event is the move to
       Yoshimine of 1243; check placement before assembly.]
n087r  布薩ノトキ五色ノ彩雲面前ノ障子ニタナビク
       At the fortnightly recitation, a five-coloured cloud trails
       across the paper screens in front of him. (Dogen at the low
       reading table on the right, an attendant behind him with a fan;
       cloud rolling in over the roof and down the length of the
       veranda; a monk with a tray at the left, another standing with
       his hands joined on the steps, monks seated in rows along the
       boards and lay people crowded in among the pines at the foot of
       the picture.) The document quoted on the facing leaves is the
       villagers' sworn statement about this day, and Ejo kept the
       screens themselves.
n094r  玄明首座ヲ擯出シ坐禪ノ床ヲ截リ取セ玉フ
       He expels the head monk Genmyo and has his sitting platform cut
       out and taken away. (Upstairs in the monks' hall, three monks
       levering up the boards with a bar and a chisel while a fourth
       leans in from below with a tool and Dogen stands at the right
       with his hands in his sleeves; below, in the corridor, two monks
       with their heads together, and one more alone at the foot of the
       steps.) The plate for the story on n093r, and it is the platform,
       not the man, that the block shows being removed.
n097r  羅漢供法會ニ佛像畫像光リヲ放チヘタ東岩ノ松ノ上ニ應現アリ
       At the dharma assembly for the offering to the arhats, the
       images and the paintings give out light, and the arhats appear
       above the pine on the rock to the east. (Three haloed figures at
       the altar under the eaves with flowers, candles and offering
       stands; five monks prostrate in a row on the boards below; two
       lay attendants at the left; and out on the right, small among
       the pine branches on the rock, the sixteen arhats and their
       retinue with their halos, above a crowd of lay people watching
       from the yard.) The pine is the one that has been called the
       Arhat Pine ever since.
