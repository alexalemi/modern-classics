KENZEIKI -- UNCERTAIN READINGS
Every character I could not read with confidence in BOTH impressions,
with the reading taken and why. A guessed character that reads
plausibly is the defect this edition cannot otherwise catch, so nothing
here is resolved silently.

n010l  第一皇子 ... 御諱ハ為仁
  The leaf opens on the REIGNING EMPEROR, not on Dogen: Tamehito is
  Tsuchimikado, first son of Go-Toba, whose mother Shomeimon-in was
  Minamoto no Michichika's daughter. The passage is dating Dogen's
  birth by the reign and establishing the eminence of the house he is
  claimed for -- it is not saying these are Dogen's own parents, and a
  translation that reads it that way would invent a false parentage on
  the book's first page. Checked against both impressions.

n010l  後來大器ト爲ル可シ
  The 可爲大器 order is written with kaeriten; rendered here in the
  Japanese order it is read in.

n018l  MY OWN ERROR, caught and corrected, and recorded because it is
  the exact defect this edition is most exposed to. My first draft of
  Koin's answer read "You should go to Eiheiji -- no: to Kenninji".
  Eiheiji appears nowhere in the passage and did not yet exist; Dogen
  founded it some thirty years later. The source says only 須ラク建仁寺
  ノ榮西ニ參ズベシ, go and consult Eisai of Kenninji. An invented place
  name that reads plausibly is precisely what no check here can see, so
  every English line is now written against the transcript rather than
  from the sense of the page.

n018l  Whether Dogen actually met Eisai, who died in 1215, is disputed
  by modern scholarship. The Kenzeiki says he entered his room, and the
  Verne rule governs: it stands as the book has it, unhedged.

n019r  親〔炙〕滅以來
  The second character of the inscription's second line is unclear in
  both impressions. Read as 炙 -- 親炙, to be close to and be taught by,
  which the first line's 徹通親 sets up, so 親炙滅以來 is "since the one
  he was taught by so closely was consumed". Marked rather than
  resolved; the whole line is a Chinese verse inscription and the
  reading is not certain.

n021l  大戒ヲ明全ニ傳授セリ
  Read as Dogen RECEIVING the precepts from Myozen, not conferring them
  on him. The particle ニ can mark the source in this construction, and
  the alternative reading reverses master and disciple: Myozen was the
  senior and Dogen twenty-three. A wrong reading here would invert the
  transmission the whole book is about, so it is recorded rather than
  passed over.
