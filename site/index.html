Political Philosophy

  • Leviathan Thomas Hobbes (1651) · ~16-hour read · epub Why we need government at all — the founding argument of the modern state, written amid civil war.
  • Two Treatises of Government John Locke (1689) · ~7-hour read · epub The demolition of the divine right of kings, and the case for government by consent.
  • The Social Contract Jean-Jacques Rousseau (1762) · ~3-hour read · epub The general will, the social contract, and the most famous opening line in political philosophy.
  • The Prince Niccolò Machiavelli (1532) · ~2-hour read · epub The infamous handbook of power politics.
  • Common Sense Thomas Paine (1776) · ~90-minute read · epub The pamphlet that talked America into independence.
  • Reflections on the Revolution in France Edmund Burke (1790) · ~7-hour read · epub The book that invented conservatism — a warning, written in 1790, that a society is not a machine you can take apart and rebuild to a drawing.
  • Cato's Letters John Trenchard and Thomas Gordon (1720–1723) · ~3-hour read · epub Eighteen selected letters on liberty, power, and free speech — the columns that taught America to argue for revolution.
  • Utopia Thomas More (1516) · ~3-hour read · epub The island where everything works — and the deadpan joke is that you can't tell how much More means it.
  • The Federalist Papers Alexander Hamilton, James Madison, and John Jay (1787–1788) · ~12-hour read · epub The Constitution's authors explain — and sell — their design.
  • Democracy in America Alexis de Tocqueville (1835/1840) · ~18-hour read · epub A young Frenchman tours the United States and sees the democratic future — flaws and all.
  • Democracy in America (From the French) Alexis de Tocqueville (1835/1840) · ~19-hour read · epub A second pass, translated directly from Tocqueville's French.

Economics

  • The Autobiography of Benjamin Franklin Benjamin Franklin (1771–1790) · ~4-hour read · epub The original American life story — runaway apprentice to statesman, told with a wink.
  • The Way to Wealth Benjamin Franklin (1758) · ~15-minute read · epub Poor Richard's proverbs rolled into one sly speech — delivered to a crowd that applauds, then ignores it.
  • The Wealth of Nations Adam Smith (1776) · ~22-hour read · epub The book that invented economics: pins, markets, and the invisible hand.
  • An Essay on the Principle of Population Thomas Malthus (1798) · ~4-hour read · epub Malthus' unsettling arithmetic of people and food.
  • Progress and Poverty Henry George (1879) · ~12-hour read · epub Why progress and poverty rise together — and George's famous remedy, the land tax.

The Ancient World

  • The Art of War Sun Tzu (c. 5th century BC) · ~4-hour read · epub The oldest book on war, with the commentary most editions cut: two thousand years of Chinese generals arguing with Sun Tzu and with each other.
  • Dialogues Plato (399–347 BC) · ~42-hour read · epub The complete dialogues — Socrates on trial, in the agora, and at the limits of knowledge.
  • The Memorabilia Xenophon (c. 371 BC) · ~4-hour read · epub Socrates remembered by his other student — practical, sociable, and useful, with the Choice of Heracles.
  • Nicomachean Ethics Aristotle (c. 340 BC) · ~6-hour read · epub The original inquiry into the good life — happiness, virtue as habit, the mean, and friendship.
  • Characters Theophrastus (c. 319 BC) · ~40-minute read · epub Thirty ruthless comic sketches of annoying Athenians — every one of them still alive and probably in your contacts.
  • Meditations Marcus Aurelius (c. 170–180 AD) · ~3-hour read · epub A Roman emperor's private notes to himself on how to live.
  • The Enchiridion Epictetus (c. 125 AD) · ~30-minute read · epub The Stoic pocket manual — fifty-one drills for keeping your head, from a philosopher born enslaved.
  • The Practice Instructions Dōgen (1227–1234) · ~40-minute read · epub Three short manuals from the founder of Sōtō Zen: how to sit, how to study, and how to cook. From the Chinese.
  • The Illustrated Life of Dōgen Kenzei (c. 1470; illustrated 1806) · ~90-minute read · epub The oldest life of the founder of Sōtō Zen, written so that ordinary people could read it — with his letters, his kitchen rules, and the farmers' sworn statement about a coloured cloud.
  • The Discourses Epictetus (c. 108 AD) · ~8-hour read · epub The lectures the Enchiridion was cut down from — a former slave telling Rome's young gentlemen that they are the ones in chains.
  • On Duties Cicero (44 BC) · ~4-hour read · epub Cicero's last book, a letter to his son — translated from the Latin: nothing dishonorable is ever truly useful.
  • Tusculan Disputations Cicero (45 BC) · ~5-hour read · epub Five days on death, pain, grief, and the passions — from the Latin, with the Sword of Damocles.
  • Roman Lives Plutarch (c. 100 AD) · ~8-hour read · epub The fall of the Republic in five lives — Caesar, Cato, Cicero, Brutus, Antony — five angles on one catastrophe.
  • Moral Letters to Lucilius Seneca (c. 65 AD) · ~13-hour read · epub All 124 letters from a Stoic — a daily course in living, from saving time to facing the end.
  • The Histories Herodotus (c. 430 BC) · ~15-hour read · epub The first history book — Persia, Egypt, and the Greek wars, with magnificent detours.
  • History of the Peloponnesian War Thucydides (c. 431 BC) · ~14-hour read · epub Power, plague, and the ruin of Athens, by the first analytical historian.
  • Commentaries on the Gallic War Julius Caesar (c. 50 BC) · ~4-hour read · epub Caesar's own dispatches from the conquest of Gaul.
  • Confessions Augustine of Hippo (c. 400 AD) · ~13-hour read · epub The first autobiography — the stolen pears, the garden at Milan, the night at Ostia — every sentence addressed to God, so that reading it is overhearing.
  • The Consolation of Philosophy Boethius (524 AD) · ~4-hour read · epub Waiting to be executed, the last Roman philosopher is visited by Philosophy, who tells him that nothing he has lost was ever his. With all thirty-nine poems.

Philosophy & Science

  • Selected Essays Michel de Montaigne (1580–1588) · ~9-hour read · epub The man who invented the essay, talking to you across four centuries about death, friendship, cannibals, and himself.
  • Dialogue Concerning the Two Chief World Systems Galileo Galilei (1632) · ~16-hour read · epub Four days of brilliant argument about whether the Earth moves — the vernacular bestseller that got its author hauled before the Inquisition, translated from Galileo's own Italian.
  • Philosophical Works René Descartes (1637–1644) · ~5-hour read · epub Descartes doubts everything and rebuilds from “I think, therefore I am.”
  • Ethics Baruch Spinoza (1677) · ~6-hour read · epub God and Nature are one thing, free will is a name for our ignorance, and forty-eight emotions are defined in order — all set out like a geometry textbook.
  • An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding David Hume (1748) · ~5-hour read · epub Why experience can never prove the sun will rise tomorrow, and why Hume thinks that need not worry you.
  • A Vindication of the Rights of Woman Mary Wollstonecraft (1792) · ~6-hour read · epub Not that women are equal, but that nobody knows what women are like, because their character is manufactured by their upbringing. Written in six weeks, and angrier than its reputation.
  • On Liberty John Stuart Mill (1859) · ~5-hour read · epub One principle pushed as far as it will go: the only ground for interfering with anyone's freedom is preventing harm to somebody else.
  • The Subjection of Women John Stuart Mill (1869) · ~4-hour read · epub The companion to On Liberty — marriage as the law then stood was bondage, and the real obstacle is that most men cannot yet imagine living with an equal.
  • On the Origin of Species Charles Darwin (1859) · ~14-hour read · epub Darwin's patient, overwhelming case for evolution by natural selection.
  • On the Various Forces of Nature Michael Faraday (1860) · ~2-hour read · epub · original text (epub) Six Christmas lectures for children on gravity, heat, magnetism and electricity, arriving at the discovery that they are one force in different forms. With the original figures.
  • The Chemical History of a Candle Michael Faraday (1861) · ~3-hour read · epub · original text (epub) The most famous science lectures ever given: the whole of chemistry drawn out of one burning candle, ending with the news that you are a slow candle yourself.
  • Sound John Tyndall (1867/1875) · ~8-hour read · epub · original text (epub) Sound taught with a row of five boys, a shivering glass tube and a flame that ducks at the letter S — then Tyndall discovers the invisible clouds that swallow fog signals on clear days.
  • Soap Bubbles and the Forces Which Mould Them C. V. Boys (1890) · ~2-hour read · epub · original text (epub) Three Christmas lectures for children on why a bubble is round, why a jet of water breaks into beads, and how to make a fountain sing.
  • Waves and Ripples in Water, Air, and Aether J. A. Fleming (1902) · ~5-hour read · epub · original text (epub) Six Christmas lectures arguing that a ripple, a ship's wake, a sung note, a petal's colour and Marconi's new wireless signal are all one thing in different media.
  • Star-land Robert S. Ball (1889/1899) · ~6-hour read · epub · original text (epub) The funniest of the Christmas lecturers takes you from the sun to the nebulae, measuring the sky with two children and a pair of scissors.
  • Light Visible and Invisible Silvanus P. Thompson (1897) · ~5-hour read · epub · original text (epub) Six Christmas lectures on light, given thirteen months after the X-ray was announced. Asked what it is, its discoverer says: I do not know.

Fiction & Ideas

  • The Life of Benvenuto Cellini Benvenuto Cellini (1558–1567) · ~13-hour read · epub A Florentine goldsmith dictates his own life while he works: two popes, the sack of Rome, several killings, a prison escape, and the Perseus cast with the roof on fire. From the Italian.
  • Grimm's Household Tales Jacob and Wilhelm Grimm (1812–1857) · ~20-hour read · epub All two hundred tales and ten legends, complete — the famous ones and the hundred and sixty that nobody prints.
  • The Thousand Nights and a Night Anonymous (c. 800–1400) · ~16-hour read · epub Shahrazad's frame and eighteen of the great tales — the Fisherman and the Jinni, the Hunchback, Sindbad, Aladdin, Ali Baba — with Burton's fake-antique costume taken off.
  • The Odyssey Homer (c. 700 BC) · ~9-hour read · epub A soldier ten years trying to get home, a wife holding off a houseful of suitors, and a son who was a baby when he left. From the Greek, all twenty-four books, as prose.
  • Paradiso Dante Alighieri (c. 1308–1321) · ~4-hour read · epub The third book of the Comedy: heaven as a bird waiting for dawn and a baby reaching for milk, and Saint Peter calling the papacy a sewer. From the Italian, tercet for tercet.
  • Purgatorio Dante Alighieri (c. 1308–1320) · ~4-hour read · epub The second book of the Comedy, where everyone is going to be saved and knows it — the dead embrace, tease, and ask after old friends. From the Italian, tercet for tercet.
  • Inferno Dante Alighieri (c. 1308–1320) · ~4-hour read · epub Led down through Hell by a dead poet, Dante meets people he knew — popes, neighbours, a former teacher — still arguing Florentine politics. From the Italian, tercet for tercet.
  • Don Quixote Miguel de Cervantes (1605–1615) · ~31-hour read · epub A gentleman reads too many books of chivalry and rides out to fix the world with a labourer who talks in proverbs. Both volumes, all 126 chapters, from the Spanish.
  • Pillow Problems Lewis Carroll (1893) · ~2-hour read · epub Seventy-two problems the author of Alice worked out in his head, in bed, in the dark — and an introduction saying exactly which thoughts he was keeping out.
  • Symbolic Logic Lewis Carroll (1896) · ~5-hour read · epub The author of Alice teaching logic to children as a board game with red and grey counters — cats that understand French, and a club with no members.
  • Euclid and His Modern Rivals Lewis Carroll (1879/1885) · ~5-hour read · epub The author of Alice puts every geometry textbook of his day on trial, as a farce: the ghost of Euclid against Herr Niemand, Mr. Nobody, who loses every case.
  • A Tangled Tale Lewis Carroll (1885) · ~2-hour read · epub Ten comic stories with a puzzle hidden in each — then Carroll's gleeful review of the wrong answers his readers sent in.
  • The Pilgrim's Progress John Bunyan (1678/1684) · ~8-hour read · epub The most famous journey in English — the Slough of Despond, Vanity Fair, and the key called Promise that was in his pocket all along. Both parts complete.
  • Metamorphoses Ovid (8 CE) · ~11-hour read · epub The great storybook of classical myth — Daphne, Phaethon, Narcissus, Icarus, Orpheus, Pygmalion, Midas and hundreds more. All fifteen books, from the Latin.
  • True History Lucian of Samosata (c. 175 AD) · ~1-hour read · epub The first science fiction story: a voyage to the moon, war between planets, and a narrator who swears that he lies.
  • The Decameron Giovanni Boccaccio (1353) · ~20-hour read · epub Ten friends flee the plague and tell a hundred stories — bawdy, sly, and humane.
  • Candide, or Optimism Voltaire (1759) · ~2-hour read · epub Voltaire's whirlwind satire of optimism in a broken world.
  • Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Sea Jules Verne (1870) · ~12-hour read · epub Captain Nemo's Nautilus carries three captives beneath every sea — the complete novel from Verne's French, not the abridged Victorian version.
  • Around the World in Eighty Days Jules Verne (1872) · ~6-hour read · epub Phileas Fogg bets £20,000 that he can circle the globe in eighty days — complete from the French, with a final trick of the calendar.
  • A Journey to the Center of the Earth Jules Verne (1864) · ~6-hour read · epub A coded parchment, a dead Icelandic volcano, and a plunge into a lost world underground — complete from the French, not the mangled Victorian version.
  • Flatland: A Romance of Many Dimensions Edwin A. Abbott (1884) · ~2-hour read · epub Geometry as social satire: a square discovers the third dimension.
