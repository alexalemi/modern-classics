MILL — RUNNING CONSISTENCY LEDGER
=================================
Read text_analysis.txt first. This file is the accumulated decisions;
anything locked here is locked, and re-glossing a locked term is a
defect, not a courtesy.

FILE / PART DISCIPLINE — THE quixote TRAP, AND IT IS LIVE HERE
--------------------------------------------------------------
NINE OF THE TWELVE FILES ARE CHAPTER PARTS. Check the manifest for
"of" > 1 BEFORE writing any file. The shape is exactly:

    Chapter Two: On the Liberty of Thought and Discussion
    (Part 2 of 3)

    <body>

Parts 2 and later NEVER re-introduce the chapter, and non-final parts
NEVER conclude it. assemble.strip_front drops the first non-blank line
of EVERY file in a group and takes the heading from part 1 only, so a
wrong heading in a later part vanishes silently and a missing part
marker is invisible to verify.py. Nothing catches this but reading the
manifest first.

Headings, exactly, and never anything else:
    Epigraph
    Dedication
    Chapter One: Introductory
    Chapter Two: On the Liberty of Thought and Discussion
    Chapter Three: On Individuality, as One of the Elements of Well-Being
    Chapter Four: On the Limits of Society's Authority over the Individual
    Chapter Five: Applications
WORD-FORM numbers, deliberately: assemble.CHAP_LINE matches only
"Chapter <digits>:" and would nest these under a Part divider that does
not exist. Cross-references in the text use the same word form
("as I argued in Chapter Two"), because the reader has one long page.

MARKUP
------
The pipeline is markup-free with ONE exception: *x* and _x_ render as
<em>. So:
  - NO asterisks except emphasis. Mill's three section breaks in
    Chapter Two ride through as "* * *" lines and must be left exactly
    as they are; they are horizontal rules, not emphasis.
  - NEVER write an all-caps line: assemble.py reads one as a heading.
  - A tab indent is a quotation block. Only the Epigraph uses one.
  - Book titles go in *emphasis*, as the source has them.
  - The opening asterisk may not follow a word character, so write
    "can *not*" and never "can*not*" -- the latter ships as literal
    asterisks and the marker count still balances.

SENSITIVE PASSAGES — DECIDED IN ADVANCE, ALL LOGGED
---------------------------------------------------
KEPT ENTIRE AND UNHEDGED (the Verne rule), with NO note anywhere in the
volume: the despotism-and-barbarians exception and "backward states of
society ... in its nonage" (Chapter One); Mormons and polygamy and
"a retrograde step in civilisation" (Chapter Four); China as a warning;
the attack on the Calvinistic theory of human nature; and every
application in Chapter Five, including his position that a man who
cannot support children should not be allowed to have them.

ONE CHANGE, and only one, in Chapter One's list of moralities produced
by an ascendant class: "between planters and negroes" is rendered
"between planters and the people they enslaved". The Verne rule
protects a dated CLAIM that is part of the author's subject; Mill's
subject there is the morality that power produces, and that claim
survives entire when the noun is replaced by the relationship. It is
MORE explicit, not less -- the ball/ Leonid precedent and the hume
"Laplander or Negro" precedent. In the same list "roturiers" becomes
"commoners". Nothing else in the sentence moves.

RATIO
-----
Target 0.90-1.05 per file. EN->EN modernisation runs about 1:1 here:
unstacking a periodic sentence costs roughly what cutting the
throat-clearing saves. Verify with --min-ratio 0.85 --max-ratio 1.15.
A file under 0.85 is summarising -- and Mill's qualifications are not
padding, they are the argument being careful.

FOOTNOTES
---------
Nine, already inlined by prep.py as "Footnote: " paragraphs. Keep the
prefix exactly, keep them where they are, and translate them in Mill's
own voice -- they are his. Every name, court and date inside them is a
fact and is checked mechanically.

CHECKS
------
    python3 verify.py mill --min-ratio 0.85 --max-ratio 1.15
    python3 mill/check.py
check.py covers what verify.py structurally cannot see: heading and
part-marker parity against the manifest, emphasis parity against the
source and that every marker actually RENDERS, the asterism count, a
counted (not set-difference) numeric diff, and the sweep for archaism
that should not have survived.
