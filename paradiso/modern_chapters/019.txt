Canto Twenty

	When the one who lights the whole world
	goes so far down out of our hemisphere
	that the day is used up on every side,

	the sky, which was kindled by him alone before,
	suddenly shows itself again
	by many lights, in which one light is shining;

	and this act of the sky came into my mind
	when the standard of the world and of its leaders
	fell silent in the blessed beak;

	because all those living lights,
	shining far more, began songs
	that slip and fall out of my memory.

	O sweet love, cloaking yourself in laughter,
	how burning you seemed in those flutes
	which had nothing but holy thoughts for breath!

	When the dear and shining stones
	I had seen the sixth light jewelled with
	put silence to the angelic pealing,

	I seemed to hear the murmuring of a river
	that comes down clear from rock to rock,
	showing how full its source is.

	And as a sound takes its shape at the neck
	of the lute, and as the wind that gets in
	takes its shape at the vent of the bagpipe,

	so, with the delay of waiting taken away,
	that murmuring of the eagle went up
	through its neck as though the neck were hollow.

	There it became a voice, and from there it came out
	through its beak in the form of words,
	such as the heart I wrote them in was waiting for.

	"The part in me that sees and bears the sun
	in mortal eagles," it began to me,
	"must now be looked at steadily,

	because, of the fires I make my figure with,
	the ones with which the eye sparkles in my head
	are the highest of all their ranks.

	He who shines in the middle as the pupil
	was the singer of the Holy Spirit,
	who carried the ark from town to town:

	now he knows what his song deserved,
	so far as it was the effect of his own counsel,
	by a reward that is just as great.

	Of the five who make a circle for my eyebrow,
	the one nearest my beak
	comforted the little widow for her son:

	now he knows how dearly it costs
	not to follow Christ, by the experience
	of this sweet life and of its opposite.

	And the one who comes next on the circumference
	I am speaking of, on the upper arc,
	put off death by true repentance:

	now he knows that the eternal judgment
	is not changed when a worthy prayer
	makes tomorrow of today down there.

	The next one, with the laws and with me,
	out of a good intention that bore bad fruit,
	became Greek in order to give way to the shepherd:

	now he knows how the harm that came out
	of his good doing does him no injury,
	although the world has been destroyed by it.

	And the one you see on the downward arc
	was William, whom that land weeps for
	which weeps because Charles and Frederick are alive:

	now he knows how heaven falls in love
	with a just king, and he shows it still
	by the look of his own brightness.

	Who would believe, down in the erring world,
	that Ripheus the Trojan was the fifth
	of the holy lights in this ring?

	Now he knows a great deal of the divine grace
	that the world cannot see,
	although his sight does not make out the bottom of it."

	As a lark that ranges through the air,
	singing first, and then falls silent, content
	with the last sweetness that fills it,

	so seemed to me the image of the stamp
	of the eternal pleasure, at whose wanting
	each thing becomes what it is.

	And although I was there to my own doubt
	as glass is to the colour it wears,
	the doubt could not bear to wait its turn in silence,

	but drove "What things are these?" out of my mouth
	by the force of its own weight,
	at which I saw great festivals of flashing.

	Then, straight after, with its eye more kindled,
	the blessed sign answered me,
	so as not to keep me hanging in my wonder:

	"I see that you believe these things
	because I say them, but you do not see how;
	so that, though they are believed, they are hidden.

	You do like a man who takes in a thing well
	by its name, but cannot see what it is in itself
	unless somebody else brings it out.

	Regnum celorum — the kingdom of heaven — suffers
	violence from warm love and from living hope,
	which beat the divine will:

	not the way one man overcomes another,
	but they beat it because it wants to be beaten,
	and, beaten, it beats them with its own kindness.

	The first life of the eyebrow and the fifth
	make you wonder, because you see
	the country of the angels painted with them.

	They did not leave their bodies, as you believe,
	as pagans, but as Christians, in firm faith
	in the feet that were to suffer and in the feet that had.

	For the one came back to his bones from hell,
	where nobody ever comes back
	to a good will; and that was the wages of living hope —

	of living hope, which put such power
	into the prayers made to God to raise him up
	that his will could be moved.

	The glorious soul I am speaking of,
	come back into the flesh, where it was a short while,
	believed in him who could help it;

	and, believing, it was kindled into such a fire
	of true love that at its second death
	it was worthy to come to this festival.

	The other, by a grace that trickles from so deep
	a spring that no creature ever pushed
	its eye as far as the first wave,

	set all his love down there on righteousness:
	so that, from grace to grace, God opened his eye
	to our redemption that was to come;

	and so he believed in it, and from then on
	he could not bear the stench of paganism any longer,
	and he rebuked the crooked people for it.

	Those three ladies you saw at the right wheel
	stood as baptism for him,
	more than a thousand years before baptizing began.

	O predestination, how far away
	your root is from the sight of those
	who cannot see the first cause whole!

	And you, mortals, keep yourselves tight
	in your judging: for we, who see God,
	do not know all the chosen yet;

	and such a lack is sweet to us,
	because our good is refined in this good,
	that what God wills, we will too."

	So, by that divine image,
	a gentle medicine was given me
	to clear my short sight.

	And as a good lute player makes the quivering
	of the string go with a good singer,
	which gives the song more pleasure,

	so, while it spoke, I remember
	that I saw the two blessed lights
	move their little flames along with the words,

	exactly as the blinking of two eyes keeps time.
