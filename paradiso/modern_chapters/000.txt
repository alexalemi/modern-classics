Canto One

	The glory of him who moves all things
	goes through the universe and shines again
	in one part more and in another less.

	I was in the heaven that takes most
	of his light, and saw things which whoever
	comes down from up there cannot and does not know how to tell again;

	because, drawing near to what it longs for,
	our understanding goes so deep
	that memory cannot follow it back.

	Still, whatever of the holy kingdom
	I was able to hoard up in my mind
	shall now be the matter of my song.

	Oh good Apollo, for this last labour
	make me such a vessel of your power
	as you require for giving the beloved laurel.

	Up to here one peak of Parnassus
	has been enough for me; but now I need
	both of them, to enter the course that is left.

	Come into my breast and breathe there
	as you did when you drew Marsyas
	out of the sheath of his own limbs.

	Oh divine power, if you lend yourself to me
	enough for me to show the shadow of the blessed kingdom
	stamped inside my head,

	you will see me come to the foot of your beloved tree
	and crown myself with the leaves
	which the matter, and you, will make me worthy of.

	So rarely, father, are they gathered
	for the triumph of a caesar or a poet —
	the fault and the shame of human wanting —

	that the Peneian branch ought to give birth to gladness
	in the glad Delphic god
	whenever it makes anybody thirsty for itself.

	A great flame follows a small spark:
	perhaps after me, with better voices,
	men will pray so that Cyrrha answers.

	The lamp of the world rises on mortals
	through different mouths; but from the one
	that joins four circles with three crosses

	it comes out on a better course and with a better star,
	and tempers and seals the wax of the world
	more after its own fashion.

	Such a mouth had made it morning there
	and evening here, and almost all that hemisphere
	was white there, and the other part black,

	when I saw Beatrice turned to her left
	and looking into the sun:
	no eagle ever fixed on it so.

	And as a second ray is used to come out
	of the first and go back up again,
	just like a pilgrim who wants to go home,

	so out of her act, poured through my eyes
	into my imagination, mine was made,
	and I fixed my eyes on the sun beyond our custom.

	Much is allowed there which is not allowed
	here to our powers, thanks to the place
	made as the proper home of the human race.

	I did not bear it long, nor so briefly
	that I did not see it sparkling all round,
	like iron that comes boiling out of the fire;

	and suddenly it seemed that day was added
	to day, as if he who has the power
	had adorned the sky with a second sun.

	Beatrice stood with her eyes fixed
	wholly on the eternal wheels; and I fixed mine
	on her, taken back from up there.

	Looking at her, I became inside
	what Glaucus became when he tasted the grass
	that made him a companion of the other gods in the sea.

	To pass beyond the human cannot be told
	in words; so let the example be enough
	for anyone whom grace keeps the experience for.

	Whether I was only that part of me
	which you created last, love who govern the heavens,
	you know, who lifted me with your light.

	When the wheel which you make everlasting
	by being longed for drew my attention to itself
	with the harmony you tune and divide,

	so much of the sky then seemed to me alight
	with the flame of the sun that rain or river
	never made a lake so wide.

	The newness of the sound and the great light
	kindled in me such a longing to know their cause
	as I had never felt so keenly.

	So she, who saw me as I saw myself,
	to quiet my stirred-up mind
	opened her mouth before I opened mine to ask,

	and began: "You make yourself dull
	with false imagining, so that you do not see
	what you would see if you had shaken it off.

	You are not on earth, as you believe;
	but lightning, running away from its own place,
	never ran as you do, coming back to yours."

	If I was stripped of my first doubt
	by those short smiling words,
	I was caught inside a new one all the more,

	and said: "I was content already, and at rest
	from great wondering; but now I wonder
	how I rise through these light bodies."

	At which she, after a sigh of pity,
	turned her eyes towards me with the look
	a mother has for a child in a fever,

	and began: "All things whatever
	have order among themselves, and this is the form
	that makes the universe like God.

	Here the high creatures see the print
	of the eternal worth, which is the end
	for which the said rule was made.

	In the order I speak of, all natures lean,
	by their different lots, nearer
	to their beginning and less near;

	so that they move to different harbours
	over the great sea of being, each one
	with an instinct given it to carry it.

	This is what carries the fire towards the moon;
	this is the mover in mortal hearts;
	this binds the earth together and makes it one;

	and this bow shoots not only the creatures
	that are outside intelligence,
	but those that have understanding and love.

	The providence that orders all this
	keeps the heaven quiet with its own light
	inside which the fastest-turning sphere goes round;

	and there now, as to a place appointed,
	the strength of that string carries us,
	which aims whatever it looses at a glad mark.

	It is true that, as a shape often does not answer
	to what the art intended,
	because the material is deaf to answering,

	so the creature sometimes turns aside
	from this course, having the power,
	though driven this way, to bend another way;

	and as one may see fire fall from a cloud,
	so the first impulse, twisted by a false pleasure,
	drives it back to the ground.

	If I judge rightly, you should wonder no more
	at your rising than at a stream
	coming down from a high mountain to the bottom.

	The marvel would be if you, with nothing
	in your way, had settled down below,
	as stillness would be in living fire on the ground."

	Then she turned her face back towards the sky.
