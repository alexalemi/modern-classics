Canto Nine

	After your Charles, beautiful Clemence,
	had enlightened me, he told me of the betrayals
	his own children would have to suffer;

	but he said: "Say nothing, and let the years go round";
	so I can say no more than this: a just weeping
	will follow the wrong that is going to be done to you.

	And already the life of that holy light
	had turned back to the Sun that fills it
	as to the good that is enough for everything.

	Ah, deluded souls, creatures without reverence,
	who twist your hearts away from such a good
	and set your foreheads towards emptiness!

	And then another of those splendours
	came towards me, and showed its will to please me
	by growing brighter outside.

	Beatrice's eyes, which were fixed on me
	as before, gave me the sure assurance
	of dear consent to what I longed for.

	"Ah, quickly make up to me what I want,
	blessed spirit," I said, "and give me proof
	that I can reflect in you what I am thinking."

	At which the light which was still new to me,
	out of its own depth, where it had been singing before,
	went on like one who takes pleasure in doing good:

	"In that part of corrupt Italy
	that lies between Venice
	and the springs of the Brenta and the Piave,

	there rises a hill, not very high,
	from which a firebrand came down once, the tyrant Ezzelino,
	and laid waste the whole country round.

	I was born from one root with him:
	Cunizza I was called, and I shine here
	because the light of this star overcame me;

	but I gladly forgive myself
	the cause of my being placed so low, and it does not trouble me,
	which perhaps would seem hard to your ordinary people to understand.

	Of this shining and precious jewel
	of our heaven, the one nearest to me, the poet Folco,
	great fame is left on earth; and before it dies

	this hundredth year will be five times over.
	See, then, whether a man ought to make himself excel,
	so that a second life, his fame, may follow the first.

	And the mob that lives now between the Tagliamento
	and the Adige gives this no thought,
	and does not repent yet, though it has been beaten;

	but soon Padua will turn the water
	that bathes Vicenza red at the marsh, with its own blood,
	because its people are stubborn against their duty;

	and at Treviso, where the Sile and the Cagnano meet,
	a man rules and goes about with his head high
	for whom the net is already being woven.

	Feltre will weep yet for the crime
	of its faithless bishop, which will be so foul
	that nobody was ever put in the prison of Malta for the like.

	The vat would have to be very wide
	to hold the blood of the men of Ferrara
	that this obliging priest will hand over

	to show himself a good party man;
	whoever weighed it ounce by ounce would be tired.
	And such gifts will suit the way that country lives.

	Above us are mirrors, the angels you call Thrones,
	in which God's judgments shine back to us;
	so we are right to speak as we do."

	Here she fell silent, and seemed to me
	to have turned to something else, by going back
	into the wheeling dance she had been in before.

	The other glad spirit, which she had already told me
	was a precious thing, became in my sight
	like a fine ruby struck by the sun.

	Up there a spirit grows brighter by being glad,
	as we laugh here; but down in Hell
	the shade grows darker outside as the mind grows sad.

	"God sees everything, and your seeing goes into him,"
	I said, "blessed spirit, so that no wish
	can steal itself away from you.

	Why then does your voice, which always delights heaven
	together with the singing of those devout fires,
	the Seraphim, who make themselves a cowl of six wings,

	not give me what I long for without my asking?
	I would not be waiting for you to ask,
	if I could see into you as you see into me."

	"The greatest valley that water fills,"
	his words began then, "apart from the ocean
	that garlands the earth, is the Mediterranean, and it

	runs so far eastward between its quarrelling shores
	that the sky's noon-line at one end
	is the horizon at the other.

	I lived on the shore of that valley
	between the Ebro in Spain and the Magra, which for a short stretch
	divides the Genoese from the Tuscans.

	On almost the same line of sunset and sunrise
	sit Bougie in Africa and the town I came from, Marseilles,
	which once made its harbour warm with its own blood, when Caesar took it.

	Folco those people called me
	to whom my name was known; and this heaven
	is stamped with me, as I was with it;

	for Dido, wronging both
	her dead husband and Aeneas's dead wife, did not burn with love more than I did,
	as long as it suited my hair, before it went grey,

	nor Phyllis of Thrace, who was deceived
	by Demophoon, nor Hercules
	when he had Iole shut in his heart.

	Yet here there is no repenting, but laughter:
	not at the fault, which does not come back to mind,
	but at the divine power that ordered and provided everything.

	Here we gaze into the art that makes
	so great a work so beautiful, and we make out the good
	by which the world above turns the world below.

	But so that you may carry away all the wishes
	that are born in this sphere fulfilled,
	I must go on further still.

	You want to know who is in this light
	which sparkles here beside me
	like a ray of the sun in clear water.

	Know now that Rahab, the harlot of Jericho, is at rest in there;
	and being joined to our order,
	she holds the highest rank in it.

	Before any other soul was taken up
	into the triumph of Christ, she came
	to this heaven, where the shadow cast by your world comes to its point.

	It was right to leave her in some heaven
	as a trophy of that high victory
	which was won with both palms, nailed to the cross,

	because she helped Joshua to his first victory
	in the Holy Land,
	which the pope hardly remembers now.

	Your city, Florence, which was planted by the one
	who first turned his back on his own maker, the devil,
	and whose envy has caused so much weeping,

	mints and spreads the accursed flower, the gold florin,
	that has led the sheep and the lambs astray,
	because it has turned the shepherd into a wolf.

	For its sake the Gospel and the great teachers of the Church
	are abandoned, and only the books of canon law
	are studied, as their well-thumbed margins show.

	This is what the pope and the cardinals attend to;
	their thoughts do not go to Nazareth,
	where Gabriel opened his wings.

	But the Vatican and the other holy places
	of Rome, which have been the burial ground
	of the soldiers who followed Peter, the martyrs,

	will soon be freed of this adultery."
