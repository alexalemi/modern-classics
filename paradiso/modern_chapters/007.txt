Canto Eight

	The world used to believe, to its own danger,
	that the beautiful Cyprian shot down mad love,
	wheeling in the third epicycle;

	so that the ancient peoples in their ancient error
	did honour not only to her
	with sacrifice and with the cry of vows,

	but honoured Dione too, and Cupid,
	the one as her mother and the other as her son,
	and said that he had sat in Dido's lap;

	and from her, whom I take my beginning from,
	they took the name of the star
	that the sun woos, now behind and now in front.

	I did not notice climbing into it,
	but that I was inside it my lady gave me
	faith enough, whom I saw grow more beautiful.

	And as a spark is seen inside a flame,
	and as one voice is made out inside another
	when one holds and the other comes and goes,

	so I saw inside that light other lamps
	moving in a circle, faster and slower,
	after the measure, I believe, of their inward sight.

	No winds ever came down from a cold cloud,
	visible or not, so fast
	that they would not have seemed hindered and slow

	to anyone who had seen those divine lights
	come towards us, leaving the circling
	begun first among the high Seraphim;

	and inside those that appeared foremost
	"Hosanna" sounded so that ever afterwards
	I have not been without the longing to hear it again.

	Then one of them came nearer to us
	and began alone: "We are all ready
	for your pleasure, so that you may take joy in us.

	We wheel with the heavenly princes
	in one circle and one wheeling and one thirst,
	to whom you once said, from the world:

	'You who by understanding move the third heaven';
	and we are so full of love that, to please you,
	a little quiet will be no less sweet."

	When my eyes had been offered
	in reverence to my lady, and she
	had made them content and sure of her,

	they turned back to the light that had promised
	so much, and "Ah, who are you?" was my voice,
	stamped with great affection.

	And how much greater and of what kind I saw it grow
	with a new gladness added, when I spoke,
	to the gladnesses it had!

	Made so, it said to me: "The world had me
	down there a short time; and if it had been longer,
	much evil will happen that would not have.

	My own gladness keeps me hidden from you,
	shining round me and covering me
	like an animal wrapped in its own silk.

	You loved me much, and you had good reason;
	for if I had stayed down there, I would have shown you
	more of my love than the leaves.

	That left bank which the Rhone washes
	after it is mixed with the Sorgue
	was waiting for me as its lord in due time,

	and that horn of Ausonia which has for its towns
	Bari and Gaeta and Catona,
	from where the Tronto and the Verde pour into the sea.

	The crown of that land already shone on my forehead
	which the Danube waters
	after it leaves the German banks.

	And beautiful Trinacria, which grows dark
	between Pachynus and Pelorus, over the gulf
	that gets the worst trouble from Eurus —

	not from Typhoeus but from sulphur being born —
	would still be waiting for her kings,
	born through me of Charles and of Rudolph,

	if bad lordship, which always breaks the heart
	of the peoples under it, had not
	moved Palermo to shout: 'Death, death!'

	And if my brother could foresee this,
	he would already be running from the greedy poverty
	of Catalonia, so that it should not damage him;

	for truly there is need for provision to be made,
	by him or by another, so that no more load
	is put on his already loaded boat.

	His nature, which came down mean from generous,
	would have needed the sort of soldiery
	that did not care about putting into the chest."

	"Because I believe the high gladness
	your words pour into me, my lord,
	is seen by you as I see it,

	there where every good ends and begins,
	it is dearer to me; and I hold this dear too,
	that you make it out by looking into God.

	You have made me glad, so make this clear to me too,
	since by speaking you have moved me to doubt
	how a bitter thing can come from a sweet seed."

	So I to him; and he to me: "If I can
	show you a truth, you will have your face
	towards what you ask, where you now have your back.

	The good that turns and contents the whole kingdom
	you are climbing makes its providence
	a power in these great bodies.

	And not only are the natures provided for
	in the mind that is perfect of itself,
	but they are, together with their welfare:

	so that whatever this bow shoots
	falls disposed to a foreseen end,
	like something aimed at its own mark.

	If it were not so, the heaven you walk through
	would produce its effects in such a way
	that they would not be works of art but ruins;

	and that cannot be, unless the intellects
	that move these stars are defective,
	and the first defective, that did not make them perfect.

	Do you want this truth made whiter still for you?"
	And I: "Not at all; for I see it is impossible
	that nature should tire in what is necessary."

	At which he again: "Now tell me: would it be worse
	for a man on earth if he were not a citizen?"
	"Yes," I answered, "and here I ask for no reason why."

	"And can he be one, if men do not live
	differently down there for different offices?
	No, if your master writes rightly for you."

	So he came deducing as far as this;
	then he concluded: "So the roots of your effects
	must be different:

	so that one is born Solon and another Xerxes,
	another Melchizedek and another the man
	who lost his son while flying through the air.

	The circling nature, which is the seal
	on the mortal wax, plies its art well,
	but does not distinguish one house from another.

	From this it comes about that Esau is parted
	from Jacob in the seed; and Quirinus comes
	from so low a father that he is given to Mars.

	Nature, once begotten, would always make
	its road like its begetters',
	if divine providence did not overrule.

	Now what was behind you is in front of you;
	but so that you may know that I take pleasure in you,
	I want a corollary to cloak you.

	Nature always makes a bad showing
	if it finds fortune out of tune with it,
	like any other seed outside its own country.

	And if the world down there paid attention
	to the foundation nature lays,
	and followed it, the people would be good.

	But you twist to religion a man
	born to gird on the sword,
	and make a king of one who is fit for preaching;

	so that your track goes off the road."
