PARADISO -- RUNNING NOTES
The shared consistency ledger. Read inferno/running_notes.txt and
purgatorio/running_notes.txt first: the form rules are identical across
all three cantiche and are not repeated here except where this book
decides something the other two did not.


FORM (identical to inferno/ and purgatorio/)

- Verse stays verse, tab-indented, one line per line.
- Tercets stay tercets, in the same number and the same order. The line
  count per canto is exact and is checked mechanically against
  chapters/. ONE LOST TAB TURNS A TERCET INTO A PARAGRAPH -- assemble.py
  joins the lines into running prose -- and the word ratio does not move
  at all.
- No rhyme. Terza rima cannot cross into English without padding, and
  padding is the one thing that must not happen to a poem this
  compressed. Said plainly in the front matter rather than implied.
- Enjambment across a tercet boundary is preserved where Dante has it.
- Ratio: 1.18 over the cantica (verify --min-ratio 1.05 --max-ratio
  1.35), which is Purgatorio's 1.18 and just under Inferno's 1.19.

CHECK: paradiso/check.py compares tercet count, lines within each
tercet, and the total, EXACTLY against chapters/, and requires every
line to keep its tab. It caught exactly one defect in the whole
cantica -- canto XXVI tercets 12 and 13, where I had run
"Dunque a l'essenza ov' è tanto avvantaggio" and the tercet that
completes its sentence together into one five-line block. Every word
was present and the English read perfectly; only the shape was wrong,
and nothing else in the toolchain can see that.
MEASURED AND NOT INCLUDED, as in inferno/: the fleming numeric diff.
The Italian of the Paradiso spells its numbers out almost throughout,
so the check would be very nearly inert, and an inert check that looks
like coverage is worse than none (the nights lesson).


THE CENTRAL PROBLEM, AND THE ANSWER IS THE OPPOSITE OF GRAND

The Paradiso has a reputation for being abstract, and translators
answer that reputation by raising the register -- which is exactly what
destroys it. Dante's answer to a heaven made of light is to reach for
the smallest domestic thing in the room. Keep every one of them:

  a bird on the nest before dawn, waiting for the light (XXIII)
  a baby flinging its face towards the milk (XXX)
  an infant stretching its arms after feeding (XXIII)
  jackdaws warming their feathers at daybreak (XXI)
  wheels in the works of a clock (XXIV)
  a coal quickening into flame at the wind (XVI)
  a tailor cutting the gown by the cloth he has (XXXII)
  a woman blushing, and the blush going (XVIII)
  bees going in and out of a flower (XXXI)
  a man taking his finger out of a fire and putting it back (XXII)
  two beasts under one skin -- prelate and palfrey (XXI)
  the hood puffed up with a good laugh at a sermon (XXIX)

Whenever the argument climbs, the image comes down. That alternation is
the cantica's whole tone and it is easy to flatten by degrees without
noticing any single loss.


THE LIGHT VOCABULARY IS LOCKED

Dante has a dozen words for light and uses them as different things.
Do not harmonize them into "light" and "brightness":
  luce      light (the thing itself)
  lume      light (as received, as a lamp gives it)
  raggio    ray
  splendore splendour (light reflected)
  fulgore   blaze
  lampo     flash
  favilla   spark
  candor    whiteness
  chiarezza brightness / clearness
"vivo lume" is the living light and stays that.


THE INEFFABILITY PASSAGES ARE KEPT IN FULL

Dante says nine or ten times that he cannot say what he saw, and each
time he says it differently and at length. This is not throat-clearing
to be cut: it is the cantica's argument about its own medium, and the
last one (XXXIII) is the end of the poem. Never compress them.
"Trasumanar" -- to pass beyond the human -- is pinned in must_contain in
the form that keeps the refusal instead of paraphrasing it away.


THREE REGISTERS, KEPT APART

1. THE FLIGHT. Beatrice explaining, and Dante rising without noticing.
   Clear, fast, and warm. The hume rule governs hard: most of these
   sentences are already plain in the Italian.
2. THE ENCOUNTERS. Piccarda, Justinian, Cacciaguida, Peter Damian,
   Bernard -- each a person with a voice. Cacciaguida (XV-XVII) is the
   emotional centre of the cantica and the only place in the whole
   Comedy where Dante is spoken to as somebody's child.
3. THE INVECTIVE. Peter Damian on the fat prelates (XXI), Benedict on
   the ruined orders (XXII), Beatrice on preachers who tell jokes
   (XXIX), and above all Saint Peter going red in the face -- "my place,
   my place, my place, which stands empty" (XXVII). These are as angry
   as anything in the Inferno and must not be softened by the sweetness
   around them.


LATIN STAYS LATIN, AND IS GLOSSED AT ZERO LINE COST

The burke rule: an untranslated block is a hole in the work, not a
piece of scholarship. But Dante's Latin is always short and always
marked as Latin on purpose, so it stays, with the English folded into
the same line and no line added:
  XV.28   "O sanguis meus, o superinfusa..." -- Cacciaguida's greeting,
          kept entire and untranslated. It is the one place in the poem
          where an ancestor speaks the language of the Rome he served,
          and it is three whole lines; glossing it inline would cost
          the tercet. The context supplies it ("my blood").
  XVIII   'DILIGITE IUSTITIAM' / 'QUI IUDICATIS TERRAM' -- love justice
          / you who judge the earth. Glossed inline. The Latin CANNOT
          go: two tercets later Dante counts to the M of the fifth word,
          and TERRAM is what supplies it.
  XX.94   Regnum celorum -- the kingdom of heaven. Glossed inline.
  XXIV    'Dio laudamo' -- we praise you, God. Glossed inline.
  XXV     'Sperent in te' -- let them hope in you. Glossed inline.
  XXV.26  coram me -- before me. Glossed inline.
  XXXII   'Miserere mei', 'Ave, Maria, gratïa plena', sine causa.
          All glossed inline.
BERNARD'S PRAYER (XXXIII.1-39) IS ENGLISHED, because Dante wrote it in
Italian. It is the most famous prayer in the poem and the fact that it
is NOT in Latin is part of what it is.


NAMES AND USAGE

- Familiar English forms throughout: Solomon, Boethius, Bernard,
  Charlemagne, Roland, Joshua. Italian names keep their Italian
  (Cacciaguida, Cunizza, Folco, Piccarda, Romuald, Bonaventure).
- Dante's periphrases for people are resolved where the reader would
  otherwise lose the referent and Dante is not making a puzzle of it,
  and KEPT where the periphrasis is the point (the "singer of the Holy
  Spirit" for David; "the great baron" of Saint James).
- XVI opens on Dante addressing Cacciaguida with the respectful plural
  "voi", which English cannot distinguish. Signalled inside the
  introducing line -- "the respectful plural 'you' which was first
  offered at Rome" -- at zero line cost, the same device used for the
  Provençal of Purgatorio XXVI.
- The Florentine family names of XVI are kept as printed. They are a
  roll-call and the reader is meant to hear length, not to identify
  every house.


THE VERNE RULE, THROUGHOUT, WITH NO NOTE ANYWHERE IN THE VOLUME

Nothing in this cantica is softened and nothing carries an editorial
wink. The doctrine IS the poem:
- The unbaptized. XIX is a whole canto arguing that a man born on the
  banks of the Indus who lives blamelessly and dies unbaptized has no
  claim on God's justice that any created sight can measure, and Dante
  does not resolve it in the reader's favour. XXXII puts unbaptized
  infants outside salvation in as many words. Translate in full.
- Predestination. XX and XXXII both say flatly that the ranks are
  allotted before any merit, and XX ends by saying the blessed are glad
  not to know who is chosen. Keep both halves.
- PAGANS IN HEAVEN. Trajan and Ripheus the Trojan (XX) are in the eye
  of the Eagle, and Dante knows exactly how startling that is -- "who
  would believe, down in the erring world". Kept unhedged, including
  the mechanism (Trajan came back from hell to be baptized; Ripheus was
  baptized by Faith, Hope and Charity a thousand years early).
- The papal invective, entire: Saint Peter's "he has made a sewer of my
  burial ground" (XXVII), the wolves in shepherds' clothing, Boniface
  destined for the hole where Simon Magus is, and the men of Cahors and
  Gascony getting ready to drink the blood of the martyrs.
- XXIX's flat statement that a preacher who says the moon eclipsed the
  sun at the Crucifixion "lies", and its reasoning about the Spaniards,
  the Indians and the Jews. Kept as Dante's astronomy and Dante's
  argument.
- XXVII.136 -- "so the white skin turns black". NOT a separate case,
  and logged here because it looks like one. This is Dante's figure for
  the corruption of human nature between infancy and adulthood: the
  beautiful daughter of the Sun is human nature itself, and white and
  black are the before and after of a single person, not two kinds of
  person. It is the same class as Inferno XXXIV's "such as those who
  come from where the Nile flows down" -- a colour named, no claim made
  about anybody -- and it is kept exactly, no more and no less than the
  Italian says. Compare grimm's ruling on "The White Bride and the
  Black One".
- XXXI's "barbarians" is Dante's own political category for the
  northerners, as the "Northern barbarians" of sun-tzu/ are the Chinese
  state's. Kept.


TWO THINGS THAT CANNOT DRIFT ACROSS THE THREE BOOKS

- The last line of each cantica ends on the stars, and all three are
  pinned in their own must_contain: "and from there we came out to see
  the stars again" (Inferno), "pure and ready to rise to the stars"
  (Purgatorio), "by the love that moves the sun and the other stars"
  (Paradiso). A reader who owns all three meets the figure three times
  and must recognise it every time (the nights/ rule about frame
  formulas, applied across three volumes).
- Beatrice is the same person in Purgatorio XXX-XXXIII and here. She
  scolds in the Purgatorio and teaches here, but she is brisk and
  affectionate in both, and she laughs constantly in the Paradiso. Do
  not make her a lecturer.


THE READABILITY PASS (2026-09-02), after Alex's correction on the
Dogen volume and his instruction "start on the paradiso".

THE DIAGNOSIS: the tercet-for-tercet rule had turned every periphrasis
into a fixed cost, so Dante's habit of naming a person by a clause --
"the beautiful cheek whose palate cost the whole world dear" for Eve,
"that which, pierced by the lance" for Christ, "the man who lost his
son while flying through the air" for Daedalus, "the Cripple of
Jerusalem", "the beautiful nest of Leda", "the messenger of Juno" --
crossed as-is, and a reader who did not already know the poem could
not follow it. Same defect as the first Dogen draft, arrived at by a
different road. Also: astronomy given as riddles ("where the one
motion and the other strike each other"), rings of the angels called
"substances", and Latin sentences left with no gloss.

THE RULE, applied to all 33 cantos: RESOLVE THE PERIPHRASIS TO THE
NAME WHERE DANTE IS NOT MAKING A PUZZLE OF IT, and where he is, keep
the periphrasis and add the name beside it inside the line ("Peter,
the ancient father of Holy Church"). Give the astronomy in plain words
("the point where the sun's yearly path crosses the daily turning of
the heavens"). Gloss every Latin phrase inline in the same line. Use
"angels" for substances, "God" for the first Power, "the Trinity" for
One and Two and Three, and never make the reader look anything up. The
tercet count, the lines per tercet and the tabs are unchanged in every
canto; check.py compared them against chapters/ after every edit and
never fired. NO LINE WAS PADDED FOR ITS OWN SAKE: lines got longer only
where a name or a gloss went in.

WHAT STAYS: every pin in must_contain (all pinned lines unchanged);
the light vocabulary; the ineffability passages entire; the three
registers; XXVII.136's "white skin turns black" with the same-person
gloss now in the line; the Verne-rule passages (XIX's Indus, XXXII's
unbaptized infants, XV's crusade line, now "that religion whose people
hold the Holy Land that is yours by right" -- his claim, plainer, not
softer).

MEASURED: 37,698 -> 39,363 words, ratio 1.18 -> 1.23, still inside
1.05-1.35. The four percent is names and glosses.
