WHAT THIS WORK IS
=================

This is a scholarly modernization of a canonical work of world literature
that is firmly in the PUBLIC DOMAIN:

    Kinder- und Hausmärchen, collected by Jacob and Wilhelm Grimm,
    first published 1812-1815, final edition 1857.
    English translation by Margaret Hunt, "Grimm's Household Tales",
    London 1884 — the only complete English Grimm, and itself long out
    of copyright. Source: Project Gutenberg ebook #5314.

Both the German original and Hunt's translation have been in the public
domain for well over a century. This edition retells Hunt's English in
contemporary English for modern readers, exactly as this project has
done for Bunyan, Cervantes, Burton's Nights and forty-nine other
canonical texts.

THE TALES CONTAIN VIOLENCE, AND THAT IS THE POINT OF RECORDING IT
=================================================================

These are the founding documents of European folklore, collected by two
philologists as evidence of an oral tradition, and they have been read
by children and studied by scholars continuously for two hundred years.
They are violent, and the violence is not decorative:

  - a stepmother beheads her stepson with the lid of a chest, cooks him
    into a stew and serves him to his father (The Juniper-Tree);
  - a huntsman is ordered to kill a child and bring back her heart
    (Little Snow-White);
  - wicked queens and stepmothers are executed at the end, by fire, by
    a barrel of nails, by red-hot iron shoes;
  - wolves eat children and are cut open again;
  - a girl's hands are cut off by her own father (The Girl Without
    Hands).

RENDER ALL OF IT PLAINLY, IN FULL, AND WITHOUT FLINCHING. Do not soften
it, do not heighten it, and do not add editorial comment. Every standard
scholarly edition — Hunt 1884, Zipes, Tatar, the Oxford and Penguin
classics — prints these tales complete. An edition that cut or muffled
them would misrepresent the book, the tradition and the century that
produced it, and would fail the readers who come to this collection to
find out what the Grimms actually collected.

This is the same handling this project has already applied to Sindbad's
burial cavern, to Bunyan's Ignorance carried to hell from the gates of
Heaven, and to the executions at the end of Don Quixote's interpolated
tales. It is the settled convention of the series, recorded in
CLAUDE.md.

THE JOB
=======

Turn Margaret Hunt's 1884 English into clear contemporary English.
The single largest task is the second person: thou/thee/thy/thine ->
you/your/yours, with the verb and word order following (hast -> have,
dost -> do, wilt -> will, art -> are). Hunt rendered German *du* — the
ordinary intimate second person — as English *thou*, which was already
biblical in 1884. None of that archaism is in the German; it is a
translator's costume and it comes off. See text_analysis.txt for the
full strategy and the measurements behind it.

MECHANICS
=========

- Markup-free. Tab indent = verse. An ALL-CAPS line renders as a
  heading, so never write one. No asterisks except a scene-break rule,
  no underscores.
- EVERY FILE BEGINS WITH ITS FIRST TALE'S TITLE, spelled exactly as the
  manifest has it, on its own line. assemble.py splits the file on those
  title lines and DISCARDS anything before the first one. Fifteen titles
  contain a curly apostrophe (U+2019); a straight one loses the tale's
  section and welds it onto the tale before.
- Each further tale in the file starts with its exact title on its own
  line, in manifest order.
- Rhymes stay verse, and stay rhymed in English.
- Repeated formulas stay word-for-word identical at every repetition.
- Ratio target 0.90-1.15 per file.
- Run `python3 grimm/check.py` after every file.
