GRIMM — RUNNING NOTES (the consistency ledger)
Read text_analysis.txt first. This file records what is LOCKED.


THE ONE JOB
===========
thou/thee/thy/thine -> you/your/yours, and the verb and word order with
it (hast->have, dost->do, doth->does, art->are, wilt->will, shalt->shall,
canst->can, mayest->may, wert->were). 3,729 instances, 99% inside
dialogue. Everything else in this book is secondary.


LOCKED WORDINGS
===============
- "Once upon a time" for Hunt's "There was once on a time" / "Once on a
  time". Openings are formulas and stay formulaic.
- "Once upon a time there was..." / "There was once a..." — either, but
  never a literary rewrite.
- "and they lived happily to the end of their days" for Hunt's various
  "lived happily until they died" endings, EXCEPT where the German ending
  is a specific joke ("and if they have not died, they are still alive
  today"), which is kept as the joke.
- "the King's daughter" and "the princess" both, as Hunt has them; not
  normalised, because the tales themselves alternate.
- "dwarf" (Hunt's "manikin"/"dwarf" for Männlein), "the little man" where
  it is Männlein and stature is the point.
- "wicked" not "vile" for böse; "stepmother" always.
- Frau Trude, Herr Korbes, Mother Holle, Allerleirauh, Fundevogel keep
  their names. "Grethel" -> "Gretel" everywhere (spelling, not name).

RHYMES ARE VERSE AND MUST RHYME
===============================
Tab-indented, one line per line, rhymed in English. A rhyme that does not
rhyme has been mistranslated; rework it until it chimes, and take a freer
sense to get there. Locked so far:

  001 The Frog-King —
        "Princess, youngest princess,
         open the door for me!
         Have you forgotten what you promised me
         yesterday, down by the cool well water?"
      (Hunt sets this unrhymed; the German is a rhymed couplet pair. Kept
       as a chant rather than forced, because the tale repeats it three
       times and a strained rhyme would grate three times.)

DECIDED BY ALEX, BEFORE ANY OF THE THREE WAS WRITTEN
====================================================
THE ANTISEMITIC TALES — "The Good Bargain" (7, file 003), "The Jew Among
Thorns" (110, file 051) and "The Bright Sun Brings It to Light" (115,
file 054). RULING: TRANSLATE ALL THREE IN FULL AND UNSOFTENED, plus ONE
short editor's note at "The Jew Among Thorns", the worst of them.

  The tale is not an incidental slur: a servant with a magic fiddle makes
  a Jew dance in a thorn bush until he is bloody and in rags, robs him,
  forces a confession out of him under threat of more, and the Jew is
  hanged as a thief while the man who robbed him walks free — presented
  as a comic happy ending.

  WHY NOT CUT: completeness is the only reason this edition exists rather
  than Lucy Crane's plainer selection. An edition that quietly dropped
  three tales would misrepresent both the book and the century.
  WHY A NOTE HERE, when nights/ mostly refused notes: the nights/ notes
  were facts about the book's construction, and so is this one — it says
  what the edition chose to do and why. It makes no comment on the tale
  itself, which stands exactly as the Grimms printed it.
  The note goes at 110 only, not at all three.

CONVENTIONS CHECKED MECHANICALLY (see check.py)
===============================================
1. Every tale title in the manifest's split_headings appears in its modern
   file, on its own line, spelled EXACTLY. A drifted title loses its
   section and welds its tale onto the previous one — invisible on the
   page except as a missing TOC entry.
2. Each file BEGINS with its first title line. assemble.py discards
   everything before the first heading.
3. Titles appear in the same ORDER as the manifest.
4. No thou-family word survives anywhere.
5. Numerals present in the source are present in the translation (the
   fleming rule).
6. No all-caps lines, no stray asterisks or underscores.
7. Verse parity: a file with N tab-indented blocks in the source should
   have about N in the translation — a rhyme silently flattened into
   prose is this book's version of silent summarisation.

LOCKS ADDED IN THE 029-034 RUN
==============================
- DUMMLING = SIMPLETON, always. Hunt translates the same German name two
  ways: "Simpleton" in The Queen Bee and The Three Feathers, "Dummling"
  in The Golden Goose. It is one character-type across three tales in two
  adjacent files, and a reader meeting both spellings in ten pages would
  read them as two different people. This is a TRANSLATOR'S inconsistency,
  not the Grimms', so it is reconciled — the same rule that reconciled
  Verne's shifting place-names, not the Verne rule that protects an
  author's own claim.
- ALLERLEIRAUH keeps her name (it is the tale's title in the manifest and
  cannot drift), glossed ONCE, in-line, at the moment the huntsmen name
  her: "Allerleirauh — All-Kinds-of-Fur". No bracketed note.
- THALER kept. It is period coinage doing real work as a quantity (two
  hundred for an apprenticeship, thirty for a greyhound, three hundred
  for a cow's hide), and no modern equivalent survives conversion.
- THE TOAD-RHYMES in The Three Feathers are set as RHYMED COUPLETS.
  Hunt's four-line rendering does not rhyme in English, but the German is
  a nonsense jingle whose whole point is the jingle. check.py counts verse
  BLOCKS, not lines, so a couplet satisfies parity — and it should: what
  the check guards against is a rhyme flattened into prose, not a rhyme
  reset in fewer lines.
- THE HARE'S BRIDE keeps its odd present tense throughout ("says the
  mother to the daughter"). It is Hunt rendering a Low German telling,
  and it is the only tale in the collection that sounds like someone
  talking rather than someone reading.

SOURCE DEFECTS REPAIRED IN THIS RUN (Hunt's text, not the Grimms')
==================================================================
- The Two Brothers, part 2: "he sprang away, have you have never seen one
  running?" is garbled for "wie ihr's nie gesehen habt" — set as "he
  sprang away as you never saw a hare run."
- The Two Brothers, part 3: "The the huntsman stood up"; and four
  speeches print with no quotation marks at all (the witch interrogation).
- The Little Peasant: the closing quotation mark falls after "carried it
  to the pasture" instead of after "All right", putting the cowherd's
  narration inside his own speech.
None of these are visible to verify.py: the words are all present, in
order, and the ratio does not move.

A RACE-ADJACENT PASSAGE, DECIDED ON THE BUNYAN-FLATTERER PRECEDENT
==================================================================
"The King's Son Who Feared Nothing" (file 056): the enchanted princess
is introduced as "quite black", and her colour is the SPELL'S PROGRESS
METER — feet white after the first night, white to the fingertips after
the second, snow-white at the end. The colour cannot be cut without
destroying the tale's own clock.
Rendered as a CONDITION, never as an identity: "she was black from head
to foot", and thereafter "the girl" / "the enchanted girl" — NOT "the
black maiden", which in modern English names a person's race and would
misidentify the character. Exactly the rule applied to Bunyan's
Flatterer ("black of body", not "a black man"). The whitening stages are
kept verbatim: they are the plot.
