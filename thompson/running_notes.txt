RUNNING NOTES — Thompson, LIGHT VISIBLE AND INVISIBLE
The shared consistency ledger. Every translation agent reads this; the
orchestrator updates it between batches from the agents' returned notes.

=========================================================================
LOCKED VOCABULARY — caps spent, no re-gloss
=========================================================================

Thompson's own terms, kept because he reasons with them:

  velocity-constant   his coinage: the velocity of light in a medium with
                      the velocity in air taken as 1, i.e. the reciprocal
                      of the refractive index. Never "1/n", never
                      silently replaced by "refractive index".
  sagitta             the depth of an arc from its chord. He explains why
                      he prefers it to "versed sine"; keep both words in
                      that one sentence and "sagitta" thereafter.
  wave-front          the running unit of the whole book. Hyphenated.
  curvature           what a lens or mirror IMPRINTS on a wave. The verb
                      is "imprint" throughout, not "give" or "impose".
  dioptre             his unit of curvature (the source prints "dioptrie"
                      / "dioptries"). Modern spelling, used from the
                      first mention.
  divergivity         his word for negative curvature. Keep it.

Modernized silently, first use onward, no gloss:

  kathode      -> cathode          reflexion  -> reflection
  anode        -> anode (unchanged) connexion -> connection
  Wurzburg     -> Würzburg          inflexion -> inflection
  Rontgen      -> Röntgen           tint      -> tint (keep; it is exact)

NOT modernized — these are his claims, not his vocabulary:

  the ether             real, and the medium light waves travel in.
                        SPELLED "ether", not "aether" — that is what
                        Thompson prints, 95 times against 2
  Röntgen rays          his primary name for X-rays; "X-rays" also his
  kathode rays          the rays; do not call them electrons
  "billions" = 10^12    British long scale; he defines it; keep the word

Instrument and material names stand as printed: Nicol prism, Geissler
tube, Crookes's tube, calc-spar, Iceland spar, tourmaline, selenite,
bolometer, thermopile, fluoroscope, phosphorescope, platinocyanide of
barium.

Numbers: EVERY numeral and fraction in this text has been checked against
the page image. Reproduce them exactly — 5½ seconds, 16,666⅔ hours, 6¾
millionths of an inch, 1/800 inch, 1/10000 inch, 186,400 miles a second,
508.8 billions per second. Do NOT spell a number out in words; the
numeric-token check will flag it, and rightly.

=========================================================================
TONE CALIBRATION
=========================================================================

The voice is a live demonstration in the continuous present: "I am now
dipping it in the water", "you see the shadow spring out on the screen".
Never convert a demonstration into a textbook statement.

Thompson against the other four lecturers in the collection: he is the
REFORMER. Boys is delighted, Faraday is affectionate, Fleming is an
engineer; Thompson has come to say that optics is taught wrongly and that
hard words, not hard ideas, are what make science look difficult. Let the
argument show.

The audience is children and he never talks down to them. Keep every "you
can try this at home", every "I want you to notice", every direct
question thrown at the room.

Register within the book varies and should be allowed to:

  - THE LECTURES: warm, live, demonstrative.
  - THE APPENDICES: written afterwards, for "students and teachers of
    optics". Cooler, more technical, third person in places. Do not
    import Christmas-lecture warmth into them; do not make them dry
    either — Appendix I is an argument for a method he believes in.
  - THE FOOTNOTES: the same man, so the same voice, but compressed.
  - THE RÖNTGEN INTERVIEW (Lecture Six): a journalist and a physicist
    talking. Plain, fast, quoted verbatim.

=========================================================================
CONVENTIONS
=========================================================================

FIGURE MARKERS. `[Figure 84]` in, `[Figure 84: caption]` out. Same set,
same order, no additions, no omissions. Captions are new writing: one
descriptive line, drawn from the adjacent prose, saying what the reader
is looking at. Compound ids (`[Figure 121-122]`) need a caption that
names every figure on the block in order, saying which is which.

TWENTY-ONE FIGURES HAVE NO PLATE — 8, 13, 22, 36, 64, 65, 73, 96, 103,
108, 123, 131, 134, 135, 150, 151, 152, 153, 156, 157. Where the text
says "see Fig. 8", describe what the reader should picture instead of
pointing at a plate that does not exist. Never invent a marker for one of
those numbers.

FOOTNOTES arrive as their own paragraph beginning `Footnote: `. Keep that
form, keep them where they are, translate them fully.

DISPLAYED FORMULAS AND TABLES arrive as indented blocks:

    F = (R₁ − R₂)(1 − h)/h                      [10]

Keep the indentation (it is what makes the page render them as a block),
keep the bracketed formula numbers (the prose cites them by number), keep
every symbol and subscript. Do not re-derive or modernize the notation:
Appendix I exists to argue that Thompson's notation beats the Cambridge
one, and prints both to prove it.

The wave-length table (Appendix IV) and the notation table (Appendix I)
are indented blocks too. Column HEADINGS may be put into plain modern
English; not one figure inside them may change.

CHAPTER HEADINGS come from the orchestrator's prompt, exactly as given.
Multi-part files: heading line, then `(Part n of k)`, then the
translation. Parts 2+ never re-introduce the lecture; non-final parts
never conclude it.

LECTURE SUMMARIES. Each lecture opens with a dash-separated list of its
contents ("How light-waves travel — Experiments with the ripple-tank —
..."). Keep it, as its own paragraph, in the same dash-separated form.
It is the book's own table of contents for that hour.

=========================================================================
FORWARD REFERENCES TO HONOR
=========================================================================

  - The RIPPLE-TANK of Lecture One is referred back to in every later
    lecture. Whatever you call the apparatus in 001, it stays.
  - The FOUR PROPOSITIONS about waves (they march at right angles to
    their own front; they bend round obstacles smaller than a
    wave-length; a lens imprints curvature; twice the sagitta measures
    the curvature) are stated in Lecture One and used to the last page.
  - "Elastic" recurs: the ether's rigidity and density set the speed of
    light. Same phrasing each time.
  - Lecture Four's taxonomy of luminescence (photo-, thermo-, tribo-,
    electro-, chemi-) is set up as a numbered list and referred back to
    in Lecture Six and its appendix. Keep the list numbering.
  - Lecture Five ends on the electromagnetic theory, which Lecture Six
    then uses to speculate about what Röntgen rays are. The two must
    agree in wording about what the theory claims.

    against the general habit of writing small numbers as words,
    because the numeric check is worth more than the elegance.
  - Twenty figures have no plate (8, 13, 22, 36, 64, 65, 73, 96, 103,

    notation:" before their formulas, and the "Prisms made in Nicol's
    way" fragment merged with the sentence it belongs to.
  - "h₁ is less than h₂" in Appendix I is spelled out in words because
    `se typogrify` unescapes a bare "<" and breaks the epub.
