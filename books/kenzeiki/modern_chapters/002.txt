Chapter Two: Mount Hiei, and the Question Nobody Could Answer

On the ninth day of the fourth month of 1213, the first year of Kenpo,
when he was fourteen, his head was shaved for the first time by Koen,
the abbot of the whole mountain. The next day, on the ordination
platform of the great temple of Enryakuji, with Koen as his preceptor,
he took the bodhisattva precepts and became a monk. From then on he
studied everything the Tendai school taught: the secret teachings that
had come from southern India, and the doctrines of the greater and the
lesser vehicles of Buddhism.

Monks of Hiei all asked to be shaved on the eighth day of the fourth
month, the Buddha's birthday. The Founder was shaved on the ninth
because the whole mountain had debated his case and held the ceremony
back, so that it took place at dawn on the ninth instead.

This abbot Koen lived at the Myohoin, below Yokawa in the village of
Okugi. He was the head of the school in his generation, a scholar
without equal in both the open and the secret teachings, and a monk of
pure conduct who kept the rules.

In 1214, at fifteen, the Founder had been right through the sutras and
the commentaries, and a doubt of his own came up in him. He put it like
this. The open teachings and the secret teachings both say that every
one of us is, from the very beginning, the Buddha-nature itself: that
we are already what we are looking for. If that is so, then why did the
buddhas of the past, the present and the future ever have to make up
their minds to seek awakening, and go looking for it?

He put the question to the people around him, and nobody had an
answer. So he was sent to Koin, the abbot of Miidera, the great temple
by the lake below the mountain, and laid it before him.

Koin said: this is not a question to be answered lightly. Our school
has its doctrine, but I am afraid the doctrine does not get to the
bottom of it. You should go to Kenninji, in the eastern part of the
capital, and study with Eisai. That was his advice.

So in the same year the Founder went to Kenninji and entered the
household of Eisai, the master who had founded it and who had brought
the Zen of the Rinzai school back from China. There, for the first
time, he heard the Zen way of teaching.
