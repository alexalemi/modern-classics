At that installation the Founder handed on to the second abbot a
kesa he had sewn himself. It passed down from hand to hand as far as
the reverend Daichi, and the old record of it survives in his own hand
at Kofukuji in Higo, the temple Daichi founded. It reads, word for
word:

	One robe, handed on in succession, together with its kesa bag.

	This robe is the kesa of the first ancestor who founded Eihei.
	The cloth of it is fine linen. Among the first ancestor's lay
	disciples there was a man of the province of Yamashiro called
	Renbo. His wife, whose faith in the first ancestor was
	undivided, prepared and wove it herself under a vow of
	abstinence, and carried it the whole long way to Eiheiji in
	Echizen and offered it there. The first ancestor was moved by
	the undividedness of her faith, cut and sewed the robe himself,
	and wore it every day. At last, in the summer of the second
	year of Hoji, he sewed a bag for it and put it away.

	In the seventh month of the fifth year of Kencho, the year of
	the ox, when he handed the abbacy of Eihei to the second abbot,
	at the moment of handing it over he entrusted this kesa to the
	second abbot along with it. Accordingly, through the eighteen
	years of his abbacy, the second abbot wore it at the discourses
	in the hall and at the recitation of the precepts. And for
	twenty-eight years after the first ancestor's death he never
	once passed a night apart from the robe, but guarded it and
	received it on his head.
