He built the monks' hall himself, by an appeal for subscriptions in
every quarter. The preamble of it reads:

	An appeal for subscriptions towards the monks' hall of
	Kannon-dori-in at Uji.

	Bowing my head, I respectfully address all the buddhas and
	bodhisattvas of the ten directions, the sages and the assembly
	of monks, gods and men, dragons and the eight kinds of beings,
	and all good men and good women: concerning the wish to found
	one place of practice out of the pure faith that gives a single
	coin.

	The Sutra of the Bodhisattva Precepts says: a child of the
	Buddha should always be teaching all living beings, and should
	found monks' quarters, mountains and woods and gardens and
	fields, and raise stupas, and set up places for the winter and
	summer retreats and for sitting zazen, and every place where
	the way is practised; and if he does not, he commits an offence
	of light defilement.

	So a temple is a place of practice for all the buddhas. The
	Buddhist temples of China copied the monasteries of India, and
	the training halls of Japan should learn from them in the same
	way.
