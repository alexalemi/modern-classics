	from those who talk at length, what is that worth setting
	against you? The days go by and go by. I hope you will not tire
	of the mallet and the whisk, so that the manner of Damei may
	not fall to the ground and the line of Caodong stay cool and
	clear for ever. Lately, as it happens, I hear that the capital
	is in an uproar, civil and military both, and I imagine you and
	all the brothers are of one mind about it. I am living in a
	borrowed robe in a small room. I have not yet had the good
	fortune to wait on you. I feel it keenly, for the sake of the
	great dharma. The rest I do not set out.

	Respectfully presented. From a man of Western Shu under the
	Song, lodging at Enkakuji in Hakata at Dazaifu, the monk
	Daolong bows and writes up.

The Founder's reply:
