The third year of Kencho, the year of the boar.

A bell was constantly heard sounding deep in the mountain. The patron
wrote to ask about it, and this is the Founder's reply:

	Since you ask about it day after day, I write. It has happened
	again and again over these seven or eight years. This year, at
	the hour of the rat on the fifth day of the first month, while
	I was discussing the buddha-dharma with the lay-monk counsellor
	of the Kazan'in in the hermitage at Ryozan'in, a bell was heard,
	some two hundred strokes of it. The sound of it was deeper than
	the bell of Kiyomizudera in Higashiyama in the capital. We
	listened to it with joy, and it seemed to me solemn and holy
	beyond words. The counsellor too
	said that this is a marvellous and holy ground, and was full of
	joy at it, and it was for that that he took the tonsure.

	The lord Kaneyori, middle captain, was in the same room and did
	not hear it. Nor did his foster-brother, the lay-monk of the
	right guard, the poor priest Keihin. And of two or three women
	besides, and seven or eight retainers who were there, all say
	they heard nothing.
