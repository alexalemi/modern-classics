He submitted a second memorial. The substance of it ran:

	I say again: the buddha-dharma reaches through worlds as many
	as the sands, and the light of the precepts shines in the ten
	directions. The sutra says, "This threefold world is all mine,
	and the beings in it are all my children." If it is all his,
	then this world of ours is the country of Shakyamuni Buddha.
	The country is already a buddha-country and the people are all
	the children of a buddha. Brothers are brothers, and the natural
	order between them is not to be confused. As I see it, the
	dharma of the buddhas and the law of the world both follow from
	this principle. The gods of heaven and the spirits of earth do
	not tolerate what is against principle. How should the judgment
	of heaven be partial? I respectfully ask that the order of
	precedence by the precepts be set right.

The emperor at that time was Ningzong. He read the memorial himself,
and was moved by it more than once, and in the end he sent down an
edict to Tiantong that the order of seating by the precepts be fixed
according to the dharma. That Japanese monks entering Chinese
monasteries were not put out of their proper standing after that is the
Founder's own doing, and from this his name went out through the Five
Mountains.

That same month the cook of Ayuwang came again, and they met and talked
and he went away. The Founder reported it to Myozen, and Myozen was
delighted.
