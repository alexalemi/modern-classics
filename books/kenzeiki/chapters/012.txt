Chapter Twelve: The Five-Coloured Cloud

This is the lifeline of the buddhas and ancestors, the very eye of a
patched-robe monk. Those outside the way do not know it; the demons of
heaven cannot bear it; only the children of the Buddha can hand it on.
Officers of the storehouse, look to this clearly and do not let it
slip.

	The founding monk, Dogen. [cipher]

The fifth year of Kangen, the year of the sheep.

On the fifteenth day of the first month, at the fortnightly recitation,
while the Founder was preaching the precepts, a five-coloured cloud
stood at the paper screens across the front of the abbot's quarters and
remained about half an hour. A good many of the monks and lay people
who had come to hear him saw it. One of them, a man who had come up
from the village in the middle of Kawaminami estate, put the particulars
in writing afterwards as evidence, in a sworn statement, which reads:

	Concerning the marvel at the abbot's quarters at Shibi.

	The fifteenth day of the first month of the fifth year of
	Kangen, the year of the sheep: the preaching of the precepts.
	From the hour of the bath until the middle of the hour of the
	monkey, across the front

of the screens there was a five-coloured light. The assembly who had
come to hear him, high and low alike, made their bows before it. Among
them were more than twenty who had set out to come from the middle
village of Kawaminami estate in Yoshida and saw it. Days of preaching
the precepts are many, but that we should have come on this particular
day is cause for rejoicing, and so this account is set down as above.

The original is kept in the treasure chest of the founder at Eiheiji.

And if any part of this account is false, may we fall for ever into the
three evil paths. That it may be handed on from this day forward, we
have gladly set it down as above.

	The Buddha's child Sonkei [cipher]
	Likewise Shoen [likewise]
	Fujii Mitsunari [likewise]
	Onakatomi Masayasu [likewise]
	Sakata Uyake [likewise]
	Kuse Arimitsu [likewise]
	— and four more names, worn away in the original.

	To Eiheiji.

The fifteenth day of the first month of the fifth year of Kangen, the
year of the sheep. At the time of the preaching of the precepts at the
fortnightly recitation, together with the five-coloured cloud before
the gate of the abbot's quarters. The people among the assembly who saw
it:

Minamoto no Mitsuyori [cipher]
	Minamoto no Tokiyuki [likewise]
	The monk Shoen [likewise]
	The monk Sonkei [likewise]
	Fujiwara no Sadamasa [likewise]

Both documents, two sheets, are on Echizen torinoko paper.

Ejo, the second abbot of Eiheiji, added a note to them in his own hand:

	When the abbot who founded this mountain was preaching the
	precepts at the fortnightly recitation in the abbot's quarters,
	a five-coloured auspicious cloud appeared at the paper screens
	across the front of the room. Those screens have grown old with
	the years and are broken now. The frames and paper of the
	broken screens are kept as a treasure of this temple. The
	record of the day and hour when the sign appeared is on a
	separate sheet. Both have now been put in order and stored
	above the ceiling of the abbot's quarters, to stand as
	evidence.

	Written on the twentieth day of the ninth month of the fourth
	year of Bun'ei. The junior monk Ejo. [cipher]

The originals are in the treasury at Eiheiji to this day.

On the first day of spring that year he wrote out in his own hand the
words Great Good Fortune at the Beginning of Spring. The sheet is kept
privately in Doshoji lane in the capital: one hanging scroll, eight
lines of uneven length, eighty-five characters. It reads:

	Great good fortune, and protection from the south for the
