Chapter Twelve: The Five-Coloured Cloud

On the fifteenth day of the first month of 1247, the fifth year of
Kangen, at the fortnightly recitation, while the Founder was preaching
the precepts, a five-coloured cloud appeared at the paper screens
across the front of the abbot's quarters and stayed there for about
half an hour. A good many of the monks and lay people who had come to
hear him saw it. Some of them, farmers from the middle village of the
Kawaminami estate, put the particulars in writing afterwards as
evidence, and swore to it:

	Concerning the marvel at the abbot's quarters at Shibi.

	The fifteenth day of the first month of the fifth year of
	Kangen, the year of the sheep: the preaching of the precepts.
	From about two in the afternoon until about half past four,
	across the front of the screens there was a five-coloured
	light. Everyone who had come to hear him, high and low alike,
	bowed before it. Among them were more than twenty of us who had
	set out from the middle village of Kawaminami in Yoshida, and we
	saw it. Days of preaching the precepts are many, but that we
	should have come on this particular day is cause for rejoicing.
	And if any part of this account is false, may we fall for ever
	into the three evil paths. That it may be handed on from this
	day forward, we have gladly set it down as above.

	The Buddha's child Sonkei. Likewise Shoen. Fujii Mitsunari.
	Onakatomi Masayasu. Sakata Uyake. Kuse Arimitsu. And four more
	names, worn away in the original. To Eiheiji.

A second sheet lists the people of standing among the assembly who saw
the cloud before the gate of the abbot's quarters that day: Minamoto
no Mitsuyori, Minamoto no Tokiyuki, the monks Shoen and Sonkei, and
Fujiwara no Sadamasa, each with his cipher. Both sheets are on Echizen
paper.

Ejo, the second abbot, added a note to them in his own hand, twenty
years later:

	When the abbot who founded this mountain was preaching the
	precepts at the fortnightly recitation in his quarters, a
	five-coloured cloud of good omen appeared at the paper screens
	across the front of the room. Those screens have grown old with
	the years and are broken now. The frames and paper of the broken
	screens are kept as a treasure of this temple. The record of the
	day and hour when the sign appeared is on a separate sheet. Both
	have now been put in order and stored above the ceiling of the
	abbot's quarters, to stand as evidence.

	Written on the twentieth day of the ninth month of the fourth
	year of Bun'ei. The junior monk Ejo.

The originals are in the treasury at Eiheiji to this day.

On the first day of spring that same year he wrote out in his own
hand the words Great Good Fortune at the Beginning of Spring. The sheet
is kept privately in Doshoji lane in the capital: one hanging scroll,
eight uneven lines, eighty-five characters. It is a blessing on the
new temple, and it reads:

	Great good fortune, and protection from the south for the
	Buddha, the teaching and the community. Great good fortune at
	the beginning of spring. Great good fortune upon the founders
	and the ancestors of this house. May the Buddha's teaching spread
	widely. Great good fortune, great good fortune. Great good
	fortune in the shining out of the ancestors' way. Great good
	fortune in the flourishing of this temple gate. May students
	gather in numbers, may we find the right people and meet the
	right times, may all under heaven turn and honour our way: great
	good fortune. Great good fortune. Great good fortune at the
	beginning of spring. Great good fortune. Great good fortune on
	Eihei, the founding mountain.

	Dogen. The first day of spring, the fifth year of Kangen. Great
	good fortune.

There is another such sheet in the abbot's room at Tendenji in Osaka,
seventy-eight characters, signed with his other name, Kigen:

	Homage and refuge to the Buddha, the teaching and the community;
	to the seven buddhas and the twenty-eight ancestors of India; to
	the six ancestors of China; to the founders of this house and the
	masters and disciples of the Soto line. May temples be founded,
	the way handed on and the work taught, gods and men brought on,
	and all be peaceful and at rest. May the cloud-robed monks
	gather in crowds, may long life and fortune flourish, may
	everything go as it should and the teaching be spread in plenty:
	great good fortune, great good fortune, great good fortune. The
	turn of the year towards the light: great good fortune, great
	good fortune, great good fortune, great good fortune.

	Kigen.
