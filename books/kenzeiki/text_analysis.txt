THE ILLUSTRATED LIFE OF DŌGEN (訂補建撕記図会)
Translation strategy. From the JAPANESE, read off two woodblock
impressions, with NO English crib and no transcription of any kind.

READ dogen/text_analysis.txt FIRST. Same author-subject, same absence
of a crib, same character-based ratio; that book is the smaller and
easier half of this problem.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT THE BOOK IS, AND WHOSE IT IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Three men, and the edition must not blur them:

  KENZEI 建撕 (1415-1474), fourteenth abbot of Eiheiji, compiled the
    original 永平開山行状建撕記 by about 1472, assembling it out of the
    Record of the Three Ancestors, the Eihei Kōroku, the Shōbōgenzō,
    the Zuimonki and Dōgen's poems, in chronological order. It is THE
    OLDEST SUBSTANTIAL LIFE OF DŌGEN and the source of nearly every
    story later told about him.
  MENZAN ZUIHŌ 面山瑞方 (1683-1769) revised and supplemented it as the
    訂補建撕記, 1754, adding annotation and argument of his own.
  DAIKEN 大賢 drew the woodcuts for the illustrated recension, 図会,
    published 1806.

MENZAN'S CORRECTIONS ARE NOT NEUTRAL and modern scholarship prefers
the older manuscripts to his text. That is a fact about this edition
and belongs in the front matter, not hidden: what is translated here is
the book as Menzan made it and Daiken pictured it, the version that
actually formed the Sōtō school's picture of its founder for two
centuries -- not a critical reconstruction of what Kenzei wrote.
Where Menzan is plainly arguing rather than narrating (he does it at
length about Dōgen's paternity), the argument is HIS and is rendered as
his, in his voice.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. TWO IMPRESSIONS, AND WHY THERE HAVE TO BE TWO
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  READING COPY   文化14 (1817), Ogawa Tazaemon, held by the National
                 Institute of Japanese Literature, biblio 100265060.
                 Clean paper, no show-through.
  CHECK COPY     文化3 (1806), 内閣文庫, National Archives of Japan,
                 aipId 4982930 / 4982931. Browned, with the reverse
                 leaf showing through.
Both are struck from Menzan's blocks and both run to exactly 119
openings, so they are page-for-page.

THIS IS NOT BELT AND BRACES. There is no transcription of the Kenzeiki
anywhere free, and every modern edition and every English treatment is
in copyright, so the text in chapters/ is MY READING OF A WOODBLOCK
PAGE and nothing else stands behind it. A misread character in a
biography is a wrong date, a wrong name or a wrong place -- content
present, in order, and wrong, which is the defect class this project
fears most and which no check in the toolchain can see.
Measured before relying on it: on a dense page of the 1806 copy I read
roughly 90-95% of characters confidently. The two impressions together
are what close that gap, and the gain is not theoretical -- the first
word of the editorial preface reads 糞 in the 1806 copy and 冀 in the
1817. Dung against hope, from one character fouled by show-through.
WHAT THE SECOND COPY CANNOT DO: both are the same blocks, so it
resolves ILLEGIBILITY and not a shared misprint. Say so.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE ILLUSTRATIONS ARE THE SPINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------
76 of the 238 half-leaves are woodcut, and the plates carry the story:
each is captioned in large clear type naming the scene -- 祖師御誕生
(the Founder's birth), 最明寺殿ノ請ニ赴キ鎌倉ニテ菩薩戒ヲ授ケ玉フ (going
to Kamakura at Tokiyori's request to confer the bodhisattva precepts).
Those captions are the most legible text in the book and they are an
independent outline of the narrative: a transcription of the prose that
contradicts the caption beside it is wrong somewhere.
They ride through the pipeline as [Figure N: caption] markers, the
soap-bubbles convention, with FIGURE_DIR=images/kenzeiki. Unlike
soap-bubbles, THE CAPTIONS ARE THE SOURCE'S OWN and are modernised, not
invented (the ball/ rule).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE VERNE RULE, AND IT GOVERNS MORE HERE THAN USUAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------
This is a fifteenth-century hagiography and it behaves like one. A
voice speaks out of the sky before Dōgen is born to say the child will
be a great sage; portents attend his birth, his ordination and his
death; the mountain god of Hakusan copies out a text for him overnight.
NONE OF IT IS HEDGED, footnoted or explained away. Kenzei believed it
and Menzan reprinted it, and a reader who is told what to make of it is
being handed a different book. The same goes for the claims about
Dōgen's noble paternity, which are the least reliable part of the text
and are exactly what Menzan argues hardest for.

5. RATIO
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Character-based, as dogen/ (a word ratio cannot fire on a CJK source;
see that book's check.py for why shipping one anyway is worse than
shipping none). Derive the band from the first finished files and pin
it. Do NOT reuse dogen/'s 1.20-1.65: this is Japanese kanji-katakana
narrative, not kanbun, and it is much less compressed.
