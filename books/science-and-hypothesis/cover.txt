Drawn for this edition, in the manner of the Dover science paperbacks of the 1950s and 60s. It is Lobatschewsky's geometry pictured as a disc, the way geometers now call Poincaré's: straight lines are arcs that meet the rim at right angles, and the shaded triangle between three of them has angles that add up to less than two right angles. Poincaré's third chapter, on the non-Euclidean geometries, asks whether experiment could ever tell us which geometry is true; the line at the top is from that argument.
