Drawn for this edition, in the manner of the Dover science paperbacks of the 1950s and 60s. A curve climbs away from its axes, and at one point on it sits Thompson's little triangle, a tiny step dx along and the tiny rise dy that goes with it, beside the tangent whose slope is their ratio. That one picture is the whole of his first chapters. The motto at the top is the one he set at the front of the book: "What one fool can do, another can."
