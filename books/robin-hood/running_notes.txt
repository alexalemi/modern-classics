VOICE LOCK (set by 000 Preface and 001 Prologue — read 001 in full first)
  A storyteller reading aloud to a bright ten-to-fourteen-year-old, and
  enjoying it. Warm, quick, funny, vivid. Plain modern words; short and
  medium sentences; Pyle's long cumulative descriptions are unstacked but
  EVERY image stays (see the Maytime paragraph in 001). Dialogue sounds
  spoken: contractions, exclamations, interruptions. The narrator talks to
  the reader exactly as Pyle does ("I'll tell you", "as you'll hear",
  "dear Reader") and every one of those asides stays.
  NO modern slang: never "okay", "guys", "cool", "kid" as a form of address,
  "awesome", "yeah". "Hey!" is allowed for Pyle's "Ho!"/"Halloa!".
  NO costume: never thou/thee/thy/thine/ye, quoth, -eth/-est verbs, marry,
  wot, ween, trow, eke, anon, fain, prythee, an (= if), good den, betwixt,
  nay/yea/ay (use no/yes), mayhap, methinks, perchance, forsooth, whilst,
  'tis/'twas. American spelling.

NUMBERS AND MEASURES
  Scores -> plain numbers (fivescore = a hundred, sevenscore = a hundred
  and forty, threescore and ten = seventy). Rods -> yards (a rod is 5.5
  yards; "threescore rods" = "more than three hundred yards"). Ell -> yard.
  Furlong -> "an eighth of a mile" or "a few hundred yards". League ->
  "three miles". Feet, yards and miles stay. se'ennight -> a week;
  fortnight -> two weeks.

SONGS AND BALLADS (locked method)
  Every song stays as verse, every stanza, every refrain. Modernize the
  words; keep the rhyme scheme and roughly the meter and the number of
  lines. A little looseness in the meter is fine; a lost rhyme is not
  (find a new rhyme pair if the old one only worked in Pyle's English).
  Format: each line indented exactly four spaces; a blank line between
  stanzas; the quotation marks Pyle opens/closes stay at the start of the
  first line and the end of the last. Drop Pyle's _italic_ underscores
  inside verse. Song titles that Pyle sets on their own line stay on their
  own line, modernized, Title Case, no final period (they render as
  subheadings).
  Where Pyle breaks off a song with "--" keep the break (use an em dash).

PART SUMMARIES
  The first chapter of each Part opens (after its head-piece marker) with
  Pyle's italic summary paragraph, wrapped in underscores: _In which..._
  Translate it into plain modern words, keep it one paragraph, keep the
  underscores. 001 shows the pattern: "_In which you will hear about..._".

  The forester's death in 001: plain, short, unsentimental, and then
  Robin's grief in full. That is the pattern for every death in the book.
  The fights are slapstick until they are not; let the comedy be physical
  and fast.
