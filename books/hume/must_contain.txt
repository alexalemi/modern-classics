# Every pin below was copied out of hume/chapters/ and then rendered
# into the modern form the translation is REQUIRED to use. Pins are
# repeated verbatim in running_notes.txt. Matching is case- and
# whitespace-insensitive.
#
# Hume is a special case for this file: several of his famous sentences
# are ALREADY modern and are pinned UNCHANGED. That is deliberate and is
# the point of the book's strategy -- the pin is what stops a translator
# from improving a sentence that does not need improving.

# Section Five. Already perfect; pinned exactly as he wrote it.
Custom, then, is the great guide of human life.

# Section Twelve, the last paragraph of the book. Pinned exactly.
Commit it then to the flames

# Section One's closing line, and the most quoted sentence in it.
# Source: "Be a philosopher; but, amidst all your philosophy, be still
# a man." The commas around the adverbial go; nothing else changes.
be still a man

# Section Four. The problem of induction, stated as a proposition about
# a sunrise. Hume italicises the two propositions and the italics are
# kept -- see running_notes on the *markers*.
that the sun will not rise tomorrow

# Section Ten, the maxim. The full sentence runs 70 words; the pin is
# the clause everything else hangs on.
no testimony is sufficient to establish a miracle
its falsehood would be more miraculous than the fact

# Section Ten, on evidence. Already modern. The source reads "A wise
# man, therefore, proportions his belief to the evidence"; the famous
# form drops the "therefore". Pin the invariant fragment rather than
# force that choice from here.
proportions his belief to the evidence

# Section Seven, the first of the two definitions of cause -- the
# conclusion of the hardest argument in the book, italicised in the
# source and italicised in the translation.
an object followed by another

# Section Seven / Four. The technical term, which must not soften.
constant conjunction

# "bread" cannot fail, and a check that cannot fail is worse than no
# check -- it reads as coverage. (The grimm lesson.)
