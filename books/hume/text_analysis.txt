HUME'S ENQUIRY CONCERNING HUMAN UNDERSTANDING — TRANSLATION STRATEGY
====================================================================

THE WORK

Published 1748, twelve sections. Hume's own rewrite of Book One of the
Treatise, which "fell dead-born from the press" — he thought the failure
was the presentation, not the argument, and spent the rest of his life
insisting the Enquiry was the version to read. It contains the problem
of induction, the critique of causation, the essay on miracles that got
him barred from two universities, and the paragraph about committing
books to the flames.

Source: Standard Ebooks' edition of the 1748 text.


THE JUSTIFICATION, AND IT IS THE PUREST CASE IN THE COLLECTION

Measured: archaism 0.47 per 1,000 words — effectively zero, below Jacobs
and Ralston, both of which were struck from the roadmap for being too
clean to need this project. Abstraction/calque score 49.1, the HIGHEST
measured anywhere in the screening pass.

That combination is the whole reason to do the book. Nothing in it
sounds old. There is no "thou", no "hath", no inversion to speak of. It
is blocked entirely by eighteenth-century philosophical vocabulary used
with total consistency: "the operations of the understanding", "matter
of fact and real existence", "subserviency to the easy and humane",
"the sceptical solution of these doubts". A reader who trips on "hath"
knows to look it up. A reader who reads "matter of fact" as the modern
idiom — meaning "prosaic" — has just lost the technical term the book's
central distinction is built on, and will not find out.

This is the class named at epictetus/ and sharpened here: difficulty you
cannot feel is a stronger case for a retelling than difficulty you can.


AND THE NUMBER OVERSTATES IT. THE TRANSLATION MUST KNOW THIS.

Hume is one of the great English prose stylists and he knew it. The
famous sentences are already perfect and are already modern:

  "Custom, then, is the great guide of human life."
  "Commit it then to the flames: for it can contain nothing but
   sophistry and illusion."
  "A wise man proportions his belief to the evidence."

The obstruction is in the APPARATUS AROUND the argument — the
section-opening throat-clearing, the transitional machinery, the
periodic sentences that set up a distinction before making it. It is not
in the arguments themselves, which are lucid, and it is not in the
punchlines, which are better than anything a modernization will produce.

THE JOB IS TO CLEAR THE APPARATUS WITHOUT FLATTENING THE PROSE, and this
is the opposite of the usual danger in this project. Elsewhere the risk
is leaving too much of the old text standing. Here the risk is
paraphrasing a sentence that did not need it.

RULE: if a sentence is already clear, LEAVE IT. Change nothing in it.
A file that comes back with every sentence rewritten has been damaged,
even if each rewrite is individually defensible.


THE VOCABULARY IS THE MACHINE — WHICH PARTS OF IT

The symbolic-logic/ lesson applies with a limit. There, Carroll's terms
WERE the machinery and all of them were kept. Here some terms are load-
bearing and some are merely eighteenth-century, and the two must be
sorted deliberately.

KEEP AND GLOSS ONCE (these are Hume's argument, not his period):

  impression / idea         The founding distinction of Section Two:
                            an impression is the lively original, an
                            idea its faded copy. Gloss at first use in
                            Section Two, then never again.
  matter of fact            Half of Hume's fork, and the phrase a modern
                            reader is guaranteed to misread. Gloss it
                            EXPLICITLY the first time and use it
                            thereafter. This is the single most
                            important gloss in the book.
  relations of ideas        The other half of the fork.
  custom / habit            The book's answer to its own problem. Never
                            soften to "familiarity" or "expectation" —
                            he means the mechanical tendency, and the
                            flatness of the word is the point.
  constant conjunction      Technical. Kept exactly.
  necessary connection      Technical, and the thing he says we never
                            observe. Kept exactly, including in the
                            section title.
  experimental reasoning    Means reasoning FROM EXPERIENCE, not
                            laboratory work. Gloss once; the modern
                            reading is wrong and is invisible.
  the understanding         Kept in the title and where he uses it as a
                            faculty; elsewhere "the mind" is often what
                            he means and reads better.
  sceptical / scepticism    Kept, with British spelling as the source
                            has it. Mitigated scepticism and Pyrrhonism
                            are distinguished in Section Twelve and the
                            distinction must survive.

MODERNISE SILENTLY (period vocabulary, carrying no argument):

  subserviency              -> usefulness / service
  the vulgar                -> ordinary people  (NOT "the vulgar")
  sensible qualities        -> qualities we perceive by the senses
  conjoined                 -> occurring together  (but see constant
                               conjunction, which is kept)
  actuated                  -> driven / moved
  peculiar                  -> distinctive  [FALSE FRIEND]
  sensible (of a fact)      -> aware  [FALSE FRIEND]
  discover                  -> reveal  [FALSE FRIEND]
  prevent                   -> go before  [FALSE FRIEND]
  want                      -> lack  [FALSE FRIEND]
  presently                 -> at once  [FALSE FRIEND]
  admit of                  -> allow
  esteem / repute           -> consider
  nice                      -> precise / subtle  [FALSE FRIEND]
  betwixt                   -> between
  entirely / wholly         often deletable throughout; he pads
  it is evident that        usually deletable; he uses it as a comma


ITALICS ARE ARGUMENT, NOT DECORATION — AND THEY NOW SURVIVE

202 emphasis spans came through prep, carried as *markers*. This is the
FIRST book in the project that depends on the emphasis rendering added
2026-08-17, and it depends on it heavily, because Hume italicises:

  - the terms he is DEFINING, at the moment of defining them;
  - the proposition under examination, set off from the discussion of
    it ("*that the sun will not rise tomorrow*");
  - the two definitions of cause in Section Seven, which are the
    conclusion of the book's hardest argument and are italicised in
    full.

KEEP EVERY ONE, on the same words. Where a sentence is rewritten and the
italicised phrase moves, the markers move with it. Where a rewrite would
break an italicised run in half, do not do the rewrite. Do not ADD
emphasis Hume did not have — this is the only book where the *markers*
mean something, and inflating them makes them mean less.

Remember the pipeline is otherwise markup-free: no other asterisks, no
underscores, never an all-caps line, tab indent for verse or table only.


THE VERNE RULE

  - SECTION TEN, OF MIRACLES, stands entire and unhedged. The argument
    against testimony for miracles, the four counter-considerations, the
    remarks about the credulity of ignorant nations, the closing line
    that the Christian religion cannot be believed without a miracle —
    all his, all in full, with no note and no wink. It is the most
    famous chapter and it is deliberately provocative; softening it
    would remove the book's point.
  - SECTION ELEVEN puts the argument against providence and a future
    state into the mouth of "a friend who loves sceptical paradoxes",
    with Hume playing Epicurus' advocate. KEEP THE FRAME. He built the
    deniability in on purpose and it is part of the text.
  - SECTION EIGHT'S compatibilism about liberty and necessity, including
    the passages on the sins of a criminal being traceable to God.
  - The eighteenth-century remarks about national character and about
    "barbarous nations" in Section Ten. They are his and they are load-
    bearing in his argument about testimony, which is precisely why they
    cannot be quietly dropped. Rendered plainly, not sharpened, not
    softened, no note.
  - SECTION NINE on the reason of animals, kept as written.

DISTINGUISH DATED CLAIM FROM DATED WORD, as at fleming/: "the vulgar"
is a dated WORD and goes; what he says about ordinary people's reasoning
is a dated CLAIM and stays.


VOICE

An urbane, extremely confident man writing for the coffee-house and not
for the university — he says so in Section One, where he distinguishes
the "easy and obvious" philosophy from the "accurate and abstruse" and
argues for doing both. He is charming, and the charm is strategic: he is
about to dismantle causation and he does it in the tone of a man
recommending a wine.

Keep:
  - the FIRST PERSON. He says "I" constantly, and stages his own
    puzzlement ("I shall allow, if you please, that the one proposition
    may justly be inferred from the other: I know in fact that it always
    is inferred").
  - the DIALOGUE with an imagined objector, which runs through Sections
    Four and Seven. Keep the objector's voice distinct from his.
  - the IRONY, which is dry, frequent and easy to lose. Section Ten is
    ironic almost throughout and reads as sincere if the register slips.
  - his DELIBERATE ANTICLIMAXES: he raises a devastating problem and
    then says, in effect, that nobody need worry, because nature has not
    left it up to reason. That structure IS the book.


STRUCTURE

Twelve sections, Roman in the source, rendered "Section One:" ...
"Section Twelve:" — the manifest convention, and the form assemble.py's
CHAP_LINE recognises. Sections Four, Five, Seven, Eight, Ten, Eleven and
Twelve have Hume's own "Part I / Part II" divisions; these are rendered
in WORD FORM ("Part One") for a reason — assemble.strip_front deletes a
line matching ^Part [IVXLC0-9]+: from a file's front matter, which is
how descartes/ lost all four Parts of the Principles.

20 files, 47,875 words. Multi-part files take the standard form:
heading, "(Part n of k)", blank, body. Parts 2+ never re-introduce the
section; non-final parts never conclude it. NOTE Section Eight is in
THREE parts and Sections Four, Five, Seven, Ten, Eleven, Twelve in two —
check the manifest before writing any file.

RATIO: EN->EN, target 0.95-1.10. Verify with --min-ratio 0.85
--max-ratio 1.3. Cutting the throat-clearing and unstacking the periodic
sentences roughly cancel. Under 0.9 means an argument has been
compressed — most likely one of Section Seven's chains, where each step
looks redundant and none of them is.
