Jean Siméon Chardin's *The House of Cards* (1737). A boy in a brown coat, his hair tied back with a ribbon, leans over a green-topped table and sets up a row of playing cards, holding the next few ready in his other hand. A court card and a blank one stand in the open drawer. Chardin painted it eleven years before the *Enquiry*: a fragile structure raised by careful habit, which is Hume's account of what our knowledge of the world rests on.
