IBN TUFAYL, حي بن يقظان (HAYY IBN YAQZAN) -- STANDING BRIEF

WHAT THIS WORK IS
A scholarly modernization of a canonical text of medieval Islamic
philosophy. Ibn Tufayl (c. 1105-1185), physician and vizier at the Almohad
court in Marrakesh, wrote this philosophical tale in Arabic in the 12th
century. A child grows up alone on an equatorial island, nursed by a doe;
when she dies he dissects her to find what has left her, and step by step,
by observation and reason alone, he works out anatomy, physics, astronomy,
the existence of a Creator, and finally mystical union with Him. Then a
religious man, Absal, arrives from a neighboring island, and the two
discover that Hayy's reason and Absal's revealed religion agree; their
attempt to teach the people of Absal's island fails. It ends with a short
address to the reader. Source: Arabic Wikisource. A PUBLIC-DOMAIN ENGLISH
CRIB exists: Simon Ockley's 1708 translation, hayy/source/ockley.txt (the
story starts at "THE HISTORY OF _HAI EBN YOKDHAN_", line 842). Use it ONLY
to check your understanding of a hard sentence; translate from the Arabic,
never copy or imitate Ockley's English, and ignore his footnotes.

THE STANDARD: READABILITY IS THE DELIVERABLE
Every sentence must be one a bright fourteen-year-old could read aloud and
follow, with every clause of the source present and nothing summarised.
This is a story of discovery: let the reader feel each step (the doe's
death, the dissection, fire, the stars, the turn inward). The later
philosophical and mystical passages are the hardest: give each abstract
step a handhold in plain words, but keep the argument whole. Where Ibn
Tufayl alludes to the Qur'an or hadith, keep the quotation and let the
sentence carry its sense; Qur'anic phrases may be set in quotation marks.
Names: Hayy ibn Yaqzan (after the first mention "Hayy"; the name means
"Alive, son of Awake" -- this gloss appears ONCE, in the prologue or first
story file); Absal; Salaman (king of the other island); Abu Ali ibn Sina
(Avicenna: "Ibn Sina, the philosopher the West called Avicenna" once);
al-Ghazali; Ibn Bajja; al-Farabi. The island: "an island of India, below
the equator".

LOCKED TERMS: الروح الحيواني "the animal spirit"; الواجب الوجود "the
Necessary Being"; الفاعل / الموجد "the Maker"; الجسم / الصورة / المادة
"body / form / matter"; الفلك "the heavens" (the celestial spheres:
"sphere"); الذات "essence"; المشاهدة "direct vision" (his mystical
seeing); الذوق "tasting" (experiential knowing); الفناء "passing away"
(self-annihilation, glossed once); النظر "reasoning" / "inquiry".
American spelling. Dates are not an issue; distances in plain words.

FORMAT (exact)
Line 1: the heading in your prompt.
Line 2: "(Part N of 4)" for story files; none for the prologue.
Blank line, then paragraphs separated by one blank line; split long Arabic
paragraphs at natural breaks, losing nothing. No other headings. Story
parts 2-4 never re-introduce; parts 1-3 never conclude. The story's last
file ends with Ibn Tufayl's closing address to his "brother": keep it.
Write hayy/modern_chapters/NNN.txt.

RETURN: (a) three sentences on what happens in your part; (b) terms and
names you settled; (c) any sentence where you relied on Ockley to resolve
the Arabic.
