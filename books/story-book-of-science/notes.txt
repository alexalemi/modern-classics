The Story-Book of Science -- witness notes (2026-10-12)

SOURCE  Project Gutenberg #56795 (pg56795-h.zip, HTML with 69 plates + cover
        + publisher's device), transcribed from an Internet Archive scan of
        the Century Co. edition, New York, August 1917.

SCANS   storybookofscien00fabr, storybookofscien00fabruoft (ABBYY OCR), and
        storybookscienc00bickgoog (Google OCR), all the 1917 printing.
        scan_diff.py --vote fabr fabruoft: 74 readings; --vote fabr goog: 62.
        Nearly all are OCR noise: plate labels read into the text, ligatures
        (antennæ, larvæ, diœcious, manœuvers), drop caps lost, running heads.

RESTORED (Gutenberg typos; all three scans agree on the printed reading)
  "found ever where and"   -> "found everywhere and"    (Ch. XXXV)
  "The net must he spun."  -> "The net must be spun."  (Ch. XXVII)

LEFT AS GUTENBERG HAS THEM
  The print's own misprints, which the transcriber corrected and which are
  not Bicknell's words: "runs, drink, enjoys", "The larva finally reached
  the ramparts" (lava), "naseau", "vetteran", "innocousness", "litle",
  "themseleves", "tour of the word", "began to bur".
  ABBYY misreadings of capital/initial R that the two Internet Archive OCRs
  share ("Barely are the clouds", "bather", "bushing", "Eomas", "eeason"):
  the Google OCR and the sense agree with Gutenberg ("Rarely are the clouds").

HEADINGS  The chapter heads, not the Contents, which differs three times:
  Contents "The Numerous Family" / head A NUMEROUS FAMILY (and running heads)
  Contents "The Metals"          / head METALS
  Contents "Running Water"       / head RUNNING WATERS (and running heads)
  "WAVES   SALT   SEAWEEDS" is printed with wide spaces and no punctuation
  in Contents, head and running heads alike; kept as "Waves Salt Seaweeds".

PLATES REPORTED, NOT FIXED (the caption pass)
  The plates are the American publisher's, not Fabre's: Copper Head (a North
  American pit viper) in the chapter on the European viper; American Hornet;
  Red-humped Apple Tree "Caterpillar"; the Virginian Railway's no. 604 as "A
  modern Locomotive"; Giant Geyser, Yellowstone, beside Uncle Paul's account
  of the Great Geyser of Iceland. Chapter XLIX's text points at letters A
  and B on the locomotive picture; neither locomotive plate carries them.
  Three diagrams in Chapter LII (be, bf, bg) have no printed label.
