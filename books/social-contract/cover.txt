Allan Ramsay's portrait of Jean-Jacques Rousseau (1766). He looks out of a dark ground in a tall fur cap and a fur-trimmed robe, one hand at his breast. Ramsay painted him in Britain, where Rousseau had gone with David Hume four years after this book was burned, still moving from one refuge to the next. The fur cap and robe were a costume he had taken to wearing in exile.
