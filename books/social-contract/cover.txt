Drawn for this edition, in the manner of the Dover science paperbacks of the 1950s and 60s, after the geometry Rousseau borrows in Book III. Lay the sovereign and the people end to end as two lengths, draw a semicircle on the whole, and the upright where the two meet is their mean proportional: in his figure, the government, which takes the sovereign's orders and passes them on to the people. Squeeze one length and the other must change to keep the proportion, which is his argument for why a bigger state needs a stronger government. The line at the top is his.
