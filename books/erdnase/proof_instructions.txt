PROOFREADING: S. W. Erdnase, Artifice, Ruse and Subterfuge at the Card Table (1902)

You are proofreading pages of a 1902 book for a RESTORED EDITION: the
author's text, unchanged, in clean modern markup. No transcription of this
book exists anywhere we can use, so YOUR READING OF THE PAGE IMAGE IS THE
TEXT. The OCR draft is a convenience and is often wrong; the image is
the authority. This is a scholarly edition of a classic of card magic and
of the history of gambling; it describes cheating techniques as its
subject, and it is to be transcribed exactly as printed.

WHAT TO WRITE
- Every word the page prints, in order, EXCEPT: the running head at the
  top ("CARD TABLE ARTIFICE.", "LEGERDEMAIN.", "INTRODUCTION.",
  "CONTENTS." with the page number), the page number itself, and the line
  "Copyright, by S. W. Erdnase, 1902." printed under some drawings (leave
  it out, but COUNT it: see the report).
- Spelling, capitals, punctuation and hyphenated compounds EXACTLY as
  printed. Do not modernise, do not correct the author's grammar or
  usage, do not "fix" an odd word. If the printer made an obvious
  misprint, transcribe it as printed and list it in your report.
- One paragraph per line: join the printed lines of a paragraph into ONE
  line. Separate paragraphs, headings and figures with ONE blank line.
- A word broken at the end of a printed line ("be-" / "tween") is joined
  ("between"). Keep the hyphen where the word is a real compound
  ("left-hand", "one-quarter", "under-cut", "in-jog", "out-jog") -- decide
  by how the author spells the same word elsewhere when you can see it;
  list any you are unsure of in the report.
- Quotation marks and apostrophes as plain ASCII " and '. Dashes as the
  em dash character —. Keep "etc.", "i. e." and the like as printed.

MARKUP (the only markup there is)
- *italic* for italic type (the "When Cards Are Riffled." in a run-in
  heading, "Second Method.", book titles, emphasised words).
- ^^Small Capitals^^ for words set in SMALL CAPITALS inside a line, as at
  the start of many paragraphs ("^^Bottom Palm^^ *When Cards Are
  Riffled*.—When the cards are riffled..."). Write the letters as they
  would be capitalised in ordinary type (initial capitals as printed large).
- A HEADING printed on its own line (centred, in capitals or in small
  capitals) is its own paragraph, written in capital letters exactly as
  printed: "TO MAINTAIN THE BOTTOM PALM WHILE DEALING." A numbered
  sub-heading on its own line is written as printed:
  "II. To Retain the Complete Stock."
- A DRAWING is a line "[Figure N]" where N is the figure number printed
  under it ("Fig. 44" -> [Figure 44]), placed exactly where the drawing
  sits between the lines of text. If the drawing interrupts a paragraph,
  write the first part of the paragraph, then the figure line, then the
  rest of the paragraph starting with "+ " (plus and space), which means
  "this continues the paragraph above; no paragraph break".
- If the page OPENS in the middle of a paragraph continued from the
  previous page (no indent, usually lower case or mid-sentence), start the
  first paragraph with "+ " too. If the page opens a new paragraph, do not.
- Tables, formulas, or card lists set in columns: one row per line, each
  line starting with a TAB, cells separated by " | ".
- Verse or a letter/signature set off: each printed line on its own line,
  starting with a TAB.
