Drawn for this edition, in the manner of the Dover science paperbacks of the 1950s and 60s, after Madison's compound republic in No. 51. The power the people give up is split first between two governments, the federal and the state, and each share is split again among separate departments, three blocks above and three below. Madison's point is that the two governments and their departments watch one another, so that the people's rights get a double security. The line at the top is his.
