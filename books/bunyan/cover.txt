Thomas Cole's *The Voyage of Life: Manhood* (1842), the third of his four paintings of a human life as a journey down a river. A lone man in a red tunic stands in a gilded boat on dark rushing water, hands clasped in prayer, between storm-lit crags, and a guardian spirit watches him from a break in the clouds. It is an allegory of the same kind as Bunyan's, a single traveller in danger on the road to the next world.
