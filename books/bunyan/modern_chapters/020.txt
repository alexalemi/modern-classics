The Land of Beulah and the River

Now I saw in my dream that by this time the Pilgrims had got over the Enchanted Ground and were coming into the country of Beulah, whose air was very sweet and pleasant. The road ran straight through it, and they took their ease there for a while. Here they heard the singing of birds all day long, and saw the flowers coming up out of the earth every day, and heard the voice of the turtledove in the land. In this country the sun shines night and day; and so this was well beyond the Valley of the Shadow of Death, and out of Giant Despair's reach as well — from this place they could not so much as see Doubting Castle.

Here they were within sight of the city they were going to, and here too some of its citizens came out to meet them; because the Shining Ones walk about freely in this land, since it is on the borders of heaven. In this land the contract between the bride and the bridegroom was renewed as well. Yes, here, "as the bridegroom rejoices over the bride, so did their God rejoice over them." Here they had no lack of grain or wine; because in this place they met with an abundance of everything they had been looking for through their whole pilgrimage.

Here they heard voices out of the city — loud voices, saying, "Say to the daughter of Zion, Look, your salvation is coming! Look, his reward is with him!" Here all the inhabitants of the country called them "The holy people," "The redeemed of the Lord," "Sought out."

Now as they walked in this land they had more joy than they had had in the parts further away from the kingdom they were bound for; and drawing near to the city, they got a still clearer view of it. It was built of pearls and precious stones, and its street was paved with gold; so that between the natural glory of the city and the reflection of the sunlight off it, Christian fell sick with longing. Hopeful also had a fit or two of the same illness. So here they lay by for a while, crying out because of the pangs of it, "If you find my Beloved, tell him that I am sick with love."

But being a little strengthened, and better able to bear their sickness, they walked on and came nearer and nearer, to where there were orchards, vineyards and gardens whose gates opened onto the highway. Now as they came up to these places, there stood the gardener in the road; and the Pilgrims said to him: Whose fine vineyards and gardens are these?

He answered: They are the King's, and they are planted here for his own delight, and for the refreshment of pilgrims as well.

So the gardener took them into the vineyards and told them to refresh themselves with the good things there. He also showed them the King's walks and the shady arbours where he liked to be; and here they stayed, and slept.

Now I saw in my dream that they talked more in their sleep this time than they had in the whole of their journey; and while I was puzzling over it, the gardener said to me: Why are you puzzling over the matter? It is the nature of the fruit of the grapes in these vineyards to go down so sweetly that it makes the lips of sleeping men speak.

So I saw that when they woke they set themselves to go up to the city. But as I said, the reflection of the sun off the city — because "the city was pure gold" — was so extremely glorious that they still could not look at it with the naked eye, but only through an instrument made for the purpose. So I saw that as they went on, two men met them, in clothing that shone like gold; and their faces shone like the light.

These men asked the Pilgrims where they had come from, and they told them. They also asked where they had lodged, and what difficulties and dangers, what comforts and pleasures they had met with along the way; and they told them.

Then the men who had met them said: You have only two difficulties left to meet, and then you are in the city.

So Christian and his companion asked the men to come along with them; and they said they would. But, they said, you must gain it by your own faith.

So I saw in my dream that they went on together until they came in sight of the gate.

Now I further saw that between them and the gate there was a river — but there was no bridge to cross by, and the river was very deep. So at the sight of this river the Pilgrims were badly shaken. But the men who were with them said: You must go through it, or you cannot reach the gate.

Then the Pilgrims began to ask whether there was no other way to the gate. To which they answered: Yes; but nobody except two — Enoch and Elijah — has been allowed to walk that path since the foundation of the world, nor will be, until the last trumpet sounds.

Then the Pilgrims, and Christian in particular, began to sink in their minds, and looked this way and that; but they could find no way at all by which they might get round the river. Then they asked the men whether the water was the same depth all the way across. They said no — and yet they could not help them there; because, they said, you will find it deeper or shallower according to how you believe in the King of the place.

So they addressed themselves to the water; and going in, Christian began to sink, and crying out to his good friend Hopeful he said: I am sinking in deep water; the waves are going over my head, all his waves are going over me!

Then the other said: Take heart, my brother. I can feel the bottom, and it is solid.

Then Christian said: Ah, my friend — "the sorrows of death have closed round me"; I shall never see the land that flows with milk and honey. And with that, a great darkness and horror came down on Christian, so that he could not see in front of him. Here too he largely lost his senses, so that he could neither remember nor talk coherently about any of the sweet refreshments he had met with on his pilgrimage. Everything he said only showed that he had a horror in his mind, and fears in his heart that he was going to die in that river and never get in at the gate.

Here also, as those standing by could tell, he was full of troubled thoughts about the sins he had committed, both since he became a pilgrim and before it. It was noticed too that he was troubled by apparitions of hobgoblins and evil spirits, because every now and then he would let as much slip in words. So Hopeful had all he could do here to keep his brother's head above water. Sometimes he would go right under, and then after a while come up again half dead.

Hopeful would try to comfort him too, saying: Brother, I can see the gate, and men standing by to receive us. But Christian would answer: It is you — it is you they are waiting for. You have been Hopeful ever since I knew you.

And so have you, said Hopeful to Christian.

Ah, brother, said he — surely if I were right with him, he would rise up now and help me. But because of my sins he has brought me into the trap and left me here.

Then Hopeful said: My brother, you have quite forgotten the text where it is said of the wicked, "There are no pangs in their death; their strength is firm. They are not in trouble like other men, nor plagued like other men." These troubles and distresses you are going through in these waters are no sign that God has abandoned you. They are sent to test you — to see whether you will call to mind what you have already received of his goodness, and live on him in your distress.

Then I saw in my dream that Christian was lost in thought for a while. And Hopeful added this word to him: Take heart — Jesus Christ makes you whole.

And with that Christian burst out in a loud voice: Oh, I see him again! And he tells me, "When you pass through the waters, I will be with you; and through the rivers, they shall not overflow you."

Then they both took courage, and after that the enemy was as still as a stone until they had got across. Christian soon found ground to stand on, and it turned out that the rest of the river was shallow. And so they got over.

Now on the bank of the river on the other side they saw the two shining men again, who were waiting there for them; so having come out of the river, they greeted them, saying: We are ministering spirits, sent out to serve those who shall be heirs of salvation.

So they went along towards the gate. Now you must note that the city stood on a mighty hill, but the Pilgrims went up that hill easily, because they had these two men to lead them up by the arms. They had also left their mortal clothing behind them in the river; because although they went in wearing it, they came out without it. So they went up with great lightness and speed, even though the foundation the city was built on was higher than the clouds. They went up through the regions of the air, talking sweetly as they went, and comforted because they had got over the river safely and had such glorious companions attending them.

The talk they had with the Shining Ones was about the glory of the place; and they told them that its beauty and glory were beyond describing. There, they said, is "Mount Zion, the heavenly Jerusalem, the innumerable company of angels, and the spirits of just men made perfect." You are going now, they said, to the paradise of God, where you shall see the tree of life and eat its never-fading fruit; and when you get there you shall be given white robes, and your walking and talking shall be with the King every day, through all the days of eternity.

There you shall not see again the things you saw when you were in the lower region on earth — sorrow, sickness, affliction and death — "for the former things have passed away." You are going now to Abraham, to Isaac and Jacob, and to the prophets: men whom God has taken away from the evil to come, and who are now resting on their beds, each one walking in his righteousness.

Then the men asked: What must we do in the holy place?

To which it was answered: There you must receive the comfort of all your labour, and have joy for all your sorrow. You must reap what you have sown — the fruit of all your prayers and tears and sufferings for the King along the way. In that place you must wear crowns of gold, and enjoy the perpetual sight and vision of the Holy One, because "there you shall see him as he is." There you shall serve him continually with praise, with shouting and thanksgiving — the one you longed to serve in the world, though with great difficulty, because of the weakness of your flesh.

There your eyes shall be delighted with the sight, and your ears with hearing the pleasant voice of the Mighty One. There you shall enjoy your friends again, who have gone there before you; and there you shall joyfully welcome every one who follows you into the holy place after you. There too you shall be clothed with glory and majesty, and given a retinue fit to ride out with the King of glory. When he comes with the sound of a trumpet in the clouds, as on the wings of the wind, you shall come with him; and when he sits on the throne of judgment, you shall sit beside him. Yes, and when he passes sentence on all the workers of wickedness, whether they are angels or men, you also shall have a voice in that judgment, because they were his enemies and yours. And when he returns to the city again, you shall go too, with the sound of the trumpet, and be with him for ever.

Now while they were drawing on towards the gate like this, a company of the heavenly host came out to meet them; and the other two Shining Ones said to them: These are the men who loved our Lord when they were in the world, and who left everything for his holy name. He has sent us to fetch them, and we have brought them this far on the journey they longed for, so that they may go in and look their Redeemer in the face with joy.

Then the heavenly host gave a great shout, saying, "Blessed are those who are called to the marriage supper of the Lamb."

At this time too there came out to meet them several of the King's trumpeters, dressed in white and shining clothes, who with loud and melodious sounds made the very heavens echo. These trumpeters greeted Christian and his companion with ten thousand welcomes from the world; and they did it with shouting, and with the sound of the trumpet.

This done, they surrounded them on every side. Some went in front, some behind, some on the right hand and some on the left, as though to guard them through the upper regions, sounding continually as they went, with melodious noise and high notes; so that the very sight of it, to anybody who could have seen it, was as if heaven itself had come down to meet them. So they walked on together; and as they walked, every now and then these trumpeters, with joyful sound, would mix their music with looks and gestures to keep showing Christian and his brother how welcome they were in their company, and how gladly they had come out to meet them.

And now these two men were, as it were, in heaven before they came to it, being swallowed up with the sight of angels and with the sound of their melodious notes. Here too they had the city itself in view, and they thought they could hear all the bells in it ringing to welcome them in. But above all, there were the warm and joyful thoughts they had about living there themselves, with such company, and that for ever and ever. Oh, what tongue or pen could ever put their glorious joy into words!

And so they came up to the gate.

Now when they had come up to the gate, there was written over it in letters of gold: "Blessed are those who do his commandments, that they may have a right to the tree of life, and may enter in through the gates into the city."

Then I saw in my dream that the Shining Men told them to call at the gate; and when they did, some looked down over the top of it — Enoch, Moses and Elijah among them — and were told: These pilgrims have come from the City of Destruction, out of the love they have for the King of this place. And then the pilgrims each handed in his certificate, the one they had been given at the beginning. So these were carried in to the King, who, when he had read them, said: Where are the men?

To which it was answered: They are standing outside the gate.

Then the King commanded that the gate be opened. "That the righteous nation," he said, "which keeps the truth, may enter in."

Now I saw in my dream that these two men went in at the gate; and as they entered, they were transfigured, and clothes were put on them that shone like gold. There were people who met them with harps and crowns and gave them to them — the harps to praise with, and the crowns as a token of honour. Then I heard in my dream that all the bells in the city rang again for joy, and that it was said to them, "Enter into the joy of your Lord." And I heard the men themselves singing with a loud voice, saying, "Blessing and honour and glory and power be to him that sits on the throne, and to the Lamb, for ever and ever."

Now just as the gates were opened to let the men in, I looked in after them; and the City shone like the sun. The streets were paved with gold, and in them walked a great many men with crowns on their heads, palms in their hands, and golden harps to sing praises with. There were some among them who had wings, and they answered one another without stopping, saying, "Holy, holy, holy is the Lord." And after that they shut the gates — which, when I had seen it, made me wish myself among them.

Now while I was gazing at all this, I turned my head to look back, and saw Ignorance come up to the riverside. But he got over quickly, and without half the difficulty the other two men had met with; because there happened to be in that place at the time a ferryman called Vain-hope, who helped him over with his boat. So he, like the others I had seen, went up the hill to come to the gate — only he came alone, and nobody met him with the least word of encouragement.

When he had come up to the gate, he looked up at the writing above it and began to knock, supposing that he would be let in at once. But he was asked by the men who looked over the top of the gate: Where have you come from, and what do you want?

He answered: I have eaten and drunk in the presence of the King, and he has taught in our streets.

Then they asked him for his certificate, so that they could go in and show it to the King. So he fumbled inside his coat for one, and found none.

Then they said: Have you none at all? But the man never answered a word.

So they told the King; but he would not come down to see him. Instead he ordered the two Shining Ones who had brought Christian and Hopeful to the City to go out and take Ignorance, and bind him hand and foot, and carry him away. So they took him up and carried him through the air to the door I had seen in the side of the hill, and put him in there.

Then I saw that there was a way to hell, even from the gates of Heaven, as well as from the City of Destruction!

So I awoke, and behold it was a dream.

The Conclusion

	Now, Reader, I have told my dream to you.
	See whether you can interpret it to me,
	or to yourself, or to your neighbour — but take care
	of misinterpreting; because that, instead
	of doing good, will only do you harm.
	Misinterpret, and evil follows on.
	Take care as well that you are not extreme
	in playing with the outside of my dream,
	and do not let my figures and comparisons
	set you off laughing, or set you quarrelling.
	Leave that to boys and fools; but as for you,
	see the substance of my matter through.
	Draw back the curtains, look behind my veil,
	turn up my metaphors, and do not fail:
	there, if you look for them, you'll find such things
	as will be helpful to an honest mind.
	Whatever dross you find there, be so bold
	as to throw it away — but do preserve the gold.
	And what if my gold should be wrapped up in ore?
	Nobody throws the apple out for the core.
	But if you cast the whole lot away as vain,
	I do not know but it will make me dream again.
