Cesare Maccari's fresco *Cicero Denounces Catiline* (1889). Cicero stands alone on the floor of the Senate, arm raised, speaking, while senators in white togas fill the curved benches and watch him. The scene is 63 BC, the year he was consul and put down Catiline's conspiracy. It was the proudest moment of his career, and in this book, written twenty years later, he is still defending what he did then.
