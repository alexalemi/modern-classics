# Every pin below was copied out of subjection/chapters/ and then
# rendered into the modern form the translation is REQUIRED to use.
# Matching is case- and whitespace-insensitive but NOT punctuation-
# insensitive, so the pins avoid internal commas wherever possible --
# which sometimes means a pin encodes a punctuation decision too.
#
# The lesson these encode, from epictetus/ and re-learned in augustine/:
# PIN FROM THE TEXT, then decide the modern form deliberately. Never
# write a pin from a recollection of how the famous quotation goes.
#
# As in mill/, SEVERAL ARE PINNED UNCHANGED. Mill's best sentences here
# are already modern, and the hume rule says leave a clear sentence
# alone; the pin is what stops a translator improving one that needs no
# improvement.

# Chapter One, the thesis of the book, in its first sentence.
# Source: "the legal subordination of one sex to the other—is wrong in
# itself, and now one of the chief hindrances to human improvement".
# Pinned on the comma-free head of it.
the legal subordination of one sex to the other

# Chapter One, the same sentence's remedy. Pinned UNCHANGED.
# Source: "it ought to be replaced by a principle of perfect equality".
a principle of perfect equality

# Chapter One. The single most-quoted line in the book, and the one a
# careless translation flattens into "women have been shaped by their
# upbringing" -- which is true, much weaker, and not what he wrote.
# Pinned UNCHANGED.
# Source: "What is now called the nature of women is an eminently
# artificial thing".
an eminently artificial thing

# Chapter Two. The claim the chapter exists to earn, and Mill means it
# literally: he has just spent pages on what the law of coverture
# actually permits. Pinned UNCHANGED.
# Source: "Marriage is the only actual bondage known to our law. There
# remain no legal slaves, except the mistress of every house."
the only actual bondage known to our law
except the mistress of every house

# Chapter Two, and the qualification MUST survive with it -- Mill is
# careful to say wives are not in general treated as slaves are before
# he says this, and dropping the care would make him cruder than he is.
# Source: "no slave is a slave to the same lengths, and in so full a
# sense of the word, as a wife is."
no slave is a slave to the same lengths

# Chapter Three. Mill's diagnosis of what the whole edifice is really
# defending, and the sentence that most surprises a modern reader who
# expects Victorian delicacy. Pinned UNCHANGED.
# Source: "the generality of the male sex cannot yet tolerate the idea
# of living with an equal."
cannot yet tolerate the idea of living with an equal

# Chapter One's closing line, which is pure contempt and has to land as
# contempt. Source: "any acquirements but those of an odalisque, or of
# a domestic servant."
odalisque

# NOT PINNED, deliberately: "equality" and "women", which cannot fail
# and would read as coverage without being any. The grimm rule.
