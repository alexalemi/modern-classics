Carl Rahl's *Kriemhild at Siegfried's Body Names Hagen as His Murderer and Swears Revenge* (1835). Siegfried lies dead on a bier beneath a cross, his wound still bleeding, while the men behind him point straight at Hagen, who stands in his winged helmet with his hand on his sword and will not look. In the poem a murdered man's wounds bleed again when his killer comes near, and that is how Kriemhild knows. Her revenge is the whole second half of the book.
