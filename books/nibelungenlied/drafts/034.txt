Chapter 35: How Iring Fought Hagen

{2028} Then Margrave Iring of Denmark called out: "I have set my
whole life on honor for a long time now, and in the storm of battle I
have done as well as the best. Now bring me my weapons. Truly, I mean
to take on Hagen."

{2029} "I would advise against that," said Hagen. "Better tell the
Huns' warriors to stand further back. If two or three of you jump
into the hall, I will send them back down the stairs in a very sorry
state."

{2030} "That will not make me give it up," said Iring. "I have tried
things just as dangerous before. Truly, I will take you on with my
sword, you alone. What good is all the bragging you have done with
words?"

{2031} Then the warrior Iring was quickly armed, and Irnfried of
Thuringia, a bold young man, and the very strong Hawart, with a good
thousand men. Whatever Iring set out to do, they all meant to stand by
him in it. {2032} The fiddler saw a very large company coming up in
arms with Iring, with many a good helmet laced on their heads, and
bold Volker grew more than a little angry at that.

{2033} "Do you see Iring walking over there, friend Hagen, the one who
vowed to take you on alone with his sword? How does lying suit a hero?
I call it a disgrace. A good thousand warriors or more are coming with
him, armed."

{2034} "Do not call me a liar," said Hawart's vassal. "I will gladly
do what I have vowed, and I will not back out of it from any fear.
However terrible Hagen may be, I will take him on alone."

{2035} Iring went down on his knees to his kinsmen and his men,
asking the warriors to let him fight alone. They did it unwillingly,
for they knew arrogant Hagen of the Burgundian land only too well.
{2036} But he begged them so long that in the end it happened: when
his household saw what he wanted, and that he was striving for honor,
they let him go. And then those two fought a grim fight.

{2037} Iring of Denmark carried his spear high and covered himself
with his shield, that splendid, noble warrior, and ran up at Hagen in
front of the hall, and a great roar went up from the warriors.
{2038} They hurled their spears with all the strength of their arms,
through the strong shields and onto the bright armor beneath, so that
the shafts went spinning high in the air; and then the two grim, bold
men reached for their swords. {2039} Bold Hagen's courage was very
great; but Iring struck at him so hard that the whole building rang,
and hall and towers echoed with their blows. Still, the warrior could
not get what he wanted.

{2040} Iring left Hagen standing there unwounded and rushed at the
fiddler instead. He thought he could beat him down with his hard
blows, but the warrior Volker knew well how to defend himself.
{2041} Then the fiddler struck a blow that sent the clasps of Iring's
shield flying over its rim, and Iring let him be — he was a dangerous
man — and ran at Gunther of Burgundy. {2042} Each of them was strong
enough for a fight, but whatever blows Gunther and Iring gave each
other, they drew no flowing blood from any wound. Their armor
prevented that; it was strong and good.

{2043} He left Gunther and ran at Gernot, and began hewing fire out of
the rings of his mail; and strong Gernot of Burgundy came very near to
striking bold Iring dead. {2044} But Iring sprang away from the prince
— he was quick enough — and in a flash the hero killed four
Burgundians, men of the noble household of Worms on the Rhine. Giselher
had never been angrier in his life.

{2045} "God knows, Lord Iring," said young Giselher, "you shall pay me
for the men who have just fallen dead in front of you." Then he ran at
him and struck the Dane so that he could not move from the spot.
{2046} He went down into the blood in front of Giselher's hands, and
they all believed the good hero would never strike another blow in
battle. But Iring lay there in front of Giselher without a wound.
{2047} From the crash of the sword and the ringing of his helmet, his
senses had gone so weak that the bold warrior no longer knew he was
alive. Strong Giselher's strength had done that to him.

{2048} When the ringing he had taken from that great blow began to
clear from his head, he thought: "I am still alive, and not wounded
anywhere. Now for the first time I know what Giselher's courage is."
{2049} He heard his enemies standing on both sides of him; if they had
known the truth, more would have been done to him. He could hear
Giselher close by him too, and he thought about how he could get away
from his enemies. {2050} How wildly he sprang up out of the blood! He
had his quickness to thank for it. He ran out of the house to where he
found Hagen again, and struck him grim blows with his strong arm.

{2051} Hagen thought: "You are going to die. Unless the foul devil
protects you, you cannot come out of this alive." But Iring wounded
Hagen through the hood of his helmet; the hero did that with Waske, a
sword that good. {2052} When Lord Hagen felt the wound, the sword
began to swing terribly in his hand, and Hawart's vassal had to give
way before him, down the stairs, with Hagen coming after him.
{2053} Bold Iring swung his shield over his head, and had that
staircase been three staircases long, Hagen would not have let him
land a single blow the whole way. Hey, what red sparks flew above his
head!

{2054} Iring got back to his own men in one piece, and Kriemhild soon
heard the whole story of what he had done to Hagen of Tronje in the
fight. The queen began to thank him warmly for it. {2055} "God reward
you, Iring, you famous good hero. You have put heart and courage back
into me. Now I see Hagen's clothes red with blood." And out of sheer
joy Kriemhild took the shield from his hand herself.

{2056} "Go easy with your thanks to him," said Hagen. "If he wanted
to try it again, that would be worthy of a warrior; and
if he came away from it alive, then he would be a bold man. The wound
I took from him does you little good. {2057} Seeing the rings of my
mail red from my wound has only roused me to the deaths of many men.
Now for the first time I am angry, like a man who knows no fear. The
warrior Iring has done me very little harm so far."

{2058} Iring of Denmark stood in the wind, cooling himself in his
mail, and unlaced his helmet. Everyone there said his courage was
good, and the margrave's spirits rose high at that. {2059} Then Iring
spoke again: "My friends, be sure of this: arm me again quickly. I
mean to try harder, and see whether I can still beat that arrogant
man." His shield was hacked to pieces, and he got a better one.

{2060} The warrior was very soon armed again, and better than before.
In his hatred he took a very strong spear, meaning to take on Hagen
with it once more, over there; and the murderous, grim man waited for
him like an enemy. {2061} The warrior Hagen could not bear to wait for
him. He ran to meet him, throwing and striking, right to the foot of
the stairs; his rage was great. Iring's strength did him very little
good then. {2062} They struck through each other's shields until it
began to blaze in fire-red gusts, and Hawart's vassal was gravely
wounded by Hagen's sword, through shield and through mail. He never
recovered from it.

{2063} When the warrior Iring felt the wound, he pulled his shield up
higher over his helmet straps. He thought the harm he had taken there
was the full measure of it; but King Gunther's vassal did him worse
afterward. {2064} Hagen found a spear lying at his feet. He hurled it
at Iring, the hero of Denmark, so that the shaft stood out of his
head. The warrior Hagen had brought him to a grim end.

{2065} Iring had to fall back to the men of Denmark. Before they had
even unlaced the warrior's helmet, they broke the spear out of his
head, and death was already drawing near him. His kinsmen wept at
that; they had reason enough. {2066} Then the queen came and bent
over him. She began to lament strong Iring and wept over his wounds;
she grieved for him bitterly. And in front of his kinsmen the bold,
brave warrior said:

{2067} "Let the lamenting be, noble lady. What good is your weeping?
Truly, I must lose my life from the wounds I have taken. Death will
not let me serve you and Etzel any longer." {2068} To the men of
Thuringia and the men of Denmark he said: "None of you must take the
queen's gift, her bright red gold, in return for this. If you take on
Hagen, you will have to look death in the face."

{2069} His color had drained away; bold Iring bore the mark of death,
and it was grief enough to them. Hawart's vassal could not live, and
then the men of Denmark had to go to battle. {2070} Irnfried and
Hawart sprang toward the hall with a good thousand heroes. A huge,
raging din was heard on every side, powerful and loud. Hey, what sharp
spears were hurled at the Burgundians!

{2071} Bold Irnfried ran at the minstrel, and got great harm at his
hands: the noble fiddler struck the landgrave through his strong
helmet — truly, he was grim enough. {2072} Then Lord Irnfried struck
the bold minstrel so hard that the clasps of his mail burst and his
coat of mail was showered with fire-red sparks. All the same, the
landgrave fell dead at the fiddler's hands.

{2073} Hawart and Hagen had come together. Anyone watching would have
seen wonders: the swords fell thick and fast in the heroes' hands.
Hawart had to die at the hands of the man from the Burgundian land.
{2074} When the Danes and the Thuringians saw their lords dead, a
terrible struggle rose in front of the hall before they won the door
with brave hands; many a helmet and shield-rim was hacked apart in
that fight.

{2075} "Give way," said Volker, "and let them come in. What they have
in mind will never be finished otherwise. They will have to die in
here in a very short time; with their deaths they will earn what the
queen is giving them."

{2076} When the arrogant men came into the hall, many a head was
bowed so low that its owner had to die from the Burgundians' swift
blows. Bold Gernot fought well, and so did the warrior Giselher.
{2077} A thousand and four came into the house, and many a swift
sword-stroke was seen flashing; in the end the warriors were all slain
inside. Great wonders could be told of the Burgundians.

{2078} After that a silence fell, as the noise died away. The blood of
the dead men ran out everywhere through the openings and down to the
gutter-stones. The men from the Rhine had done that with their great
courage. {2079} Then the men of Burgundy sat down to rest again. They
laid their weapons and their shields out of their hands. But the bold
minstrel still stood in front of the tower, watching in case anyone
else wanted to come at them in battle.

{2080} The king grieved bitterly, and so did his wife; the girls and
the ladies tormented themselves with grief. I think Death itself had
sworn an oath against them: many more of the warriors were still to be
lost there at the guests' hands.
