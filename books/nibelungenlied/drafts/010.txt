Chapter 11: How Siegfried Took His Wife Home

{690} When the guests had all ridden away, Siegmund's son said to his
household: "We should get ready too, and go home to my lands." His
wife was glad when she understood that he meant it. {691} She said to
her husband: "When are we to leave? I would rather not rush off in
such a hurry. First my brothers must divide the lands with me."
Siegfried did not like it when he heard that from Kriemhild.

{692} The princes came to him, and all three of them said: "Know this,
Lord Siegfried: our loyal service is yours, ready at your call until
the day we die." He bowed to the warriors when it was offered so
kindly. {693} "We will share with you as well," said young Giselher,
"the lands and castles that belong to us, and whatever part of the
wide kingdom is subject to us. You and Kriemhild shall have a good
share of it."

{694} When Siegmund's son heard and saw what the lords intended, he
said to the princes: "God grant that your inheritance brings you
blessing forever, and the people in it too. My dear wife {695} can
easily do without the share you wanted to give her. Where she is going
to wear a crown, if I live to see it, she will be richer than anyone
alive. Whatever else you command, I am at your service."

{696} Then Lady Kriemhild said: "You may do without the inheritance,
but with the Burgundian warriors it is not settled so easily. A king
would gladly lead men like these to his land. Truly, my dear brothers'
hands must share them with me."

{697} Lord Gernot said: "Then take whoever you want. You will find
plenty here who would gladly ride with you. Of our three thousand
warriors we give you a thousand men to be your own household."
Kriemhild sent {698} for Hagen of Tronje and for Ortwin, to ask whether
they and their kinsmen would be hers. At that Hagen flew into a rage.
He said: "Gunther cannot give us away to anyone on earth. {699} Let the
rest of your household follow you. You know the custom of the men of
Tronje well enough: we must stay here at court with the kings. We will
go on serving the lords we have followed until now."

{700} They let the matter drop and got ready to leave. Lady Kriemhild
took a noble retinue for herself: thirty-two maidens and five hundred
men. Count Eckewart went with Siegfried. {701} They all took their
leave, knights and squires, maidens and ladies, as was only right. They
parted with kisses, and left King Gunther's land in high spirits.
{702} Their kinsmen escorted them a long way on the road, and all
through the kings' land, wherever they chose to stop, lodging was
ordered for them for the night.

Then messengers were sent racing ahead to Siegmund, {703} so that he
and Sieglinde should know that his son was coming, and with him Ute's
child, beautiful Kriemhild, from Worms across the Rhine. No news could
have pleased them more. {704} "How lucky I am," said Siegmund, "to have
lived to see beautiful Kriemhild walk crowned here. All my inheritance
is the richer for it. My son, noble Siegfried, shall be king here
himself."

{705} Then noble Sieglinde gave out red samite in plenty, and silver,
and heavy gold: that was the messengers' reward. She rejoiced at the
news, and her household dressed with care, as was fitting for them.
{706} They were told who was coming into the land with him, and at once
seats were ordered to be set up where he would walk crowned before his
kinsmen. Then King Siegmund's men rode out to meet him.

{707} If anyone was ever better received than those famous heroes in
Siegmund's land, I do not know of it. Beautiful Sieglinde rode out to
meet Kriemhild with many lovely ladies, and handsome knights followed
her, {708} a full day's journey, before the guests came into sight. The
people of the country and the strangers both had a hard, uncomfortable
road until they reached a broad castle called Xanten, where the two of
them would later wear the crown.

{709} With laughing faces Sieglinde and Siegmund kissed Kriemhild again
and again for love, and Siegfried too; their sorrow was lifted from
them. All their company was warmly welcomed. {710} The guests were
brought before Siegmund's hall, and the beautiful young ladies were
lifted down from their palfreys. There were men by the hundred, and
they set about serving the lovely ladies with a will.

{711} However great their wedding feast on the Rhine was known to have
been, here the heroes were given far better clothes than they had ever
worn in all their days. Great wonders could be told of their wealth.
{712} When they sat there in their high honor with everything they
could want, what gold-colored gowns their retinue wore, with pearls and
precious stones worked into them! So carefully did Sieglinde, the noble
queen, look after them.

{713} Then Lord Siegmund said before his kinsmen: "I make it known to
all of Siegfried's family: he shall wear my crown before these
warriors." The people of the Netherlands were glad to hear it. {714} He
handed over to him his courts of justice, his crown, and his land, and
from then on Siegfried was master of them all. Whenever he sat in
judgment and had to give a ruling, it was carried out in such a way
that men went in great fear of beautiful Kriemhild's husband.

{715} In this high honor he lived, it is the plain truth, and ruled
under the crown until the tenth year, when his beautiful wife bore a
son. That was just what the king's kinsmen had wished for. {716} They
hurried to have him baptized and gave him a name: Gunther, after his
uncle, and he had no need to be ashamed of that. If he took after his
kinsmen, things would go well for him. They raised him with care, and
with good reason.

{717} In those same days Lady Sieglinde died. Then the noble daughter
of Ute held all the power, as was right for so great a lady over the
lands. Many people grieved that death had taken Sieglinde from them.

{718} Now on the Rhine as well, so we hear tell, beautiful Brunhild had
borne mighty Gunther a son, in Burgundy. For the hero's sake they named
him Siegfried. {719} How carefully they had him watched over! Noble
Gunther gave him tutors who knew how to raise him into a fine man. Oh,
how much misfortune later robbed him of his kinsmen!

{720} Stories were told all the time of how gloriously the handsome
warriors lived in Siegmund's land, and Gunther lived the same way with
his distinguished kinsmen. {721} The Nibelung land served Siegfried
here, and Nibelung's warriors, and the wealth of both; none of his
kinsmen was ever richer than he. The bold man held his head all the
higher for it. {722} The greatest treasure a hero ever won, apart from
those who had held it before him, the bold man now had: he had won it
with his own hand before a mountain, and for its sake he had struck
many a fine knight dead. {723} He had honor to his heart's content, and
even if he had not, anyone would still have to admit that the noble
warrior was one of the best men who ever sat a horse. Men feared his
strength, and they were right to.
