Chapter 2: Siegfried, and How He Was Raised

{20} In the Netherlands, meanwhile, there grew up the son of a most
noble king. His father was Siegmund, his mother Sieglinde, and their
home was a mighty castle, famous far and wide, far down the lower
Rhine: it was called Xanten. {21} The boy was named
Siegfried, a quick and good fighter. His courage drove him to try his
strength against many kingdoms, and his strength carried him into many
lands. Ah, what quick fighters he would meet in Burgundy later on!

{22} In his prime, in the days of his youth, there were wonders to tell
of Siegfried: how honor grew on him, and how handsome he was. Later the
loveliest women would lose their hearts to him. {23} He was raised with
all the care he deserved, and the virtues he took on came from his own
nature. In time his father's lands would shine for it, because in
everything he did he showed himself so truly a lord.

{24} He was grown enough now to ride to court. People liked to look at
him, many a lady and many a girl, and they hoped his heart would bring
him there again and again. Plenty of them were fond of him, and the
young lord was well aware of it. {25} The boy was very seldom allowed
to ride out without a guard. Siegmund and Sieglinde had him dressed in
fine clothes, and men of experience who knew what honor was had charge
of him too. So he was well on his way to winning both people and land.

{26} He was strong enough now to carry weapons well, and whatever that
called for, he had plenty of. With good sense he began to court
beautiful women, and they loved bold Siegfried, honorably and well.
{27} Then his father Siegmund let his vassals know that he meant to
hold a festival with his dear kinsmen. The news was carried into other
kings' lands, and to strangers and to his own people alike he gave
horses and good clothes. {28} Wherever a boy was found whose birth and
family entitled him to be a knight, he was invited to the land for the
festival; these noble youths would take the sword alongside the young
king's son.

{29} There were wonders to tell of that festival. Siegmund and
Sieglinde knew how to win great honor with their wealth, and their
hands dealt out plenty of it; and so many a worthy man was seen riding
into their land. {30} Four hundred young men were to put on knightly
clothes together with Siegfried, and many a beautiful girl was kept
busy with the sewing, because they were all fond of him. The ladies set
many a precious stone in the gold {31} that they would work with braid
onto the clothes of those proud young warriors. It had to be done. The
host had seats set out for a great many bold men, for the midsummer
when his son Siegfried would be given the name of knight.

{32} Then many a rich squire and many a noble knight went to the
cathedral. The older men were right to serve the young ones, as had
been done for them in their day. There were pastimes, and the hope of
great joy ahead. {33} A Mass was sung in God's honor, and then a great
crush rose among the crowd as the young men were made knights in the
knightly way, with honors so great that the like of them may never be
seen again. {34} They ran to where many a saddled horse stood waiting.
In Siegmund's courtyard the buhurt, the mock battle on horseback, grew
so fierce that the palace and the hall rang with it; the high-spirited
warriors made a tremendous din. {35} From the seasoned men and the
green ones alike came clash after clash, and the crack of breaking
shafts rang up to the sky. Splinters flew out in front of the palace
from many a warrior's hand: they went at it with a will. {36} At last
the host asked them to stop, and the horses were led away. Many a stout
shield-boss lay broken there, and many precious stones had fallen on
the grass from the bright shield-mounts, knocked loose in the clash.

{37} Then the host's guests went where they were told to sit. Fine food
in plenty took away their weariness, and the very best wine, which was
carried to them in great quantities. Strangers and the king's own
people alike were shown every honor. {38} However much sport they had
all day long, many of the traveling entertainers gave up any thought of
rest: they performed for the gifts, which were rich there, and all of
Siegmund's land was covered in praise for it. {39} The lord had young
Siegfried granted land and castles, as he himself had once been given
them, and Siegfried's hand gave freely to the companions who had taken
the sword with him. The guests were glad they had made the journey to
that land.

{40} The festival lasted until the seventh day. Mighty Sieglinde
followed the old custom, and for love of her son gave out red gold; she
knew how to earn her son the people's affection. {41} Hardly a poor
entertainer could be found there. Horses and clothes flew from their
hands as if they had not one more day to live. I do not think any
household ever showed such generosity. {42} The festival ended with
honor and praise. Later, the great lords were heard to say that they
wanted the young man for their ruler, but Lord Siegfried, that handsome
man, wanted nothing of the kind. {43} As long as Siegmund and Sieglinde
both lived, their beloved child would wear no crown. And yet he meant
to be master over every act of violence that was feared in those lands,
that bold and daring warrior.
