Chapter 9: How Siegfried Was Sent to Worms with the News

{529} When they had been on the water a full nine days, Hagen of Tronje
spoke up: "Now listen to what I say. We are slow with our news for
Worms on the Rhine. Your messengers should be in Burgundy by now."

{530} King Gunther said: "You are right. And no one would be so well
suited to that errand as you, my friend Hagen. Ride to my country. No
one can tell them about our journey home to court better than you."

{531} Hagen answered: "I am no good as a messenger. Let me look after
the baggage and stay on the river. I will stay with the ladies and
guard their clothes until we bring them into the land of Burgundy.
{532} Ask Siegfried to carry the message. He has the strength and the
courage to do it well. And if he refuses you the journey, then you must
ask him courteously, as a friend, for your sister's sake."

{533} Gunther sent for the warrior, and he came as soon as he was
found. The king said: "Since we are drawing near my own lands, I ought
to send messengers to my dear sister and my mother, to say that we are
coming to the Rhine. {534} That is what I ask of you, Siegfried. Do
what I want in this, and I will be in your debt forever," said the good
warrior. But bold Siegfried refused him, until Gunther began to plead
with him in earnest.

{535} "You must ride," he said, "for my sake and for the sake of
Kriemhild, that beautiful girl, so that the glorious princess, like me,
will owe you something for it." When Siegfried heard that, the warrior
was very ready to go.

{536} "Then tell me whatever you want sent. None of it will go
unspoken. I will gladly carry it for the sake of that beautiful girl.
Why should I refuse anything to the one I hold in my heart? Whatever
you command, consider it done."

{537} "Then tell my mother, Queen Ute, that we are in high spirits on
this voyage. Let my brothers know how we have fared. You must let our
kinsmen hear this news as well. {538} And keep nothing from my
beautiful sister: tell the girl that Brunhild and I send her our
service, and tell the household too, and all my men. How well I have
finished the thing my heart was always set on! {539} And tell Ortwin,
my dear nephew, to have seats built at Worms on the Rhine, and let the
rest of my kinsmen know that I mean to hold a great wedding feast with
Brunhild. {540} And tell my sister this: once she has heard that I have
come home to the country with my guests, she is to take great pains to
welcome my dear bride. For that I will be in Kriemhild's service
forever."

{541} Lord Siegfried quickly took his leave of Lady Brunhild, as was
proper, and of all her household, and rode to the Rhine. There could
not have been a better messenger in all the world. {542} He rode to
Worms with twenty-four warriors. He came without the king, and when
word of that got out, the whole household was in distress; they feared
their lord had been left dead over there.

{543} The riders dismounted in high spirits. Young Giselher, the good
king, came quickly to meet them, and his brother Gernot with him, and
how fast Giselher spoke when he saw that King Gunther was not at
Siegfried's side! {544} "Welcome, Siegfried. You must tell me where you
have left my brother the king. I think Brunhild's strength has taken
him from us, and then her high love has brought us a terrible loss."

{545} "Set that fear aside, for yourself and for his kinsmen. My
dear comrade sends you his service. I left him safe and well. He sent me
ahead to be his messenger, with news for your country. {546} However
you arrange it, see to it quickly that I may see the queen and your
sister. I have to tell them the message Gunther and Brunhild have sent.
Both of them are doing very well."

{547} Then young Giselher said: "You must go to them, for you have
brought my sister great joy. She is sick with worry about my brother.
The girl will be glad to see you — I will vouch for that."

{548} Lord Siegfried said: "Whatever I can do to serve her, I will do
willingly and loyally. Now who will tell the ladies that I want to go
to them?" And handsome Giselher became the messenger for that.

{549} Swift Giselher went to his mother and his sister, and found them
together, and said: "Siegfried, the hero from the Netherlands, has come
to us. My brother Gunther has sent him here to the Rhine. {550} He
brings us news of how things stand with the king. You should give him
leave to come to court. He will tell you the true story straight from
Isenland." Up to then the noble ladies had known nothing but worry.

{551} They sprang up for their clothes and dressed, and then they asked
Siegfried to come to them at court. He did so gladly, because he was
glad to see them. Noble Kriemhild spoke to him kindly: {552} "Welcome,
Lord Siegfried, knight worthy of all praise. Where is my brother
Gunther, the noble, mighty king? I think we have lost him to Brunhild's
strength. Oh, poor girl that I am, that I was ever born into this
world!"

{553} The bold knight said: "Now give me my messenger's reward, you
beautiful ladies, for you are weeping for nothing. I left him safe and
well; I promise you that. The two of them have sent me to you both with
this news. {554} He and his bride send you their loving service, with
a kinsman's affection, noble princess. Now stop your weeping. They will be
here soon." She had not heard news so welcome in a long time.

{555} With her snow-white hem she wiped the tears from her lovely eyes,
and she began to thank the messenger for the news he had brought her.
All her great sadness and her weeping were gone. {556} She asked the
messenger to sit, and he was very ready to. Then the lovely girl said:
"It would not trouble me at all to give you my gold as a messenger's
fee. But you are too rich for that. Instead I will be your friend
forever."

{557} "If I alone owned thirty kingdoms," he said, "I would still
gladly take a gift from your hand."

"Then it shall be done," said the gracious princess, and she sent her
chamberlain to fetch the messenger's reward. {558} Twenty-four
bracelets set with fine stones: that was what she gave him for his
fee. And such was the hero's spirit that he would not keep them. He gave
them away at once to her closest attendants, the women he found in her
chambers.

{559} Her mother offered him her service with great kindness. "I have a
message to give you," said the bold man, "about what Gunther asks of
you when he comes to the Rhine. If you do it, my lady, he will be
grateful to you forever. {560} I heard him ask that you receive his
noble guests well, and that you grant him this: that you ride out to
meet him on the riverbank in front of Worms. The king reminds you of
this in all true loyalty."

{561} The lovely girl said: "I am very ready to do it. Whatever I can
do to serve him I will not refuse. It shall be done with a kinsman's
loyalty." And her color deepened with the joy she felt. {562} No
prince's messenger was ever received better. If she had dared to kiss
him, the lady would have done it. How lovingly he parted from the women!

Then the Burgundians did as Siegfried advised. {563} Sindold and Hunold
and Rumold the warrior had to work hard, setting up the seats on the
riverbank outside Worms, and the king's stewards were found hard at
work too. {564} Ortwin and Gere would not be left out: they sent to
summon their kinsmen from every direction and told them about the
wedding feast that was to be held, and the beautiful girls dressed
themselves up for it. {565} The great hall and its walls, all of
Gunther's palace, were decorated for the guests, and the king's hall
was richly fitted out for the many strangers. So this magnificent
feast began with great joy.

{566} Then along every road in the land rode the kinsmen of the three
kings, who had been sent for to wait on the travelers who were on their
way home. Many a rich garment was taken out of the chests. {567} Then
came the news that Brunhild's people had been seen riding in, and there
was great commotion from the crush of people in the Burgundian land.
Oh, what bold warriors were to be found there on both sides!

{568} Beautiful Kriemhild said: "My girls, those of you who want to be
with me for the welcome, pick out the very best clothes from the
chests, so that the guests will praise us and honor us."

{569} Then the warriors came too, and had the splendid saddles carried
out, all of red gold, on which the ladies would ride to the Rhine at
Worms. There could be no better riding gear anywhere. {570} Oh, how the
bright gold shone on the black horses! Many a precious stone gleamed at
them from the bridles. The golden mounting-stools were set on bright
silk and brought to the ladies, and they were in a happy mood.
{571} In the courtyard the ladies' horses stood ready for the noble
young women, as I have told you. You could see the horses wearing
narrow breast-straps of the best silk anyone could tell you of.

{572} Eighty-six ladies were seen coming out, wearing the headdresses
of married women. They came to Kriemhild, those beautiful women, in
their bright dresses, and many a lovely girl came as well, all dressed
up, {573} fifty-four of them from the land of Burgundy, and they were
the highest-born to be found anywhere. You could see them walking with
their fair hair under bright ribbons. What the king had asked for was
done with care.

{574} They wore rich silks, the best to be found, and so many fine
garments in front of the foreign warriors that their great beauty was
shown exactly as it deserved. Anyone who could feel ill will toward any
of them would have to be a poor sort of man. {575} There were many
clothes of sable and ermine to be seen, and many an arm and hand was
finely adorned with bracelets over the silk sleeves they were to wear.
No one could tell you about all this finery to the end. {576} Many a
hand swung a finely worked belt, rich and long, over a bright gown,
over fine skirts of Arabian silk. The noble young
women were in very high spirits. {577} Many a beautiful girl had a
brooch sewn at her breast with loving care. Any girl whose bright
complexion did not shine against her clothes had reason to be sorry
for it. No king's family today has so beautiful a household.

{578} When the lovely women had put on the clothes they were to wear,
the men who were to escort them arrived at once: a great force of
proud warriors, and with their shields they carried many an ashen
spear.
