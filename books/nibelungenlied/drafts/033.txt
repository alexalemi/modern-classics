Chapter 34: How They Threw the Dead from the Hall

{2009} Worn out, the lords sat down. Volker and Hagen went out in
front of the hall and the two proud men leaned on their shields, and
there was a good deal of sharp talk between them.

{2010} Then Giselher of Burgundy, the warrior, said: "Dear friends,
you cannot rest yet. You must carry the dead out of the hall. We are
going to be attacked again, I tell you truly. {2011} They must not
lie here under our feet any longer. Before the Huns overwhelm us by
storm, we will hew some wounds yet, and that will do me good. I have
my mind firmly set on it," said Giselher.

{2012} "Lucky me, to have such a lord," said Hagen. "The advice my
young lord has given us today would suit no one but a warrior. Every
one of you Burgundians can be glad of it."

{2013} They followed his advice and carried seven thousand dead to
the door and threw them out. They fell to the ground in front of the
hall stairs, and a great cry of grief went up from their kinsmen.
{2014} Some of them were so lightly wounded that, handled more
gently, they would have recovered; the high fall left them dead.
Their friends grieved for that, and truly they had cause.

{2015} Then Volker the fiddler, a gallant hero, said: "Now I see for
myself the truth of what I was told. The Huns are cowards; they wail
like women. They would do better to look after the badly wounded."

{2016} A margrave thought he meant that kindly. He saw one of his
kinsmen lying in the blood, wrapped his arms around him and started
to carry him away. The bold minstrel shot him dead over the body.
{2017} When the others saw that, they turned and ran, and all of
them cursed that same minstrel. He snatched up a javelin, very sharp
and hard, that a Hun had thrown up at him, {2018} and hurled it with
all his strength out across the courtyard, far over the heads of
Etzel's men. That sent them looking for a place to stand farther from
the hall. Everywhere, people feared his great strength.

{2019} Many thousands of men were standing in front of the hall. Now
Volker and Hagen began to tell King Etzel everything that was on
their minds; the bold good heroes came to grief for it later.

{2020} "It would suit the leaders of this people very well," said
Hagen, "to fight in the front rank, the way every one of my lords
here does. They hew through the helmets, and the blood runs after
their swords."

{2021} Etzel was so bold that he grabbed his shield. "Go carefully
now," said Lady Kriemhild, "and offer the warriors gold over your
shield-rim instead. If Hagen gets to you over there, you have death
in your hand." {2022} The king was so brave that he would not turn
back — a rare thing nowadays from so mighty a prince. They had to
drag him back by his shield-strap. And grim Hagen began to taunt him
again.

{2023} "That was a distant kinship," said Hagen the warrior, "that
Etzel and Siegfried shared. He loved Kriemhild before she ever laid
eyes on you. Wretched king, why are you plotting against me?"

{2024} The noble king's wife heard what he said, and it tormented
Kriemhild that he dared insult her like that in front of Etzel's men.
So she began plotting against the guests once more. {2025} She said:
"Whoever kills Hagen of Tronje for me and brings me his head, I will
fill Etzel's shield with red gold for him, and give him fine castles
and lands besides as his reward."

{2026} "Now I know what they are waiting for," said the minstrel. "I
never saw heroes stand there like such cowards, with pay that high on
offer. Truly, Etzel should never be kind to them again. {2027} The
men who eat the prince's bread so shamefully and now fail him in his
greatest need — I see plenty of them standing here like cowards, and
they still want to be counted as brave. Shame on them forever."
