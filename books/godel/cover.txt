Drawn for this edition, in the manner of the Dover science paperbacks of the 1950s and 60s. It is the book's own worked example: the formula (∃x)(x = sy), its ten signs set across the top, and its Gödel number, 2⁸ × 3⁴ × 5¹¹ × 7⁹ × 11⁸ × 13¹¹ × 17⁵ × 19⁷ × 23¹³ × 29⁹, strung as an abacus, one wire for each prime and one bead for each power. It is the step on which the whole proof turns: a statement about formulas becomes a statement about a number.
