Chapter 14: The Prime of Life

As for men in the prime of life, it is clear that in character they will stand between the young and the old, with the excess of each taken away. They are neither too confident, for that is rashness, nor too much afraid, but they stand as they should towards both.

They neither trust everyone, as the young do, nor distrust everyone, as the old do, but judge rather by what is true. They live neither for the noble alone nor for the useful alone, but for both. They are neither tight-fisted nor wasteful with money, but spend as is fitting.

It is the same with temper and with appetite: they have self-control together with courage, and courage together with self-control. In the young and the old these two are divided, for the young are brave and self-indulgent, and the old have self-control and are cowardly. To put it generally: whatever advantages youth and old age have divided between them, the prime of life has both of; and wherever the young and the old go too far or fall short, the prime of life has the moderate amount, the amount that is fitting.

The body is in its prime from the age of thirty to thirty-five, and the soul at about forty-nine, a year short of fifty.

So much, then, for youth, old age and the prime of life, and for the kind of character that belongs to each of them.
