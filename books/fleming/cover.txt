Drawn for this edition, in the manner of the Dover science paperbacks of the 1950s and 60s. Ripples spread in rings from two points on a pond and cross, and where they cross they make a pattern of their own: crest on crest builds a bigger wave, crest on trough cancels to calm. Fleming calls it interference, and he finds the same thing in water, in sound and in light, which is the argument of the whole book: that all three are waves.
