Jean-Léon Gérôme's *The Death of Caesar* (1867). The conspirators, in white togas, crowd out through the archway with their daggers raised, while the body lies abandoned in the lower corner of the empty hall at the foot of a statue, and one old senator stays slumped in his seat. Plutarch tells how Caesar fell at the base of Pompey's statue, and Gérôme shows the minute after, when the murderers have already turned their backs on him.
