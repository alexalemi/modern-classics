Asa Gray (1810–1888) was the leading American botanist of his century. He trained as a physician in upstate New York, gave up medicine for plants, and from 1842 held the Fisher Professorship of Natural History at Harvard, where he built up the university's herbarium. His Manual of the Botany of the Northern United States (1848) was the standard handbook for the plants of the eastern states for generations. He was also Darwin's closest American correspondent, one of the few people shown the argument of the Origin of Species before it was published, and afterwards its leading defender in the United States.

How Plants Grow is the book he wrote for children. It was entered for copyright in 1858, the year before the Origin appeared, and it was reprinted for decades as a school text; this copy is a later printing, which adds four pages numbered 104¹ to 104⁴ on how to use the Flora. It opens on the lilies of the field and speaks throughout of the plan and purpose of the Creator, which is the language of American natural history before Darwin, and Gray kept that faith after it.

The book has two parts. The first explains, in numbered paragraphs, how a plant grows from a seed, what roots, stems, leaves, flowers, fruits and seeds are, what plants do, and how botanists classify and name them. The second is a Popular Flora: an arrangement of the common wild and cultivated plants of the country in 105 families, with keys that lead a beginner from a plant in the hand to its name. It ends with a dictionary of the botanical terms the book uses.

A few conventions are worth knowing before the Flora. The marks over Latin names show which syllable is stressed. The signs °, ′ and ″ mean feet, inches and lines (twelfths of an inch), so "leaves 1° long" means a foot long. N., S., E. and W. give the part of the country where a plant grows wild. The keys are read by choosing: at each step follow the line that fits your plant, and each step further in narrows the choice until a name is reached.

This is a restored edition. Gray's words are unchanged. No transcription of the book existed, so every page was read from the scan and the words were checked against two other copies. All 467 woodcut blocks, carrying figures Gray numbered from 1 to 519, were cut from the page and given descriptions for readers who cannot see them. In the keys, where the book sends the reader to a page, this edition gives the family's own number instead. Elsewhere, page references are kept as printed and refer to the pages of the original book. The index to the Flora, which is page numbers only, is left out. Three figure numbers that were misprinted, and so contradict their own figures, are corrected.
