Drawn for this edition, in the manner of the Dover science paperbacks of the 1950s and 60s, after one of Gray's own figures in his first chapter. A young Red Maple seedling stands in the soil: two long, narrow seed-leaves, b, spread flat on the stemlet, a, with the bud of the next pair, d, between them and the root, c, below. It is where Gray begins his account of how a plant grows, from the seed up.
