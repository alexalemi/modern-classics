Pierre-Joseph Redouté's plate of *Lilium superbum*, the American Turk's-cap lily, from the years 1802–1816. Four red flowers with spotted yellow throats hang from a stem ringed with whorls of leaves, beside an unopened bud, with the pistil and stamens drawn apart on either side. It is a wild flower of the eastern states, the plants Gray's Flora sets out to name, drawn the way he teaches a beginner to look: the parts taken apart and shown one by one.
