RULINGS FROM THE PILOT (these override anything above that disagrees)
1. A WOODCUT THAT WRAPS ROUND TEXT (an L-shape, a frame, a cut beside
   several lines): the box field may carry text regions to BLANK OUT after
   the box, joined by " - ": "120,300,1700,1900 - 400,320,1690,1200". Each
   minus-box is whitened in the cut, so no body text ships inside an image.
   Check by cropping WITH the minus-boxes painted white. The marker goes
   where the woodcut begins in reading order (after any heading above it).
   A woodcut beside several paragraphs: marker after the first paragraph
   it mostly sits beside.
2. PRINTED LABEL of a woodcut that prints only its number: "Fig. N." (so
   "5" under a cut is written "Fig. 5."). A woodcut with no printed number
   and no caption: leave the label field empty (two tabs in a row).
3. A FOOTNOTE WITH SEVERAL PARAGRAPHS: "Footnote: *" + first paragraph on
   one line, each further paragraph on the next line (no blank line); prep
   sets them as separate paragraphs.
4. A note with no reference mark in the body (a "⁂" note): an ordinary
   paragraph, as printed.
5. A heading on its own line in BOLD or italic type ("The Root"): its own
   paragraph, as printed, no markup. A bold run-in heading at the start of a
   paragraph: plain text, then the paragraph continues.
6. The book's space before a colon or semicolon ("grow :") is closed up:
   "grow:". This is typography, not the author's text.
7. Ditto marks in a table: ASCII ". Contents-style tables: the key
   convention (TAB, two spaces per indent step).
8. A leaf that prints no page number: "page: none".
9. MARKS: a dagger is written † and a double dagger ‡ (never "+-" or
   "++"). An asterisk used as a MARK in the text (Gray's "* Fennel-flower",
   a star before a species) is written ⁎ (U+204E), so it cannot be read as
   italic markup. *...* is only ever italic.
