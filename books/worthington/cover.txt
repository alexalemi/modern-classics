One of Worthington's own photographs from *A Study of Splashes* (1908): Series V, Fig. 14, taken a tenth of a second after a sandpapered stone ball 1.5 centimetres across dropped into milky water. A single tapering column of liquid stands straight up out of the pool, thick at the foot and narrowing to a thread, with the last low ring of the crater around its base. A smooth ball dropped the same way throws up almost nothing, and the book's longest argument is about why. Jets like this one are now called Worthington jets.
