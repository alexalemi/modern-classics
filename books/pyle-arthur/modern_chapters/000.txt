Foreword

[Figure front: King Arthur of Britain — The young King stands crowned and robed, holding a tall scepter in one hand and an orb in the other, with a castle among trees behind him.]

[Figure aa: Pyle's drawn title page: the title in an arched panel, with a lady holding a lily on the left and a kneeling knight in armor on the right, among dark leaves and a castle under a stormy sky.]

[Figure ab: Headpiece to the Foreword: a young woman with a book open on her knee blows a long trumpet across the hills.]

For several years I have thought about and turned over the matters in this book, and now at last, by the grace of God, I have been able to write it, and with such pleasure of spirit that if it gives you even a part of the joy it has given me, I will be very well content with what I have done.

For as I followed this history and came to think about the high nobility of spirit that moved these excellent men to act as they did, I have felt that they set such a perfect example of courage and humility that anyone would do exceedingly well to follow their way of behaving, as far as he is able.

For I believe that King Arthur was the most honorable and gentle knight who ever lived in all the world. And his fellows of the Round Table, who took him as the mirror in which they saw what a knight should be, made up altogether such a company of noble knights that it is hard to imagine their like will ever be seen in this world again. That is why I have taken such extraordinary pleasure in watching how those famous knights behaved whenever circumstances called on them to do their utmost.

So in the year of our Lord nineteen hundred and two I began to write this history of King Arthur and his Knights of the Round Table, and, if I am able, I mean to finish it lovingly at some later time, in another book, to the satisfaction of anyone who may care to read the story.
