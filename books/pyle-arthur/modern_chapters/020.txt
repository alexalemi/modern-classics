Chapter 1: How Queen Morgan le Fay Plotted Against King Arthur, and How She Sent a Damsel to Charm the Enchanter Merlin

[Figure bh: The Enchantress Vivien — Vivien, young and calm, stands with a wand twined by a snake, gold coins hanging from her ears and a heavy necklace at her throat.]

[Figure bi: Headpiece to the Story of Merlin: a young woman with roses in her hair reaches out toward a castle on a distant hill.]

Now Morgan le Fay was a very skillful enchantress, and she had so mastered magic that by her powerful spells she could work her will on all things, whether living or dead. For Merlin himself had been her teacher in times past, and he had taught her his arts while she was still a young damsel at the court of Uther Pendragon. So it was that, next to Merlin, she was at that time the most powerful enchanter in all the world. Even so, she lacked Merlin's knowledge of things to come and his gift of prophecy. Those were things he could not pass on to anyone, and so she had never learned them from him.

*Queen Morgan plots evil against King Arthur*.

Now, after Queen Morgan le Fay had come to the Island of Avalon, as you have already heard, she brooded a great deal over the insult she believed King Arthur had done to her house. And the more she brooded on it, the bigger it grew in her mind, until at last it seemed to her that she could take no pleasure in life unless she could punish King Arthur for what he had done. Yes; she would have been glad to see him lying dead at her feet, so great was the anger she felt against him.

But Queen Morgan knew very well that she could never do the King, her brother, any harm so long as Merlin was there to guard him. For Merlin would surely foresee any danger that threatened the King, and he would undo it. And so she knew that if she wanted to destroy the King, she must first destroy Merlin.

Now at the court of Queen Morgan le Fay there was a certain damsel of such marvelous and bewitching beauty that her equal could hardly be found anywhere in the world. This damsel was fifteen years old and of royal blood, for she was the daughter of the King of Northumberland, and her name was Vivien. And this damsel, Vivien, was wise and crafty beyond all measure for someone so young. What was more, she had no heart at all, and she was cold and cruel to anyone who stood in the way of what she wanted. Because she was so clever and so wise, Queen Morgan liked her, and taught her many of the things of magic and sorcery that she knew. But for all that Queen Morgan did for her, this maiden felt no love at all for her mistress, for she was entirely without a heart.

*Queen Morgan talks with the Damsel Vivien*.

One day this damsel and Queen Morgan le Fay were sitting together in a garden on that magic island of Avalon. The garden lay on a very high terrace overlooking the sea. The day was very fair, and the sea was so wonderfully blue that it seemed as though the blue sky had melted into the water and the water into the sky. As Vivien and the Queen sat in this beautiful place, the Queen said to the damsel, "Vivien, what would you rather have than anything else in the world?" And Vivien answered, "Lady, I would rather have wisdom like yours than anything else."

At that Queen Morgan laughed and said, "You can be as wise as I am, and wiser too, if you will do as I tell you. For I know a way by which you can gain wisdom."

"How can I gain that wisdom, Lady?" said Vivien.

Then Queen Morgan le Fay said, "Listen and I will tell you. You must know that Merlin, whom you have seen several times at the court of King Arthur, is master of all the wisdom that anyone can possibly have in this world. Everything I know of magic Merlin taught me, and he knows many things that he did not teach me, but kept back from me. Merlin taught me when I was a young damsel at the court of my mother's husband, because I was beautiful in his eyes. For Merlin loves beauty more than anything else in the world, and so he taught me many things of magic and was very patient with me.

"But Merlin has one gift that belongs to him alone, and that he cannot pass on to anyone else, for it is born in him. That gift is the gift of seeing into the future and the power to prophesy what he sees.

"Yet though he can foresee the fate of others, he is blind to his own. He confessed as much to me several times: that he could not tell what was going to happen in his own life when it concerned himself alone.

"Now you, Vivien, are far more beautiful than I was at your age, and so I believe you will easily win Merlin's affection. And if I give you, besides, a certain charm that I own, I can make Merlin love you so much that he will teach you a great deal more of his wisdom than he ever taught me when I was his pupil.

"But you must understand, Vivien, that in winning this gift of knowledge from Merlin you will put yourself in great danger. For by and by, when the charm of your beauty has faded for him, he may well regret having given you his wisdom. And then there will be great danger that he may lay some spell on you to take your powers away. For it would be impossible for both you and he to live in the same world, each of you knowing so much of the craft of magic."

Vivien listened to all this very closely, and when Queen Morgan had finished, the damsel said, "Dear Lady, everything you tell me is very wonderful, and I am filled with a fierce desire to gain that kind of knowledge of magic. So if you will help me in this, so that I can charm Merlin's wisdom out of him, I will be in your debt for as long as I live. And as for any danger that may come to me in this business, I am entirely willing to take it on myself. For I have great hopes that I can protect myself from Merlin so that no harm will come to me. When I have drawn out of him all the knowledge I can get, I will use that same knowledge to cast such a spell on him that he will never be able to harm me or anyone else again. In this I will play my wits against his wisdom and my beauty against his skill, and I believe I will win that game."

At that Queen Morgan burst out laughing beyond all measure, and when she had stopped laughing, she cried, "Ha, Vivien! You are truly more crafty than anyone I ever heard of, and I believe you are as wicked as you are crafty. For who ever heard of a child fifteen years old speaking words like the ones you have just spoken? Or who could ever suppose that so young a girl could dream of bringing about the downfall of the wisest magician who ever lived?"

*Queen Morgan gives Vivien two enchanted rings*.

Then Queen Morgan le Fay set to her lips a small whistle of ivory and gold and blew a very shrill note on it, and in answer a young page of her court came running. Queen Morgan ordered him to bring her a certain casket of alabaster, skillfully carved, decorated with gold, and set with several precious stones. And Queen Morgan opened the box and took out of it two rings of pure yellow gold, beautifully made and set. The one ring held a clear white stone of extraordinary brilliance, and the other a stone as red as blood. Then Queen Morgan said, "Vivien, look at these two rings! Each of them holds a spell of wonderful power. For if you wear the ring with the white stone, then whoever wears the ring with the red stone will love you with such a passion that you can do with him whatever you please. So take these rings and go to King Arthur's court, and use them however your cleverness may plan."

So Vivien took the two rings and thanked Queen Morgan le Fay for them beyond all measure.

*Vivien appears before King Arthur at the Feast of Pentecost*.

Now King Arthur took great pleasure in holding a great feast every Pentecost, when his court was gathered around him with much merriment and rejoicing. At such times it delighted him to have some excellent entertainment to amuse himself and his court, and so it nearly always happened that something took place that gave the King great entertainment. So the Feast of Pentecost came, and King Arthur sat at table with a great many noble and lordly people and several kings and queens. And as they all sat at that feast, their spirits high with merriment and good cheer, there suddenly came into the hall a very beautiful young damsel, and with her a dwarf, wonderfully misshapen and with a very hideous face. The maiden was dressed all in flame-colored satin, very rich, with beautiful embroidery of gold and of silver. Her hair, which was red like gold, was coiled up in a net of gold. Her eyes were as black as coals, and extraordinarily bright and glittering. And around her throat she wore a necklace of gold of three strands, so that with all that gold and those bright clothes she shone with wonderful splendor as she came into the hall. The dwarf who came with her was likewise dressed all in flame color, and in his hands he carried a cushion of flame-colored silk with tassels of gold, and on the cushion he carried a ring of great beauty set with a red stone.

So when King Arthur saw this beautiful maiden, he supposed that here was some excellent entertainment, and he rejoiced at it a very great deal.

But when he looked closely at the damsel, it seemed to him that he knew her face, and so he said to her, "Damsel, who are you?" "Sir," she said, "I am the daughter of the King of Northumberland, and my name is Vivien." And with that King Arthur was satisfied.

Then King Arthur said to her, "Lady, what is that you have on the cushion there, and why have you honored us by coming here?" And Vivien answered, "Lord, I have here a very fine entertainment to give you pleasure at this Feast of Pentecost. For here is a ring of such a kind that only the wisest and most worthy of all the men here may wear it." And King Arthur said, "Let us see the ring."

So Vivien took the ring from the cushion the dwarf held, and she came and brought it to King Arthur, and the King took the ring in his own hand. And he saw that the ring was extraordinarily beautiful, so he said, "Maiden, do I have your permission to try this ring on my finger?" And Vivien said, "Yes, lord."

*King Arthur tries on the ring*.

So King Arthur tried to put the ring on his finger; but the ring shrank in size so that it would not go past the first joint. And so King Arthur said, "It seems that I am not worthy to wear this ring."

*Merlin wins the ring*.

Then the damsel Vivien said, "Do I have my lord's permission to offer this ring to others of his court?" And King Arthur said, "Let the others try the ring." So Vivien took the ring to the different people of the court, both lords and ladies, but not one of them could wear it. Then, last of all, Vivien came to the place where Merlin sat, and she knelt on the ground before him and offered him the ring. And because this concerned Merlin himself, he could not see into the future to know that harm was meant for him. Even so, he looked sourly at the damsel and said, "Child, what is this silly trick you are offering me?" "Sir," said Vivien, "I beg you to try this ring on your finger." Then Merlin looked at the damsel more closely, and he saw that she was very beautiful, and his heart softened toward her a great deal. So he spoke to her more gently and said, "Why should I take the ring?" And she answered, "Because I believe that you are the wisest and the most worthy of any man in this whole place, and so the ring should belong to you." Then Merlin smiled, and took the ring and put it on his finger; and it fitted his finger exactly. At that Vivien cried out, "Look! The ring has fitted his finger, and he is the wisest and the most worthy!" And Merlin was greatly pleased that the ring the beautiful damsel had given him had fitted his finger like that.

Then, after a while, he would have taken the ring off again, but to his astonishment he could not, for the ring had grown onto his finger as though it were part of its flesh and bone. At this Merlin became deeply troubled in spirit and very anxious, for he did not understand what the magic of the ring might mean. So he said, "Lady, where did this ring come from?" And Vivien said, "Sir, you know all things. Do you not know, then, that this ring was sent here by Morgan le Fay?" Then once again Merlin was filled with doubt, and he said, "I hope there may be no evil in this ring." And Vivien smiled at him and said, "What evil could there be in it?"

By this time the great magic that was in the ring had begun to work on Merlin's spirit, and he gazed at Vivien very steadily, and suddenly he took great delight in her beauty. Then the magic of the ring took complete hold of him, and a wonderful passion seized his heart all at once and wrung it so that it was pierced as if with a violent agony.

And Vivien saw what was passing in Merlin's mind, and she laughed and turned away. Several others who were there also noticed the very strange way in which Merlin gazed at her, and they said among themselves, "Surely Merlin is bewitched by the beauty of that young damsel."

*The ring works its charm on Merlin the Wise*.

So from that time on, the enchantment of Morgan le Fay's ring worked so strongly on Merlin's spirit that he could in no way free himself from Vivien's witchery. From that day forward, wherever she went, he could be found not far away. If she was in the garden, he would be there; if she was in the Hall, he would be there too; and if she went hawking, he would be on horseback as well. All the court noticed these things, and many of them made merry over it and joked about it. But Vivien hated Merlin with all her might, for she saw that everyone was laughing at Merlin's folly, and he wearied her with his attentions. Still, she hid her dislike from him and behaved toward him in every way as though she felt a great friendship for him.

Now it happened one day that Vivien was sitting in the garden in wonderfully pleasant summer weather, and Merlin came into the garden and saw Vivien where she sat. But when Vivien saw Merlin coming, she suddenly felt such a dislike for him that she could not bear to be near him just then, so she got up in haste, meaning to escape from him. But Merlin hurried and caught up with her, and he said to her, "Child, do you hate me, then?" And Vivien said, "Sir, I do not hate you." But Merlin said, "In very truth, I believe you do hate me." And Vivien was silent.

Then after a little Merlin said, "I wish I knew what I could do for you so that you would stop hating me, for I find that I have a wonderful love for you." At this Vivien looked at Merlin very strangely, and after a while she said, "Sir, if only you would share your wisdom and your skill with me, then I believe I could love you a very great deal. For look, I am only a young child in knowledge, and you are so old and so wise that I am afraid of you. If you would teach me your wisdom so that I might be your equal, then perhaps I might come to feel for you the way you would have me feel."

At this Merlin looked very steadily at Vivien and said, "Damsel, you are surely not the foolish child you claim to be, for I see that your eyes are very bright with a cleverness beyond your years. Now, I fear that if I were to teach you the wisdom you long to have, it would be either your undoing or else mine."

Then Vivien cried out in a very loud and piercing voice, "Merlin, if you love me, teach me your wisdom and the secrets of your magic, and then I will love you more than anyone else in all the world!"

But Merlin sighed very deeply, for his heart was full of foreboding. Then after a while he said, "Vivien, you shall have your way, and I will teach you all the things of wisdom and magic that you want to know."

At this Vivien was filled with such a fierce agony of joy that she did not dare let Merlin look into her face, for fear he would read what was written there. So she cast down her eyes and turned her face away from him. Then after a little while she said, "Master, when will you teach me that wisdom?"

Merlin answered, "I will not teach you today, or tomorrow, or in this place. For I can only teach you these things in a solitude where nothing will disturb your studies. But tomorrow you must tell King Arthur that you have to go back to your father's kingdom. Then we will set out together, along with your attendants; and when we have come to some secluded place, I will build a dwelling there by means of my magic, and we will stay in it until I have taught you wisdom."

Then Vivien was filled with joy, and she caught Merlin's hand in hers and kissed it with great passion.

*Merlin and Vivien leave the court of the King*.

So the next day Vivien begged King Arthur to give her permission to go back to her father's court, and on the third day she and Merlin, and a number of attendants who served the damsel, left the court of King Arthur and set out as though they were on their way to the Kingdom of Northumberland.

But when they had gone some little distance from the court of the King, they turned to the east and made their way toward a certain valley that Merlin knew of. It was such a fair and pleasant place that it was sometimes called the Valley of Delight and sometimes the Valley of Joyousness.
