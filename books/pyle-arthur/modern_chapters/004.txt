Chapter 2: How Arthur Twice Did the Miracle of the Sword in Front of Sir Ector, and How He Learned Who He Really Was

[Figure aj: Sir Kay shows the magic sword to Sir Ector — In a dim hall Kay points to the sword lying on a covered table while his father leans forward to look at it.]

So Arthur hurried to the part of the lists where Sir Ector sat with the people of his household. He stood in front of his father and said, "Sir, my brother Kay has sent me here to ask you to come straight to the pavilion where we have made our lodging. And truly, I think something very extraordinary has happened, for my brother Kay has a look on his face such as I have never seen him wear."

Sir Ector wondered very much what could have made Sir Kay leave the fighting and send for him at such a time, so he got up from where he sat and went with Arthur. They went to the pavilion, and when Sir Ector came in, there stood Sir Kay in the middle of it. Sir Ector saw that his face was as white as wood ash and that his eyes shone with a strange brightness. And Sir Ector said, "My son, what is the matter with you?" Sir Kay answered, "Sir, here is a very wonderful thing." With that he took his father by the hand and led him to the table that stood in the pavilion. On the table lay a cloak, and there was something wrapped inside the cloak. Then Sir Kay opened the cloak, and there lay the sword from the anvil, its hilt and its blade glittering with dazzling splendor.

*Sir Ector sees the sword*.

Sir Ector knew that sword at once, and where it had come from. He was filled with such astonishment that he did not know what to do. For a while his tongue would not speak at all. Then at last he found his voice and cried out loudly, "What is this I see with my own eyes!"

Sir Kay answered, "Sir, I have the sword that until a little while ago stood fixed in the anvil on the block of marble in front of the great cathedral. So I ask you to tell me what this may mean."

Then Sir Ector said, "How did you come by that sword?"

For a while Sir Kay said nothing. But after a while he said, "Sir, I broke my sword in the fight I was just fighting, and then I found myself this sword in its place."

Sir Ector was completely bewildered and did not know whether to believe what he was hearing. After a while he said, "If it is true that you drew this sword out of the anvil, then it must also be true that you are the rightful King of Britain, for that is what the writing on the sword proclaims. But if you really did draw it out of the anvil, then you will be able just as easily to thrust it back again into the place you took it from."

At this a great trouble came over Sir Kay's spirit, and he cried out in a very loud voice, "Who could do such a thing as that? Who could work so great a miracle as to thrust a sword into solid iron?" Sir Ector answered, "That miracle is no greater than the one you have already worked in drawing it out. For who ever heard of a man who could draw a sword out of a place and yet could not thrust it back where he drew it from?"

Sir Kay did not know what to say to his father, and he was very much afraid that he would not be able to work that miracle. Even so, he comforted himself as well as he could, saying to himself, "If my young brother Arthur could work this miracle, why shouldn't I be able to work one just like it? I am certainly no less worthy than he is. If he drew the sword out so easily, perhaps I shall be able to thrust it back into its place just as easily." And so he took what comfort he could from these thoughts.

*Sir Kay tries to put back the sword, but fails*.

He wrapped the sword in the cloak again, and when he had done so, he and Sir Ector left the pavilion and made their way to the place in front of the cathedral where the marble block and the anvil stood. Arthur went along with his father and his brother, and they did not forbid him. When they came to the place where the sword had been, Sir Kay climbed up onto the block of marble and looked at the face of the anvil. And the face of the anvil was perfectly smooth, without a scratch or a mark of any kind. Sir Kay said to himself, "What is this my father wants me to do? What man alive could thrust a sword blade into a solid anvil of iron?" But even so, he could not back out of that impossible task, and he was forced to attempt the miracle. So he set the point of the sword against the iron and pushed down on it with all his strength. But it was impossible for him to do it. Though he strove with all his might, pressing the sword against the face of the anvil, he did not pierce the iron by so much as the breadth of a hair.

So after he had tried for a long time, he finally stopped and climbed down from where he stood. And he said to his father, "Sir, no man alive can work that miracle."

Sir Ector answered, "Then how is it possible that you could have drawn the sword out, as you say, and yet cannot put it back again?"

Then young Arthur raised his voice and said, "Father, may I speak?" And Sir Ector said, "Speak, my son." And Arthur said, "I wish I might try my hand with that sword." Sir Ector replied, "By what right would you handle that sword?" And Arthur said, "Because it was I who drew that sword out of the anvil for my brother. And as you say, drawing it out is no harder than thrusting it back again. So I believe I shall be able to set it back into the iron I drew it from."

Then Sir Ector stared at young Arthur so strangely that Arthur did not know why he looked at him that way. So he cried out, "Sir, why are you staring at me so strangely? Are you angry with me?" Sir Ector answered, "Before God, my son, I am not angry with you." Then he said, "If you wish to handle the sword, you certainly may attempt that miracle."

*Arthur works the miracle of the sword and the anvil*.

So Arthur took the sword from his brother Kay and leaped up onto the marble block. He set the point of the sword on the anvil and pressed down very hard, and the sword slid smoothly into the center of the anvil until it stood halfway deep in it, and there it held fast. And after he had worked that miracle, he drew the sword out again, very quickly and easily, and then thrust it back once more, just as he had done before.

When Sir Ector saw what Arthur had done, he cried out in a tremendously loud voice, "Lord! Lord! What is this miracle I see with my own eyes!" And when Arthur came down from the marble block, Sir Ector knelt down before him and put his hands together, palm to palm.

But when Arthur saw what his father was doing, he cried out like someone in great pain, and he said, "My father! My father! Why are you kneeling to me?"

Sir Ector answered him, "I am not your father. And now it has been made plain that you are certainly of very high birth, and that the blood of kings flows in your veins, or you could never have handled that sword as you have done."

At that Arthur began to weep beyond all measure, and he cried out as if in great anguish of spirit, "Father! Father! What are you saying? I beg you, get up, and do not kneel to me."

So Sir Ector got up from his knees and stood face to face with Arthur, and he said, "Arthur, why are you weeping?" And Arthur said, "Because I am afraid."

All this while Sir Kay had been standing nearby, and he could neither move nor speak, but stood there like someone under a spell. And he said to himself, "What is this? Is my brother a king?"

Then Sir Ector spoke. "Arthur," he said, "the time has come for you to know who you are, for the truth about your life has until now been kept completely hidden from you.

*Sir Ector tells Arthur the story of his infancy*.

"Now I will confess everything to you, and it is this. Eighteen years ago a certain man came to me, a very wise man and high in the favor of Uther Pendragon, and that man was the enchanter Merlin. Merlin showed me the signet ring of Uther Pendragon, and by the authority of that ring he commanded me to be at a certain place at a particular time that he named. The place he named was the postern gate, the small back gate, of Uther Pendragon's castle, and the time he named was midnight of that very day.

"He told me to tell no one anything about what he had said to me, and so I kept his secret, as he wished me to.

"So I went to that postern gate at midnight as Merlin had commanded. And there Merlin came to me with another man, and the other man was Sir Ulfius, who was the chief knight of Uther Pendragon's household. And I tell you that those two great men stood closer to Uther Pendragon than any other men in all the world.

"Now when those two came to me, I saw that Merlin was carrying something in his arms, wrapped in a scarlet cloak of fine cloth. He opened the folds of the cloak, and there I saw a newborn child wrapped in swaddling clothes. I saw the child by the light of a lantern that Sir Ulfius carried, and I could see that he was very beautiful of face and large of bone. And you were that child.

"Then Merlin gave me my orders. I was to take that child and raise him as my own. He said the child was to be called Arthur, and that no one in all the world was to know anything except that the child was mine. I told Merlin I would do as he wished, and I took the child and carried him away with me. And I announced that the child was my own, and everyone believed me, so that no one ever knew anything but that you were my own son. And the lady who was my wife took that secret with her to Paradise when she died. From then until now, no one in all the world knew anything of this matter except me and those two great men I have named.

"And until now I have never known who your father was. But now I begin to suspect who he was, and that very high and kingly blood flows in your veins. And I have it in my mind that perhaps your father was Uther Pendragon himself. For who but the son of Uther Pendragon could have drawn that sword out of the anvil as you have done?"

When Arthur heard his father say this, he cried out in a very loud and passionate voice, "Woe! Woe! Woe!" saying the word three times. And Sir Ector said, "Arthur, why are you so full of sorrow?" And Arthur said, "Because I have lost my father, and I would rather have my father than be a King!"

*Merlin and Sir Ulfius appear to the three*.

Now while all this was happening, two men came to that place, very tall, and wonderfully noble and proud in their bearing. And when these two men drew near, Arthur and Sir Ector and Sir Kay saw that one of them was the enchanter Merlin and the other was Sir Ulfius, for those two men were very famous and well known to all the world. And when the two had come up to the three, Merlin spoke and said, "What news?" And Sir Ector answered, "News of a very wonderful kind. For look, Merlin! This is the child you brought to me eighteen years ago, and you can see that he has grown to manhood."

Then Merlin said, "Sir Ector, I know very well who this youth is, for I have kept close watch over him all this time. And I know that in him lies the hope of Britain. What is more, I tell you that this very day, in the surface of an enchanted looking glass, I have seen everything he has done since morning. I know how he drew the sword out of the anvil, and how he thrust it back again; and I know how he drew it out and thrust it back a second time. And I know everything you have been saying to him just now, and I declare that you have told him the very truth. And now the spirit of prophecy is upon me, and I can see into the future. I see that you, Arthur, will become the greatest and most famous King who ever lived in Britain. I see that many knights of extraordinary excellence will gather around you, and that men will tell of their marvelous deeds for as long as this land lasts. I see that through these knights your reign will be full of splendor and glory. And I see that the most marvelous adventure of all, the quest for the Holy Grail, will be achieved by three of the knights of your Court, to your lasting fame, for you will be the King in whose reign that holy cup is won. All these things I foresee. And the time is now at hand when the glory of your House will once again be shown to the world, and all the people of this land will rejoice in you and in your kingship. So, Sir Ector, for the next three days I charge you to guard this young man as the apple of your eye, for in him lies the hope and salvation of this whole realm."

*Sir Ector asks a favor of Arthur*.

Then Sir Ector raised his voice and cried out to Arthur, "A favor! A favor!" And Arthur said, "Oh, what is this? My father, do you ask a favor of me, when you may have everything in the world that is mine to give? Ask whatever you wish, and it is yours!" Then Sir Ector said, "I beg this of you: that when you are King, your brother Kay may be Seneschal of all this realm." And Arthur said, "It shall be as you ask." And he said, "As for you, it shall be even better for you, for you shall be my father to the end!" And as he said this, he took Sir Ector's head in his hands and kissed him on the forehead and on the cheeks, and so sealed the promise he had given.

But all this while Sir Kay had stood there like a man struck by lightning. He did not know whether to be lifted up to the skies or cast down into the depths, now that his young brother had passed him by and been raised to such an extraordinary height of fortune. And so he stood there like someone with no life or motion left in him.

And let it be said here that Arthur kept every promise he made to his father. For in later times he made Sir Kay his Seneschal, and Sir Ector was a father to him until the day of his death, which came five years after that time.

So I have told you how Arthur's royal birth was first discovered. And now, if you will listen, you shall hear how it was confirmed before all the world.
