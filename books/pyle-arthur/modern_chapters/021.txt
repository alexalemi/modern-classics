Chapter 2: How Merlin Traveled with Vivien to the Valley of Joyousness and Built Her a Castle There, How He Taught Her the Secrets of Magic, and How She Used Them to Bring Him Down

[Figure bj: Vivien bewitches Merlin — The old enchanter bends toward Vivien as she offers him a cup of wine across a table set with a pitcher and goblets.]

*Merlin and Vivien come to an enchanted forest*.

So Merlin and Vivien and those who were with them traveled east for three days, until, toward the end of the third day, they came to the edge of a very dark and gloomy forest. There before them they saw trees so thickly woven together that no eye could see any of the sky through the thickness of the leaves. And they saw that the branches and roots of the trees looked like serpents all twisted together. So Vivien said, "Sir, this is a very gloomy woodland." "Yes," said Merlin, "so it seems. Even so, inside this forest lies the place that some call the Valley of Joyousness and others the Valley of Delight, because of its great beauty. And there are several paths running through this forest by which a man may reach that valley, whether on horseback or on foot."

And after a while they found that it was as Merlin said, for by and by they came upon one of those paths, and they entered it and went deep into the forest. And inside that dismal woodland it was so dark that it seemed as though night had fallen, although it was bright daylight outside its borders, so that many of the party were very much afraid. But Merlin kept cheering them on, and so they went forward on their way. And by and by they came out of that place at last into the open again, and they were greatly relieved and took much comfort.

*Merlin and Vivien come to the Valley of Joyousness*.

By this time evening had come, very peaceful and still, and wonderfully beautiful. In the center of the valley lay a small lake, so smooth and as clear as crystal that it looked like an oval shield of pure silver laid down on the ground. And all around the edge of the lake were level meadows covered with an unbelievable multitude of flowers of every color and kind, very beautiful to see.

When Vivien saw this place she cried out to Merlin, "Master, this is truly a very joyous valley, for I do not believe the blessed meadows of Paradise are more beautiful than this." And Merlin said, "Very well; let us go down into it." So they went down, and as they went, night fell quickly, and the round moon rose into the sky. And it was hard to tell whether the valley was more beautiful by day or more beautiful when the moon shone down on it like that.

So at last they all came to the shore of the lake, and they saw that there was neither house nor castle in that place.

At this Merlin's followers grumbled among themselves, saying, "This enchanter has brought us here, but how is he going to provide us with a resting place to shelter us from the changes of bad weather? For the beauty of this spot alone cannot shelter us from rain and storm." And Merlin overheard their grumbling, and he said, "Peace! Do not trouble yourselves about that, for I will very soon provide you with a good resting place." Then he said to them, "Stand a little way off until I show you what I will do." So they drew back a little, as he commanded, and he and Vivien stayed where they were. And Vivien said, "Master, what will you do?" And Merlin said, "Wait a little and you will see."

With that he began a certain very powerful spell, so that the earth began to tremble and shake, and something like a great cloud of red dust rose into the air. In this dust various shapes and forms began to appear, and these shapes and forms rose very high into the air, until by and by those who were watching saw that a great building was taking shape in the middle of the cloud of red dust.

*Merlin builds a castle by means of his magic*.

Then, after a while, everything grew quiet and the dust slowly faded from the air, and there before them stood a marvelous castle such as no one there had ever seen before, even in a dream. For its walls were of ultramarine and vermilion, decorated and adorned with figures of gold, so that the castle shone in the moonlight like a pure vision of great glory.

Vivien saw everything Merlin had done, and she went to him and knelt on the ground before him and took his hand and pressed it to her lips. And as she knelt there, she said, "Master, this is surely the most wonderful thing in the world. Will you teach me such magic that I can build a castle like this one out of the elements?" And Merlin said, "Yes; all this I will teach you, and more besides. For I will teach you not only how to create a building like this out of invisible things, but also how, with a single touch of your wand, to scatter that castle instantly into the air, just as a child with a stroke of a straw can burst a beautiful shining bubble, which one instant is there and the next instant is not. And I will teach you more than that, for I will teach you how to change and transform one thing so that it looks like something else; and I will teach you spells and charms such as you have never heard of before."

Then Vivien cried out, "Master, you are the most wonderful man in all the world!" And Merlin looked at Vivien, and her face was very beautiful in the moonlight, and he loved her a very great deal. So he smiled at her and said, "Vivien, do you still hate me?" And she said, "No, master."

But she did not speak the truth, for in her heart she was evil, and Merlin's heart was good, and what is evil will always hate what is good. So, though Vivien hungered for the knowledge of the black arts, and though she spoke so lovingly with her lips, yet in her spirit she both feared and hated Merlin because of his wisdom. For she knew very well that, if it were not for the enchantment of the ring he wore, Merlin would no longer love her as he did. And so she said in her heart, "If Merlin teaches me all his wisdom, then the world cannot hold both him and me."

*Merlin teaches magic to Vivien*.

Merlin stayed with Vivien in that place for a year and a little more, and in that time he taught her all the magic he was able to pass on. And at the end of that time he said to her, "Vivien, I have now taught you so much that I believe there is no one in all the world who knows more than you do of the things of magic you have studied in this time. For not only do you have such powers of sorcery that you can make the invisible elements take shape at your will, and not only can you change one thing at your will so that it looks like something altogether different, but you have such powerful magic in your possession that you can tangle any living soul in its meshes, unless that person has some very good charm to defend himself against your wiles. Even I myself have not very much more power than what I have given you."

So said Merlin, and Vivien was filled with great joy. And she said in her heart, "Now, Merlin, if I have the good fortune to tangle you in my spells, you will never see the world again."

*Vivien prepares a feast for Merlin*.

When the next day came, Vivien had a very splendid feast prepared for herself and Merlin. And by means of the knowledge Merlin had taught her, she made a certain very powerful sleeping potion that had no taste at all. She herself mixed this potion into a certain fine wine, and she poured the wine into a golden chalice of extraordinary beauty.

So when the feast was over, and while she and Merlin sat together, Vivien said, "Master, I have a mind to do you a great honor." And Merlin said, "What is it?" "You will see," said Vivien. With that she clapped her hands, and at once a young page came to where they were, carrying that chalice of wine in his hand, and he gave it to Vivien. Then Vivien took the chalice and went to where Merlin sat, and knelt down before him and said, "Sir, I beg you to take this chalice and drink the wine that is in it. For just as that wine is very noble and very precious, so your wisdom is very noble and very precious; and just as the wine is held in a chalice of priceless worth, so your wisdom is held in a life that has been beyond all value to the world." And with that she put her lips to the chalice and kissed the wine that was in it.

Merlin suspected no evil, but took the chalice and drank the wine very cheerfully.

*Merlin is overcome by the wine that Vivien gives him*.

After that, in a little while, the fumes of that powerful drink began to rise into Merlin's brain, and it was as though a cloud came down over his sight. And when this came upon him, he knew at once that he was betrayed, and he cried out three times, in a voice very bitter and full of agony, "Woe! Woe! Woe!" And then he cried out, "I am betrayed!" And with that he struggled to rise from where he sat, but he could not.

All this while Vivien sat with her chin in her hands and watched him very steadily, smiling at him strangely. So before long Merlin stopped struggling and sank into a sleep so deep that it was almost as if he had died. And when that had happened, Vivien got up and leaned over him and laid a very powerful spell on him. She stretched out her forefinger and wove an enchantment all around him, so that it was as if he were wrapped all about in a silver web of enchantment. And when she had finished, Merlin could move neither hand nor foot, nor even so much as a fingertip, but was exactly like some great insect that a clever and beautiful spider had caught in a net of fine, strong web.

*Vivien bewitches Merlin*.

When the next morning came, Merlin woke from his sleep and saw Vivien sitting across from him, watching him very closely. They were in the same room in which he had fallen asleep. And when Vivien saw that Merlin was awake, she laughed and said, "Merlin, how are you?" And Merlin groaned with great passion, saying, "Vivien, you have betrayed me."

At this Vivien laughed again, very shrilly and piercingly, and she said, "Look, Merlin, you are completely in my power, for you are wound all about in the very enchantments that you yourself taught me. Why, you cannot move a single hair without my will. And when I leave you, the world will see you no more, and all your wisdom will be my wisdom, and all your power will be my power, and there will be no one else in the whole world who has the wisdom I have."

Then Merlin groaned so deeply that it seemed his heart would burst. And he said, "Vivien, you have brought me to such shame that even if I were freed from this spell I could not bear for any man ever to see my face again. For I do not grieve so much for my ruin as I grieve at the folly that has turned my own wisdom against me to destroy me. So I forgive you everything you have done to betray me; yet there is one thing only that I beg of you."

And Vivien said, "Does it concern you?" And Merlin said, "No, it concerns someone else." Then Vivien said, "What is it?"

*Merlin makes one request of Vivien*.

Then Merlin said, "It is this. My gift of foresight has come back to me, and I see that King Arthur is at this moment in great danger of his life. So I beg you, Vivien, to go at once to where he is in danger, and to use your powers of sorcery to save him. By doing this one good deed, you may perhaps lessen the sin of what you have done in betraying me."

Now at that time Vivien was not so wholly bad as she later became, for she still felt some small pity for Merlin and some small respect for King Arthur. So now she laughed and said, "Very well, I will do what you ask in this matter. Where shall I go to save that King?"

Merlin answered, "Go into the West Country, to the castle of a certain knight named Sir Damas le Noir, and when you get there you will see at once how you can help the good King." At this Vivien said, "I will do this thing for you, for it is the last favor anyone will ever do for you in this world."

With that Vivien clapped her hands and called many of her attendants. And when they had come in, she showed Merlin to them and said, "See how I have bewitched him! Go! See for yourselves! Feel his hands and his face, and see if there is any life in him." And they went to Merlin and felt him, his hands and arms and face, and they even plucked at his beard, and Merlin could not move in any way, but could only groan in great misery. So they all laughed and made merry over his woeful state.

*Vivien places Merlin beneath the stone*.

Then by means of her magic Vivien made a great chest of stone appear in that place. And she commanded those who were there to lift Merlin up and lay him in it, and they did as she commanded. Then by means of her magic she had a huge slab of stone, such as ten men could hardly lift, placed on top of that chest, and Merlin lay beneath that stone like a man who was dead.

Then Vivien made the magic castle vanish in an instant, and it happened just as she willed. Then she made a mist rise up over that place, and the mist was of such a kind that no one could make his way into it or break through it, and no human eye could see what was inside. And when she had done all this, she went away from that valley with all her attendants, rejoicing greatly that she had triumphed over Merlin.

Even so, she did not forget her promise, but went to the castle of Sir Damas le Noir; and after a while you will hear everything that happened there.

That was the passing of Merlin. And may God grant that you never so misuse the wisdom He gives you that it is turned against you to your undoing. For there can be no greater bitterness in the world than this: that a man should be betrayed by someone to whom he himself has given the power to betray him.

And now let us turn to King Arthur, to learn how things went with him after Merlin had been betrayed to his undoing like this.
