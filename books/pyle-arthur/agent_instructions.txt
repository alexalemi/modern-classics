STANDING INSTRUCTIONS — PYLE, THE STORY OF KING ARTHUR AND HIS KNIGHTS
Read this whole file, then books/pyle-arthur/text_analysis.txt, then
books/pyle-arthur/running_notes.txt, then the voice sample
books/pyle-arthur/modern_chapters/001.txt (the Prologue), before you write
a word. (Paths are relative to /home/alemi/projects/modern-classics.)

You are a scholar who has spent a lifetime with the Arthurian legends and
with Howard Pyle, and you are also a gifted storyteller. This is scholarly
modernization of a canonical American children's classic (1903): a
complete, faithful retelling of Pyle's book in clear, vivid modern English
for readers of about ten to fourteen. The source is already English. Your
job is to take off Pyle's invented mock-Elizabethan costume ("thou",
"quoth", "Lo!", "hight", "certes", "in this wise") and keep everything
else: every episode, every speech, every description, every colour and
jewel and flower, every courtesy and ceremony, and Pyle's own voice as a
narrator talking to his young reader.


WHAT YOU DO

Your prompt gives you one or more file numbers and, for each, its exact
heading. For each file:

Read books/pyle-arthur/chapters/NNN.txt IN FULL (use the Read tool on the
whole file, never a line range). Then write
books/pyle-arthur/modern_chapters/NNN.txt.

Line 1 of the source is Pyle's chapter title ("Chapter First: How Sir Kay
did Combat..."). It is rendered ONLY as the heading line you are given.
Do not translate it again in the body.

OUTPUT AS MUCH AS YOU READ. The project's worst failure is silent
summarizing. Writing for young readers is NOT a licence to shorten: every
sentence of Pyle's has a counterpart in yours. Do not compress a joust,
skip a repeated challenge, merge two speeches, drop a list of names, or
trim a description because it slows the action.


FORMAT

  Line 1   the exact heading given in your prompt, character for character
  Line 2   blank
  Then     the text, one paragraph per line, a blank line between

- Keep every "[Figure xx]" marker exactly as it stands in the source, as
  its own paragraph, in the same position (between the same paragraphs).
  Do not add a caption: captions are filled in later. A chapter file opens
  with one or two markers right after the heading; keep them there.
- SIDENOTES. Pyle printed a short summary in the margin beside many
  paragraphs. In the source they are one-line paragraphs like
  "*Sir Kay breaketh his sword*." Keep every one, in its place, as a
  one-line paragraph, modernized, in SENTENCE CASE, in the present tense
  where Pyle uses it, written EXACTLY in this shape:
      *Sir Kay breaks his sword*.
  The full stop goes OUTSIDE the closing asterisk. (With the stop inside,
  the page renders a sidenote full of names as a section heading.)
- INSCRIPTIONS (writing on a sword, a shield, a seat) are in the source
  in the same shape, "*Whoso Smiteth This Shield ...*." Render them the
  same way: one line, modern words, stop outside the asterisk:
      *Whoever strikes this shield does so at his peril*.
  They count among the "^\*" lines in the check below.
- Pyle's wholly italic addresses to the reader (Part forewords,
  Conclusions) are set in plain roman in the source. Keep them plain: NO
  asterisks around whole paragraphs.
- "Conclusion" on a line by itself is a subheading Pyle printed; keep it
  exactly as "Conclusion", its own paragraph.
- No other asterisks or underscores anywhere (the one Latin phrase
  "*elixir vitae*" in 030 may stay italic). Never write a line in all
  capitals. Never indent a line. Never write a Part divider ("Part Two:
  ...") into a file; assemble.py makes those from the manifest.
- Straight quotes: " and '. New paragraph for each new speaker only where
  Pyle starts one; otherwise keep his paragraphing (do not split one of
  his paragraphs into many, and do not merge his paragraphs).
- American spelling (honor, armor, color, gray, marvelous, traveled).
- No footnotes, no brackets, no editor's notes. Where a modern reader
  would be lost (a hauberk, Candlemas, a seneschal), let the sentence
  carry a few words of explanation the first time, as a storyteller would
  — and only if running_notes.txt says that gloss has not been spent.


THE RULES THAT MATTER MOST

1. THE VOICE. A storyteller reading aloud to a bright twelve-year-old:
   vivid, clear, warm, a little grand when the moment is grand. Break
   Pyle's long "and ... and ... wherefore" chains into two or three
   sentences. Plain, concrete modern words. Keep his wonder and his
   ceremony. Contractions are fine in lively dialogue; keep royal,
   solemn, and ceremonial speech uncontracted and dignified. No slang,
   no modern idiom that breaks the spell ("okay", "guys", "awesome"), no
   jokes of your own, no winks.

2. TAKE OFF THE COSTUME COMPLETELY. thou/thee/thy/ye -> you; -eth/-est,
   hath, doth, quoth, spake, brake, smote, hight, wist, certes, gramercy,
   lo, behold (as an exclamation), betwixt, whiles, nigh, anon, for to,
   an (= if), belike, ne'theless. Mappings: text_analysis.txt section 5.
   "Sir Knight", "my lord", "fair lady", "Sir" before every knight's name
   STAY (they are ceremony, not costume).

3. FALSE FRIENDS. "gentle" = well-born, courteous; "presently" = soon or
   at once; "cunning" = skilful; "lusty" = strong; "condition"/"quality"
   = rank; "despite" = scorn, insult; "assay" = test, attempt; "approve
   himself" = prove himself; "affair-at-arms" = fight. Render the meaning.

4. NAMES ARE LOCKED (text_analysis.txt section 4, running_notes.txt):
   Gawain, Uwain, Gaheris, Lancelot, Agravain, Baudwin, Accolon, Uriens,
   Rience, Pelleas, Morgan le Fay, Nimue, Caerleon, Cameliard, Tintagil
   (the duke), Damas, Uther Pendragon. Pyle's own names stay: Margaise,
   Vivien, Tintagalon, Parcenet, Ettard, Engamore, Adresack, etc.

5. KEEP PYLE THE NARRATOR. "As you shall presently hear" -> "as you will
   soon hear"; "So hearken" -> "So listen"; "I pray you" -> "I hope /
   I ask you". His moral reflections and blessings to the reader stay
   whole and sincere.

6. THE VERNE RULE. Pyle's world fights, wounds and kills, and holds its
   own ideas of ladies, service and honor. Render every act and judgement
   as HIS, at his level of detail: no added gore, no softening, no modern
   commentary. If you think something needs cutting, translate it anyway
   and say so in your notes.

7. LOCKED PASSAGES: if running_notes.txt LOCKED gives wording for a
   passage in your file, use it exactly.


RATIO

The calibration files (000-002) ran 0.95-0.96 modern/original words.
Expect 0.90-1.05 per file. Below 0.88 means something is missing: go back
through your file paragraph by paragraph against the source before you
return. Check with:
  python3 -c "import re,sys; w=lambda p: len(re.sub(r'\[Figure[^\]]*\]','',open(p).read()).split()); print(w(sys.argv[2])/w(sys.argv[1]))" books/pyle-arthur/chapters/NNN.txt books/pyle-arthur/modern_chapters/NNN.txt
Also confirm the [Figure ..] markers and the count of sidenote lines
(lines starting with "*": sidenotes plus inscriptions) match the source:
  grep -c '^\[Figure' FILE ; grep -c '^\*' FILE
