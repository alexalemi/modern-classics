Drawn for this edition, in the manner of the Dover science paperbacks of the 1950s and 60s. The poem has no figures, so the drawing is its argument from Book Two: left to themselves, the atoms would fall straight down through the empty void like rain and never meet. One swerves a little out of line, strikes its neighbour, and they cling together, and from collisions like that comes everything there is. The line at the top is Lucretius's.
