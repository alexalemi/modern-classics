TEXT ANALYSIS — Henry Dunant, Un souvenir de Solférino (1862)

SOURCE AND EDITION
The first edition: Geneva, Imprimerie Jules-Guillaume Fick, 1862 (printed
November 1862, 115 pages, not offered for sale). Text from French
Wikisource, proofread to 100% against the scan of that edition
("Dunant - Un souvenir de Solférino, 1862.djvu", pp. 4-118). The 31
footnotes are Dunant's own. About 30,000 French words including notes.

NO CRIB. No public-domain English translation exists. The only early one,
the American National Red Cross's "A Memory of Solferino" (1939, preface by
Norman H. Davis, A136038), was renewed on 18 January 1967 (R402843, Catalog
of Copyright Entries renewals, NYPL cce-renewals 1967-1.tsv) and is in
copyright in the US until 2034; it was not consulted. The ICRC's later
translations are in copyright too. This is a translation straight from the
French with no second witness (boltzmann/ and dogen/ precedent); the
introduction says so. The numeral diff (check.py) is the mechanical guard.

WHAT KIND OF BOOK IT IS
A Genevan businessman of thirty-one, "a mere tourist", happens on the
largest battle in Europe since Leipzig and spends the following week in the
churches of Castiglione among some nine thousand wounded. Three years
later he writes it down to make one argument. The book has three movements
and no divisions:
  1. The battle of 24 June 1859, told largely from the official accounts
     (he did not see it; he arrived that evening), as a pageant of named
     officers and terrible episodes — the deliberate set-up: here is the
     glory.
  2. The aftermath, which he did see: the field on the 25th, the
     Chiesa Maggiore at Castiglione, the women of the town who nursed
     French and Austrian alike saying "Tutti fratelli", then Brescia and
     Milan. Here is the price.
  3. The proposal: permanent volunteer relief societies in every country,
     formed in peacetime, and an international principle, "conventional and
     sacred", to protect them. This is what the first two movements are for.

DIVISION RULING (no chapters in the original)
Seven files at the natural joints, each with a plain descriptive heading of
ours (no "Chapter N", because Dunant numbered nothing; no "Part N:", which
assemble.strip_front deletes). The introduction says the headings are the
editor's.
  000 The Battle                       24 June, from Magenta to the end of the fighting
  001 The Night and the Morning After  retreat, storm, night, the field on the 25th
  002 Castiglione                      the convoys, the Chiesa Maggiore, Tutti fratelli
  003 The Road to Brescia              the 27th-28th: Cavriana, the night drive, MacMahon, Brescia
  004 The Hospitals of Brescia         the wards, amputations, the dying
  005 Milan                            the trains, the families of Milan
  006 A Proposal                       the relief societies and the convention

STYLE AND VOICE
- Romantic-humanitarian, breathless, devout. The battle is in the historic
  present ("À six heures le feu est sérieusement engagé"); keep the present
  wherever he uses it, past where he uses past. The tense change is his.
- Long cumulative sentences joined by semicolons. Break them for an English
  reader, keep every clause and every image, and keep the pile-up rhythm in
  the catalogues of horror: that pile-up is his rhetoric.
- Exclamations ("Que de drames… !") and apostrophes ("Il eût mieux valu pour
  toi, pauvre martyr…") stay as exclamations and apostrophes.
- The roll call of officers is long and must stay complete: every name.
  Glory is the point of the battle section; the proposal turns on it.
- He is a witness and an "I". Keep his modesty and his occasional
  self-reproach (Note 28: "how can anyone think of making lint before there
  is a single wounded man?").
- Measured register for the wounds: precise, flat, never relishing, never
  softened. He is graphic because he wants the reader to stop reading for
  glory. Translate exactly what is there — skulls, maggots, gangrene,
  amputations without chloroform — at his strength and no more.

VOCABULARY (locked; see running_notes.txt for the ledger)
- ambulance (1859) = a field hospital / dressing station, never a vehicle.
  "ambulances volantes" = "mobile field hospitals".
- l'Intendance = "the Intendance, the army's supply service" once; then
  "the supply service" / "the Intendance".
- charpie = lint (the scraped linen used for dressings).
- cantinière = cantinière, glossed once in the sentence.
- feldzeugmeister = "Feldzeugmeister" glossed once as the Austrian rank just
  below field marshal; lieutenant-feldmaréchal = "lieutenant field marshal".
- tirailleurs algériens = "Algerian riflemen"; chasseurs d'Afrique =
  "Chasseurs d'Afrique" (French light cavalry raised in Algeria); voltigeurs,
  zouaves, uhlans, hussars, bersaglieri keep their names.
- mitraille = grapeshot; obus = shell; boulet = cannonball; biscaïen =
  canister ball.
- le duc de Magenta = MacMahon (Marshal MacMahon, Duke of Magenta, once).
- Allies = the French and Sardinian (Piedmontese) armies; "Franco-Sardes".
- Names of people and places in their own forms as he gives them
  (Solferino without the accent in English running text, as the title page
  of most English editions; Médole -> Medole, Crémone -> Cremona, Pavie ->
  Pavia, Goïto -> Goito, Plaisance -> Piacenza, Mantoue -> Mantua, Vérone ->
  Verona: the standard English forms of Italian places).

UNITS (memory modern-units-in-retellings)
His lieue is the 4 km one — he gives the battlefront as "cinq lieues" and
"plus de vingt kilomètres". 1 lieue ~ 2.5 miles.
  cinq lieues (front) -> about twelve miles
  plus de vingt kilomètres -> more than twelve miles
  cinq kilomètres (Cavriana-Castellaro) -> about three miles
  six lieues (Castiglione from Brescia, Note 7) -> about fifteen miles
  trois cents lieues (the deaf Frenchman) -> some seven hundred and fifty miles
  quelques lieues -> a few miles / ten miles or so
  une lieue (Note 1) -> two or three miles
  trois à cinq pieds -> three to five feet (French foot within 7%; kept)
Francs and centimes are money and stay. Clock times are already modern.

FOOTNOTES — 31, all Dunant's own. Disposition: FOLD every one as its own
paragraph directly after the paragraph that cites it, opening
"Author's note:", with the [n] marker removed. They are narrative, not
apparatus: Fournier's biography, Mme N.'s lint proposal, Nightingale,
Dufour's letter. Dropping them would lose content and drag the proposal's
ratio toward 0.7.

SENSITIVE CONTENT
The wounds, amputations, the dying, the looting of the dead, the prisoners'
terror, the Croats finishing off wounded men and the Algerian riflemen doing
the same: all the author's subject, kept in full at his register.
RULING (first draft cut the phrase; Alex RESTORED it 2026-10-12 — kept,
named in the introduction. Original reasoning below): in the Mont Fontana paragraph he has the Algerian
riflemen charge "avec la rage de l'Africain et le fanatisme du Musulman …
comme des tigres altérés de sang". The ACT (they avenged their colonels by
killing without quarter) is history and stays, with the tigers; the
attribution of it to race and religion is cut. It is the only place the
book explains conduct by race or creed — the Croats in the next paragraph
are described by what they do, not what they are — so the reader loses no
information and does not gain a false one. See running_notes.txt.
"Les Croates" stay "the Croats": a regiment's nationality is information.

FAMOUS PASSAGES (must_contain.txt)
- "Tutti fratelli" — the women of Castiglione.
- The two questions of the proposal: whether relief societies could be
  formed in peacetime, and an "international principle, sanctioned by a
  convention".
