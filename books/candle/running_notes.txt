RUNNING NOTES — The Chemical History of a Candle. The consistency ledger.
Read before writing; locks are binding.

VOICE LOCK (set by 001)
  Live demonstration in the continuous present, as in soap-bubbles — but
  WARMER. Faraday says "my boys and girls", "I trust", "I hope you will",
  "if you will allow me". He admires things out loud and interrupts
  himself to do it. Keep every one of those moves; the delight is the
  reason the book survived. Sentences unstack into short modern ones, but
  the affection and the courtesy never get trimmed as padding.
  He addresses young people as a peer, never down to them: "I claim the
  privilege of speaking to young people as a young person myself."

EDITOR'S NOTES
  Crookes's 19 notes are inlined as their own paragraphs, prefixed
  exactly "Editor's note: " and always ending in a period. Register is
  DRY, third-person, technical — never the lecturer's voice. They are
  more chemically precise than the lecture on purpose; do not compress.

FIGURE CAPTIONS — house style, same as soap-bubbles
  One descriptive sentence, present tense, no flourish. Most plates are
  simple line woodcuts of apparatus: say what the apparatus is and what
  it is doing. No captions on the source side — they are new writing.
  Figures start at Fig. 1 in file 002 (the wick/capillary sequence).

FORWARD REFERENCES TO HONOUR
  - 001 ends mid-argument on the flame being fenced off from the fuel;
    002 opens straight into the wick and capillary attraction. Neither
    file recaps the other.
  - Lecture III is where water first appears as a product; Lectures IV
    and V take it apart. The "candle = slow respiration" analogy is
    reserved for Lecture VI — do not foreshadow it earlier, Faraday
    doesn't.
  - The closing of Lecture VI (file 012) is the famous farewell and is
    fixed near-verbatim by must_contain.txt. Nothing before it should
    borrow its cadence.
  - The Lecture on Platinum (013-014) is a separate occasion, February
    1861, not part of the Christmas course. Its opening must not address
    the same audience as continuing; it is its own lecture.
