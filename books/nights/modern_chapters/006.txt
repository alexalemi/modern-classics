The Third Old Man's Story

Know, Sultan and head of the jinn, that this mule was my wife.

It happened that I went away and was gone for a whole year; and when I came back from my journey I came to her at night, and saw a Black slave lying with her on the carpet bed. They were talking and playing with one another, laughing and kissing and coupling.

When she saw me she got up and came at me in a hurry with a water jar; and muttering spells over it, she sprinkled me and said, "Come out of this shape of yours and into the shape of a dog." And that instant I was a dog.

She drove me out of the house, and I ran out through the doorway and did not stop running until I came to a butcher's stall, where I stopped and started eating the bones that were lying there.

When the owner of the stall saw me he took me and led me into his house; but the moment his daughter caught sight of me she covered her face and cried out, "Are you bringing men in to me? Do you walk in here with them?"

"Where is the man?" her father asked.

"This dog," she said, "is a man whose wife has put a spell on him. And I can release him."

When her father heard that, he said, "In Allah's name, daughter — release him."

So she took a water jar and, after saying words over it, sprinkled a few drops on me and said, "Come out of that shape and into the shape you had before." And I went back to being myself.

Then I kissed her hand and said, "I wish you would transform my wife the way she transformed me."

At that she gave me some water and said, "As soon as you see her asleep, sprinkle this on her and say the words you heard me say, and she will become whatever you want her to be."

I went to my wife and found her fast asleep; and as I sprinkled the water on her I said, "Come out of that shape and into the shape of a she-mule." And that instant she became a she-mule — and she is the one you are looking at now with your own eyes, Sultan and head of the Kings of the Jinn.

Then the jinni turned to her and said, "Is this true?"

And she nodded her head and answered by signs: It is the truth. That is my story, and that is what happened to me.

Now when the old man had finished speaking, the jinni shook with pleasure and gave him the third part of the merchant's blood:—

And Shahrazad saw the dawn breaking, and fell silent.

Then Dunyazad said, "Sister, how pleasant your tale is, and how good to listen to — how sweet, and how welcome!"

She answered, "And what is this compared to what I could tell you tomorrow night, if I live and the King spares me?"

Then the King thought, "By Allah, I will not kill her until I have heard the rest of her tale, for it is truly a wonder."

So they rested that night in each other's arms until dawn. After that the King went out to his Hall of State, and the vizier and the troops came in and the court filled up; and the King gave his orders and gave judgement, appointed men and dismissed men, commanding and forbidding through the rest of the day. Then the council broke up, and King Shahryar went into his palace.

When it was the Third Night,

and the King had had his will of the vizier's daughter, her sister Dunyazad said to her, "Finish that tale of yours for us."

And she answered, "With joy and goodwill! It has reached me, O fortunate King, that when the third old man had told the jinni a tale more wonderful than the two before it, the jinni marvelled beyond all marvelling and, shaking with delight, cried, 'There — I have given you what was left of the merchant's punishment, and for your sake I have let him go.'"

At which the merchant embraced the old men and thanked them; and the three of them wished him joy of his deliverance, and each went off to his own city.

But this tale is not so wonderful as the fisherman's story.

The King asked, "What is the fisherman's story?"
