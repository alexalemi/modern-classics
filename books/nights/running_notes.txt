RUNNING NOTES — THE THOUSAND NIGHTS AND A NIGHT
================================================

The shared consistency ledger. Every translator reads this before every
file; the orchestrator updates it between batches. Anything here is
LOCKED — do not re-decide it, do not re-gloss it, do not "improve" it.


THE LOCKED FORMULAE
-------------------

These recur 147 times between them and their power is that they are
identical every time. Do not vary them.

THE NIGHT-BREAK, all three parts, exactly:

    ...mid-sentence, then:—And Shahrazad saw the dawn breaking, and fell
    silent.

    When it was the Eighth Night,

    she went on: It has reached me, O fortunate King, that ...

  - Part one ALWAYS "saw the dawn breaking, and fell silent". Burton has
    two wordings for it (70 "perceived", 76 "was surprised by"); that is
    his inconsistency, not the Arabic's, and we use one.
  - Part two keeps the night NUMBER exactly as printed, spelled out, and
    keeps the TRAILING COMMA. Without the comma assemble.py reads the
    line as a subheading and sets it as an <h4>.
  - Part three always "It has reached me, O fortunate King, that".
    Burton alternates "O auspicious King" (68) and "O King of the Age"
    (77); one wording throughout.
  - THE CUT IS ALWAYS MID-SENTENCE. That is the whole joke of the
    device. Do not tidy it into a sentence break.

OTHER SET PHRASES, locked:

    "Hearing and obedience!"                  (Burton: Hearkening and
                                               obedience)
    "There is no Majesty and there is no Might save in Allah!"
    "O Commander of the Faithful"
    "on the two of them be peace!"            (of prophets)
    "extolled and exalted be He!"             (of God)
    "if Allah will"                           (not "God willing")
    "in the old days, and in the old time before them"  — the tale-
        opening formula (Burton: "in tide of yore and in times and years
        long gone before"). Keep the cadence; it is saj'.
    "he wept till he wetted his gown"         — kept as printed; it is a
        set phrase, not a description, and it recurs.

BURTON'S COSTUME — GOES SILENTLY
--------------------------------

whereupon, quoth, aught, naught, whereat, forthright, ere, sooth,
albeit, wot, whenas, withal, hight, forthwith, anent, belike,
peradventure, methinks, prithee, whilome, wone, eke, lief, perchance,
fain, wist, trow; "an"/"and" for if; "for that" for because; "the
which"; "needs must"; "he ceased not to" for he kept on; "fared forth";
inverted "quoth he".

And his home-made words: verroterie (glassware), fid (chunk), Gobbo
(the hunchback), stound (moment), leven (lightning), undight
(unveiled).

  LOCKED WORDINGS ESTABLISHED HERE:
    "In the name of Allah, the Compassionate, the Merciful!" — the
        bismillah, opening the book
    "in the old days, and in the old time before them" — the tale-opening
        formula, first used in the preface paragraph
    "Hearing and obedience!"
    the house of Sasan            (Burton: the Banu Sasan)
    Samarkand, away in the outer lands   (Burton: "in Barbarian land",
        his rendering of the Arabic for the non-Arab countries. Not
        "Barbarian" — a modern reader hears something the text does not
        say.)
    Saeed / Sa'ad al-Din Saood    as printed, both forms; the second is
        the man announcing his own full name and the queen laughs at it

  THE RACE PASSAGE, as applied — the pattern for every later one:
    Burton's "a big slobbering blackamoor with rolling eyes which showed
    the whites, a truly hideous sight" became "a huge Black man,
    slack-mouthed, the whites of his eyes showing as they rolled — a
    hideous sight, as Shah Zaman saw him from the window."
      - the FACTS all stay: his size, his mouth, his eyes, and the
        judgement passed on him
      - the slur-vocabulary goes ("blackamoor" 11x, "negro" 8x across
        the book -> "the Black man", "the Black slave")
      - the judgement is LOCATED IN THE WATCHER rather than asserted by
        the narration. This is not softening: Shah Zaman is at the
        window, it is his scene, and the contempt is his. Nothing is
        laundered and nothing is added.
      - Shah Zaman's own speech keeps his contempt intact and unmarked
        ("in love with the filthiest slave in his household") — it is
        his line, in his mouth, and the Verne rule holds it there.

  RATIO: 1.05. NOTE THE SPLIT-FILE TRAP THAT NEARLY BIT HERE — the
  source file 000 ends on the lead-in "Even so the poet Utayyah hath
  excellently said:—" and the VERSE ITSELF IS THE FIRST THING IN FILE
  001. I translated the poem at the end of 000 and had to pull it back
  out. assemble.py stitches the parts together, so the reader would have
  seen nothing wrong; the poem would simply have been translated twice,
  once in each file, and both ratios would have been off. When a file
  ends on a colon, the thing it introduces belongs to the NEXT file.

FILE 001 — the ifrit's wife; the killing begins; Shahrazad volunteers

  GLOSS SPENT:

  "the two damsels who (Inshallah!) will presently be mentioned" is a

FILE 002 — the Bull and the Ass; Shahrazad goes to the King

  BURTON'S IMPORTED COSTUME, replaced. He reaches for European animal
  names that have nothing behind them in the Arabic: "Chanticleer" and
  "Dame Partlets" (Chaucer) for the cock and hens, and "Aliboron" (La
  Fontaine) for the donkey. All three go. The Ass is "Father of Waking"
  throughout — the one epithet, used consistently, where Burton drifts
  between that, "Father Wakener" and "Aliboron"; the Bull is
  "Broad-Brow" / "Father of a Bull".

  PROVERB ECHO — worth watching for generally. The Ass's "I was in joy
  and gladness, nought save my officiousness brought me this sadness" is
  THE SAME PROVERB the vizier quotes in file 001. Burton words it
  differently in the two places and the echo dies. Both now read "I was
  lying comfortably; nothing but my own meddling made me uncomfortable."
  The Ass repeating the father's proverb is the joke of the frame.

  "in spite of your nose" (Arabic idiom, not Burton's invention) is KEPT
  literal when aimed at another person, where the context carries it
  ("I will marry myself to him in spite of your nose"). In file 003 it
  is reflexive — the merchant goes to his death "despite his own nose" —
  where literal English says nothing, so it is unpacked: "though every
  part of him refused to go."

  THE WIFE-BEATING is the tale's moral and its punchline: the merchant
  "learned how to run a household from his Rooster". Verne rule. Played
  straight, at full length, no softening and no comment.

FILES 003-004 — the Trader and the Jinni; the First Old Man

  THE NIGHT-BREAK'S FIRST OUTING, and the three-part formula works as
  locked. Note what sits BETWEEN the parts here: Dunyazad's praise,
  Shahrazad's teaser, the King deciding not to kill her yet, a whole day
  of court business, and the vizier still carrying his daughter's
  shroud. That surrounding frame material is not boilerplate and is
  translated in full every time.

  Burton's opener varies again — "It is related, O auspicious King" and
  "It hath reached me, O auspicious King and Heaven directed Ruler". Both
  are normalised to the locked "It has reached me, O fortunate King,
  that". One wording, always.

  TALE TITLES: "The First Shaykh's Story" -> "The First Old Man's
  Story", and the same for the second and third. The manifest keeps
  Burton's word (it drives only the original-text edition); the modern
  heading comes from the file. Shaykh means old man here and all three
  are introduced as old men in the prose.

  RECURRING FORMULA, now locked: "if it were engraved with needles on
  the inner corners of the eye, it would be a warning to anyone willing
  to take warning." It comes back many times in the book.

  KEPT AS PRINTED, all genuine Arabic idiom rather than Burton's
  costume: "drunk without any wine", "the very core of your heart",
  "the life-blood of my liver", "her blood is lawful to you".

PREP FIX AFTER FILE 003 — THE VERSE REBUILD, and it is the silent class
again. verse_lines() un-wrapped Burton's couplets line by line, on the
rule that a wrapped continuation begins in lower case. A hemistich that
OPENS ON A CAPITAL breaks it both ways at once: "Yet ne'er a star but /
Sun and Moon by eclipse is overta'en" was torn in half, while its
neighbour was left carrying two hemistichs welded together. Every word
present, in order, with the right total — invisible to the ratio, to
must_contain, to everything.
  Now the run is joined and cut on the ASTERISKS first, and each
  between-asterisks chunk is split at the sentence end nearest its
  midpoint (split_middle). The midpoint rule matters: taking the FIRST
  sentence end instead would cut "Lot! Enjoy the Present passing well"
  after "Lot!". Suspect lines fell from 22 to 11, and the 11 that remain
  are real hemistichs with internal exclamations.
  Word counts are IDENTICAL before and after (217,747), so no manifest
  boundary moved and no finished file was invalidated.

FILES 007-012 — the Fisherman cycle, and the nested tales inside it

  GLOSSES SPENT:
    ifrit    "an ifrit is a jinni of the fiercer kind" — placed in file
             001, which is where the word FIRST appears. I had written
             007's gloss first and then found 001 had used the word

    marid    "the name of the mightiest and most rebellious of the jinn"
             (007)
    ghulah   "a ghulah, an ogress" — Burton glosses it himself (011)
    the tribe of Ad  "the ancient giants of the tribe of Ad" (012)

  NAME DECIDED: Solomon, not Sulayman. English settled this long ago,
  "the seal of Solomon" is the known phrase, and Burton himself writes
  "Solomon's seal" a few lines after writing "Sulayman". Asaf son of
  Barkhiya and Sakhr the Jinni are kept as printed.

  "pall and mall" -> polo, which is the game the Arabic means (008).

  BURTON'S CAPITALISATION IS NOT A SIGNAL. Source file 008 heads its
  night "When It Was The Fifth Night," in title case where every other
  one is sentence case. Normalised. A heading whose case varies at
  random cannot be carrying information.

  ONE GLOSS ADDED FOR SENSE, and the standard for when to do it: the
  proverb about "Ummi Amir's neighbour" (007) is meaningless in English
  — Umm Amir is the hyena — so it reads "gets what the man got who took
  in the hyena". The rule is the soap-bubbles annotation rule: a gloss
  only where a modern reader genuinely cannot follow, and worked into
  the line rather than bracketed. Umamah and Atikah (011) get no gloss,
  because there the speaker is interrupted before he can explain and
  the interruption IS the point.

  SAJ' KEPT WHERE IT RHYMES IN THE SOURCE: the King's message to the
  cook (011) — "I have not stored you up, O treasure of mine, except
  against a hard time of mine" — keeps the rhyme because the rhyme is
  the reason the sentence exists.

  RACE, second and third applications of the file 000 rule. In 006 the
  wife is found with "a black slave"; the fact stays, the sex is
  matter-of-fact, no ornament is added. In 012 the guardian who bursts
  through the wall is "like a huge rock or a remnant of the tribe Ad" —
  that comparison is about SIZE and is kept in full; "blackamoor"
  becomes "the huge Black man" on its second mention.

  KEPT AS PRINTED, all comic and all in the Arabic's own register: the
  fisherman wetting himself when the ifrit kicks the jar into the sea
  (011); "not knowing Sunday from Thursday"; "His staff broke at the
  first blow!" (012).

FILE 013 — the Ensorcelled Prince. THE HARDEST RACE PASSAGE IN THE BOOK
SO FAR, and it splits three ways. Recorded in full because the same
three-way split will recur.

  1. WHAT IS THE TEXT'S, AND STANDS UNTOUCHED (Verne rule):
     - The queen's lover is a Black enslaved man, and the tale's whole
       horror is that a queen abases herself to a man living in a mud hut
       on cane trash, eating grilled rat bones and beer dregs. The
       squalor, the leprosy, the paralysis, the rags: all kept.
     - HIS SPEECH ASSERTING BLACK MANHOOD OVER WHITE — "by the courage
       and the honour of Black men, and do not imagine our manhood is
       the poor manhood of white men... Vilest of the vile whites!" This
       is in the Arabic and it is not a one-way slur: it is the enslaved
       man asserting superiority over the class that holds him. Kept in
       full, unsoftened. Cutting it would flatten the passage into
       something simpler and less true than what is there.
     - The husband's own rage against her, obscenity and all, stays in
       his mouth; only the slur-noun is replaced ("every slave you hire
       to have at you").
     - Her endearment names his blackness affectionately ("My darling!
       My black one!") and that is kept, because the contrast between
       her tenderness to him and her hatred of her husband is the scene.

  2. WHAT IS BURTON'S ADDITION, AND GOES — THE MIMICRY SCENE. When the
     King kills the slave and impersonates him to fool the sorceress,
     Burton writes the impersonation as MINSTREL EYE-DIALECT: "'lack!
     'lack! there be no Ma'esty and there be no Might save in Allauh,
     the Gloriose, the Great", "O my cuss", "hubby", "evenin' till
     mawnin'". That is blackface, and it is Burton's invention.
     THE MECHANISM SURVIVES WITHOUT IT, and the text supplies the means
     itself: it says he "lowered his voice and twisted his tongue". So
     he "spoke thickly and indistinctly, the way a man speaks who is
     very weak and whose throat has been cut" — which is not only not a
     minstrel turn, it is BETTER MOTIVATED than Burton's version, since
     the man she is expecting to hear has had his throat cut and has not
     spoken in three years. She is fooled for a reason.
     "O my cuss" is Burton's eye-dialect for "O my curse", and is kept
     as "my curse" — a bitter joke, and the King's, not the slave's.

  3. THE GROTESQUE LIP DESCRIPTION is kept as the size image it is in
     the Arabic ("upper lip like the lid of a pot and lower lip like the
     pot itself"), set down plainly and not played for caricature.

  THE FOUR FAITHS AND THE FOUR COLOURS are the tale's machinery and are
  exact: Muslims white, Magians red, Christians blue, Jews yellow.

  KEPT: Mount Kaf, bhang, Mustafa and Murtaza (Muhammad and Ali), the
  hair shirt, "the kings of Persia" for Burton's "Kisra-kings".

FILE 014 — the Porter and the Three Ladies. The erotic-comic centre.

  THE THREE LADIES ARE NAMED BY ROLE and the naming is now locked,
  because Burton drifts across four words for one of them (cateress,
  provisioner, procuratrix, provisioneress):
    the caterer      — the one who does the shopping, hires the porter
    the doorkeeper   — the one who opens the gate (Burton: portress)
    the eldest lady  — the mistress of the house, on the couch
  In file 021 she is titled "Tale of the Portress" in the manifest; the
  modern heading there will be "The Doorkeeper's Tale".

  THE NAMING GAME is the most explicit passage in the volume and is
  translated in full, with nothing cut and nothing sanitised. The comedy
  is mechanical: he offers a blunt word, gets slapped, escalates; the
  answer they want is a lyrical kenning. Both kennings are Burton's
  literal renderings of the Arabic and both are kept exactly — "the
  basil of the bridges" and "the husked sesame seed" — because they ARE
  the joke. The porter's four guesses run slit / womb / cunny /
  clitoris: blunt, escalating, and stopping short of the one word that
  would read as pornography rather than as farce. Register is farce
  throughout — the slaps, his aching neck, his surrender to "the basil
  of the bridges" — never titillation.

  GLOSS SPENT: houris, "the maidens of Heaven".

  KEPT WHOLE: the shopping catalogue, every fruit, flower, sweet and
  scent of it. It is a page of pure list and it is one of the best
  things in the book — the Baghdad market inventoried by a woman with
  money. Do not compress a catalogue.

  "straight as the letter I" is Burton rendering the Arabic letter alif,
  a single upright stroke. Rendered as the image ("straight as a single
  upright stroke of the pen") rather than as the letter, which means
  nothing in English.

FILES 015-016 — the Porter, parts two and three

  GLOSS SPENT: kalandar — "three Persian kalandars, wandering holy
  beggars under a vow". Placed at their first appearance (015).

  THE DOOR INSCRIPTION IS THE ALL-CAPS TRAP, and it is set in capitals
  in the source: "WHOSO SPEAKETH OF WHAT CONCERNETH HIM NOT, SHALL HEAR
  WHAT PLEASETH HIM NOT!" Written out in sentence case. assemble.py
  reads an all-caps line as a section heading, so reproducing the
  source's capitals would have put a spurious <h2> in the middle of the
  scene. WATCH FOR THIS ON EVERY INSCRIPTION, LETTER AND PLACARD — the
  book has several more.

  LOCKED FORMULA: "rub your head and be on your way" — the lady's
  formula of pardon, and it recurs after each of the five tales told in
  this house.

  THE THIRD KENNING: "the Inn of Abu Mansur" (Burton: Khan), completing
  the set with the basil of the bridges and the husked sesame. All three
  are needed for the porter's punchline, which strings them together.

  NORMALISED: Burton's "Prince of the Faithful" -> "Commander of the
  Faithful", which is the locked form and the one in must_contain.

  "Hammal" is simply the Arabic for porter and is not kept as a name.

FILE 017 — the First Kalandar's Tale

  SOURCE INCONSISTENCY RECONCILED. The kalandar is blinded in his LEFT
  eye by the usurping vizier ("he thrust his finger into my left eye"),
  and all three kalandars are established in 015 as blind in the LEFT
  eye — but he closes the tale saying "the manner of my losing my right
  eye". Corrected to left. This is the quixote rule: reconcile a slip
  that a reader could only read as OURS, and log it. Nothing else in
  the tale moves.

  INCEST, and the Verne rule holds. The cousin and his own sister wall
  themselves into an underground room and are burnt black by fire from
  heaven; the father spits in the corpse's face and beats it with his
  slipper, and says the judgement to come will be worse. Translated in
  full, at the text's own pitch, with no softening and no note. The
  narrator's protest at his uncle's hardness is what the text supplies
  in place of a comment, and it is enough.

  "Prince of the Faithful" normalised to "Commander of the Faithful"
  again — Burton alternates freely and it is locked.

FILES 018-020 — the Second Kalandar's Tale and the Envier/Envied

  NAME RECONCILED: the ifrit is "Jirjis son of Rajmus" at his first
  naming (018) and "Jirjaris" when the princess names him (020). One
  spelling, Jirjis. Burton's variant is a transliteration wobble, not a
  second character.

  PLACE LOCKED: the Ebony Islands (Burton: "the Islands of Abnus" —
  abnus IS ebony). Distinct from the Black Islands of file 013, which is
  a different kingdom, so both names are needed and neither can drift.

  THE SIX CALLIGRAPHIC HANDS ARE KEPT BY NAME — Rayhani, Thuluth (Burton
  spells it Suls), Naskh, Tumar, Muhakkak, plus the ordinary letter
  hand. The whole joke of the scene is an ape demonstrating six distinct
  scripts, so collapsing them into "he wrote beautifully" would destroy
  it. Each gets the one clause of description Burton gives it and no
  more.

  THE FOOD POEM (019) is a comic tour de force and every dish in it
  stays — partridges, fries, marinated stews, sand grouse, omelette,
  browned fowls, the two fish on new-made scones, vermicelli, eggs with
  hash and fritters, pot-herbs in oil and vinegar, meat pudding, sweets
  from brocaded trays. Burton even drops into French for the fish. It is
  a menu played as an elegy and the humour is entirely in the
  completeness.

  THE SHAPE-SHIFTING DUEL (020) is the book's best set-piece and the
  chain must stay exact and in order: lion -> scorpion / serpent ->
  vulture / eagle -> tomcat / piebald wolf -> worm in the pomegranate ->
  scattered seeds -> white cock -> the one missed seed -> fish -> fire.
  The princess dies because she overlooked a single seed. Every link is
  load-bearing.

  RELIGIOUS FRAMING KEPT WHOLE (Verne rule): the princess burns the
  ifrit only after urging him to embrace Islam, dies making the
  profession of faith, and the King is converted afterwards. The
  victory-cry naming those who do not believe is kept as printed.

FILE 021 — the Third Kalandar, part one; the Magnet Mountain

  NAME RECONCILED: the narrator is "Ajib son of Kazib" in his opening
  sentence and "Ajib bin Khazib" / "son of Khazib" everywhere after,
  including in the prophecy that names him. One spelling: KHAZIB.

  PLACE LOCKED: the Magnet Mountain. Burton calls the same peak the
  Magnet Mountain, the Loadstone Mountain and the Mountain Magnet within
  three paragraphs. It is named four more times in this tale and in the
  prophecy that drives the plot, so it cannot be allowed to drift.

  "laton" (latten) is rendered "brass" throughout, which is what Burton
  himself calls it half the time — the horseman, the horse, the lance
  and the dome are all the one metal and the reader needs to see that.

  A SOURCE TYPO: "I lighted a large wax candle and trimmed the ramps and
  the three lanterns" — ramps for lamps. Rendered as lamps.

FILES 022-023 — the Third Kalandar concluded; the frame resolves

  THE PROVERB ECHO, AND A DRIFT CAUGHT LATE. The saying "I was sitting
  at my ease, and nothing but my own meddling brought me unease" occurs
  FIVE times across the book so far, in four different mouths: the
  vizier warning Shahrazad (001), the Ass regretting his advice (002),
  the ten one-eyed young men chanting it over their trays of soot (022,
  twice) and Ajib repeating it as he is driven out (023). Burton words
  it differently nearly every time, and I had followed him for the first
  two before locking the wording — so 001 and 002 read "I was lying
  comfortably; nothing but my own meddling made me uncomfortable" and
  the later three did not match. All five now use the one form.
  IT IS THE BOOK'S MORAL IN ONE LINE and the whole point is that the
  reader hears it come back. A ledger entry is not enough on its own:
  grep the finished files for the locked phrase after every batch.

  KEPT AS PRINTED: the Abbasid genealogy Ja'afar recites when he reveals
  the Caliph — Harun al-Rashid, brother of Musa al-Hadi, son of
  al-Mansur, son of Mohammed the brother of al-Saffah son of Mohammed.
  It is a formal pedigree announced in a throne room and its whole
  effect is the weight of the list.

  A NICE INTERNAL ECHO WORTH NOT FLATTENING: the winged black horse in
  the forbidden fortieth room feeds from a crystal manger of HUSKED
  SESAME — the same phrase the three ladies used as their kenning in
  file 014. Burton did not invent it and I have kept it identical in
  both places.

FILES 024-025 — the Eldest Lady and the Doorkeeper; the cycle closes

  THE BAGHDAD CYCLE IS NOW COMPLETE (files 000-025, 75,130 words). Every
  question the frame opened has been answered: the two beaten bitches
  are the eldest lady's sisters, transformed by a grateful jinniyah; the
  whip scars belong to the doorkeeper, beaten by the Caliph's own son
  Al-Amin for breaking an oath. Harun marries the caterer, marries the
  three sisters to the three kalandars, and has the whole thing written
  into the archives — which is the Nights' own joke about itself.

  TITLE: the manifest keeps Burton's "Tale of the Portress"; the modern
  heading is "The Doorkeeper's Tale", following the role-names locked in
  file 014.

  PLACE NORMALISED: the jinniyah promises to come "even if I were beyond
  Mount Kaf". Burton writes "beyond Caucasus-mountain" here and "Mount

FILE 026 — the Hunchback's Tale. THE COMIC CYCLE OPENS.

  REGISTER: deadpan. Four men in succession shove past the crowd to
  confess to a murder none of them committed, and the executioner's line
  — "I am tired of this slow work. We bring out this one and change him
  for that one, and nobody gets hanged after all" — only lands if the
  whole thing is played perfectly straight. Do not signal the joke.

  BURTON'S CHAUCERIAN COSTUME, and it goes. He dresses the four
  tale-tellers as a Canterbury company: the Tailor, the Jewish Doctor,
  the Nazarene Broker and THE REEVE. "Reeve" is an English manorial
  officer and means nothing here; the Arabic is the controller of the
  Sultan's kitchen. He is THE STEWARD throughout, and file 029's heading
  is "The Steward's Tale" (the manifest keeps Burton's word).
  Also gone: "Gobbo" (Burton's Italian nickname for the hunchback) and
  "Charley", which is VICTORIAN LONDON SLANG for a night watchman and
  has no business within a thousand miles of this book.

  KEPT WHOLE, and it is the Verne rule: the Jewish physician's panic in
  his own idiom — "O Ezra! O Moses! O Aaron! O Joshua son of Nun! O the
  Ten Commandments! ... O by the hooves of the ass of Ezra!" The comedy
  of this cycle is that a Muslim, a Jew and a Christian are each made
  ridiculous in exactly the same measure and each behaves exactly as
  well as the others, and softening any one of the three breaks the
  symmetry that is the whole point. Esdras -> Ezra, which is the settled
  English form of the name.

  Also kept: the steward's prayer to "Thou the Veiler" (ya Sattar, a
  real invocation), and the Christian broker's "O Messiah! O Virgin!"

FILES 027-028 — the Christian Broker's Story

  HEADING: "The Christian Broker's Story" (the manifest keeps Burton's
  "Nazarene"). Note this is a nested tale told BY the broker ABOUT a
  young Baghdadi merchant, so the young man's own long narration is
  primary narration inside it, per the Ovid convention — no enclosing
  quotes around the whole of it.

  A SLUR WITH NO CONTENT BEHIND IT, and therefore replaced. The young
  man, abasing himself before the lady, says in Burton "I am thy slave,
  thy negro slave". This is NOT the file-000 case: he is a Baghdadi
  merchant and nothing in the tale turns on anyone's colour. It is the
  common Arabic formula of humility ("your black slave" = your utter
  servant), and the word carries no fact a reader needs. Rendered "I am
  your slave, your bondsman" — the abasement kept entire, the word gone.
  CONTRAST file 013, where the same vocabulary DOES carry fact and
  therefore stays. The test is always: does the reader lose information?

  CAIRO'S TOPOGRAPHY IS ALL REAL AND ALL KEPT: the Khan al-Jawali, the
  Gate of Victory, the Khan al-Masrur, Bayn al-Kasrayn (glossed on the
  spot, as Burton does, "Between the Two Palaces"), the Exchange of
  Jaharkas, the Zuwaylah Gate, the Habbaniyah quarter, Darb al-Munkari.
  This tale is a street map and the density of it is the pleasure.

  ardabb glossed once — "the great Egyptian measure for grain" — because
  the arithmetic depends on it (fifty ardabbs at a hundred dirhams).
  Shroff -> money-changer, which Burton glosses himself.

FILE 029 — the Steward's Tale (the cumin ragout)

  "O black of face!" is the bride's insult to her husband when she comes
  back to maim him. In Arabic "blackened of face" means DISGRACED — it is
  the standard idiom for shame and has nothing to do with anyone's
  colour. Rendered "You disgrace!" A literal English rendering would
  hand the reader a racial reading the Arabic does not contain, which is
  the opposite of faithfulness. Same test as file 028: the reader loses
  no fact, and would gain a false one.

  Note the tale's arithmetic, which the numeral check now guards: five
  thousand dirhams paid, a thousand dinars taken, ten thousand dinars
  the value the eunuch is threatened with, ten thousand for the wedding
  portion, fifty thousand for the house, a hundred and twenty washings
  (forty soap, forty potash, forty galangal). The washings are the
  tale's punchline and are counted twice.

  KEPT: the bride cuts off his thumbs and both great toes with a razor
  for eating with an unwashed hand, and the comedy is that the
  punishment is monstrous and she is entirely serious. Played straight.

FILE 031 — the Tailor's Tale; the Barber arrives

  THE SEVEN BROTHERS' NAMES ARE THE JOKE and all seven are kept with
  their glosses, because the whole cycle turns on them: Al-Bakbuk the
  Prattler, Al-Haddar the Babbler, Al-Fakik the Gabbler, Al-Kuz
  al-Aswani the Long-Necked Jug, Al-Nashshar the Tattler, Shakashik or
  Many Clamours, and Al-Samit the Silent Man — who is the barber, and
  who never stops talking. Six of the seven names mean "chatterbox" and
  the seventh means "silent". Do not translate the names away.
  SLIP RECONCILED: Burton glosses Al-Nashshar as "the tattler and tale
  teller" here and as "the Babbler" in file 038, which is already
  Al-Haddar's gloss. One gloss each: Al-Nashshar is THE TATTLER.

  MORE BURTON COSTUME OFF: "argal" (his comic Hamlet corruption of
  ergo), "tota re scibili" (a Latin tag in an Arab barber's boast),
  "fou and wood" (Scots for drunk and mad). None of it is in the
  Arabic and all of it breaks the voice.

  "yon black o'face" — the young man's insult to the barber, and the
  SAME idiom as file 029's "O black of face". Disgraced, not dark.
  Rendered "that disgrace".

  EVERY NUMBER IN THE BARBER'S ASTROLOGY IS KEPT EXACTLY and check.py
  now guards them: the tenth of Safar, the six hundred and fifty-third
  year of the Hegira, the seven thousand three hundred and twentieth
  year of the era of Alexander, eight degrees and six minutes, three
  full hours to prayer. The comedy is the PRECISION — a man computing
  the heavens to six decimal places while his customer sits there with
  half a shaved head. Round any of it off and the joke goes.
  Same for the hundred and three dinars, whose itemised breakdown
  (one for the observation, one for the conversation, one for the
  bloodletting, a hundred for the poem) is the punchline of the
  anecdote inside the anecdote.

FILES 033-039 — the Barber and his six brothers. THE ROLL CALL HAD TO BE
CHECKED AGAINST THE TALES, and it did not match.

  In file 033 the barber lists his brothers' injuries: "One is a
  monocular, another palsied, a third stone blind, a fourth cropped of
  ears AND NOSE and a fifth shorn of both lips, while THE SIXTH is a
  hunchback and a cripple." Read against the six tales themselves:

    1  Al-Bakbuk    the Prattler     HUNCHBACK, and lamed at the end
    2  Al-Haddar    the Babbler      PARALYTIC
    3  Al-Fakik     the Gabbler      BLIND
    4  Al-Kuz       Long-Necked Jug  BLIND OF ONE EYE
    5  Al-Nashshar  the Tattler      CROPPED OF BOTH EARS
    6  Shakashik    Many Clamours    SHORN OF BOTH LIPS

  So all six INJURIES in the list are right, but the ordinal is wrong —
  the hunchback is the FIRST brother, not the sixth — and no brother
  anywhere loses his nose. Rendered as the unordered catalogue it
  actually is ("One of them is one-eyed; one is palsied; one is stone
  blind; one is cropped of both his ears; one is shorn of both his lips;
  and one is a hunchback and a cripple"), and the nose dropped.
  This is the quixote rule again: a reader who reaches the first brother
  and finds a hunchback where the sixth was promised can only read it as
  OUR error. Nothing else in the passage moves.

  NOTE the second slip in the same family, already logged from file 031:
  file 038 opens by calling Al-Nashshar "the Babbler", which is
  Al-Haddar's gloss. He is THE TATTLER throughout.

FILES 034-039 — the six brothers. The densest run of farce in the book.

  BARMECIDE, not "Barmaki". Burton uses both within one paragraph of
  file 039. The English form is locked because THE BARMECIDE FEAST — a
  banquet of empty dishes served with elaborate commentary — passed into
  English as an idiom out of this very tale, and it is the one thing a
  reader may already know before opening the book. "Invisible soap in
  imperceptible water" is Burton at his best and is kept exactly.

  Al-Nashshar is THE TATTLER wherever he appears (file 038 opens by
  calling him "the Babbler", which belongs to Al-Haddar). Reconciled.

  MORE BURTON SLANG OUT: "sham-abraham" (036) is British cant for a
  malingerer and is rendered "the impostor"; "verroterie" (038) is his
  French for glassware.

  THE FIFTH BROTHER'S DAYDREAM (038) is the volume's most famous set
  piece after the Barmecide feast, and its whole architecture is
  ARITHMETIC: 100 dirhams to 200 to 400 to 4,000 to a hundred thousand,
  then a thousand dinars of settlement and a thousand more as a gift,
  then five hundred for the dressing-women. Every figure is kept, and
  the check guards them. Burton lets the unit slip from dirhams to
  dinars mid-daydream; reconciled to dirhams, since a reader can only
  read the switch as ours.

  THE MUTILATIONS ARE ALL AT FULL LENGTH and none is dwelt on: the ears
  docked by thieves, the lips cut off by the Bedouin, the castration
  that follows. This is farce with real blood in it, which is the
  register of the whole cycle, and softening the injuries would leave
  the barber's cheerful summary — that he supports all six of them and
  is the upright one of the family — with nothing to be cheerful over.

FILES 044-045 — Sindbad's third and fourth voyages

  A FOURTH APPLICATION OF THE RACE TEST, and it comes out the same way
  as 028 and 029. In the fourth voyage the pepper-gatherers ask how
  Sindbad escaped "these blacks who swarm in the island". The islanders
  are cannibals, and their CANNIBALISM is the fact the tale turns on;
  their colour carries nothing and is not mentioned when they are
  described (Burton introduces them as "naked men" and calls them "the
  savages" and "the naked folk" everywhere else). Rendered "these
  savages", which is his own majority usage. No fact is lost.
  CONTRAST the third voyage's ogre, who is described as "black in
  colour, tall and big of bulk as a great date palm, with eyes like
  coals of fire" — that is a monster's description, it is kept entire,
  and only Burton's noun "blackamoor" is replaced.

  THE FOURTH VOYAGE IS THE STRONGEST OF THE SEVEN and needs no help:
  Sindbad refuses the cannibals' food and so keeps his mind while his
  companions are fattened like cattle; escapes; invents the SADDLE for a
  people who ride bareback and grows rich on it; is married into the
  royal household as an honour; and then learns that when a wife dies
  her husband is buried alive with her. The saddle-making is described
  step by step — saddle tree, felt, leather, girth, stirrup leathers,
  stirrups, bit, silk fringes — and the detail is the point: it is the
  one voyage where he prospers by MAKING something.

FILE 046 — the burial cavern. THE DARKEST PASSAGE IN SINDBAD.

  Sindbad escapes the pit by beating to death, with a dead man's
  leg-bone, every living person who is subsequently lowered into it —
  "men and women alike" — and robbing the corpses of their grave
  jewellery. He then sells the jewellery, comes home rich, and gives
  alms to the widow and the orphan.
  THE VERNE RULE, and it is the whole point: this is rendered in full,
  flatly, with no softening, no flinch and no editorial comment. The
  text offers it as survival and does not moralise, and the horror is
  left exactly where the Arabic leaves it — standing, unremarked, in the
  middle of an adventure story that goes cheerfully on. A modern reader
  will notice. That noticing is the reading experience and must not be
  done for them.

FILES 055-067 — ALADDIN. Thirteen parts.

  AN EDITOR'S NOTE OPENS THE TALE — the volume's third, and the reason
  is structural. Aladdin's night numbering RESTARTS AT 515, after the
  City of Brass ended at 578, because it comes from Burton's
  Supplemental volume and a different manuscript tradition. That
  collision is an artefact of OUR selection order, not of Burton, so it
  is ours to explain. The note gives the fact that explains it and is
  the best fact about the Nights anyway: Aladdin and Ali Baba are the

  either; Galland took them down in Paris in 1709 from Hanna Diyab, a

  afterwards. A fact about the book's construction, like the Calcutta
  note in 050, and not a claim of the author's.

  error. check.py already holds "O King of the Age" as a FAILING variant
  of the resumption lock, so the harmonisation is enforced, not merely
  intended.

  keeping because the volume answers this question two different ways.

  Burton's buyer is "a man which was a Jew, a viler than the Satans",
  thereafter "the accursed Jew" — twelve times — and the honest goldsmith
  who exposes him says that "the Jews ever hold lawful to them the good
  of Moslems... and always defraud them".
  RULING: KEEP THE CHARACTER, CUT THE LIBEL. He remains a Jewish dealer
  and remains dishonest — a Jewish merchant in a Muslim market is real
  social history, and his cheating is the plot that brings the honest
  goldsmith on. What goes is the narrator's slur-epithets, which carry no
  information beyond contempt, and the goldsmith's GENERALISATION, which
  becomes a statement about this dealer. Not one plot point moves.
  WHY THIS DIFFERS FROM FILE 026, where the Jewish physician is kept
  ENTIRE, panic-idiom and all: there a Muslim, a Jew and a Christian are
  made ridiculous in precisely equal measure, and the symmetry IS the
  joke, so softening any one of the three breaks it. Here there is no
  symmetry — one Jewish character, a cheat, and a group libel the story
  never answers. The test that separates them is the one this book has
  used throughout: does the reader lose information, or gain a false one?
