TURGOT, "RÉFLEXIONS SUR LA FORMATION ET LA DISTRIBUTION DES RICHESSES"
-- STANDING BRIEF. Read this whole file before translating.

WHAT THIS WORK IS
A scholarly modernization of a canonical text in the history of economics.
Anne-Robert-Jacques Turgot (1727-1781), then royal administrator of the
Limousin and later France's finance minister, wrote these 100 numbered
reflections in 1766 for two Chinese students returning home from France.
In a few dozen pages they explain how exchange, money, capital, interest
and the classes of society arise, ten years before Adam Smith's Wealth of
Nations. Source: Daire's 1844 edition (French Wikisource). The editors'
notes are already removed. Do NOT use or imitate the old English version
(Ashley, 1898). You translate from the French.

THE VOICE
Clear, calm, step-by-step reasoning from simple cases (a man with land and
no other) to a whole economy. Short numbered sections, each building on
the last. Make every step easy to follow for a bright fourteen-year-old:
plain modern words, short sentences, the logic visible. Keep his examples
(the farmer, the artisan, the landowner, the merchant, grain, cloth,
silver). Keep his theory as his (the physiocrats' view that land alone
yields a surplus): no corrections, no notes.

THE STANDARD
Faithful but not literal: every sentence, every step of the argument, in
his order. Complete: never summarise, never skip, never merge sections.
English runs about the same length as the French.
