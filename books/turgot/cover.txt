Claude-Joseph Vernet's *The Construction of a Road* (1774). Gentlemen on horseback in tricorn hats look over the work while labourers break and lay stones in the foreground, carts and barrows move along the cutting, and a windmill and a walled hill town stand above. Vernet painted it in the year Turgot became controller-general. Roads in France were then built largely by the corvée, the unpaid labour peasants owed the crown, and Turgot's edict of 1776 abolished it.
