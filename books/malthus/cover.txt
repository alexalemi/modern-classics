Pieter Bruegel the Elder's *The Harvesters* (1565). Half a field of wheat is cut and stacked in sheaves; one man walks out through the standing grain with a jug in his hand, another sleeps sprawled under a pear tree, and beside him the reapers sit down to bread and drink. Past the field lie a village, orchards and a wide bay. Malthus's whole argument turns on the harvest, and on how many mouths one field can feed.
