Proposition III.19 (Theorem)

If *a straight line ⟦g:039b⟧ be a tangent to a circle, the straight line ⟦g:0157⟧, drawn perpendicular to it from point of the contact, passes through the centre of the circle.*

For, if it be possible, let the centre be without ⟦g:0157⟧, and draw ⟦g:039c⟧ from the supposed centre to the point of contact.

∵ ⟦g:039c⟧ ⊥ ⟦g:039b⟧ (prop. III.18)

∴ ⟦g:039d⟧ = ⟦g:02e2⟧ , a right angle;

but ⟦g:039e⟧ = ⟦g:02e2⟧ (hyp.),

and ∴ ⟦g:039d⟧ = ⟦g:039e⟧ , a part equal to the whole, which is absurd.

Therefore the assumed point is not the centre; and in the same manner it can be demonstrated, that no other point without ⟦g:0157⟧ is the centre.
