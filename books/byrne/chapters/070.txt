Proposition III.1 (Problem)

[Figure adx]

To *find the centre of a given circle ⟦g:02d9⟧.*

Draw within the circle any straight line ⟦g:02da⟧, make ⟦g:004f⟧ = ⟦g:01dc⟧ , draw ⟦g:004a⟧ ⊥ ⟦g:02da⟧ ; bisect ⟦g:004a⟧, and the point of bisection is the centre.

For, if it be possible, let any other point as the point of concourse of ⟦g:00c3⟧, ⟦g:02db⟧ and ⟦g:02dc⟧ be the centre.

Because in ⟦g:02dd⟧ and ⟦g:02de⟧ ⟦g:00c3⟧ = ⟦g:02dc⟧ (hyp. and def. I.15), ⟦g:004f⟧ = ⟦g:01dc⟧ (const.) and ⟦g:02db⟧ common, ⟦g:02df⟧ = ⟦g:02e0⟧ (prop. I.8), and are therefore right angles; but ⟦g:02e1⟧ = ⟦g:02e2⟧ (const.) ⟦g:02e0⟧ = ⟦g:02e1⟧ (ax. I.11) which is absurd; therefore the assumed point is not the centre of the circle; and in the same manner it can be proved that no other point which is not on ⟦g:004a⟧ is the centre, therefore the centre is in ⟦g:004a⟧, and therefore the point where ⟦g:004a⟧ is bisected is the centre.

Q. E. D.
