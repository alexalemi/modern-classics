Proposition III.26 (Theorem)

[Figure afe]

In *equal circles ⟦g:0341⟧ and ⟦g:03da⟧, the arcs ⟦g:03db⟧, ⟦g:03dc⟧ on which stand equal angles, whether at the centre or circumference, are equal.*

For, let ⟦g:03dd⟧ = ⟦g:03de⟧ at the centre,

draw ⟦g:03df⟧ and ⟦g:03e0⟧.

Then since ⟦g:0341⟧ = ⟦g:03da⟧ ,

⟦g:03e1⟧ and ⟦g:03e2⟧ have

⟦g:00c3⟧ = ⟦g:0046⟧ = ⟦g:03e3⟧ = ⟦g:03e4⟧ ,

and ⟦g:03dd⟧ = ⟦g:03de⟧ ,

∴ ⟦g:03df⟧ = ⟦g:03e0⟧ (prop. I.4).

But ⟦g:03e5⟧ = ⟦g:03e6⟧ (prop. III.20);

∴ ⟦g:03e7⟧ and ⟦g:03e8⟧ are similar (def. III.11);

they are also equal (prop. III.24)

If therefore the equal segments be taken from the equal circles, the remaining segments will be equal;

hence ⟦g:03e9⟧ = ⟦g:03ea⟧ (ax. I.3);

and ∴ ⟦g:03db⟧ = ⟦g:03dc⟧ .

But if the given equal angles be at the circumference, it is evident that the angles at the centre, being double of those at the circumference, are also equal, and therefore the arcs on which they stand are equal.

Q. E. D.
