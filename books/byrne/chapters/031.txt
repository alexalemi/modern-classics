Proposition I.26 (Theorem)

If *two triangles have two angles of the one respectively equal to two angles of the other (* ⟦g:0133⟧ = ⟦g:0134⟧ *and* ⟦g:0135⟧ = ⟦g:0136⟧ *), and a side of the one equal to a side of the other similarly placed with respect to the equal angles, the remaining sides and angles are respectively equal to one another.*

[Figure aca]

 Case I.

Let ⟦g:0138⟧ and ⟦g:0139⟧ which lie between the equal angles be equal,

then ⟦g:00c4⟧ = ⟦g:013a⟧ .

For if it be possible, let one of them ⟦g:013a⟧ be greater than the other;

make ⟦g:00c4⟧ = ⟦g:013b⟧ , draw ⟦g:013c⟧.

In ⟦g:013d⟧ and ⟦g:013e⟧ we have

⟦g:00c4⟧ = ⟦g:013b⟧ , ⟦g:0133⟧ = ⟦g:0134⟧ , ⟦g:0138⟧ = ⟦g:0139⟧ ;

∴ ⟦g:0135⟧ = ⟦g:013f⟧ (prop. I.4)

but ⟦g:0135⟧ = ⟦g:0136⟧ (hyp.)

and therefore ⟦g:013f⟧ = ⟦g:0136⟧ , which is absurd;

hence neither of the sides ⟦g:00c4⟧ and ⟦g:013a⟧ is greater than the other;

and ∴ they are equal;

∴ ⟦g:0140⟧ = ⟦g:0141⟧ , and ⟦g:0142⟧ = ⟦g:0143⟧ , (prop. I.4).

 Case II.

Again, let ⟦g:0098⟧ = ⟦g:0116⟧ , which lie opposite the equal angles ⟦g:0145⟧ and ⟦g:0146⟧.

If it be possible, let ⟦g:0147⟧ > ⟦g:0138⟧ , then take ⟦g:0148⟧ = ⟦g:0138⟧ , draw ⟦g:0149⟧.

Then in ⟦g:014a⟧ and ⟦g:014b⟧ we have ⟦g:0098⟧ = ⟦g:0116⟧ , ⟦g:0138⟧ = ⟦g:0148⟧ and ⟦g:014c⟧ = ⟦g:014d⟧ ,

∴ ⟦g:0145⟧ = ⟦g:014e⟧ (prop. I.4)

but ⟦g:0145⟧ = ⟦g:0146⟧ (hyp.)

∴ ⟦g:014e⟧ = ⟦g:0146⟧ which is absurd (prop. I.16).

Consequently, neither of the sides ⟦g:0138⟧ or ⟦g:0147⟧ is greater than the other, hence they must be equal. It follows (by (prop. I.4)) that the triangles are equal in all respects.

Q. E. D.
