Proposition III.11 (Theorem)

[Figure aej]

If *two circles ⟦g:0346⟧ and ⟦g:0347⟧ touch one another internally, the right line joining their centres, being produced, shall pass through a point of contact.*

For, if it be possible, let ⟦g:0348⟧ join their centres, and produce it both ways; from a point of contact draw ⟦g:0349⟧ to the centre of ⟦g:0346⟧, and from the same point of contact draw ⟦g:034a⟧ to the centre of ⟦g:0347⟧.

Because in ⟦g:034b⟧; ⟦g:0348⟧ + ⟦g:0349⟧ > ⟦g:034a⟧ (prop. I.20),

and ⟦g:034a⟧ = ⟦g:034c⟧ as they are radii of ⟦g:0347⟧,

but ⟦g:0348⟧ + ⟦g:0349⟧ > ⟦g:034c⟧ ;

take away ⟦g:0348⟧ which is common,

and ⟦g:0349⟧ > ⟦g:034d⟧ ;

but ⟦g:0349⟧ = ⟦g:034e⟧ ,

because they are radii of ⟦g:0346⟧,

and ∴ ⟦g:034e⟧ > ⟦g:034d⟧ a part greater than the whole, which is absurd.

The centres are not therefore so placed, that a line joining them can pass through any point but a point of contact.

Q. E. D.
