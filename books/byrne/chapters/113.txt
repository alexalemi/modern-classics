Proposition IV.6 (Problem)

[Figure agg]

In *a given circle ⟦g:0497⟧ to inscribe a square.*

Draw the two diameters of the circle ⊥ to each other, and draw ⟦g:01d9⟧, ⟦g:00a3⟧, ⟦g:004f⟧ and ⟦g:0050⟧.

⟦g:0498⟧ is a square.

For, since ⟦g:0499⟧ and ⟦g:049a⟧ are, each of them, in a semicircle, they are right angles (prop. III.31),

∴ ⟦g:0050⟧ ∥ ⟦g:00a3⟧ (prop. I.28):

and in like manner ⟦g:004f⟧ ∥ ⟦g:01d9⟧ .

And ∵ ⟦g:049b⟧ = ⟦g:049c⟧ (const.),

and ⟦g:01ac⟧ = ⟦g:0087⟧ = ⟦g:00f4⟧ (def. I.15).

∴ ⟦g:00a3⟧ = ⟦g:004f⟧ (prop. I.4);

and since the adjacent sides and angles of the parallelogram ⟦g:0498⟧ are equal, they are all equal (prop. I.34); and ∴ ⟦g:0498⟧, inscribed in the given circle, is a square.

Q. E. D.
