Proposition VI.6 (Theorem)

[Figure agy: The diagram for Proposition VI.6, in Byrne’s colours.]

If *two triangles (⟦g:05a7⟧ and ⟦g:05a8⟧) have one angle (⟦g:05a9⟧) of the one, equal to one angle (⟦g:05aa⟧) of the other, and the sides about the equal angles proportional, the triangles shall be equiangular, and have those angles equal which the homologous sides subtend.*

From the extremities of ⟦g:00c3⟧, one of the sides of ⟦g:05a8⟧, about ⟦g:05aa⟧,

draw ⟦g:00bb⟧ and ⟦g:05ab⟧,

making ⟦g:05ac⟧ = ⟦g:05a9⟧ ,

and ⟦g:05ad⟧ = ⟦g:00e0⟧ ;

then ⟦g:05ae⟧ = ⟦g:05af⟧ (prop. I.32),

and two triangles being equiangular,

⟦g:019e⟧ : ⟦g:03e3⟧ :: ⟦g:05ab⟧ : ⟦g:00c3⟧ (prop. VI.4);

but ⟦g:019e⟧ : ⟦g:03e3⟧ :: ⟦g:03a6⟧ : ⟦g:00c3⟧ (hyp.)

∴ ⟦g:05ab⟧ : ⟦g:00c3⟧ :: ⟦g:03a6⟧ : ⟦g:00c3⟧ (prop. V.11),

and consequently ⟦g:05ab⟧ = ⟦g:03a6⟧ (prop. V.9);

∴ ⟦g:05a8⟧ = ⟦g:05b0⟧ in every respect (prop. I.4).

But ⟦g:05ad⟧ = ⟦g:00e0⟧ (const.),

and ∴ ⟦g:05b1⟧ = ⟦g:00e0⟧ ;

and since also ⟦g:05aa⟧ = ⟦g:05a9⟧ ,

⟦g:05b2⟧ = ⟦g:05af⟧ (prop. I.32);

and ∴ ⟦g:05a7⟧ and ⟦g:05a8⟧ are equiangular, with their equal angles opposite to homologous sides.

Q. E. D.
