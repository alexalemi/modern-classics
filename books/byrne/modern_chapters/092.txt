Proposition III.23 (Theorem)

[Figure afb: The diagram for Proposition III.23, in Byrne’s colours.]

Upon *the same straight line, and upon the same side of it, two similar segments of circles cannot be constructed, which do not coincide.*

For if it be possible, let two similar segments

⟦g:03cb⟧ and ⟦g:03cc⟧ be constructed;

draw any right line ⟦g:03cd⟧ cutting both the segments,

draw ⟦g:03ce⟧ and ⟦g:01d9⟧.

Because the segments are similar,

⟦g:03cf⟧ = ⟦g:03d0⟧ (def. III.11),

but ⟦g:03cf⟧ > ⟦g:03d0⟧ (prop. I.16)

which is absurd;

therefore no point in either of the segments falls without the other, and therefore the segments coincide.

Q. E. D.
