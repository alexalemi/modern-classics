Proposition III.13 (Theorem)

One *circle cannot touch another, either externally or internally, in more points than one*

 Figure I.

[Figure ael: The diagram for Proposition III.13, in Byrne’s colours.]

For, if it possible, let ⟦g:0355⟧ and ⟦g:0356⟧ touch one another internally in two points; draw ⟦g:0030⟧ joining their centres, and produce it until it pass through one of the points of contact (prop. III.11);

draw ⟦g:0031⟧ and ⟦g:0357⟧.

But ⟦g:02db⟧ = ⟦g:0357⟧ (def. I.15),

∴ if ⟦g:0030⟧ be added to both,

⟦g:0358⟧ = ⟦g:0030⟧ + ⟦g:0357⟧ ;

but ⟦g:0358⟧ = ⟦g:0031⟧ (def. I.15),

and ∴ ⟦g:0030⟧ + ⟦g:0357⟧ = ⟦g:0031⟧ ;

but ∴ ⟦g:0030⟧ + ⟦g:0357⟧ > ⟦g:0031⟧ (prop. I.20),

which is absurd.

 Figure II.

[Figure aem: The diagram for Proposition III.13, in Byrne’s colours.]

But if the points of contact be the extremities of the right line joining the centres, this straight line must be bisected in two different points for the two centres; because it is the diameter of both circles, which is absurd.

 Figure III.

[Figure aen: The diagram for Proposition III.13, in Byrne’s colours.]

Next, if it be possible, let ⟦g:0355⟧ and ⟦g:035b⟧ touch externally in two points; draw ⟦g:035c⟧ joining the centres of the circles, and passing through one of the points of contact, and draw ⟦g:02db⟧ and ⟦g:0161⟧.

⟦g:02db⟧ = ⟦g:0067⟧ (def. I.15);

and ⟦g:035d⟧ = ⟦g:0161⟧ (def. I.15);

∴ ⟦g:0161⟧ + ⟦g:02db⟧ = ⟦g:035c⟧ ;

but ⟦g:0161⟧ + ⟦g:02db⟧ > ⟦g:035c⟧ (prop. I.20), which is absurd.

There is therefore no case in which two circles can touch one another in two points.

Q. E. D.
