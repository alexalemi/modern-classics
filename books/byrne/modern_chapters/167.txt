Proposition VI.7 (Theorem)

[Figure agz: The diagram for Proposition VI.7, in Byrne’s colours.]

If *two triangles (⟦g:05b4⟧ and ⟦g:05b5⟧) have one angle in each equal (⟦g:05b6⟧ equal to ⟦g:05b7⟧), the sides about two other angles (* ⟦g:00f1⟧ : ⟦g:00ad⟧ :: ⟦g:05b8⟧ : ⟦g:05b9⟧ *), and each of the remaining angles (⟦g:05ba⟧ and ⟦g:05bb⟧) either less or not less than a right angle, the triangles are equiangular, and those angles are equal about which the sides are proportional.*

First let it be assumed that the angles ⟦g:05ba⟧ and ⟦g:05bb⟧ are each less than a right angle: then if it be supposed that ⟦g:05bc⟧ and ⟦g:05bd⟧ contained by the proportional sides, are not equal, let ⟦g:05bc⟧ be greater, and make ⟦g:05be⟧ = ⟦g:05bd⟧ .

∵ ⟦g:05b7⟧ = ⟦g:05b6⟧ (hyp.) and ⟦g:05be⟧ = ⟦g:05bd⟧ (const.)

∴ ⟦g:05bf⟧ = ⟦g:05bb⟧ (prop. I.32);

∴ ⟦g:00f1⟧ : ⟦g:0056⟧ :: ⟦g:05b8⟧ : ⟦g:05b9⟧ (prop. VI.4),

but ⟦g:00f1⟧ : ⟦g:00ad⟧ :: ⟦g:05b8⟧ : ⟦g:05b9⟧ (hyp.)

∴ ⟦g:00f1⟧ : ⟦g:0056⟧ :: ⟦g:00f1⟧ : ⟦g:00ad⟧ ;

∴ ⟦g:0056⟧ = ⟦g:00ad⟧ (prop. V.9),

and ∴ ⟦g:05ba⟧ = ⟦g:05c0⟧ (prop. I.5).

But ⟦g:05ba⟧ is less than a right angle (hyp.)

∴ ⟦g:05c0⟧ is less than a right angle;

and ∴ ⟦g:05bf⟧ must be greater than a right angle (prop. I.13), but it has been proven = ⟦g:05bb⟧ and therefore less than a right angle, which is absurd. ∴ ⟦g:05bc⟧ and ⟦g:05bd⟧ are not unequal;

∴ they are equal, and since ⟦g:05b7⟧ = ⟦g:05b6⟧ (hyp.)

∴ ⟦g:05ba⟧ = ⟦g:05bb⟧ (prop. I.32), and therefore the triangles are equiangular.

But if ⟦g:05ba⟧ and ⟦g:05bb⟧ be assumed to be each not less than a right angle, it may be proved as before, that the triangles are equiangular, and have the sides about equal the angles proportional (prop. VI.4).

Q. E. D.
