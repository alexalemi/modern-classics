Proposition III.12 (Theorem)

[Figure aek: The diagram for Proposition III.12, in Byrne’s colours.]

If *two circle ⟦g:0350⟧ and ⟦g:0351⟧ touch one another externally, the straight line ⟦g:0352⟧ joining their centres, passes through the point of contact.*

If it be possible, let ⟦g:0352⟧ joining the centres, and not pass through a point of contact; then from a point of contact draw ⟦g:033a⟧ and ⟦g:000d⟧ to the centres.

∵ ⟦g:033a⟧ + ⟦g:000d⟧ > ⟦g:0352⟧ (prop. I.20)

and ⟦g:0353⟧ = ⟦g:033a⟧ (def. I.15),

and ⟦g:00f0⟧ = ⟦g:000d⟧ (def. I.15),

∴ ⟦g:0353⟧ + ⟦g:00f0⟧ > ⟦g:0352⟧ ,

a part greater than the whole, which is absurd.

The centres are not therefore so placed, that the line joining them can pass through any point but the point of contact.

Q. E. D.
