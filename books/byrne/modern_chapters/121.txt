Proposition IV.14 (Problem)

[Figure ago: The diagram for Proposition IV.14, in Byrne’s colours.]

To *describe a circle about a given equilateral and equiangular pentagon.*

Bisect ⟦g:0505⟧ and ⟦g:0506⟧ by ⟦g:01ac⟧ and ⟦g:00f4⟧, and from the point of section, draw ⟦g:0507⟧, ⟦g:04ae⟧ and ⟦g:0508⟧.

⟦g:0505⟧ = ⟦g:0506⟧ ,

⟦g:0509⟧ = ⟦g:050a⟧ ,

∴ ⟦g:00f4⟧ = ⟦g:01ac⟧ (prop. I.6);

and since in ⟦g:050b⟧ and ⟦g:050c⟧,

⟦g:039b⟧ = ⟦g:00ee⟧ , and ⟦g:01ac⟧ common,

also ⟦g:050d⟧ = ⟦g:0509⟧ ;

∴ ⟦g:0508⟧ = ⟦g:00f4⟧ (prop. I.4).

In like manner it may be proved that

⟦g:04ae⟧ = ⟦g:0507⟧ = ⟦g:0508⟧ .

and therefore ⟦g:04ae⟧ = ⟦g:0508⟧ = ⟦g:01ac⟧ = ⟦g:00f4⟧ = ⟦g:0507⟧ .

Therefore if a circle be described from the point where these five lines meet, with any one of them as a radius, it will circumscribe the given pentagon.

Q. E. D.
