Proposition I.14 (Theorem)

[Figure abo: The diagram for Proposition I.14, in Byrne’s colours.]

If *two straight lines (⟦g:00c3⟧ and ⟦g:0042⟧), meeting a third straight line (⟦g:00c4⟧), at the same point, and at opposite sides of it, make with it adjacent angles (⟦g:00c5⟧ and ⟦g:00c6⟧) equal to two right angles; these straight lines lie in one continuous straight line.*

For, if possible let ⟦g:0045⟧, and not ⟦g:0042⟧,

be the continuation of ⟦g:00c3⟧,

then ⟦g:00c5⟧ + ⟦g:00c7⟧ = ⟦g:0023⟧

but by the hypothesis ⟦g:00c5⟧ + ⟦g:00c6⟧ = ⟦g:0023⟧

∴ ⟦g:00c7⟧ = ⟦g:00c6⟧ (ax. I.3); which is absurd (ax. I.9).

∴ ⟦g:0045⟧ is not the continuation of ⟦g:00c3⟧, and the like may be demonstrated of any other straight line except ⟦g:0042⟧, ∴ ⟦g:0042⟧ is the continuation of ⟦g:00c3⟧.

Q. E. D.
