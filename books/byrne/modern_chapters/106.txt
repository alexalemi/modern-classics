Proposition III.37 (Theorem)

[Figure afu: The diagram for Proposition III.37, in Byrne’s colours.]

If *from a point outside of a circle two straight lines be drawn, the one ⟦g:044f⟧ cutting the circle, the other ⟦g:0450⟧ meeting it, and if the rectangle contained by the whole cutting line ⟦g:044f⟧ and its external segment ⟦g:0451⟧ be equal to the square of the line meeting the circle, the latter ⟦g:0450⟧ is a tangent to the circle.*

Draw from the given point ⟦g:0452⟧, a tangent to the circle,

and draw from the centre ⟦g:0453⟧, ⟦g:0454⟧, and ⟦g:0455⟧,

⟦g:0452⟧ ² = ⟦g:044f⟧ × ⟦g:0451⟧ (prop. III.36)

but ⟦g:0450⟧ ² = ⟦g:044f⟧ × ⟦g:0451⟧ (hyp.),

∴ ⟦g:0450⟧ ² = ⟦g:0452⟧ ²,

and ∴ ⟦g:0450⟧ = ⟦g:0452⟧ .

Then in ⟦g:0456⟧ and ⟦g:0457⟧

⟦g:0454⟧ and ⟦g:0450⟧ = ⟦g:0455⟧ and ⟦g:0452⟧ ,

and ⟦g:0453⟧ is common,

∴ ⟦g:0458⟧ = ⟦g:0459⟧ (prop. I.8);

but ⟦g:0459⟧ = ⟦g:02e2⟧ a right angle (prop. III.18),

∴ ⟦g:0458⟧ = ⟦g:02e2⟧ a right angle,

and ∴ ⟦g:0450⟧ is a tangent to the circle (prop. III.16).
