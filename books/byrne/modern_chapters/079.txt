Proposition III.10 (Theorem)

[Figure aei: The diagram for Proposition III.10, in Byrne’s colours.]

One *circle ⟦g:0341⟧ cannot intersect another ⟦g:0342⟧ in more points than two.*

For, if it be possible, let it intersect in three points; from the centre of ⟦g:0341⟧ draw ⟦g:0343⟧, ⟦g:00f5⟧ and ⟦g:0344⟧ to the points of intersection;

∴ ⟦g:0343⟧ = ⟦g:00f5⟧ = ⟦g:0344⟧ (def. I.15), but as the circles intersect, they have not the same centre (prop. III.5):

∴ the assumed point is not the centre of ⟦g:0342⟧, and ∴ as ⟦g:0343⟧, ⟦g:00f5⟧ and ⟦g:0344⟧ are drawn from a point not the centre, they are not equal (prop. III.7 and III.8); but it was shown before that they were equal, which is absurd; the circles therefore do not intersect in three points.

Q. E. D.
