Proposition VI.26 (Theorem)

[Figure ahs: The diagram for Proposition VI.26, in Byrne’s colours.]

If *similar and similarly posited parallelograms (⟦g:0665⟧ and ⟦g:0666⟧) have a common angle, they are about the same diagonal.*

For, if possible, let ⟦g:0667⟧ be the diagonal of ⟦g:0666⟧ and draw ⟦g:0068⟧ ∥ ⟦g:00b7⟧ (prop. I.31).

Since ⟦g:0668⟧ and ⟦g:0666⟧ are about the same diagonal ⟦g:0667⟧, and have ⟦g:0669⟧ common, they are similar (prop. VI.24);

∴ ⟦g:066a⟧ : ⟦g:066b⟧ :: ⟦g:066c⟧ : ⟦g:066d⟧ ;

but ⟦g:066a⟧ : ⟦g:066e⟧ :: ⟦g:066c⟧ : ⟦g:066d⟧ (hyp.),

∴ ⟦g:066a⟧ : ⟦g:066b⟧ :: ⟦g:066a⟧ : ⟦g:066e⟧ ,

and ∴ ⟦g:066b⟧ = ⟦g:066e⟧ (prop. V.9), which is absurd.

∴ ⟦g:0667⟧ is not the diagonal of ⟦g:0666⟧ in the same manner it can be demonstrated that no other line is except ⟦g:066f⟧.

Q. E. D.
