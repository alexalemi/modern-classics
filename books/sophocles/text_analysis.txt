SOPHOCLES: THE SEVEN TRAGEDIES
Translation strategy. From the GREEK (Perseus' Jebb text), with Jebb's
facing English prose under reference/ as a per-file crib ONLY.

READ FIRST: odyssey/text_analysis.txt (the from-the-Greek pipeline and
the crib-is-Victorian warning), boethius/ (verse kept as verse, and the
verse-integrity check), and euclid-rivals/ (a play, and what a lost
speaker tag does).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHY THIS BOOK
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Measured 2026-08-31 against the shelf, and the result is not close:

    Sophocles (Campbell, verse)   arch 39.10   verse-set 87%
    Sophocles (Storr, verse)      arch 37.97   verse-set 72%
    Augustine (Pusey)             arch 35.9    <- the previous record
    Marcus Aurelius               arch 34.7
    Burton's Nights               arch 27.3

39.10 is THE HIGHEST ARCHAISM SCORE EVER MEASURED IN THIS PROJECT. Every
public-domain Sophocles is Victorian or Edwardian VERSE, and none of the
archaism is in the Greek -- Sophocles wrote the ordinary literary Attic
of his own day, for an audience of fifteen thousand people sitting in
the open air who had to follow it in real time, at speed, once.

So this is the nights/ case in its purest form, and it should be said in
the front matter: we are not removing the twenty-four centuries between
the reader and the author. We are removing a costume that Campbell,
Storr, Plumptre and Murray put on him, and that is why the retelling can
be plain without being a simplification.

Aristophanes was screened in the same pass and STRUCK: the Athenian
Society prose measures arch 2.11 / 1.77 and stage-diction 1.21 / 0.76,
below Grimm and far below Bunyan. It is clean, it is unexpurgated, and
by this project's rules it is not a candidate. (It has a separate open
question -- the French slang "mottes" for a Greek obscenity suggests the
1912 text came via Talbot's French rather than "from the Greek Tongue"
as its title page claims. That would make it a RETRANSLATION job, not a
modernisation one. Settle it before taking Aristophanes on.)

2. THE FORM -- ALEX'S RULING, AND IT IS DERIVABLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Greek tragedy has two registers and the difference is structural, not
decorative:

  SPOKEN scenes, iambic trimeter    -> MODERN PROSE
  SUNG odes, lyric metres           -> VERSE, tab-indented
  CHANTED anapaests, hexameter      -> VERSE (with the odes)

Nearly every translation blurs this, printing the whole play in one
register, and a reader then cannot tell that the chorus is SINGING. The
Greeks could not miss it: the odes were danced.

THE DISTINCTION IS IN THE SOURCE. Perseus marks every division
`subtype="episode"` or `"choral"/"strophe"/"antistrophe"/"anapests"`,
so prep.py derives it and check.py asserts it. This is the difference
between a convention applied by hand five hundred times and an
invariant. `[Spoken]` and `[Sung]` markers in chapters/ say which is
which; they are prep's, not Sophocles', and they do NOT appear in the
translation -- the tab indent carries it (assemble.py sets an indented
block as verse, the boethius/inferno convention).

TAKE SUNG/SPOKEN FROM THE GREEK. The English markup disagrees (The
Women of Trachis: 111 sung speeches in the Greek, 24 in the English).
The metre is a fact about the Greek.

THE ODES ARE NOT RHYMED, and must not be. Sophocles' lyric metres are
quantitative and have no English equivalent; rhyme is what Campbell and
Murray ADDED, and it is what makes their odes unreadable. Same ruling as
boethius/, and for the same reason. What the verse setting must carry is
that these lines are SUNG: shorter than the prose around them, rhythmic,
image-dense, and syntactically simpler than the spoken scenes -- because
an audience has to follow a song in one hearing.

STROPHE AND ANTISTROPHE ARE A PAIR. They were sung to the SAME music by
the chorus dancing one way and then back. Keep them recognisably
matched in length and shape; do not let one run to twice the other.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
3. VOICE
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Sophocles is the plainest of the three tragedians and the most
frightening, and the two facts are the same fact. He does not decorate.
His most famous lines are short, flat declaratives that a translator
into verse is forced to pad:

  - Do not warm the plays up. The horror in Oedipus is administered in
    ordinary sentences by people being reasonable.
  - The STICHOMYTHIA -- one line each, back and forth, sometimes for
    fifty lines -- is the engine of every play, and it is where a
    Victorian translation dies. Render it as fast, clipped, modern
    dialogue. It should read like an interrogation, because it is one.
  - THE CHARACTERS ARGUE. These are not declamations; they are people
    scoring points off each other, with the audience judging. Creon and
    Haemon, Antigone and Ismene, Oedipus and Teiresias, Ajax and
    Tecmessa, Neoptolemus and Odysseus -- all of it is argument, and
    the arguments are good on BOTH sides. Never make the loser stupid.
  - Keep the SILENCES. Characters stop answering, walk off without a
    word, or say one thing and leave. Do not fill those in.
  - The Chorus is not a narrator and not the author's opinion. It is a
    group of frightened, partisan, usually wrong local men (or women).
    Let them be wrong.

4. LOCKED VOCABULARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Fixed here so the seven plays agree with each other; the ledger is
running_notes.txt. The false friends do the real damage:

  ate         RUIN -- the madness sent from outside that destroys a
              house. Never "sin", never "guilt".
  hubris      OUTRAGE done to another person; a public act, not an
              attitude. NOT "pride" -- that mistranslation is the whole
              modern misreading of Greek tragedy.
  moira       what is allotted; "fate" only where it is a person.
  nomos       LAW, and also custom -- Antigone turns on the ambiguity.
              Keep it visible; do not resolve it silently.
  dike        both JUSTICE and a lawsuit, and Sophocles means both.
  daimon      not "demon". A god's power working in a life; sometimes
              just "what happened to me".
  arete       EXCELLENCE at what you are for; not "virtue".
  eugeneia    good birth, and the character that is supposed to come
              with it -- Neoptolemus' whole problem.
  philos      not "friend" but the people you OWE: kin, host, ally.
              Antigone buries her brother because he is philos.
  sophos      clever/skilled AND wise, and the plays keep sliding
              between them, usually as an accusation.
  xenos       guest AND stranger AND host, one word, an obligation.
  Hades       the place and the god; keep the name, not "hell".

NAMES: familiar English forms (Ajax not Aias, Oedipus not Oidipous,
Clytemnestra, Teiresias, Jocasta, Creon, Haemon, Neoptolemus, Philoctetes,
Deianeira, Heracles). Resolve cult-title periphrasis to plain names, as
ovid/ does, EXCEPT where the periphrasis is the point.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
5. STAGE DIRECTIONS ARE JEBB'S, NOT SOPHOCLES'
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The Greek carries NONE -- ancient manuscripts have no stage directions,
and all thirty in the English are Jebb's editorial reconstructions from
the dialogue. So they are ours to write plainly and to modernise freely,
and they are not protected by the Verne rule. Keep them SPARSE and
factual (entrances, exits, who is carried on), never interpretive; where
the text says what is happening, the direction is redundant and goes.
Set them as their own paragraph in parentheses.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
6. THE VERNE RULE
--------------------------------------------------------------------
No editorial note anywhere in the volume. Translated in full, unhedged:

  - AJAX slaughters livestock believing them to be the Greek generals,
    recovers, understands, and kills himself on stage-adjacent ground
    in the only on-stage suicide in surviving tragedy. His speech about
    why he must do it is a REASONED ARGUMENT and is rendered as one.
  - Tecmessa is his SLAVE and the mother of his son, and she says so;
    the household runs on slavery throughout all seven plays and it is
    never euphemised. Not "handmaid" (the odyssey/ ruling on dmoai).
  - ANTIGONE's claim that she would not have done this for a husband or
    a child, only for a brother, because a brother is irreplaceable
    once the parents are dead. Readers since Goethe have wished it away.
    It stands.
  - OEDIPUS' self-blinding, described in full by the messenger, and
    Jocasta's suicide. No softening of either.
  - The exposure of the infant Oedipus with his ankles pinned.
  - DEIANEIRA poisons her husband by accident with what she believes is
    a love charm, and Heracles' final scene -- in agony, demanding his
    son marry the girl he burned a city to capture -- is as ugly as it
    is meant to be. His last order to Hyllus stands as written.
  - PHILOCTETES' wound, its smell, and the fact that the Greeks left a
    man on an island for ten years because they could not stand it.
  - The gods of these plays are not just and are not said to be. Where
    a character says the gods are good, that is THAT CHARACTER'S claim,
    and the play usually disagrees with them.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
7. RATIO
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Greek verse -> English prose runs high. Jebb's own prose is 92,381
English words against 59,795 Greek, a ratio of 1.54; our odes are verse
and slightly tighter, our prose slightly looser. Target 1.45-1.70 per
file; verify --min-ratio 1.35 --max-ratio 1.80. UNDER 1.35 MEANS
SUMMARISING, which in a play means a speech has been trimmed.
