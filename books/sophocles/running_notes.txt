FORM (settled before file 000; see text_analysis.txt section 2)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
- Spoken scenes: modern prose. Speaker tag on its own, then the speech.
  Format is "Antigone." on its own line, then the prose beneath it --
  the euclid-rivals convention, NOT the modern playscript
  "ANTIGONE:" and never ALL CAPS (assemble.py reads an all-caps line as
  a heading).
- Sung odes: TAB-INDENTED verse, unrhymed, one line per line.
- The [Spoken] / [Sung] markers in chapters/ are prep's scaffolding and
  do NOT appear in the translation. The tab indent carries it.
- Line numbers in chapters/ are anchors for the crib. They do NOT appear
  in the translation either.
- Stage directions: own paragraph, in parentheses, sparse and factual.
  They are Jebb's, not Sophocles' -- ours to write.
- NO markup except _x_ / *x* for emphasis, which these plays barely
  need. Structure comes from convention alone.

CHORUS ADDRESS: the Chorus speaks as "I" in the odes (Sophocles' own
singular) and as "we" in spoken dialogue. Keep the difference; do not
harmonise it. It is why the odes sound like one frightened man and the
dialogue like a delegation.

FORMULAS THAT RECUR ACROSS PLAYS
--------------------------------------------------------------------
- The Chorus' closing tag -- most plays end with four or five chanted
  anapaestic lines drawing a moral. They are a FORM, like the Nights'
  night-formulas, and a reader who owns the volume meets them seven
  times. Keep them recognisably the same kind of thing each time:
  short, plain, verse, and slightly inadequate to what just happened.
- "Call no man happy until he is dead" (Oedipus the King's closing
  anapaests, and Herodotus I.32, which the collection already has).
  CHECKED, not remembered (the epictetus rule). herodotus/ has Solon
  say: "But until he's dead, hold off on the word 'happy.' Call him
  fortunate, if you like, but not happy" -- which keeps the olbios /
  eutuches distinction. Sophocles' lines are NOT a quotation of
  Herodotus and must not be made into one; render them so a reader who
  owns both books hears the echo, and no closer than that. In
  particular do not import Solon's fortunate/happy contrast, which
  Sophocles does not draw there.
