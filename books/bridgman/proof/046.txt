+ Now a pound force is that force which imparts to a mass of one pound an acceleration of \(32.17 \text{ ft}/\text{sec}^2\).

Hence \[ 1 \text{ lb force} = 32.17 \frac{1 \text{ lb (mass) } 1 \text{ ft}}{(1 \text{ sec})^2} \] and \[ \begin{aligned} Y_1 &= 66000 \times 32.17 \frac{\text{lb mass } 1 \text{ ft}}{(1 \text{ sec})^2} \cdot \frac{1 \text{ ft}}{1 \text{ min}}. \\ &= 32.17 \times 66000 \frac{\tfrac{1}{2000} \text{ ton } (\tfrac{1}{5280} \text{ mi})^2}{(\tfrac{1}{3600} \text{ hour})^2 \tfrac{1}{60} \text{ hour}} \\ &= 2.962 \times 10^4 \frac{\text{ton mi}^2}{\text{hour}^3}. \end{aligned} \] or, writing in the standard form \[ 3.380 \times 10^{-5} Y_1 = \text{ton}^1 \text{ mi}^2 \text{ hour}^{-3}. \]

Again \[ Y_2 = 3 \frac{\text{ft}}{\text{sec}} = 3 \frac{\tfrac{1}{5280} \text{ mi}}{\tfrac{1}{3600} \text{ hour}} = \frac{45}{22} \frac{\text{mi}}{\text{hour}}, \] or \[ .4889 \, Y_2 = \text{ton}^0 \text{ mi}^1 \text{ hour}^{-1}. \] And again \[ Y_3 = 5 \text{ ergs} = 5 \frac{1 \text{ gm } (1 \text{ cm})^2}{(1 \text{ sec})^2} = 5 \frac{1.103 \times 10^{-6} \text{ tons } (6.214 \times 10^{-6} \text{ mi})^2}{(\tfrac{1}{3600} \text{ hour})^2}, \] or \[ 3.622 \times 10^8 \, Y_3 = \text{tons}^1 \text{ mi}^2 \text{ hour}^{-2}. \]

We rewrite these to obtain our system of equations in the standard form \[ \begin{aligned} 3.380 \times 10^{-5} \, Y_1 &= \text{ton}^1 \text{ mi}^2 \text{ hour}^{-3} \\ .4889 \, Y_2 &= \text{ton}^0 \text{ mi}^1 \text{ hour}^{-1} \\ 3.622 \times 10^8 \, Y_3 &= \text{ton}^1 \text{ mi}^2 \text{ hour}^{-2}. \end{aligned} \]

We now solve for the ton, mile, and hour in terms of \(Y_1\), \(Y_2\), and \(Y_3\). We first find the determinant of the exponents. \[ \Delta = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 2 & -3 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 2 & -2 \end{vmatrix} = 1. \]

This is pleasingly simple.
