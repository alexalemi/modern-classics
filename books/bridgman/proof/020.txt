Then we know that if the two sides of the equation are to remain equal no matter how the fundamental units are changed in magnitude, the dimensions of every one of the product terms on the right-hand side must be the same as those of the left-hand side, that is, the dimensions must be \(T\). Assume that a typical product term is of the form \[ m_1^{\alpha} m_2^{\beta} G^{\gamma} r^{\delta}. \] This must have the dimensions of \(T\). That is, \[ M^{\alpha} M^{\beta} (M^{-1} L^3 T^{-2})^{\gamma} L^{\delta} = T. \] Writing down the conditions on the exponents gives \[ \left. \begin{aligned} \alpha + \beta - \gamma &= 0 \\ 3\gamma + \delta &= 0 \\ -2\gamma &= 1 \end{aligned} \right\}. \] Hence \[ \left. \begin{aligned} \gamma &= -\tfrac{1}{2} \\ \delta &= \tfrac{3}{2} \\ \alpha &= -\beta + \tfrac{1}{2} \end{aligned} \right\}. \]

The values of \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are not uniquely determined, but only a relation between them is fixed. This is as we should expect, because we had only three equations of condition, and four unknown quantities to satisfy them with. The relation between \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) shows that \(m_1\) and \(m_2\) must enter in the form \(m_2^{-\frac{1}{2}} \left( \dfrac{m_2}{m_1} \right)^{x}\), where there is no restriction on the value of \(x\).
