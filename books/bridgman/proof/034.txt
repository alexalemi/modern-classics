We have stated that the requirement of the absolute significance of relative magnitude imposes definite restrictions on the operations by which secondary quantities may be measured in terms of primary quantities. Let us formulate this restriction analytically. We call the primary quantities in terms of which the secondary quantity are measured \(\alpha\), \(\beta\), \(\gamma\), etc. Measurements of the primary quantities are combined in a certain way to give the measure of the secondary quantity. We represent this combination by the functional symbol \(f\), putting the secondary quantity \(= f(\alpha, \beta, \gamma, ----)\). Now if there are two concrete examples of the secondary quantity, the associated primary quantities have different numerical magnitudes. Let us denote the set associated with the first of the concrete examples by the subscript 1, and that with the second set by the subscript 2. Then \(f(\alpha_1, \beta_1, \gamma_1, ----)\) will be the measure of the first concrete example, and \(f(\alpha_2, \beta_2, \gamma_2, ----)\) will be the measure of the second. We now change the size of the fundamental units. We make the unit in terms of which \(\alpha\) is measured \(1/x\)th as large. Then, as we have shown, the number measuring \(\alpha\) will be \(x\) times as large, or \(x\alpha\). In the same way make the unit measuring \(\beta\) \(1/y\)th as large, and the measuring number becomes \(y\beta\). Since our rule of operation by which the numerical measure of the secondary quantity is obtained from the associated primary quantities is independent of the size of the primary units, the number measuring the secondary quantity now becomes \(f(x\alpha, y\beta, ----)\). The measures of the two concrete examples of the secondary quantity will now be \(f(x\alpha_1, y\beta_1, ----)\) and \(f(x\alpha_2, y\beta_2, ----)\).

Our requirement of absolute significance of relative magnitude now becomes analytically \[ \frac{f(\alpha_1, \beta_1, ----)}{f(\alpha_2, \beta_2, ----)} = \frac{f(x\alpha_1, y\beta_1, ----)}{f(x\alpha_2, y\beta_2, ----)}. \]

This relation is to hold for all values of \(\alpha_1, \beta_1, ----, \alpha_2, \beta_2, ----\) and \(x, y, z, ----\).

We desire to solve this equation for the unknown function \(f\). Rewrite in the form \[ f(x\alpha_1, y\beta_1, ----) = f(x\alpha_2, y\beta_2, ----) \times \frac{f(\alpha_1, \beta_1, ----)}{f(\alpha_2, \beta_2, ----)}. \]
