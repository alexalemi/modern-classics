Gerard van Honthorst's *Solon and Croesus* (1624). The king sits crowned and robed in ermine, pointing, while a servant holds up a rich cloth and a pot spilling gold coins, a gilt goblet and a silver ewer stand at his feet. At the right edge the old Athenian waits with his staff, barefoot and unimpressed. Croesus wants to hear that he is the happiest man alive, and Solon will not say it: the story near the start of Book One that sets the tone for the whole history.
