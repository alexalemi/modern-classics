The Void Scroll

The Way of strategy of the Two Swords as One: I write it down here as the Void scroll.

The idea of the void, kū, is this. Where there is nothing, where nothing can be known, we call it the void. The void, of course, is nothing. Knowing what exists, you know what does not exist: that is the void.

In the world, people who see things wrongly look at what they do not understand and call it the void. That is not the true void; it is only a confused mind. In the Way of strategy too, when a warrior following his Way does not know the warrior's law, that is not the void. He has all sorts of confusions, cannot see what to do, and calls that the void, but it is not the true void.

A warrior learns the Way of strategy surely, works hard at the other martial arts as well, and is not in the least in the dark about the Way a warrior must walk. His mind has no confusion in it. Every morning and every hour he never slackens; he polishes both parts of his mind, the heart that feels and the will that decides, and sharpens both his eyes, the eye that perceives and the eye that looks. When there is not the slightest cloud left, when the clouds of confusion have cleared away, know that this is the true void.

Until you know the true Way, whether you follow the Buddha's law or the law of the world, each of you will think that your own way is sure and good. But if you look at it from the straight path of the mind, and measure it against the great square and line of the world, you will see that each person's private favoritism and each person's crooked eye has turned him away from the true Way. Know that spirit, make what is straight your foundation, take the true mind as your Way, practice strategy broadly, rightly, and clearly, and grasp the large view. Then take the void as the Way, and see the Way as the void.

In the void there is good and no evil. Wisdom exists. Principle exists. The Way exists. The mind is void.

Shōhō 2, the twelfth day of the fifth month (June 1645). Shinmen Musashi.
To Terao Magonojō.
