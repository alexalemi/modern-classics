Chapter 13: The Field and the Knower of the Field -- Besant's 1922 English, crib only. Verse numbers are the

Arjuna said: Matter and Spirit,[1] even the Field and the Knower of the Field, wisdom and that which ought to be known, these I fain would learn, O Keshava.

The Blessed Lord said:
[13.1] This body, son of Kuntî, is called the Field; that which knoweth it is called the Knower of the Field by the Sages.

[13.2] Understand Me as the Knower of the Field in all Fields, O Bhârata. Wisdom as to the Field and the Knower of the Field, that in My opinion is the wisdom.

[13.3] What that Field is and of what nature, how modified, and whence it is, and what He[2] is and what His powers, hear that now briefly from Me.

[13.4] Rishis have sung in manifold ways, in many various chants, and in decisive Brahma-sûtra verses[3] full of reasonings.

[13.5] The great Elements, Individuality[4], Reason[5], and also the unmanifested, the ten senses and the one, and the five pastures of the senses[6];

[13.6] Desire, aversion, pleasure, pain, combination[7], intelligence, firmness; these, briefly described, constitute the Field and its modifications.

[13.7] Humility, unpretentiousness, harmlessness, forgiveness, rectitude, service of the teacher, purity, steadfastness, self-control.

[13.8] Dispassion towards the objects of the senses, and also absence of egoism, insight into the pain and evil of birth, death, old age and sickness,

[13.9] Unattachment, absence of self-identification with son, wife or home, and constant balance of mind in wished-for and unwished-for events,

[13.10] Unflinching devotion to Me by yoga, without other objects, resort to sequestered places, absence of enjoyment in the company of men,

[13.11] Constancy in the wisdom of the Self,[8] understanding of the object of essential wisdom; that is declared to be the Wisdom; all against it is ignorance.

[13.12] I will declare that which ought to be known, that which being known immortality is enjoyed—the beginningless supreme Eternal, called neither being nor non-being.

[13.13] Everywhere That hath hands and feet, everywhere eyes, heads, and mouths; all-hearing, He dwelleth in the world, enveloping all,

[13.14] Shining with all sense-faculties without any senses; unattached supporting everything and free from qualities[9] enjoying qualities;[9]

[13.15] Without and within all beings, immovable and also movable; by reason of His subtlety imperceptible; at hand and far away is That.

[13.16] Not divided amid beings, and yet seated distributively; that is to be known as the supporter of beings; He devours and He generates.

[13.17] That, the Light of all lights, is said to be beyond darkness; Wisdom, the Object of Wisdom, by Wisdom to be reached, seated in the hearts of all.

[13.18] Thus the Field, Wisdom and the Object of Wisdom, have been briefly told. My devotee, thus knowing, enters into My Being.

[13.19] Know thou that Matter[10] and Spirit[11] are both without beginning; and know thou also that modifications and qualities[12] are all Matter[10]-born.

[13.20] Matter[10] is called the cause of the generation of causes and effects; Spirit[11] is called the cause of the enjoyment of pleasure and pain.

[13.21] Spirit[11] seated in Matter[10] useth the qualities born of Matter[10]; attachment to the qualities[12] is the cause of his births in good and evil wombs.

[13.22] Supervisor and permitter, supporter, enjoyer, the great Lord, and also the supreme Self; thus is styled in this body the supreme Spirit.[10]

[13.23] He who thus knoweth Spirit[11] and Matter[10] with its qualities,[12] in whatsoever condition he may be, he shall not be born again.

[13.24] Some by meditation behold the Self in the self by the Self; others by the Sânkhya Yoga, and others by the Yoga of Action;

[13.25] Others also, ignorant of this, having heard of it from others, worship; and these also cross beyond death, adhering to what they had heard.

[13.26] Whatsoever creature is born immobile or mobile, know thou, O best of the Bhâratas, that it is from the union between the Field and the Knower of the Field.

[13.27] Seated equally in all beings, the supreme Lord, unperishing within the perishing—he who thus seeth, he seeth.

[13.28] Seeing indeed everywhere the same Lord equally dwelling, he doth not destroy the Self, and thus treads the highest Path.

[13.29] He who seeth that Matter[10] verily performeth all actions, and that the Self is actionless, he seeth.

[13.30] When he perceiveth the diversified existence of beings as rooted in One, and spreading forth from it, then he reacheth the Eternal

[13.31] Being beginningless and without qualities[12], the imperishable supreme Self, though seated in the body, O Kaunteya, worketh not nor is affected.

[13.32] As the omnipresent ether is not affected, by reason of its subtlety, so seated everywhere in the body the Self is not affected.

[13.33] As the one sun illumineth the whole earth, so the Lord of the Field illumineth the whole Field, O Bhârata.

[13.34] They who by the eyes of Wisdom perceive this difference between the Field and the Knower of the Field, and the liberation of beings from Matter[13] they go to the Supreme.
