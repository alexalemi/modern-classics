Chapter 17: The Three Kinds of Faith

Arjuna said:

Krishna, what about people who set aside the rules of scripture but
still make their sacrifices full of faith? Where do they stand? Is it in
goodness, or in passion, or in darkness?

Krishna said:

The faith of those who live in bodies is of three kinds, and it
springs from each person's own nature: faith of goodness, faith of
passion, and faith of darkness. Hear about them. Everyone's faith
takes the shape of his own nature, Arjuna. A person is made of his faith:
whatever his faith is, that is what he is.

People of goodness worship the gods. People of passion worship the
yakshas and the fiends. And the others, the people of darkness, worship
the ghosts of the dead and hordes of spirits.

There are people who practice savage austerities that no scripture
ever ordered, full of hypocrisy and ego, driven by the force of their
desire and passion. Mindlessly they torment the elements that make up
their bodies, and they torment me too, who live inside the body. Know
that their resolve is demonic.

Even the food that people like is of three kinds, and so are
sacrifice, austerity and giving. Hear how they differ.

The foods that people of goodness love are the ones that increase
life, purity, strength, health, happiness and good cheer: foods that are
tasty, rich, nourishing and good for the heart. People of passion
want foods that are bitter, sour, salty, too hot, sharp, dry and
burning, foods that bring pain, misery and sickness. People of
darkness love food that has gone stale and lost its taste, that is
rotten and left over from the day before, scraps from someone else's
plate, and food unfit for an offering.

The sacrifice of goodness is offered as the rules lay down, by
people who want no fruit from it, who have set their minds on this one
thought: "This is a sacrifice that ought to be made." But a
sacrifice offered with an eye on its fruit, or just for show, Arjuna:
know that sacrifice is of passion. A sacrifice
that ignores the rules, where no food is given out, no sacred words are
chanted, no fee is paid to the priests, and there is no faith in it, is
called a sacrifice of darkness.

Honoring the gods, the twice-born, those initiated into the Vedas, your teachers and the wise;
purity, honesty, chastity and doing no harm: these are called the
austerity of the body. Words that cause no one distress, words that
are true, kind and helpful, and the regular study and recitation of the
Vedas: these are called the austerity of speech. A serene mind,
gentleness, silence, self-control and a pure heart: these are called the
austerity of the mind.

When disciplined people practice this threefold austerity with the
highest faith, wanting no fruit from it, it is called austerity of
goodness. Austerity practiced to win respect, honor and reverence,
or out of hypocrisy, is called austerity of passion here; it is
unsteady, and it does not last. Austerity practiced out of some
foolish notion, by torturing yourself, or in order to ruin someone else,
is called austerity of darkness.

A gift given because it is right to give, to someone who can do
nothing for you in return, at the right place and the right time, to
the right person: that gift is held to be of goodness. But a gift
given to get something back, or with an eye on its fruit, or given
grudgingly, is held to be of passion. A gift given at the wrong
place and the wrong time, to the wrong people, without respect and with
contempt, is called a gift of darkness.

Om Tat Sat: these three words are taught as the threefold name of
Brahman. By this name, long ago, the Brahmins, the Vedas and the sacrifices
were set in their places. That is why those who speak of Brahman
always begin their acts of sacrifice, giving and austerity, as the rules
lay down, by saying the word Om. Those who seek freedom say Tat,
which means "that," and perform their many acts of sacrifice, austerity
and giving without aiming at any fruit.

Sat, which means both "real" and "good," is used for what is real
and for what is good; and so too, Arjuna, the word Sat is used for an action worthy of
praise. Being steadfast in sacrifice, austerity and giving is also
called Sat, and any action done for the sake of that is called Sat as
well. But whatever is offered in the fire, or given, or done as
austerity, or done at all, without faith, is called asat, "unreal,"
Arjuna. It counts for nothing, either after death or here in this
world.
