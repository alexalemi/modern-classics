Chapter 7: Knowledge and Insight

Krishna said:

Arjuna, listen to how you will come to know me completely, beyond
any doubt, if you keep your mind fixed on me, practice yoga, and make me
your refuge. I will teach you this knowledge, and the insight that
goes with it, leaving nothing out. Once you know it, there will be
nothing left in this world for you to know.

Among thousands of people, hardly one strives for perfection. And
even among those who strive and succeed, hardly one knows me as I truly
am.

Earth, water, fire, air, space, mind, understanding, and ego: this
is my nature, divided eight ways. That is my lower nature. But know
that I have another, beyond it, my higher nature, Arjuna: the one that
has become life itself, by which this whole world is held up.
Understand that all beings are born from these two natures. I am
where the whole world comes from, and I am where it ends. There is
nothing whatever higher than I am, Arjuna. All of this is strung on me,
like pearls on a thread.

I am the taste in water, Arjuna. I am the light in the moon and the
sun, the sacred syllable Om in all the Vedas, the sound in space, the
manhood in men. I am the pure scent of the earth and the blaze in
the fire. I am the life in all beings, and the austerity in ascetics.
Know that I am the eternal seed of all beings, Arjuna. I am the
understanding of those who understand, the brilliance of the brilliant.
I am the strength of the strong, free of desire and passion. And in
all beings, Arjuna, I am the desire that does not go against what is
right.

And whatever states of being there are, of goodness, of passion, or
of darkness, know that they come from me alone. But I am not in them;
they are in me. This whole world is confused by these three states
that the gunas make, and so it does not recognize me, who stand beyond
them and never change. For this divine illusion of mine, made of
the gunas, is hard to get past. But those who come to me, and to me
alone, cross over this illusion.

Evildoers do not come to me: the deluded, the lowest of men, whose
knowledge has been stolen by illusion and who have taken on the nature
of demons. Four kinds of good people worship me, Arjuna: the one
who is suffering, the one who wants to know, the one who wants to gain
something, and the one who knows. Of these, the one who knows,
always disciplined, devoted to me alone, is the best. For I am dear
beyond measure to the one who knows, and he is dear to me. All of
them are noble. But the one who knows I count as my very self, for with
his self disciplined he has set himself on me alone, the highest goal
there is. At the end of many births the person of knowledge comes
to me, knowing that Krishna, Vasudeva's son, is everything. Such a great
soul is very hard to find.

People whose knowledge has been carried off by this desire or that
turn to other gods, following this rule or that, driven by their own
natures. Whatever form a devoted person wants to worship with
faith, I make that person's faith in it unshakable. Held by that
faith, he seeks to win that god's favor, and from it he gets what he
desires; but it is I who grant those things. Yet the fruit that
these people of little understanding win does not last. Those who
sacrifice to the gods go to the gods; those who are devoted to me come
to me.

People without understanding think that I, the unmanifest, have
come into visible form. They do not know my highest state, which never
changes and has nothing above it. I am not revealed to everyone,
for I am veiled by the illusion my yoga makes. This deluded world does
not recognize me, unborn and unchanging. I know all the beings that
have been, Arjuna, all that are now, and all that are still to come; but
no one knows me.

All beings are born into confusion, Arjuna, led astray by the pairs
of opposites that spring from longing and loathing. But people
whose deeds are good, in whom sin has come to an end, are freed from the
confusion of the pairs of opposites, and they worship me, firm in their
vows.

Those who take refuge in me and strive to be free of old age and
death know Brahman, the whole of it; they know the adhyatma, all that
belongs to the self; and they know action, entire. Those who know
me together with the adhibhuta, what belongs to the world of beings, the
adhidaiva, what belongs to the gods, and the adhiyajna, what belongs to
sacrifice, will know me even at the hour of death, with their minds held
steady.
