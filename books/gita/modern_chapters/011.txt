Chapter 12: The Way of Devotion

Arjuna said:

There are those who are always disciplined, devoted to you, who
worship you; and there are those who worship the imperishable, the
unmanifest. Which of them knows yoga best?

Krishna said:

Those who fix their minds on me and worship me, always disciplined,
filled with the highest faith: those, I hold, are the most disciplined
of all.

But those who worship the imperishable, which no words can describe,
the unmanifest, present everywhere, beyond all thought, unchanging,
unmoving, constant, who hold the whole crowd of their senses in
check, whose understanding is even toward everything, who delight in the
good of every creature: they too reach me. The struggle is greater
for those whose minds cling to the unmanifest, because the way to the
unmanifest is hard for anyone who has a body to travel.

But those who renounce all their actions in me, who have me as their
highest goal, who worship me and meditate on me with a discipline that
looks to nothing else, whose minds have entered into me: for them,
Arjuna, I soon become the one who lifts them out of the ocean of death,
the round of birth and death.

Fix your mind on me alone. Let your understanding enter into me.
Then from now on you will live in me. There is no doubt of it. But
if you cannot hold your thoughts steady on me, then try to reach me
through the yoga of practice, Arjuna. If you can't manage practice
either, then make my work your highest aim. Even just doing actions for
my sake, you will reach perfection. And if you are not able to do
even this, then take refuge in my discipline, hold yourself in check,
and give up the fruit of all your actions.

For knowledge is better than practice; meditation is better than
knowledge; giving up the fruit of action is better than meditation; and
from giving up, peace comes at once.

The one who hates no creature, who is friendly and compassionate,
without any thought of "mine," without ego, the same in pain and
pleasure, patient, always content, disciplined, self-controlled,
firm in his resolve, whose mind and understanding are offered up to me,
who is devoted to me: that person is dear to me. The one the world
does not shrink from, who does not shrink from the world, who is free of
elation and resentment, of fear and agitation: that person is dear to
me. The one who wants nothing, who is pure and capable, who stands
aside, untroubled, who has given up every undertaking, who is devoted to
me: that person is dear to me. The one who neither rejoices nor
hates, neither grieves nor craves, who has given up both good fortune
and bad, who is full of devotion: that person is dear to me.

The one who is the same to enemy and friend, the same in honor and
disgrace, the same in cold and heat, pleasure and pain, free from
attachment, who takes blame and praise alike, who keeps silent,
content with whatever comes, with no home of his own, steady of mind,
full of devotion: that man is dear to me.

But those who hold to this nectar of immortality, this teaching of
what is right, just as I have given it, full of faith, with me as their
highest goal, devoted to me: they are the dearest of all to me.
