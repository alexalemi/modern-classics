Chapter 14: The Three Gunas

Krishna said:

I will tell you once more the highest knowledge, the best of all
knowledge. All the sages who learned it went from here to the highest
perfection. Those who have taken refuge in this knowledge have come
to share my own nature. They are not born even when the world is
created, and they do not suffer when it is dissolved.

The great Brahman is my womb. In it I plant the seed, and from that,
Arjuna, all beings are born. Whatever forms come to birth in any
womb at all, the great Brahman is their womb, and I am the father who
gives the seed.

Goodness, passion and darkness: these gunas, which come from nature,
bind the imperishable one who lives in the body fast to the body,
Arjuna. Of these, goodness, because it is spotless, gives light and
health. It binds you through attachment to happiness and attachment to
knowledge, you who are without fault. Know that passion is made of
desire, and that it springs from thirst and attachment. It binds the one
who lives in the body through attachment to action. And know that
darkness is born of ignorance, and that it bewilders everyone who lives
in a body. It binds through carelessness, laziness and sleep.

Goodness attaches you to happiness, and passion to action; but
darkness covers over knowledge and attaches you to carelessness.
Sometimes goodness rises, overpowering passion and darkness;
sometimes passion, overpowering goodness and darkness; and sometimes
darkness, overpowering goodness and passion.

When the light of knowledge shines out through every gate of the
body, then you can know that goodness has grown strong. Greed,
activity, the undertaking of actions, restlessness, longing: these
arise, Arjuna, when passion has grown strong. Dimness, inertia,
carelessness and confusion: these arise when darkness has grown strong.

If someone who lives in a body dies when goodness is strong, he
goes to the spotless worlds of those who know the highest. Dying in
passion, he is born among people attached to action; and dying in
darkness, he is born in the wombs of creatures without understanding.

The fruit of a good action, they say, is pure, and of goodness; the
fruit of passion is pain; and the fruit of darkness is ignorance.
From goodness, knowledge is born; from passion, greed; and from
darkness come carelessness and confusion, and ignorance too. Those
who stand in goodness go upward; those of passion stay in the middle;
and those of darkness, sunk in the ways of the lowest guna, go down.

When the one who sees sees no doer other than the gunas, and knows
what is higher than the gunas, he comes into my own state of being.
When the one who lives in the body goes beyond these three gunas,
from which the body arises, he is set free from birth and death, old age
and pain, and he tastes immortality.

Arjuna said:

Lord, what are the marks of someone who has gone beyond these three
gunas? How does he live? And how does he get beyond the three gunas?

Krishna said:

Arjuna, he does not hate light, or activity, or even confusion when
they come, and he does not long for them when they go. He sits like
an onlooker, and the gunas do not shake him. Knowing that it is only the
gunas at work, he stands firm and does not waver. He is the same in
pain and pleasure, at home in himself; a lump of earth, a stone and a
piece of gold are all the same to him. He is steady and wise, the same
toward what he likes and what he dislikes, the same when he is blamed
and when he is praised. He is the same in honor and disgrace, the
same toward friend and enemy, and he has given up every undertaking: he
is said to have gone beyond the gunas.

And the one who serves me with the yoga of devotion, never
straying, goes beyond these gunas and is fit to become Brahman. For
I am the foundation of Brahman, of the immortal that never perishes, of
the eternal law, and of a happiness that is complete.
