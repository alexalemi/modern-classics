Chapter 18: Freedom Through Renunciation

Arjuna said:

Krishna, mighty one, I want to know the truth about renunciation,
and about giving up, and how the two are different.

Krishna said:

The seers understand renunciation as setting aside the
actions people do out of desire. The wise call it giving up when you
give up the fruit of every action. Some thinkers say that action
should be given up because it is full of faults. Others say that acts
of sacrifice, giving and austerity should never be given up.

Hear what I have settled on about giving up, Arjuna. For giving up,
you who are a tiger among men, has been described as being of three
kinds. Acts of sacrifice, giving and austerity should not be given
up. They should be done, because sacrifice, giving and austerity make
the wise pure. But even these actions should be done with
attachment given up, and the fruit given up too, Arjuna. That is my
final and highest judgment.

It is not right to renounce an action that is your duty to do. To
give it up out of delusion is called giving up of darkness. If
someone gives up an action just because it is hard, out of fear of
bodily pain, his giving up is of passion, and he will never win the
fruit of giving up. But when someone does the action that is his
duty, Arjuna, simply because it ought to be done, and gives up both
attachment and fruit, his giving up is held to be of goodness.

The one who truly gives things up, filled with goodness, wise,
his doubts cut away, does not hate unpleasant work and does not cling to
pleasant work. No one who lives in a body can give up action
entirely. But whoever gives up the fruit of action is called a person
who has truly given up. The fruit of action is of three kinds,
unwanted, wanted and mixed, and it comes after death to those who have
not given up. It never comes at all to those who have renounced.

Learn from me, Arjuna, the five causes that bring every action
about, as they are taught in Sankhya, the doctrine that brings action
to its end: the body, which is the base; the doer; the many
different instruments; the many separate movements; and the fifth, the
power of the gods. Whatever action a person undertakes with body,
speech or mind, whether it is right or the reverse, these five are its
causes. That being so, anyone who sees himself, the self alone, as
the doer sees nothing, because his understanding is untrained. His mind
is perverse.

Someone who has no sense of "I am the doer," whose understanding
is not smeared by it, does not kill, even if he kills these people, and
he is not bound.

Knowledge, what is known, and the knower are the three things that
drive action. The instrument, the action and the doer are the three
things that action is made of. The teaching on the gunas says that
knowledge, action and doer are each of three kinds, according to the
gunas. Hear about these too, as they truly are.

The knowledge by which a person sees one being, unchanging, in all
beings, undivided in what is divided: know that knowledge is of
goodness. But the knowledge that sees in all beings many different
beings of different kinds, each one separate: know that knowledge is of
passion. And the knowledge that clings to a single thing as though
it were everything, without reason, without grasping the truth, small
and narrow: that is called knowledge of darkness.

An action that is a duty, done without attachment, without love or
hatred, by someone who wants no fruit from it, is called action of
goodness. But an action done by someone who wants his desires
satisfied, or out of ego, with a great deal of strain, is called action
of passion. An action begun out of delusion, without a thought for
the consequences, the loss, the harm to others, or one's own strength,
is called action of darkness.

A doer who is free of attachment, who does not talk about himself,
who is full of resolve and energy, and unchanged by success or failure,
is called a doer of goodness. A doer who is full of passion, who
wants the fruit of his actions, who is greedy, violent and impure, and
swings between joy and grief, is called a doer of passion. A doer
who is undisciplined, coarse, stubborn, deceitful, malicious, lazy,
gloomy and always putting things off is called a doer of darkness.

Now hear, Arjuna, in full and one by one, how understanding and
resolve are each of three kinds, according to the gunas. The
understanding that knows when to act and when to hold back, what should
be done and what should not, what to fear and what not to fear, what
binds and what sets free: that understanding, Arjuna, is of goodness.
The understanding that gets right and wrong, and what should be
done and what should not, all mixed up: that understanding, Arjuna, is
of passion. The understanding that is wrapped in darkness and
thinks wrong is right, and sees everything upside down: that
understanding, Arjuna, is of darkness.

The resolve that never wavers, by which a person holds steady,
through yoga, the workings of the mind, the breath and the senses: that
resolve, Arjuna, is of goodness. But the resolve by which a person
holds on to duty, pleasure and wealth, clinging to them and
wanting their fruit: that resolve, Arjuna, is of passion. The
resolve by which a fool will not let go of sleep, fear, grief, gloom and
arrogance: that resolve, Arjuna, is of darkness.

And now, Arjuna, hear from me about the three
kinds of happiness. There is a happiness a person comes to enjoy with
practice, and in which he reaches the end of sorrow. At first it
is like poison, but in the end it is like the nectar of immortality.
That happiness is called happiness of goodness, and it is born from the
clarity of an understanding that knows the self. The happiness that
comes from the senses meeting the things of the senses is like nectar at
first, but in the end it is like poison. That happiness is held to be of
passion. The happiness that deludes the self from beginning to end,
the happiness that comes from sleep, laziness and carelessness, is
called happiness of darkness.

There is no being on earth, or among the gods in heaven, that is
free of these three gunas born of nature.

The work of Brahmins, kshatriyas, vaishyas and shudras, Arjuna, is
divided according to the gunas that spring from their own natures.
Calm, self-control, austerity, purity, patience and honesty,
knowledge, insight and belief in God: these are the work of a Brahmin,
born of his nature. Courage, vigor, resolve, skill, not running
away from battle, generosity and the bearing of a ruler: these are the
work of a kshatriya, born of his nature. Farming, herding cattle
and trade are the work of a vaishya, born of his nature. And work that
consists of serving others is the work of a shudra, born of his nature
too.

A person who is devoted to his own work wins perfection. Hear how
someone devoted to his own work finds perfection. By worshipping
with his own work the one from whom all beings come forth, the one who
pervades this whole world, a human being finds perfection.

Better your own duty done imperfectly than another's duty done
well. When you do the work your own nature lays down, you incur no sin.
You should not give up the work you were born to, Arjuna, even if
it has faults; for every undertaking is wrapped in faults, the way fire
is wrapped in smoke.

A person whose understanding is unattached everywhere, who has
mastered himself, whose cravings are gone, reaches through renunciation
the highest perfection: freedom from action. Hear from me in brief,
Arjuna, how someone who has reached perfection reaches Brahman, which is
the highest point of knowledge.

Disciplined by a purified understanding, holding himself in check
with resolve, setting aside sound and the other things of the senses,
casting off longing and loathing, living in solitude, eating
little, controlling speech, body and mind, always intent on the yoga of
meditation, relying on detachment, letting go of ego, force,
arrogance, desire, anger and possessions, with no thought of "mine," at
peace: such a person is fit to become one with Brahman. Having
become one with Brahman, serene in himself, he neither grieves nor
craves. He is the same toward all beings, and he reaches the highest
devotion to me. Through devotion he comes to know me as I truly am,
how great I am and who I am; and once he knows me as I truly am, he
enters into me at once.

Even while doing every kind of action, if he always relies on me,
then by my grace he reaches the eternal home that never perishes.
In your heart, renounce all your actions to me. Make me your
highest goal. Rely on the discipline of understanding, and keep your
mind on me always. With your mind on me, you will cross over every
difficulty by my grace. But if your ego will not let you listen, you
will be destroyed.

If you rely on your ego and think, "I will not fight," your resolve
is for nothing. Your own nature will drive you to it. You are bound,
Arjuna, by your own work, born of your own nature. What you do not want
to do, out of delusion, you will do anyway, even against your will.

The Lord lives in the heart of every being, Arjuna, and by his
power of illusion he makes all beings turn round and round, as if they
were riding on a machine. Go to him for refuge with your whole
being, Arjuna. By his grace you will reach the highest peace, the home
that lasts forever.

That is the knowledge I have taught you, more secret than any
secret. Think it over fully, and then do as you choose.

Listen once more to my highest word, the most secret of all. You
are deeply loved by me, so I will tell you what is for your good.
Keep your mind on me; be devoted to me; sacrifice to me; bow down
to me. You will come to me — I promise you truly, for you are dear to
me. Let go of every dharma, every duty and every law you hold
yourself to, and take refuge in me alone. I will set you free from every
sin. Don't grieve.

Never tell this to anyone who has no austerity or no devotion, or
who does not want to listen, or who speaks against me. But whoever
teaches this highest secret to those who are devoted to me, showing the
highest devotion to me himself, will come to me; there is no doubt of
it. No one among human beings does me dearer service than he does,
and no one on earth will ever be dearer to me than he is. And
whoever studies this sacred conversation of ours will worship me with
the sacrifice of knowledge. That is my belief. Even someone who
only listens to it, with faith and without scorn, will be set free and
will reach the bright worlds of those who have done good.

Have you heard all this, Arjuna, with your mind fixed on one point?
Has the confusion your ignorance caused been destroyed?

Arjuna said:

My confusion is destroyed, Krishna, and through your grace I
remember again what I had lost. I stand firm, my doubts are gone. I will
do what you say.

Sanjaya said:

That is how I heard this wonderful conversation between Krishna,
Vasudeva's son, and the great-souled Arjuna, and it made my hair stand
on end. By the grace of Vyasa, who gave me the power to see and
hear all this, I heard this highest secret, this yoga, from Krishna
himself, the master of yoga, as he spoke it with his own lips.

My king, as I remember this wonderful and holy conversation
between Krishna and Arjuna, and remember it again, I rejoice again and
again. And as I remember that most wonderful form of Krishna's, and
remember it again, my king, my amazement is great, and I rejoice again
and again.

Wherever Krishna is, the master of yoga, and wherever Arjuna is,
the archer, there you will find good fortune, victory, prosperity, and
wise rule that does not waver. That is what I believe.
