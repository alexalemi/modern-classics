Chapter 13: The Field and the Knower of the Field

Krishna said:

{1} This body, Arjuna, is called the field. The one who knows it is
called the knower of the field by those who understand these things.
{2} And know that I am the knower of the field in every field. Knowledge
of the field and of the knower of the field: that, as I see it, is
knowledge.

{3} Now hear from me, in brief, what that field is, what it is like, how
it changes, and where each thing in it comes from; and who the knower
is, and what his powers are. {4} Seers have sung of it many times over,
each in his own way, in sacred verses of every kind, and in the sayings
about Brahman, reasoned and conclusive.

{5} The great elements, ego, understanding, and the unmanifest; the ten
senses and the one more, the mind; the five ranges of the senses;
{6} desire and hatred, pleasure and pain, the body as a whole, awareness
and steadiness: this, in brief, is the field, with all its changes.

{7} Freedom from pride and from pretense, not harming any living thing,
patience, honesty, service to your teacher, purity, steadiness,
self-control; {8} turning away from the things of the senses, and
freedom from ego; seeing clearly the pain and the evil in birth and
death, old age and sickness; {9} not clinging, not being wrapped up in
son and wife and home and the rest, and a mind that stays even, always,
whether what happens is wanted or unwanted; {10} devotion to me that
never strays, with a discipline that looks to nothing else; a liking for
lonely places, and no pleasure in crowds; {11} keeping always to the
knowledge of the self, and keeping in view what knowing the truth is
for: this is called knowledge, and whatever is different from it is
ignorance.

{12} I will tell you what there is to know, and whoever knows it tastes
immortality. It is the highest Brahman, which has no beginning, and it
is said to be neither what is nor what is not.

{13} Its hands and feet are everywhere; its eyes and heads and mouths
are everywhere; its ears are everywhere in the world. It stands,
enfolding everything. {14} It shines with the powers of all the senses,
yet it has no senses at all. It clings to nothing, yet it holds
everything up. It is free of the gunas, yet it is the one who
experiences the gunas.

{15} It is outside all beings and inside them, unmoving and moving. It
is too subtle to be known. It is far away, and it is near. {16} It is
undivided, and yet it stands among beings as if it were divided. Know it
as the one that sustains all beings, that swallows them up, and that
brings them into being. {17} It is called the light of all lights,
beyond all darkness. It is knowledge, and what is to be known, and what
knowledge leads to, and it is set in the heart of everyone.

{18} So I have told you in brief about the field, about knowledge, and
about what there is to know. Someone devoted to me who understands this
comes into my own state of being.

{19} Know that nature and spirit both have no beginning, and know that
all the changes, and the gunas, are born of nature. {20} Nature is said
to be the cause behind every effect, every means of producing it, and
every doing; spirit is said to be the cause behind the experience of
pleasure and pain. {21} For spirit, living in nature, experiences the
gunas that nature produces, and its attachment to the gunas is why it is
born in good wombs and in bad.

{22} The supreme spirit in this body is called the witness, the one who
gives consent, the one who sustains, the one who experiences, the great
Lord, and also the supreme self. {23} Whoever knows spirit, and nature
with its gunas, in this way, will never be born again, however he may
live.

{24} Some see the self in themselves, by themselves, through meditation;
others through the yoga of Sankhya; and others through the yoga of
action. {25} Still others do not know this, but they hear it from others
and worship. They too cross beyond death, devoted to what they have
heard.

{26} Whatever comes into being, Arjuna, still or moving, know that it
comes from the union of the field and the knower of the field. {27} The
one who sees the supreme Lord standing the same in all beings, not
perishing when they perish: whoever sees this, sees. {28} For when he
sees the same Lord standing equally everywhere, he does not harm the
self by his own self, and so he goes to the highest goal.

{29} Whoever sees that all actions, in every way, are done by nature
alone, and so sees that the self is not the doer: he sees. {30} When he
sees that the separate existence of beings rests in the One, and that
from there alone it spreads out, then he reaches Brahman.

{31} Because it has no beginning and is free of the gunas, this supreme
self never perishes. Even though it lives in the body, Arjuna, it does
nothing, and nothing stains it. {32} Space is everywhere, and because it
is so subtle, nothing stains it; in the same way the self, present
everywhere in the body, is never stained. {33} Just as the one sun
lights up this whole world, so the owner of the field lights up the
whole field, Arjuna.

{34} Those who see with the eye of knowledge this difference between the
field and the knower of the field, and see how beings are set free from
nature, go to the highest.
