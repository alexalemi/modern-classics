Chapter 10: The Divine Glories

Krishna said:

{1} Listen again, Arjuna, to my highest word. You take delight in what I
say, and because I want what is good for you, I will tell you more.
{2} Neither the hosts of the gods nor the great seers know where I come
from, for I am the beginning of the gods and of the great seers, every
one of them. {3} Whoever knows me as unborn, without beginning, the
great Lord of the worlds, is free of confusion among mortals, and is set
free from every sin.

{4-5} Understanding, knowledge, freedom from confusion, patience, truth,
self-control, calm, pleasure and pain, being and not-being, fear and
fearlessness too, not harming, evenness of mind, contentment, austerity,
giving, fame and disgrace: all these different states of beings come
from me alone. {6} The seven great seers of old, and the four Manus came
from my own being, born of my mind; and from them come all the creatures
in the world.

{7} Whoever truly knows this glory of mine, and this power of my yoga,
is joined to me by a yoga that never wavers. There is no doubt about it.
{8} I am the source of everything; everything comes forth from me.
Knowing this, the wise worship me, full of love. {9} With their thoughts
on me, their lives given to me, they awaken one another and talk about
me always, and they are content, and they rejoice. {10} To those who are
always disciplined and worship me with love, I give the discipline of
understanding, by which they come to me. {11} Out of compassion for
them, I dwell in their own hearts, and with the shining lamp of
knowledge I destroy the darkness that ignorance breeds.

Arjuna said:

{12} You are the supreme Brahman, the supreme home, the supreme
purifier; the eternal and divine Person, the first of the gods, unborn
and present everywhere. {13} All the seers say so of you, and the divine
seer Narada, and Asita, Devala and Vyasa; and now you tell me so
yourself. {14} I believe that everything you tell me is true, Krishna.
For neither the gods nor the demons, my Lord, know how you show
yourself. {15} You alone know yourself, by yourself, Supreme Person: you
who bring beings into being, Lord of beings, God of gods, master of the
world!

{16} Please tell me, leaving nothing out, of your own divine glories,
the glories by which you fill these worlds and remain in them. {17} How
shall I come to know you, master of yoga, by meditating on you always?
In what forms of being, my Lord, should I think of you? {18} Tell me
again, in full, Krishna, about your power and your glory. For I can
never hear enough of these words; they are the nectar of immortality to
me.

Krishna said:

{19} Very well. I will tell you of my divine glories, Arjuna, but only
the chief of them, for there is no end to how far I reach.

{20} I am the self, Arjuna, that dwells in the heart of every being. I
am the beginning of beings, their middle, and their end. {21} Among the
Adityas, the sons of Aditi, I am Vishnu; among lights, the shining sun.
Among the Maruts, the gods of the wind, I am Marichi; among the stars of
the night, the moon. {22} Among the Vedas I am the Sama Veda, the Veda
of chants; among the gods, Indra. Among the senses I am the mind, and in
living beings I am awareness.

{23} Among the Rudras, the fierce gods, I am Shankara, who is Shiva.
Among the yakshas, the spirits of the wild, and the fiends, I am Kubera,
the lord of wealth. Among the Vasus, the gods of the elements, I am
Agni, the fire; among mountain peaks, Meru, the mountain at the center
of the world. {24} Among household priests, Arjuna, know that I am the
chief, Brihaspati, the priest of the gods. Among generals I am Skanda,
the god of war; among bodies of water, the ocean.

{25} Among the great seers I am Bhrigu. Among words I am the one
syllable, Om. Among sacrifices I am the sacrifice of quiet prayer,
repeated over and over; among things that do not move, the Himalaya.
{26} Among all trees I am the ashvattha, the sacred fig; among the
divine seers, Narada. Among the Gandharvas, the musicians of heaven, I
am Chitraratha; among those who have reached perfection, the sage
Kapila.

{27} Among horses, know that I am Ucchaihshravas, the horse that rose
from the ocean when the gods churned it for the nectar of immortality.
Among lordly elephants I am Airavata, and among men, the king.
{28} Among weapons I am the thunderbolt; among cows, Kamadhuk, the
wishing cow. I am Kandarpa, the god of love, who brings new life into
being; among serpents I am Vasuki, their king.

{29} Among the nagas, the snake spirits, I am Ananta, the endless one;
among the creatures of the waters, Varuna, their lord. Among the
ancestors I am Aryaman, their chief; and among those who keep order, I
am Yama, the god of death. {30} Among the Daityas, the demon race, I am
Prahlada, and among those who count, I am Time. Among beasts I am the
lion, the king of beasts, and among birds, Garuda, the son of Vinata.

{31} Among things that purify, I am the wind. Among those who bear arms,
I am Rama. Among the creatures of the sea I am the makara, the great sea
monster, and among rivers, the Ganges. {32} Of all creation I am the
beginning, the end, and the middle too, Arjuna. Among the kinds of
knowledge I am the knowledge of the self; and in those who debate, I am
the reasoning itself.

{33} Among letters I am the letter A, and among ways of joining words,
the compound that simply pairs them. I alone am Time that never runs
out; I am the one who sets all things in order, facing every direction.
{34} I am death, which carries off everything, and the birth of all that
is still to be. And among the powers that are feminine, I am fame,
fortune, and speech, memory, wisdom, steadfastness, and patience.

{35} Among the chants of the Sama Veda I am the Brihat Saman, the great
chant; among metres, the Gayatri. Among months I am Margashirsha, when
the year turns cool; among seasons, spring, the bringer of flowers.
{36} In those who cheat, I am the game of dice. I am the brilliance of
the brilliant. I am victory, I am resolve, I am the goodness of the
good.

{37} Among the Vrishnis, my own clan, I am Krishna, Vasudeva's son;
among the Pandavas, I am Arjuna, you. Among the sages I am Vyasa as
well, and among poets, the poet Ushanas. {38} In those who tame others I
am the rod of punishment; in those who want to win, I am good policy.
Among secret things I am silence, and in those who know, I am their
knowledge.

{39} Whatever is the seed of all beings, Arjuna, that is what I am.
There is nothing, moving or unmoving, that could exist without me.
{40} There is no end to my divine glories, Arjuna. What I have told you
is only a sketch of how far my glory reaches. {41} Whatever is glorious,
whatever is splendid or mighty, understand that every one of these was
born from a single spark of my brilliance.

{42} But what use is it to you, Arjuna, to know all this in detail? I
hold up this whole world with a single fragment of myself, and remain.
