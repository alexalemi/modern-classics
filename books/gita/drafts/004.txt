Chapter 5: The Way of Renunciation

Arjuna said:

{1} Krishna, you praise renunciation of action, and then you praise yoga
again. Tell me for certain: which one of the two is better?

Krishna said:

{2} Renunciation and the yoga of action both lead to the highest good.
But of the two, the yoga of action is better than the renunciation of
action. {3} The person who neither hates nor craves should be known as
one who has always renounced. Free from the pairs of opposites, Arjuna,
he is easily set free from his bonds.

{4} It is children, not the learned, who talk about Sankhya and yoga as
if they were different. Anyone who truly commits himself to just one of
them gets the fruit of both. {5} The place the followers of Sankhya
reach is reached by the followers of yoga too. Whoever sees that Sankhya
and yoga are one, sees truly.

{6} But renunciation is hard to reach without yoga, Arjuna. A sage who
is disciplined in yoga reaches Brahman before long. {7} The one who is
disciplined in yoga, whose self is pure, who has mastered himself and
conquered his senses, whose self has become the self of all beings — he
is not stained, even when he acts.

{8} The disciplined person who knows the truth will think, "I am not
doing anything at all," even while he is seeing, hearing, touching,
smelling, eating, walking, sleeping, breathing, {9} talking, letting go,
taking hold, even opening and closing his eyes. He keeps it firmly in
mind that it is only the senses at work on the things of the senses.

{10} A person who acts while setting all his actions down in Brahman and
giving up attachment is not stained by sin, just as a lotus leaf is not
wetted by the water. {11} Yogis do their work with the body, with the
mind, with the understanding, even with the senses alone, giving up
attachment, so as to make themselves pure.

{12} The disciplined person gives up the fruit of action and reaches a
peace that lasts. The undisciplined person, driven by desire and
attached to the fruit, is bound.

{13} The one who lives in the body, once he is master of himself and has
renounced all action in his mind, sits at ease in the city of nine
gates, the body, neither acting nor making anything act. {14} The Lord
does not create anyone's sense of being the doer, or anyone's actions,
or the link between action and its fruit. It is the nature of things
that does all that. {15} The Lord who is everywhere does not take on
anyone's sin, or anyone's good deeds either. Knowledge is covered over
by ignorance, and that is why living creatures are confused.

{16} But in those whose ignorance has been destroyed by knowledge of the
self, knowledge shines like the sun and lights up that highest reality.
{17} Their understanding is fixed on that, their self is in that, they
stand firm in that, and that is their whole aim. Their stains washed
away by knowledge, they go to the place from which there is no coming
back.

{18} The learned look with the same eye on a Brahmin rich in learning
and good manners, on a cow, an elephant, a dog, and an outcaste who
cooks dogs for food. {19} Those whose minds rest in that sameness have
conquered the world of birth here and now. Brahman is flawless and the
same in everything, so they stand firm in Brahman.

{20} Such a person does not rejoice when he gets what he likes, or
shudder when he gets what he dislikes. His understanding is steady, he
is not confused; he knows Brahman, and he stands firm in Brahman.
{21} When his self is not attached to the touch of outside things, he
finds the happiness that is in the self. His self disciplined in the
yoga of Brahman, he enjoys a happiness that never fades.

{22} The pleasures born of contact with the world are nothing but wombs
of pain, Arjuna. They have a beginning and an end, and the wise take no
delight in them. {23} The person who can hold out against the surge of
desire and anger here in this life, before he is freed from his body —
he is disciplined, and he is happy.

{24} The person whose happiness is within, whose delight is within, and
whose light is within: that yogi becomes Brahman and reaches the nirvana
of Brahman. {25} Seers whose stains have worn away, whose doubts have
been cut through, who have mastered themselves and who delight in the
good of all beings, reach the nirvana of Brahman. {26} For those who
strive, who are free of desire and anger, who have mastered their
thoughts and come to know the self, the nirvana of Brahman is all around
them.

{27} The sage shuts out the touch of the outside world and fixes his
gaze between his eyebrows. He makes the breath going out and the breath
coming in even as they pass through his nostrils. {28} His senses, his
mind and his understanding are under control. Freedom is his highest
aim, and longing, fear and anger have left him. A sage like that is free
for good.

{29} Whoever knows me as the one who enjoys every sacrifice and every
austerity, the great Lord of all the worlds, the friend of all beings,
reaches peace.
