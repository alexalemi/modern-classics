Chapter 11: The Vision of the Universal Form

Arjuna said:

{1} Out of kindness to me, you have told me the highest secret, the
teaching about the self, and your words have driven this confusion of
mine away. {2} I have heard from you in full how living beings come into
existence and how they pass away, Krishna, you whose eyes are like lotus
petals, and I have heard about your greatness, which never fails.

{3} It is just as you say, supreme Lord. But I want to see it: your form
as God, Supreme Person. {4} If you think I am able to see it, master of
yoga, then show me your own imperishable self.

Krishna said:

{5} Look, Arjuna. See my forms, by the hundreds and by the thousands, of
every kind, divine, of every color and every shape. {6} See the gods of
every order: the Adityas, the Vasus, the Rudras, the two Ashvins, and
the Maruts too. See wonders, many wonders, that no one has ever seen
before. {7} Here, today, see the whole universe, everything that moves
and everything that does not, gathered in one place in my body, and
anything else you want to see.

{8} But you cannot see me with these eyes of yours. I will give you a
divine eye. See the power that makes me Lord!

Sanjaya said:

{9} When he had said this, my king, Krishna, the great master of yoga,
showed Arjuna his highest form, his form as Lord of all. {10} It had
countless mouths and eyes, countless marvelous things to see, countless
heavenly ornaments, countless heavenly weapons raised to strike. {11} He
wore heavenly garlands and heavenly robes, he was anointed with heavenly
perfumes: God, made of every wonder there is, endless, with faces turned
in every direction.

{12} If the light of a thousand suns were to blaze up in the sky all at
once, it might be something like the light of that great being.

{13} There, in the body of the God of gods, Arjuna, the son of Pandu,
saw the whole universe gathered in one place, with all its countless
divisions. {14} Then, overwhelmed with wonder, his hair standing on end,
Arjuna bowed his head to God, joined his palms, and spoke.

Arjuna said:

{15} God, I see the gods in your body, and every kind of living thing,
crowd upon crowd: Brahma the lord, seated on his lotus throne, and all
the seers, and the heavenly serpents. {16} I see you everywhere, endless
in form, with arms and bellies and mouths and eyes beyond counting. I
see no end of you, no middle, and no beginning, Lord of the universe,
you whose form is everything.

{17} I see you crowned, carrying a mace and a discus, a mass of light
blazing on every side, so bright that from any direction it is hard to
look at you: the glare of a burning fire, of the sun, beyond all
measure. {18} You are the imperishable, the highest thing there is to
know. You are the final resting place of this universe. You never fail;
you are the guardian of the eternal law. You are the everlasting Person.
That is what I believe.

{19} I see you without beginning, middle or end, your strength endless,
your arms endless, the sun and the moon your eyes, your mouth a blazing
fire that devours the offerings, scorching this whole universe with your
heat. {20} The space between heaven and earth, and every direction there
is, are filled with you alone. Seeing this wonderful and terrible form
of yours, great one, the three worlds are shaking.

{21} There, crowds of gods are entering into you. Some, terrified, join
their palms and call out your praises. Crowds of great seers and of
Siddhas, the beings made perfect, cry "May all be well!" and praise you
with hymns, rich and full. {22} The Rudras and the Adityas, the Vasus
and the Sadhyas, the All-Gods, the two Ashvins, the Maruts and the
Ushmapas, the ancestors who drink the steam of the offerings, crowds of
Gandharvas, of yakshas, of demons and Siddhas: all of them are staring
at you in amazement.

{23} You of the mighty arms, seeing your vast form, with its many mouths
and eyes, its many arms and thighs and feet, its many bellies, its many
terrible tusks, the worlds are shaking, and so am I. {24} When I see you
touching the sky, blazing, colored every color, your mouths gaping wide,
your huge eyes on fire, my innermost self shakes, and I can find no
courage and no peace, Vishnu.

{25} When I see your mouths, jagged with tusks, glowing like the fire of
Time that burns the world at its end, I lose all sense of direction, and
I find no shelter. Have mercy, Lord of gods, home of the world!

{26} And all those sons of Dhritarashtra, together with the crowds of
kings, Bhishma, Drona, and Karna there, the charioteer's son, along with
the chief warriors on our side too, {27} are rushing into your mouths,
terrible with tusks, frightening to see. Some hang caught between your
teeth, their heads crushed to powder.

{28} As the many rushing currents of the rivers race toward the sea, so
these heroes of the world of men pour into your flaming mouths. {29} As
moths fly faster and faster into a blazing fire, to their death, so
these people rush faster and faster into your mouths, to their death.
{30} You lick your lips again and again, swallowing whole worlds on
every side with your flaming mouths. Your fierce rays fill the whole
universe with their fire, and they scorch it, Vishnu.

{31} Tell me who you are, you with this terrible form! I bow to you,
greatest of the gods: have mercy! I want to understand you, you who were
there at the beginning, for I do not know what you are doing.

Krishna said:

{32} I am Time, the destroyer of worlds, grown to my full strength, and
I am at work here to sweep these worlds away. Even without you, not one
of these warriors drawn up in the opposing ranks will live.

{33} So stand up and win glory! Defeat your enemies and enjoy a rich
kingdom. I have already killed them myself, long before this. Be only
the instrument, Arjuna, you who can draw a bow with either hand.
{34} Drona and Bhishma, Jayadratha and Karna, and the other great
warriors too: I have killed them already, and you must kill them. Don't
be afraid. Fight! You will defeat your rivals in battle.

Sanjaya said:

{35} When he heard these words of Krishna's, Arjuna, the wearer of the
crown, joined his palms, trembling, and bowed. Then, terrified,
stammering, bowing down again, he spoke to Krishna once more.

Arjuna said:

{36} It is right, Krishna, that the world rejoices and delights in your
glory, that the fiends run in terror in every direction, and that all
the crowds of Siddhas bow down to you. {37} And why should they not bow
to you, great one? You are greater even than Brahma; you are the first
maker of all. Endless one, Lord of gods, home of the world: you are the
imperishable, what is and what is not, and what lies beyond them both.

{38} You are the first of the gods, the ancient Person. You are the
final resting place of this universe. You are the knower and what is to
be known, and the highest home. You run all through this universe, you
whose forms are endless. {39} You are Vayu, the wind; you are Yama,
Agni, Varuna, and the moon. You are Prajapati, and the great-grandfather
of all. I bow to you, I bow to you a thousand times, and again, and once
more I bow to you, I bow to you! {40} I bow to you in front of you and
behind you; I bow to you on every side, you who are everything. Your
strength is endless, your might beyond measure. You fill everything, and
so you are everything.

{41} Whatever I said rashly, thinking of you as my friend, calling out
"Krishna!" or "Yadava!" as if you were just one more man of your clan,
or "Hey, friend!" because I did not know this greatness of yours,
whether out of carelessness or out of love; {42} and whatever disrespect
I showed you as a joke, when we were playing or resting, sitting or
eating, alone or in front of others: for all of it I ask you to forgive
me, you who are beyond all measure.

{43} You are the father of the world, of everything that moves and
everything that stands still. You are the one it worships, its teacher,
greater than any teacher. There is no one equal to you in all three
worlds. How could anyone else be greater, you whose power has no match?
{44} So I bow down, I lay my body at your feet, and I beg your grace,
Lord who deserves all praise. As a father bears with his son, as a
friend with his friend, as a lover with the one he loves, so please,
God, bear with me.

{45} I have seen what no one ever saw before, and I am thrilled, but my
mind is shaking with fear. Show me that other form of yours, God, the
one I know. Have mercy, Lord of gods, home of the world! {46} I want to
see you just as before, crowned, holding the mace, with the discus in
your hand. Take on that four-armed form again, you of the thousand arms,
you whose body is the universe.

Krishna said:

{47} Arjuna, I was pleased with you, and so, by my own power, I showed
you this highest form, made of light, the whole universe, endless, the
first of all, a form that no one but you has ever seen. {48} Not by the
Vedas, not by sacrifice or by study, not by gifts, not by rituals, not
by fierce austerities can I be seen in this form in the world of men by
anyone but you, greatest hero of the Kurus.

{49} Don't be shaken, and don't be bewildered at the sight of this
terrible form of mine. Put away your fear, let your heart be glad, and
look again: here is my form, the one you know.

Sanjaya said:

{50} When Krishna, Vasudeva's son, had said this to Arjuna, he showed
him his own form once more. The great being took on his gentle shape
again and comforted the terrified man.

Arjuna said:

{51} Krishna, now that I see this gentle human form of yours, I have
come to my senses, and I am myself again.

Krishna said:

{52} This form of mine that you have seen is very hard to see. Even the
gods long, always, for a sight of it. {53} Not by the Vedas, not by
austerity, not by giving and not by sacrifice can I be seen as you have
seen me. {54} But by devotion, devotion to me alone, Arjuna, I can be
known like this, and seen as I truly am, and entered into.

{55} Whoever does my work, whoever has me as his highest goal, whoever
is devoted to me, free from attachment, without hatred for any creature:
that person comes to me, Arjuna.
