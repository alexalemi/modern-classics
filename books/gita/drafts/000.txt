Chapter 1: Arjuna's Despair

Dhritarashtra said:

{1} Sanjaya, tell me. My sons and the sons of Pandu have gathered at
Kurukshetra, the field of the Kurus, which is also the field of dharma,
the sacred order that holds the world together. They have met there
spoiling for battle. What did they do?

Sanjaya said:

{2} King Duryodhana, your son, saw the army of the Pandavas drawn up for
battle. He went over to his teacher, Drona, and spoke to him.

{3} "Teacher, look at this huge army of the sons of Pandu, put into
battle order by Drupada's son, your own clever pupil. {4} Here are
heroes, mighty archers, every one of them a match for Bhima and Arjuna
in a fight: Yuyudhana, and Virata, and Drupada, a great chariot-fighter;
{5} Dhrishtaketu and Chekitana, and the brave king of Kashi; Purujit and
Kuntibhoja, and Shaibya, a bull among men; {6} Yudhamanyu the bold and
Uttamaujas the brave; Subhadra's son, and the sons of Draupadi. Great
chariot-fighters, every one.

{7} "Now hear about ours, best of Brahmins: the finest men on our side,
the commanders of my army. I will name them for you so that you know who
they are. {8} There is you yourself, and Bhishma, and Karna, and Kripa,
who wins his battles; Ashvatthaman and Vikarna, and Somadatta's son as
well. {9} And there are many other heroes ready to give their lives for
me, armed with every kind of weapon, and all of them masters of war.
{10} And yet this strength of ours, with Bhishma guarding it, is not
enough, while that strength of theirs, with Bhima guarding it, is
enough. {11} So all of you, every one, hold your positions at every
point along the line, and above all, protect Bhishma."

{12} Then the oldest of the Kurus, Bhishma, grandfather to both armies
and a mighty warrior, let out a lion's roar and blew his conch, loud and
high, to put heart into Duryodhana. {13} At that, conches and
kettledrums, tabors and drums and trumpets of cow-horn all broke out at
once, and the noise was deafening.

{14} Then Krishna and Arjuna, the son of Pandu, standing in their great
chariot yoked to white horses, blew their heavenly conches. {15} Krishna
blew Panchajanya; Arjuna blew Devadatta; and Bhima, the man with a
wolf's appetite and a terrible hand in a fight, blew his great conch,
Paundra. {16} King Yudhishthira, the son of Kunti, blew Anantavijaya;
Nakula and Sahadeva blew Sughosha and Manipushpaka. {17} The king of
Kashi, a superb archer, and Shikhandi, the great chariot-fighter,
Dhrishtadyumna and Virata, and Satyaki, who had never been beaten,
{18} Drupada and the sons of Draupadi, and Subhadra's strong-armed son:
all of them, my king, on every side, blew their conches, each his own.
{19} That roar tore at the hearts of your sons, and the uproar made the
sky and the earth ring.

{20} Then Arjuna, the son of Pandu, whose banner bears the monkey, saw
your sons standing in their ranks. The arrows were about to start
flying. He raised his bow, {21} and he said this to Krishna, my king:
"Krishna, drive my chariot out and halt it between the two armies,
{22} so that I can look at these men standing here hungry for battle,
and see whom I have to fight in this struggle that is about to begin.
{23} I want to see the men who have come here to fight, eager to do a
favor in battle for Dhritarashtra's wicked son."

{24} When Arjuna had said this, my king, Krishna drove that finest of
chariots out between the two armies and halted it {25} in front of
Bhishma and Drona and all the rulers of the earth, and said, "Arjuna,
look at all the Kurus gathered here."

{26} And there Arjuna saw, standing in both armies, fathers and
grandfathers, teachers, uncles, brothers, sons and grandsons, and
friends, {27} fathers-in-law and dear companions. The son of Kunti
looked at them all, all these kinsmen standing there in their ranks,
{28} and he was overcome by a deep pity. In despair, he said:

"Krishna, when I see these kinsmen of mine standing here, eager to
fight, {29} my legs give way under me and my mouth goes dry. My body is
shaking and my hair is standing on end. {30} Gandiva, my bow, is
slipping from my hand, and my skin is burning all over. I can't stand
still, and my mind seems to be spinning. {31} I see bad omens, Krishna,
and I can see no good coming from killing my own people in battle.

{32} "I don't want victory, Krishna, or a kingdom, or pleasures. What
use is a kingdom to us, or enjoyment, or even life? {33} The very people
for whose sake we wanted a kingdom, enjoyments and pleasures are
standing here in battle, ready to give up their lives and their riches:
{34} teachers, fathers, sons, and grandfathers too, uncles,
fathers-in-law, grandsons, brothers-in-law and other relations. {35} I
don't want to kill them, Krishna, even if they kill me. I would not do
it to rule all three worlds, heaven and earth and the sky between, so
how could I do it for this earth alone?

{36} "What joy could we ever have from killing Dhritarashtra's sons,
Krishna? Sin would cling to us for killing them, even though they came
at us with weapons drawn. {37} So it is not right for us to kill
Dhritarashtra's sons and their kin. How could we be happy, Krishna,
after killing our own people? {38} Their minds are ruined by greed, and
they can see nothing wrong in destroying a family and no crime in
betraying friends. But even so, {39} we can see the wrong in destroying
a family, Krishna, so why shouldn't we know enough to turn back from
this evil?

{40} "When a family is destroyed, its ancient sacred laws are lost, and
when those laws are lost, lawlessness overruns the whole family.
{41} And when lawlessness takes over, Krishna, the women of the family
are corrupted; and when the women are corrupted, the classes become
mixed. {42} That mixing drags to hell both the family and the men who
destroyed it, for their ancestors fall too, cut off from the offerings
of rice and water that the living make to their dead. {43} Through these
crimes of the men who destroy families, which bring about the mixing of
the classes, the laws of birth and the eternal laws of the family are
wiped out. {44} And men whose family laws have been wiped out, Krishna,
are sure to live in hell. So we have always been told.

{45} "Oh, how terrible! We are set on committing a great sin, ready to
kill our own people out of greed for the pleasures of a kingdom. {46} If
Dhritarashtra's sons, weapons in hand, killed me in battle while I stood
unarmed and did not fight back, it would be better for me."

{47} With these words, there on the battlefield, Arjuna sank down onto
the seat of his chariot. He let his bow and arrows fall, and his heart
was drowned in grief.
