Chapter 4: Knowledge and the Giving Up of Action

Krishna said:

{1} I taught this yoga, which never perishes, to Vivasvat, the sun.
Vivasvat taught it to Manu, the father of the human race, and Manu told
it to Ikshvaku, the first of the kings. {2} Handed down like this from
one to the next, it was known to the royal seers, the kings who were
also sages. But over a great stretch of time, Arjuna, this yoga was lost
to the world. {3} And this is the same ancient yoga I have taught you
today, because you are devoted to me and you are my friend. It is the
highest secret there is.

Arjuna said:

{4} But you were born recently, and Vivasvat was born long ago. How am I
supposed to understand that you taught it to him in the beginning?

Krishna said:

{5} I have lived through many births, Arjuna, and so have you. I know
them all. You do not know yours.

{6} Though I am unborn, and my self never changes, and though I am the
Lord of all beings, I take command of my own nature and come into being
by my own power of illusion. {7} Whenever dharma fails, Arjuna, and
lawlessness rises up, then I send myself forth. {8} To protect the good,
to destroy those who do evil, and to set dharma firmly in its place
again, I come into being age after age.

{9} Whoever truly understands my birth and my action, and that they are
divine, does not come back to be born again when he leaves his body. He
comes to me, Arjuna. {10} Many people, free of longing and fear and
anger, filled with me, taking refuge in me, made pure by the austerity
of knowledge, have come into my own state of being. {11} However people
come to me, that is how I welcome them. Everywhere, Arjuna, people
follow my path.

{12} People who want their actions to succeed make sacrifices here to
the gods, since in the world of human beings success that comes from
action comes quickly. {13} I created the four classes, dividing them
according to their gunas and their work. Know that though I made them, I
am not a doer, and I never change. {14} Actions do not stain me, and I
have no longing for the fruit of action. Whoever knows me like this is
not bound by his actions. {15} The people of old who wanted freedom knew
this, and they acted. So you too should act, just as the people of old
acted long ago.

{16} What is action, and what is inaction? Even the wise are confused
about this. So I will tell you what action is, and once you know it you
will be set free from evil. {17} You have to understand what action is,
you have to understand what wrong action is, and you have to understand
what inaction is. The way of action is deep and hard to see into.

{18} The person who sees inaction in action, and action in inaction, is
the one with real understanding among human beings. He is disciplined,
even while he is doing every kind of work. {19} When everything a person
sets out to do is free of desire and of selfish intention, and his
actions have been burned up in the fire of knowledge, the wise call him
a truly learned man.

{20} He has given up his attachment to the fruit of action. He is always
content, and he leans on nothing. Even when he throws himself into
action, he is not really doing anything at all. {21} He hopes for
nothing; his mind and his self are under control; he has given up
everything he owned. Doing only what the body has to do, he commits no
fault. {22} He is content with whatever comes to him by chance. He has
gone beyond the pairs of opposites, and he envies no one. He is the same
in success and failure. Even when he acts, he is not bound.

{23} When a person has let go of attachment, when he is free, when his
mind stands firm in knowledge, and when he does his work as a sacrifice,
all of his action melts away.

{24} The offering is Brahman, the butter poured out is Brahman, and it
is poured by Brahman into the fire that is Brahman. A person whose every
action is deep concentration on Brahman will go to Brahman.

{25} Some yogis offer sacrifice to the gods. Others offer sacrifice
itself as a sacrifice into the fire of Brahman. {26} Some offer hearing
and the other senses into the fires of restraint. Others offer sound and
the other things of the senses into the fires of the senses. {27} Others
offer all the work of the senses and all the work of the breath into the
fire of the yoga of self-control, kindled by knowledge.

{28} There are others too, those who strive, bound by strict vows, whose
sacrifice is their wealth, or their austerity, or their yoga, or
reciting the Vedas to themselves and knowing them. {29} Others again,
devoted to the control of the breath, offer the outgoing breath into the
incoming breath and the incoming breath into the outgoing, and stop the
passage of both. {30} Still others eat only a measured amount of food
and offer their life-breaths into their life-breaths.

All of these people know what sacrifice is, and their sacrifices have
washed away their stains. {31} Those who eat what is left over from the
sacrifice, the nectar of immortality, go to Brahman, which lasts
forever. Even this world is not for someone who makes no sacrifice,
Arjuna, so how could the other one be? {32} Sacrifices of many kinds are
spread out like this, offered into the mouth of Brahman. Know that all
of them are born of action. Once you know that, you will be set free.

{33} The sacrifice of knowledge is better than any sacrifice of things,
Arjuna. All action, every bit of it, comes to its fulfillment in
knowledge. {34} Learn it by bowing at the feet of those who know, by
asking them questions, by serving them. The wise, who see things as they
truly are, will teach you knowledge. {35} Once you know it, Arjuna, you
will never fall into confusion like this again, and by it you will see
every being, without exception, in the self, and then in me.

{36} Even if you were the worst sinner of all sinners, you would cross
over all your wrongdoing on the raft of knowledge alone. {37} Just as a
blazing fire burns its firewood to ash, Arjuna, the fire of knowledge
burns all actions to ash. {38} Nothing in this world purifies like
knowledge. A person who has been perfected in yoga finds it, in time, in
himself.

{39} A person with faith gains knowledge, if he is devoted to it and has
his senses under control, and once he has gained knowledge he soon
reaches the highest peace. {40} But the ignorant person, who has no
faith and whose very self is doubt, is lost. For the one whose self is
doubt, there is neither this world nor the next, and no happiness
either.

{41} Actions do not bind a person who has renounced action through yoga,
who has cut his doubt to pieces with knowledge, and who is
self-possessed, Arjuna. {42} So take up your own sword of knowledge, cut
away this doubt that was born of ignorance and is sitting in your heart,
and hold fast to yoga. Stand up, Arjuna!
