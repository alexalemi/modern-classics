Chapter 8: The Imperishable

Arjuna said:

{1} Krishna, Supreme Person, what is that Brahman? What is the adhyatma?
What is action? And what is it that is called the adhibhuta, and what is
meant by the adhidaiva? {2} Who is the adhiyajna here in this body,
Krishna, and how? And how are you to be known at the hour of death by
those who have mastered themselves?

Krishna said:

{3} Brahman is the imperishable, the highest. The adhyatma is a being's
own essential nature. Action is the name for the creative outpouring
that brings beings into existence. {4} The adhibhuta is everything that
perishes. The adhidaiva is the spirit, the Person. And the adhiyajna is
I myself, here in this body, Arjuna, best of all who wear a body.

{5} Whoever leaves the body at the hour of death and goes forth thinking
of me alone comes into my own state of being. There is no doubt about
it. {6} Whatever state of being a person is thinking of when he finally
leaves the body, Arjuna, that is the state he goes to, since he has
always been shaped by it. {7} So at all times remember me, and fight.
With your mind and your understanding fixed on me, you will come to me
alone, beyond any doubt.

{8} A person whose thoughts are disciplined by the yoga of steady
practice, and never stray to anything else, and who keeps meditating on
the Supreme Person, the divine one, goes to him, Arjuna. {9} Whoever
keeps in mind the seer, the ancient one, the ruler of all, finer than
the finest atom, the one who holds up everything, whose form no mind can
picture, shining like the sun beyond all darkness; {10} whoever, at the
hour of death, with an unwavering mind, held by devotion and by the
power of yoga, draws the breath of life up between the eyebrows and
holds it there: he reaches that supreme, divine Person.

{11} What those who know the Vedas call the imperishable, what those who
strive, free of passion, enter, what people long for when they take the
vow of the celibate student: that place I will now describe to you
briefly. {12} Whoever closes every gate of the body, shuts the mind up
in the heart, draws the breath of life up into the head, and stands firm
in the concentration of yoga, {13} and then leaves the body and goes
forth saying Om, the one syllable that is Brahman, and remembering me,
reaches the highest goal.

{14} When someone thinks of me constantly, day after day, his thoughts
never turning to anything else, Arjuna, I am easy to reach for that
yogi, who is always disciplined. {15} Once they have come to me, these
great souls do not come back to rebirth, that home of suffering, that
passes away. They have reached the highest perfection.

{16} All the worlds, up to and including the world of Brahma, the
creator god, return again and again, Arjuna. But for whoever comes to
me, there is no rebirth. {17} People who truly understand day and night
know that a day of Brahma lasts a thousand ages, and that his night ends
only after a thousand ages more. {18} When his day dawns, everything
that can be seen comes streaming out of the unmanifest; when his night
falls, all of it dissolves back into that same thing, which is called
the unmanifest. {19} This same great multitude of beings comes into
existence again and again, Arjuna, and dissolves helplessly when night
falls, and streams out again when day dawns.

{20} But beyond that unmanifest there is another state of being, another
unmanifest, eternal, which does not perish when all beings perish.
{21} This unmanifest is called the imperishable, and they say it is the
highest goal. Those who reach it never come back. That is my supreme
home. {22} That Supreme Person, Arjuna, in whom all beings live and by
whom this whole world is spread out, can be reached by devotion that
turns to nothing else.

{23} Now I will tell you about the times at which yogis who go forth
from the body either never return or return again, Arjuna. {24} Fire,
light, daytime, the bright half of the month, the six months when the
sun travels north: those who know Brahman and go forth then go to
Brahman. {25} Smoke, night, the dark half of the month, the six months
when the sun travels south: the yogi who goes forth then reaches the
light of the moon, and returns. {26} These two paths, the bright and the
dark, are thought to be the world's eternal paths. By one a person goes
and does not return; by the other he comes back again.

{27} Arjuna, no yogi who knows these two paths is ever confused. So at
all times, be disciplined in yoga. {28} There is a reward of merit laid
down for the Vedas, for sacrifices, for austerities, and for giving. The
yogi who knows all this goes beyond every one of those rewards, and
reaches the supreme place, where everything began.
