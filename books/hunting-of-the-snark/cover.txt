Winslow Homer's *The Fog Warning* (1885). A fisherman in oilskins and a sou'wester rows a small dory over a heavy sea, a catch of halibut in the stern, and looks back over his shoulder at a bank of cloud coming up on the horizon. Homer's sailor is a working man in real danger, not one of the Bellman's crew, but he is at sea in a small boat with something he cannot quite see approaching, and that is the feeling the poem keeps up for eight fits.
