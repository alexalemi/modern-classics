aa	The frontispiece: the trial. The King and Queen of Hearts sit enthroned under a canopy of hearts, the King in his crown and judge’s wig; the White Rabbit stands by with his scroll, the Knave of Hearts waits in chains below, and the dish of tarts lies on the table in front of the court.
ab	The White Rabbit in his checked waistcoat, an umbrella under his arm, taking a watch out of his waistcoat-pocket and frowning at it.
ac	Alice lifts a long curtain in the hall with one hand, the little golden key in the other, and finds the little door behind it.
ad	Alice stands beside the glass table holding the little bottle, its label reading DRINK ME.
ae	Alice after the cake: her neck has stretched to a great length, her small head far above her body, which stands in a little dress on the floor below.
af	Alice, now too large for the hall, kneels on the floor and looks round as the White Rabbit scurries off into the darkness, his gloves and fan dropped at her knee.
ag	Alice swimming in the pool of her own tears, only her head and one arm above the water, reaching out.
ah	Alice and the Mouse swimming side by side, the Mouse ahead of her with its tail trailing under the water.
ai	The party on the bank, wet and dripping: the Mouse in the middle, Alice sitting on the ground with her back to us, and around them the Dodo, the Lory, the Duck and the other birds and animals, an old Crab and her daughter among them.
aj	The Dodo, leaning on a stick, solemnly presents Alice with her own thimble as the other birds and animals crowd round to watch.
ak	Alice, grown enormous, fills the Rabbit’s little room, lying on the floor with one arm out of the window and her head bent against the ceiling.
al	Alice’s giant hand snatches at the air from the window; below it the White Rabbit tumbles backwards into the cucumber-frame, among broken glass.
am	Bill the Lizard shoots up out of the chimney-pot like a sky-rocket, limbs spread, in a cloud of soot and smoke.
an	An enormous puppy crouches among the thistles, eyes wide, as Alice, small beside it, holds out a little stick for it to chase.
ao	The Caterpillar sits on top of the mushroom with its arms folded round its hookah, while Alice stands on tiptoe below, her eyes just level with the edge.
ap	Father William stands on his head in a hayfield, his legs in the air and his coat-tails falling round him, while the young man looks on with a beribboned stick over his shoulder; a rake lies on the ground between them.
aq	Father William turns a back-somersault in at the door, upside down in mid-air, his hat flying off, as his son starts back in surprise.
ar	Father William sits at table, very fat and very pleased with himself, while his son leans across the cloth towards him, holding up a great dish-cover.
as	Father William balances an eel upright on the end of his nose, arms spread, in a meadow by a river, while his son stares at him.
at	The Fish-Footman, in livery and a curled wig, hands the Frog-Footman a great letter sealed with a crown, the invitation from the Queen to play croquet.
au	The Duchess’s kitchen: the Duchess sits nursing the howling baby, the cook stirs the soup behind her with a pepper-box in her other hand, the Cheshire Cat grins on the hearth, and Alice stands looking on.
av	Alice stands in the wood holding the baby, which is turning into a pig in her arms, its snout and ears already showing.
aw	The Cheshire Cat lies along a branch high in a tree, grinning, while Alice stands far below with her hands behind her back, looking up at it.
ax	The Cheshire Cat fading away on its branch: its body has nearly gone among the leaves, and only the grin is still clear.
ay	The Mad Tea-Party: Alice sits in a large arm-chair at one end of the long table, the March Hare and the Hatter lean on the cloth at the other, and the Dormouse sleeps between them.
az	The Hatter sings “Twinkle, twinkle, little bat”, his eyes shut, mouth wide and one hand waving, with the Dormouse asleep at his side among the teacups.
ba	The Hatter and the March Hare push the Dormouse head-first into the teapot.
bb	Three gardeners shaped like playing-cards, the Two, Five and Seven of Spades, paint the white roses of a rose-tree, brushes in hand and a paint-pot at their feet.
bc	The Queen of Hearts glares and points at Alice, the King beside her, while the Knave of Hearts holds the crown on a cushion behind; the court of cards stands round them in the garden.
bd	Alice holds her flamingo under her arm as a croquet mallet, its long neck twisting up to look her in the face, while the hedgehogs serve as balls on the ground.
be	The Cheshire Cat’s head floats in the sky above the croquet-ground, while below the executioner, the King and the Queen argue about whether a head with no body can be cut off, the White Rabbit listening.
bf	The Duchess walks arm in arm with Alice, keeping close to her, the flamingo tucked under Alice’s other arm.
bg	The Gryphon lies asleep in the sun, wings folded and head down on its claws.
bh	The Mock Turtle sits on a rock weeping, with the Gryphon and Alice sitting below it on the shore listening.
bi	The Lobster-Quadrille: the Mock Turtle and the Gryphon dance solemnly round Alice, treading on her toes.
bj	The Lobster, dressed and standing upright at a looking-glass, smoothing his hair with a brush in one great claw: “You have baked me too brown, I must sugar my hair.”
bk	The White Rabbit as the court herald, in a tabard covered with hearts, blowing a trumpet and holding a long parchment scroll.
bl	The Hatter in the witness-box, a teacup in one hand and a piece of bread-and-butter in the other, trembling so much that his shoes come off.
bm	The Hatter hurries out of the court with his teacup and bread-and-butter, without even waiting to put his shoes on.
bn	Alice jumps up in the court, grown to her full size, and her skirt tips over the jury-box, spilling the jurymen out, the Lizard, a frog, a squirrel, birds and others, in a heap.
bo	The King of Hearts reads the letter through his spectacles from his throne; below, the Knave of Hearts stands at the table with the tarts, beside two of the jurors.
bp	The pack of cards rises into the air and comes flying down on Alice, who throws up her arms to beat them off, while the creatures of the court scatter at her feet.
