THUS SPOKE ZARATHUSTRA -- TEXT ANALYSIS AND TRANSLATION STRATEGY
Friedrich Nietzsche, Also sprach Zarathustra: Ein Buch für Alle und Keinen
(Parts One and Two 1883, Part Three 1884, Part Four printed privately in
forty-odd copies in 1885). Translated FROM THE GERMAN (Gutenberg #7205),
with Thomas Common's 1909 English (Standard Ebooks) as a crib only.

This is a scholarly modernization of a canonical philosophical text. Every
claim in it is Nietzsche's, rendered as his: measured, never softened,
never sensationalized, never annotated.


1. THE WORK AND ITS SHAPE

A prophet, Zarathustra (Nietzsche borrows the name of the Persian founder
of Zoroastrianism, the first to make good and evil the engine of the
world, so that he can be the one to undo it), comes down from ten years of
solitude on a mountain to teach. The Prologue is a story: the sun, the old
saint in the forest who has not heard that God is dead, the marketplace
crowd waiting for a tightrope walker, the speech about the overman and
the last man, the crowd's jeering, the walker's fall and death, the
corpse carried into the forest. Then come the discourses -- eighty short
sermons and songs, each closed by the refrain "Thus spoke Zarathustra."
Part One preaches to disciples in a town called the Motley Cow. Part Two
returns to them on the Blessed Isles, and holds the three songs (Night,
Dance, Grave) and the first statements of the will to power. Part Three
is the homecoming and the confrontation with the "most abysmal thought",
the eternal recurrence (the vision of the gateway, the shepherd and the
snake, the Convalescent). Part Four is a comic, almost picaresque
narrative: Zarathustra meets the "higher men" one by one (two kings with
a donkey, a scholar of leeches, a magician, the retired last pope, the
ugliest man who murdered God, a voluntary beggar, his own shadow), feeds
them a Last Supper, watches them fall to worshipping the donkey, and
leaves them at dawn when his sign, a laughing lion among doves, arrives.

Structure, from Nietzsche's own contents list (asserted by prep.py and
again, independently, by check.py): Prologue + 22 discourses / 22 / 16 /
20 = 81 sections, plus the epigraphs that open Parts Two, Three and Four.
85 files: one section per file, one epigraph per file, and "On Old and New
Tablets" (5,057 German words, 30 numbered sections) split in two at its
section XVII. 84,117 German words (\w-tokens) / 82,393 whitespace words.


2. WHOSE ARCHAISM IS IT? (the governing decision)

The crib screens at archaism 30.2 per 1,000 words, one of the highest
measured in the collection, and almost all of it is one costume: thou /
thee / ye / -eth / -est / spake / hath / verily / lo / unto. Apply the
whose-archaism-is-it test feature by feature:

  THE GRAMMAR IS COMMON'S. Nietzsche's German of 1883 uses du / ihr / Sie
  exactly as any German of 1883 did: "du" is the ordinary intimate
  singular, "ihr" the ordinary plural. Common turned both into King James
  English, which makes Zarathustra MORE formal and remote than Nietzsche
  wrote him -- the Augustine/Pusey case exactly. All of it goes, with no
  exemption list. check.py sweeps it.

  THE RHETORIC IS NIETZSCHE'S, and it is a deliberate parody of Luther's
  Bible: the title formula "Also sprach" (Luther's "So spricht der Herr"),
  "Wahrlich, ich sage euch" (Verily I say unto you), "Siehe!", parables,
  beatitude-like series ("I love the one who..."), "Es sei denn, dass ihr
  umkehret und werdet wie die Kindlein", the Last Supper, the litany at
  the Donkey Festival, the Mount of Olives. A reader who cannot hear the
  Bible under the sermon misses half the jokes and most of the blasphemy.
  So the CADENCE STAYS -- parallelism, anaphora, refrains, the short
  declarative, the apostrophe ("Oh my soul", "Oh noon"), the closing
  formula -- and is carried in MODERN WORDS. The test is the register of
  a modern English Bible, not the King James: "Truly I tell you", "Look!",
  "unless you turn and become like little children". That is still
  recognisably scripture to a modern ear and contains no word a
  fourteen-year-old has to look up.

  Locked consequences: "Also sprach Zarathustra." -> "Thus spoke
  Zarathustra." (the book's English title and the refrain everyone knows;
  "thus" is the ONE survival, and it is the formula, not the grammar).
  "Wahrlich" -> "truly" ("Truly I tell you" for the full formula).
  "Siehe!" -> "Look!" (never "Lo" or "Behold"). "O/Oh" -> "oh" (lowercase
  unless it opens a sentence). No "thou" anywhere, including when
  Zarathustra addresses Life, Eternity, his soul or the noon.

THE HUME RULE ALSO APPLIES. Much of Zarathustra is already short, plain,
and declarative ("God is dead." "Man is a rope." "Write with blood.").
If a German sentence is already clear, the English should be clear in the
same shape. Do not decorate it, explain it or "improve" it. A file in
which every sentence has been rewritten has been damaged.


3. VOICE

A prophet who is also a poet, a satirist and, increasingly, a comedian.
Four registers, often in one discourse:
  - SERMON: rhythmic, parallel, aphoristic, addressed to "my brothers".
  - SONG: the Night Song, Dance Songs, Grave Song, Seven Seals, the Song
    of Melancholy, the Daughters of the Desert, the Midnight Song --
    lyrical, intimate, incantatory.
  - NARRATIVE: the Prologue and most of Part Four are storytelling, often
    slapstick (the jester leaping over the tightrope walker, the leech
    scholar with his arm in the swamp, the donkey's "Yeah-aw").
  - MOCKERY: the scholars, poets, tarantulas, the "culture" of the present,
    the higher men -- sharp, funny, contemptuous.
Keep the register of each passage. The parody of solemnity is solemn on
the surface: never wink at the reader.

Nietzsche is a punster and a coiner of words. Rebuild every wordplay on
the English word, never carry it over dead (the Quixote/Sancho lesson):
  - untergehen / Untergang: the sun "goes under" at evening; Zarathustra
    must "go under" as men call it -- descend to them and perish. LOCKED:
    "go under / going under". Prologue 1 makes the sunset explicit once.
  - über / unter: Übermensch / Untergang / Übergang / überwinden: overman,
    going under, going across (a crossing), overcome. This is the reason
    for "overman" (section 4).
  - "Freuden- und Leidenschaften" (joy-passions and suffering-passions)
    -> the title "On Joys and Passions"; inside, let the English play on
    the passions that are joys and the passions that are sufferings.
  - "Hinterweltler" (people of the world behind the world) -> "believers
    in worlds behind".
  - "unbefleckte Erkenntnis" parodies "unbefleckte Empfängnis" (the
    Immaculate Conception) -> "Immaculate Perception".
  - I-A, the donkey's bray, is also "Ja" (yes) -> "Yeah-aw" (section 4).
  - Esel (donkey) / Eselin; Weib / Weiblein (little old woman); the
    "tarantulas" (Taranteln) of equality.
Allusions are folded into the sentence, never footnoted (the Verne rule):
Zarathustra, the Motley Cow, the Blessed Isles, Ariadne, the Sibyl,
Empedocles at Etna (Of Great Events), Luther's "Here I stand, I can do no
other" (Daughters of the Desert), the Pope "retired" from service.


4. LOCKED VOCABULARY

  Übermensch -> overman (lowercase; "overmen" plural). WHY: "superman"
  belongs to the comic book and to Shaw, and fixes the wrong picture
  (a strong individual) where Nietzsche means a goal humanity has not yet
  reached. Keeping the German would make the book's central word the one
  word the reader cannot read, and would cut it off from the over/under
  family the Prologue is built on (overman / going under / going across /
  overcome). "Overman" keeps the family audible in English. The editor's
  introduction gives the German once. (Decision flagged for Alex.)
  der letzte Mensch -> the last man (the last men). Famous; locked.
  der Mensch (as a kind) -> man ("Man is something that must be
  overcome"; "Man is a rope"). Elsewhere "human beings", "people",
  "humanity" as the sentence needs; die Menschen -> people / human beings.
  der höhere Mensch -> the higher man (the higher men) -- Part Four's
  visitors are all men, and the phrase is a title.
  Mitleid / Mitleiden -> pity (never "compassion", which praises it);
  die Mitleidigen -> the pitying.
  Ekel -> disgust (the visceral word, "nausea" where physical).
  Geist -> spirit ("the three metamorphoses of the spirit"; "the spirit of
  gravity" for Geist der Schwere); "mind" only where plainly intellect.
  Leib -> the body. das Ich -> the "I"; das Selbst -> the Self (capital S
  inside "On the Despisers of the Body", where the two are opposed).
  Wille zur Macht -> will to power. Wille -> will.
  ewige Wiederkunft -> eternal recurrence ("the teacher of the eternal
  recurrence"); wiederkommen -> come again / return.
  der Augenblick (the gateway) -> "Moment" (the gateway's name), "moment".
  der grosse Mittag -> the great noon. Mittag -> noon.
  Tugend -> virtue. schenkende Tugend -> gift-giving virtue.
  der Schaffende -> the creator; schaffen -> create.
  der Erkennende -> the seeker of knowledge; Erkenntnis -> knowledge.
  Einsamkeit -> solitude (Verlassenheit -> forsakenness: The Homecoming
  sets them against each other).
  der Nächste -> the neighbor; Nächstenliebe -> love of the neighbor;
  Fernsten-Liebe -> love of the farthest.
  die Viel-zu-Vielen -> the many-too-many.
  Pöbel -> the mob; Gesindel -> the rabble (distinct words, kept distinct).
  Volk -> the people; Völker -> peoples. Heerde -> the herd.
  Weib -> woman; Frau -> woman / wife; Weiblein -> little old woman (the
  diminutive is Nietzsche's and stays); Mann -> man.
  meine Brüder -> my brothers (his address; not "my friends").
  Thor / Thorheit -> fool / folly. heilig -> holy. Ja-sagen -> saying Yes;
  "ein heiliges Ja-sagen" -> "a holy Yes".
  Esel -> donkey (not "ass": modern Bibles use donkey, and "ass" sends a
  fourteen-year-old's attention somewhere else). I-A -> "Yeah-aw" (keeps
  both "yeah" and the bray; flagged for Alex).
  Hinterwelt -> the world behind the world; Hinterweltler -> believers
  in worlds behind.
  Wahrlich -> truly; Siehe -> look; also sprach -> thus spoke.
  Spelling: AMERICAN (neighbor, honor, color).

  Rule of use: a locked term is locked in meaning AND in form; never
  re-gloss it once the ledger marks it spent.


5. FAMOUS PASSAGES (pinned in must_contain.txt; locked renderings)

  Prologue 1: "Like you, I must *go under*, as men call it" (emphasis is his)
  Prologue 2: "this old saint in his forest has not yet heard that God is
    dead!" -- the line is *Gott todt*, emphasised: "that *God is dead*!"
  Prologue 3: "I teach you the overman. Man is something that must be
    overcome." / "The overman is the meaning of the earth." / "remain
    faithful to the earth"
  Prologue 4: "Man is a rope, tied between beast and overman -- a rope over
    an abyss." / "What is great in man is that he is a bridge and not a
    goal"
  Prologue 5: "You must still have chaos in you to give birth to a dancing
    star." / "What is love? What is creation? What is longing? What is a
    star?" asks the last man, and blinks. / "We have invented happiness,"
    say the last men, and they blink.
  001: the spirit becomes a camel, the camel a lion, and the lion at last a
    child; "a holy Yes".
  007: "Write with blood, and you will discover that blood is spirit."
    "I would believe only in a god who could dance."
  011: "State is the name of the coldest of all cold monsters."
  026 and the Part Four epigraph: "God is dead; God died of his pity for
    man." / "Even God has his hell: it is his love of man."
  048: the gateway named "Moment"; "Was that life? Well then! Once more!"
  060: "I come again, with this sun, with this earth, with this eagle,
    with this serpent"; "the teacher of the eternal recurrence".
  062 and 083: the Midnight Song (locked text in running_notes.txt),
    "All joy wants eternity".
  063: "For I love you, oh eternity!" (seven times).
  065: "Become who you are!"
  080: "The desert grows: woe to him who harbors deserts!"
  Also: "will to power", "the great noon", "Thus spoke Zarathustra."
Every pin is a locked rendering in agent_instructions.txt; an agent who
reaches one writes it exactly.


6. HEADINGS AND CONVENTIONS

TITLES. The German titles "Von den ..." are sermon titles (Latin "De ...").
Locked as "On ..." -- the form modern English uses for a treatise or
essay title -- and every title is in prep.TITLES and manifest.json, from
which agents copy line 1 verbatim. Titles without "Von" stay without it
("The Night Song", "The Leech"). Choices worth recording: "On the Three
Metamorphoses" (the established English name; "transformation" in the
body is fine); "On the Believers in Worlds Behind" (Hinterweltler; NOT
"otherworldly", which means dreamy in English); "On Little Old and Young
Women" (Weiblein); "On Involuntary Bliss" (wider Willen); "On Human
Prudence"; "The Seven Seals (Or: The Yes-and-Amen Song)" (subtitle kept in
the heading line); "The Magician" (Zauberer: a conjurer and a fraud, the
Wagner portrait); "Retired" (Ausser Dienst: the last pope, out of
service); "The Donkey Festival" (the medieval Feast of the Ass);
"The Sleepwalker's Song" (Das Nachtwandler-Lied, the title in the first
edition and in Gutenberg; later Archive editions renamed it "Das trunkne
Lied", The Drunken Song, which is why many English readers know it so).

PARTS. part_before "Part One" ... "Part Four", word form (PART_LINE trap),
bare: Nietzsche gives the Parts no titles. (His "Vierter und letzter
Theil" -- "Fourth and last Part" -- cannot be carried in a form
build_ebook.parse_heading reads; flagged for Alex.)

"DIE REDEN ZARATHUSTRA'S" (Zarathustra's Discourses) stands in the
original between the Prologue and the first discourse, a heading with no
text of its own. It closes file 000 as its last line, "Zarathustra’s
Discourses", which the renderer sets as an h4 immediately above "On the
Three Metamorphoses". (A part_before would make it a fifth Part and the
epub would number the Parts 1-5; flagged for Alex.)

EPIGRAPHS. Parts Two, Three and Four open on a motto quoted from
Zarathustra himself (from 022, 007 and 026). Each is its own file
(023, 046, 064) titled "Epigraph to Part Two" etc. -- the mill/de-kruif
precedent -- carrying part_before. The quoted text MUST be word for word
the rendering in the discourse it comes from, so the epigraph files are
written after their sources. The citation is the last paragraph, in the
form   Zarathustra, “On the Gift-Giving Virtue”
(with the curly quotes and the comma: that form is what keeps
assemble.is_subheading from setting it as a heading).

NUMBERED SECTIONS. Nietzsche numbers the sections of longer discourses
(1., 2., ...). They are lone roman-numeral lines ("III") in chapters/,
reference/ and the translation; assemble sets a lone roman numeral as an
h4. check.py asserts the sequence.

VERSE. Prose discourses stay prose; songs set in lines stay in lines,
tab-indented (assemble renders a tabbed block as <pre>). The lineation and
the stanza breaks follow the GERMAN (chapters/), not the crib: the crib
runs the Sibyl's rhyme in 067 as prose and divides 078/080 differently.
Free verse stays free; where Nietzsche rhymes -- the Midnight Song (062,
quoted line by line in 083), the Sibyl’s couplets (067), the Donkey
Festival's little rhyme, the Song of Melancholy's refrains -- rhyme in
English, never at the cost of sense. 6 files carry verse: 062, 067, 069,
078, 080, 083.

EMPHASIS. Nietzsche's letter-spaced words (_x_ in the source) are *x* in
chapters/ and must land on the corresponding English word: same count of
spans per file (check.py). Never add emphasis he did not have.

DASHES. Nietzsche's dash is a large part of his voice ("—", "—:", a
sentence left hanging "—"). Keep his pauses as real em dashes. Never "--".

QUOTATION. Zarathustra's discourses are mostly unquoted direct speech.
Narrative passages (Prologue, Part Four) use quotation marks; follow the
German's quotation structure, curly quotes, single quotes inside double.


7. SENSITIVE CONTENT -- render HIS argument as his

Nietzsche is the most misread author in the collection, in both
directions: conscripted by the people he despised and softened by his
admirers. The edition does neither. Each ruling below is the reason the
agent does NOT need to add, cut, or note anything.

  - THE OVERMAN is a goal beyond humanity, offered as a meaning for the
    earth; it is not a race, a nation, a party or a strongman. Render the
    German; the text says what it means.
  - WOMEN. "On Little Old and Young Women": Zarathustra gives his views
    (woman as a riddle whose solution is pregnancy; man to be trained for
    war, woman for the recreation of the warrior) and these are HIS, set
    down plainly. The notorious last line -- "You are going to women? Do
    not forget the whip!" -- is spoken by THE OLD WOMAN, as the "little
    truth" she gives him to carry home wrapped up. The attribution IS the
    ruling: make it unmistakable who says it, and change nothing else.
    "Weiblein" is Nietzsche's own diminutive (the mulierculis precedent):
    keep "little old woman". Elsewhere Life and Wisdom are women who
    mock and outwit Zarathustra; that is his figure too.
  - WAR AND WARRIORS. "You say it is the good cause that hallows even war?
    I tell you: it is the good war that hallows every cause." Kept exact,
    at the same pitch. Part of the book's argument is that "war" and
    "warrior" are figures for the struggle for knowledge ("my brothers in
    war"); do not resolve the ambiguity for the reader either way.
  - THE MOB, THE RABBLE, THE MANY-TOO-MANY, THE HERD: contempt is the
    point of those passages and stays. Pöbel and Gesindel are kept as two
    words.
  - "ON FREE DEATH" argues for dying at the right time, by choice, and
    against both clinging to life and preaching weariness of it. A
    philosophical argument in a measured register; nothing added,
    nothing dramatized, nothing about method because the text has none.
  - THE PALE CRIMINAL, PUNISHMENT, REVENGE ("On the Tarantulas": the
    preachers of equality as tarantulas of revenge): render the argument.
  - RELIGION. God is dead, the priests, the believers in worlds behind,
    the retired pope, the ugliest man who murdered God, the Last Supper,
    the Donkey Festival's litany (a parody of Revelation 7:12 and of the
    Mass) -- blasphemy is the book's method and is kept at full strength,
    in a measured voice, never in a sneering one.
  - THE SIBYL'S RHYME (067, Conversation with the Kings): a drunken Sibyl
    laments the decline of the world, "Rome's Caesar sank to beast, God
    himself -- became a Jew!" It is Zarathustra's parody of the kings'
    decline-talk, sung in mock-Sibylline couplets. Kept exactly, inside
    the Sibyl's quotation marks, no softening and no note. (Nietzsche's
    published contempt for the antisemites of his day belongs in the
    editor's introduction if anywhere, never in the text.)
  - "MEN", "BROTHERS": Zarathustra addresses men. Keep it; do not
    modernize the audience.


8. SOURCE NOTES

German: Gutenberg #7205, credited to Peter Bellen. It keeps the first
editions' orthography (Theil, gieng, diess, Noth, -niss), and it is an OCR
text. prep.FIXES applies 40 line-pinned corrections:
  - every "9." section number was read as "8." (four discourses);
  - "gieng" was read as the REAL WORD "gierig" (greedy) seven times;
  - "Es kommt die Weit des verächtlichsten Menschen" for "Zeit" -- in the
    last-man passage itself; "Weit" for "Welt" in the Part Four motto;
  - "Ring de Wiederkunft" seven times in the Seven Seals refrain;
  - lost umlauts (fuhrt, Stuck), l read as I (wilI, VieIe), m for rn
    (Göttem), and a dozen more. Each is commented in prep.py.
These were found by witness.py, which diffs the Gutenberg text against
zeno.org (Schlechta's 1954 edition, modernised spelling) after folding
the spelling reform away. ~330 disagreements remain; they are compound
spacing (mit einander / miteinander), elision (lieb / liebe, gehn /
gehen), edition variants, and zeno's own typos, and were classified but
not each adjudicated. A translator who finds a German word that makes no
sense should check the crib, and report it -- do not silently correct.
zeno is a witness only; nothing from it is published.

Crib: Thomas Common's 1909 translation (the Levy Complete Works), from
Standard Ebooks (cleaner than Gutenberg #1998, which adds Elisabeth
Förster-Nietzsche's introduction and Ludovici's notes; neither is used).
Common is literal and reliable on sense, and is the source of the whole
"thou-and-eth" costume. Use him to check what a German sentence says,
never for how to say it. Where he and the German differ (he occasionally
normalizes, and runs some verse as prose), the German wins.


9. RATIO AND CHECKS

English/German whitespace-word ratio: the crib runs 0.99-1.17 per file
(median 1.09). A complete modern rendering should land about 1.05-1.25.
check.py FAILS outside 0.95-1.45, WARNS outside 1.02-1.32. Run verify as
  python3 verify.py zarathustra --min-ratio 0.95 --max-ratio 1.45
Expected English total: ~90,000-100,000 words.
check.py: structure against Nietzsche's contents list; per file heading,
sub-section sequence, "Thus spoke Zarathustra" parity, verse-block parity,
emphasis parity, archaism sweep with no exemptions, banned terms,
renderability (no paragraph that assemble would set as a heading), house
form, ratio.
RENDERABILITY IS A REAL RISK HERE: assemble.is_subheading sets any short
one-line paragraph with mostly capitalised words and no full stop as an
h4, and Zarathustra speaks in short exclamations ("Oh Zarathustra!").
Lowercase "oh" and lowercase "overman" avoid most of it; check.py catches
the rest, and the fix is a rewording inside the meaning, never a merged
or dropped paragraph.
