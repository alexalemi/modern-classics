On Old and New Tablets
(Part 2 of 2)

XVII

There lies the boat, — over there, perhaps, the way leads into the great nothing. — But who wants to step into this “Perhaps”?

Not one of you wants to step into the boat of death! Then how can you claim to be *weary of the world*!

Weary of the world! And you have not even been lifted away from the earth! I always found you still lusting after the earth, still in love with your own weariness of the earth!

Not for nothing does your lip hang down: — a little earthly wish still sits on it! And in your eye — is there not a little cloud of unforgotten earthly pleasure floating there?

There are many good inventions on earth, some useful, others pleasant: for their sake the earth is to be loved.

And there are some things so well invented that they are like a woman’s breast: useful and pleasant at the same time.

But you who are weary of the world! You who are lazy about the earth! You should be beaten with rods! With strokes of the rod your legs should be made lively again.

For: if you are not sick people and worn-out wretches of whom the earth is weary, then you are sly sloths or sweet-toothed, skulking pleasure-cats. And if you do not want to *run* merrily again, then you shall — pass away!

One should not want to be a physician to the incurable: so Zarathustra teaches: — so you shall pass away!

But it takes more *courage* to make an end than to make a new verse: all physicians and poets know that. —

XVIII

Oh my brothers, there are tablets that weariness created, and tablets that laziness created, the laziness that rots: though they speak alike, they want to be heard unalike. —

Look at this man here, wasting away! He is only a hand’s breadth from his goal, but out of weariness he has defiantly lain down here in the dust: this brave one!

Out of weariness he yawns at the way and the earth and the goal and himself: he will not take one step further, — this brave one!

Now the sun glares down on him, and the dogs lick at his sweat: but he lies there in his defiance and would rather waste away: —

— waste away a hand’s breadth from his goal! Truly, you will yet have to drag him by the hair into his heaven, — this hero!

Better still, leave him lying where he has lain down, so that sleep may come to him, the comforter, with cooling, rustling rain:

Let him lie until he wakes up of his own accord, — until of his own accord he takes back all his weariness and what weariness taught through him!

Only, my brothers, chase the dogs away from him, the lazy sneaks, and all that swarming vermin: —

— all that swarming vermin of the “cultured,” which — regales itself on the sweat of every hero! —

XIX

I draw circles around me and holy boundaries; fewer and fewer climb with me up higher and higher mountains, — I am building a mountain range out of holier and holier mountains. —

But wherever you may climb with me, oh my brothers: see to it that no *parasite* climbs with you!

A parasite: that is a crawling thing, a creeping, cringing thing, that wants to grow fat on your sick and sore corners.

And *this* is its art: it guesses where climbing souls are weary: in your grief and your gloom, in your tender shame, it builds its disgusting nest.

Where the strong are weak, where the noble are all too mild, — in there it builds its disgusting nest: the parasite lives where the great have small sore corners.

What is the highest kind of all that is, and what is the lowest? The parasite is the lowest kind; but whoever is of the highest kind feeds the most parasites.

For the soul that has the longest ladder and can go down the deepest: how could the most parasites not sit on that one? —

— the most comprehensive soul, which can run and wander and roam the farthest within itself; the most necessary soul, which out of joy flings itself into accident: —

— the soul that is, which dives into becoming; the soul that has, which *wants* to plunge into willing and desiring: —

— the soul that flees itself, which catches up with itself in the widest circle; the wisest soul, which folly talks to most sweetly: —

— the soul that loves itself most, in which all things have their streaming out and streaming back, their ebb and flood: — oh, how could *the highest soul* not have the worst parasites?

XX

Oh my brothers, am I cruel, then? But I say: what is falling, one should also push!

Everything of today — it is falling, it is decaying: who would want to hold it up! But I — I *want* to push it as well!

Do you know the delight that rolls stones down into steep depths? — These people of today: just look at them, how they roll down into my depths!

I am a prelude to better players, oh my brothers! A precedent! *Do* as I have done before you!

And whoever you do not teach to fly, teach him — to fall faster! —

XXI

I love the brave: but it is not enough to be a swashbuckler, — one must also know: look before you slash, and at *whom*!

And often there is more bravery in holding back and passing by: *so that* one saves oneself for a worthier enemy!

You shall have only enemies who are to be hated, but not enemies to be despised: you must be proud of your enemy: so I taught once before.

For the worthier enemy, oh my friends, you shall save yourselves: that is why you must pass many things by, —

— especially much of the rabble, which makes a din in your ears about the people and peoples.

Keep your eye clean of their for and against! There is much right there, and much wrong: whoever looks on grows angry.

To look in, to lash in — there it is all one: so go away into the forests and lay your sword to sleep!

Go *your* ways! And let the people and peoples go theirs! — dark ways, truly, on which not a single hope flickers anymore, not even like summer lightning!

Let the shopkeeper rule there, where everything that still glitters — is shopkeepers’ gold! It is no longer the time of kings: what calls itself a people today does not deserve kings.

Just look how these peoples themselves now act like shopkeepers: they pick even the smallest advantages out of every heap of rubbish!

They lie in wait for one another, they lie in wait to get something out of one another, — that is what they call “good neighborliness.” Oh, blessed, distant time, when a people said to itself: “I want to be — *master* over peoples!”

For, my brothers: the best shall rule, and the best also wants to rule! And where the teaching says otherwise, there — the best is *lacking*.

XXII

If *they* had — bread for free, woe! What would *they* cry out for! Their keep — that is what really keeps them busy; and they shall have it hard!

They are beasts of prey: in their “working” — there is still preying, in their “earning” — there is still outwitting! That is why they shall have it hard!

So they shall become better beasts of prey, finer, shrewder, *more like man*: for man is the best beast of prey.

Man has already robbed all the animals of their virtues: that is because, of all the animals, man has had it the hardest.

Only the birds are still above him. And if man were to learn to fly as well, woe! *To what height* — would his lust for prey fly!

XXIII

This is how I want man and woman: the one fit for war, the other fit for bearing children, but both fit for dancing with head and legs.

And let every day be lost to us on which we did not dance even once! And let every truth be called false to us that did not bring even one laugh!

XXIV

Your concluding of marriages: see to it that it is not a bad *conclusion*! You concluded too quickly: so there *follows* from it — the breaking of marriages!

And better the breaking of marriages than the bending of marriages, the lying of marriages! — A woman once said to me: “Yes, I broke my marriage, but first my marriage broke — me!”

I have always found the badly paired to be the worst of the vengeful ones: they make all the world pay for it that they can no longer run alone.

That is why I want honest people to say honestly to each other: “We love each other: let us *see to it* that we keep our love! Or shall our vow turn out to be an oversight?”

— “Give us a term and a little marriage, so that we may see whether we are fit for the great marriage! It is a great thing, always to be two!”

This is my advice to all honest people; and what would my love of the overman and of everything that is to come be worth, if I advised and spoke otherwise!

Not only to plant yourselves onward, but *upward* — for that, oh my brothers, may the garden of marriage help you!

XXV

Whoever has grown wise about old origins, look, he will in the end search for the springs of the future and for new origins. —

Oh my brothers, it will not be long before *new peoples* spring up and new springs rush down into new depths.

For the earthquake — it buries many wells, it makes many go thirsty: it also lifts inner powers and secret things into the light.

The earthquake reveals new springs. In the earthquake of old peoples, new springs break out.

And whoever calls out: “Look, here is a well for many who thirst, one heart for many who long, one will for many tools”: — around him a *people* gathers, that is: many who are trying.

Who can command, who must obey — that is what is tried there! Ah, with what long searching and guessing and missing and learning and trying again!

Human society: it is an experiment, so I teach, — a long search: but what it searches for is the one who commands! —

— an experiment, oh my brothers! And *not* a “contract”! Break, break this word of the soft-hearted and the half-and-halfs!

XXVI

Oh my brothers! With whom does the greatest danger to all mankind’s future lie? Is it not with the good and the just? —

— as with those who say, and feel in their hearts: “We already know what is good and just, and we have it too; woe to those who are still searching here!” —

And whatever harm the evil may do: the harm the good do is the most harmful harm!

And whatever harm the world-slanderers may do: the harm the good do is the most harmful harm.

Oh my brothers, someone once looked into the hearts of the good and the just and said: “They are the Pharisees.” But no one understood him.

The good and the just themselves were not allowed to understand him: their spirit is imprisoned in their good conscience. The stupidity of the good is unfathomably shrewd.

But this is the truth: the good *must* be Pharisees, — they have no choice!

The good *must* crucify the one who invents his own virtue! That *is* the truth!

But the second one to discover their country, the country, heart and soil of the good and the just: that was the one who asked: “Whom do they hate most?”

The *creator* is the one they hate most: the one who breaks tablets and old values, the breaker, — him they call the lawbreaker.

For the good — they *cannot* create: they are always the beginning of the end: —

— they crucify the one who writes new values on new tablets, they sacrifice the future to *themselves*, — they crucify all mankind’s future!

The good — they have always been the beginning of the end. —

XXVII

Oh my brothers, did you understand this word as well? And what I once said about the “last man”? —

With whom does the greatest danger to all mankind’s future lie? Is it not with the good and the just?

Break, break the good and the just! — Oh my brothers, did you understand this word as well?

XXVIII

You are running from me? You are frightened? You tremble at this word?

Oh my brothers, when I told you to break the good and the tablets of the good: only then did I put man out to sea, onto his high sea.

And only now does the great terror come over him, the great looking-around, the great sickness, the great disgust, the great seasickness.

The good taught you false coasts and false securities; you were born in the lies of the good and harbored in them. Everything has been lied crooked and bent out of true, right down to the bottom, by the good.

But whoever discovered the land “man” also discovered the land “mankind’s future.” Now you shall be seafarers for me, brave and patient ones!

Walk upright in good time, oh my brothers, learn to walk upright! The sea is storming: many want to pull themselves upright again by you.

The sea is storming: everything is in the sea. Come on! Up and away! You old sailors’ hearts!

Fatherland, indeed! *There* our helm wants to go, where our *children’s land* is! Out there, stormier than the sea, our great longing storms! —

XXIX

“Why so hard?” the kitchen coal once said to the diamond. “Are we not close relatives?” —

Why so soft? Oh my brothers, this is what *I* ask you: are you not — my brothers?

Why so soft, so yielding and giving way? Why is there so much denial and self-denial in your hearts? So little fate in your look?

And if you do not want to be fates and inexorable ones: how could you one day — conquer with me?

And if your hardness does not want to flash and cut and cut to pieces: how could you one day — create with me?

For the creators are hard. And it must seem bliss to you to press your hand on millennia as if on wax, —

— bliss to write on the will of millennia as if on bronze, — harder than bronze, nobler than bronze. Only the noblest is entirely hard.

This new tablet, oh my brothers, I place over you: Become hard! —

XXX

Oh you my will! You turning of all need, you *my* necessity! Preserve me from all small victories!

You dispensation of my soul, which I call destiny! You-in-me! Over-me! Preserve me and save me for one great destiny!

And your last greatness, my will, save it for your last, — so that you are inexorable *in* your victory! Ah, who has not been defeated by his own victory!

Ah, whose eye has not grown dark in this drunken twilight! Ah, whose foot has not staggered and in victory forgotten how — to stand! —

— That I may one day be ready and ripe at the great noon: ready and ripe like glowing bronze, like a cloud pregnant with lightning, like a swelling udder of milk: —

— ready for myself and for my most hidden will: a bow burning for its arrow, an arrow burning for its star: —

— a star, ready and ripe in its noon, glowing, pierced through, blissful before annihilating arrows of the sun: —

— a sun itself, and an inexorable sun-will, ready to annihilate in victory!

Oh will, turning of all need, you *my* necessity! Save me for one great victory! —

Thus spoke Zarathustra.
