The Seven Seals (Or: The Yes-and-Amen Song)

I

If I am a soothsayer, and full of that soothsaying spirit that wanders on a high ridge between two seas —

wanders between the past and the future like a heavy cloud — enemy to sultry lowlands and to everything that is weary and can neither die nor live —

ready for lightning in its dark breast and for the redeeming beam of light, pregnant with lightning bolts that say Yes! and laugh Yes!, ready for soothsaying flashes of lightning: —

— but blessed is the one who is pregnant in this way! And truly, whoever is one day to kindle the light of the future must hang long on the mountain like a heavy storm! —

Oh how could I not burn for eternity, and for the wedding ring of rings — the ring of recurrence!

Never yet have I found the woman by whom I wanted children, except this woman, whom I love: for I love you, oh eternity!

For I love you, oh eternity!

II

If my anger ever burst open graves, moved boundary stones, and rolled old tablets, broken, into steep depths:

If my scorn ever blew moldering words away, and I came like a broom to the cross spiders, and like a sweeping wind to old, stale burial chambers:

If I ever sat rejoicing where old gods lie buried, blessing the world, loving the world, beside the monuments of the old slanderers of the world: —

— for I love even churches and the graves of God, once the sky looks with its pure eye through their broken roofs; I like to sit on broken churches, like grass and red poppies —

Oh how could I not burn for eternity, and for the wedding ring of rings — the ring of recurrence!

Never yet have I found the woman by whom I wanted children, except this woman, whom I love: for I love you, oh eternity!

For I love you, oh eternity!

III

If ever a breath came to me from the creative breath, and from that heavenly necessity which compels even accidents to dance the round dances of the stars:

If I ever laughed with the laughter of the creative lightning, which the long thunder of the deed follows, rumbling but obedient:

If I ever played dice with gods at the gods’ table of the earth, so that the earth quaked and broke open and snorted up rivers of fire: —

— for the earth is a table of the gods, and trembles with creative new words and the throws of gods’ dice: —

Oh how could I not burn for eternity, and for the wedding ring of rings — the ring of recurrence!

Never yet have I found the woman by whom I wanted children, except this woman, whom I love: for I love you, oh eternity!

For I love you, oh eternity!

IV

If I ever drank in full draughts from that foaming bowl of spice and mixing, in which all things are well mixed:

If my hand ever poured the farthest into the nearest, and fire into spirit, and joy into sorrow, and the worst into the kindest:

If I myself am a grain of that redeeming salt which makes all things mix well in the mixing bowl: —

— for there is a salt that binds good to evil; and even the most evil is worthy to be a spice and to make the last foaming over: —

Oh how could I not burn for eternity, and for the wedding ring of rings — the ring of recurrence!

Never yet have I found the woman by whom I wanted children, except this woman, whom I love: for I love you, oh eternity!

For I love you, oh eternity!

V

If I am fond of the sea and of everything that is of the sea’s kind, and fondest of all when it angrily contradicts me:

If that seeking joy is in me which drives the sails toward what is undiscovered, if a seafarer’s joy is in my joy:

If my rejoicing ever cried: “The coast has vanished — now the last chain has fallen from me —

— the boundless roars around me, far out space and time shine for me, come on! up and away! old heart!” —

Oh how could I not burn for eternity, and for the wedding ring of rings — the ring of recurrence!

Never yet have I found the woman by whom I wanted children, except this woman, whom I love: for I love you, oh eternity!

For I love you, oh eternity!

VI

If my virtue is a dancer’s virtue, and I have often leaped with both feet into a golden-emerald rapture:

If my malice is a laughing malice, at home among slopes of roses and hedges of lilies:

— for in laughter all that is evil is gathered together, but pronounced holy and absolved by its own bliss: —

And if this is my alpha and omega, that all that is heavy should become light, every body a dancer, every spirit a bird: and truly, that is my alpha and omega! —

Oh how could I not burn for eternity, and for the wedding ring of rings — the ring of recurrence!

Never yet have I found the woman by whom I wanted children, except this woman, whom I love: for I love you, oh eternity!

For I love you, oh eternity!

VII

If I ever spread still skies above me and flew with my own wings into my own skies:

If I swam playfully in deep distances of light, and the bird-wisdom of my freedom came to me: —

— but this is how bird-wisdom speaks: “Look, there is no above, no below! Throw yourself around, out, back, you light one! Sing! Speak no more!

— are not all words made for the heavy? Do not all words lie to the light one! Sing! Speak no more!” —

Oh how could I not burn for eternity, and for the wedding ring of rings — the ring of recurrence!

Never yet have I found the woman by whom I wanted children, except this woman, whom I love: for I love you, oh eternity!

For I love you, oh eternity!
