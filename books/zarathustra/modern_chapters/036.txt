On the Sublime Ones

Still is the floor of my sea: who would guess that it hides playful monsters!

Unshakable is my depth: but it glitters with swimming riddles and laughter.

I saw a sublime one today, a solemn one, a penitent of the spirit: oh, how my soul laughed at his ugliness!

His chest lifted up, and like those who are drawing in their breath: that is how he stood there, the sublime one, and silent:

Hung all over with ugly truths, the spoils of his hunt, and rich in torn clothes; many thorns hung on him too — but I saw no rose yet.

He had not yet learned laughter and beauty. Gloomy, this hunter came back from the forest of knowledge.

He came home from the fight with wild beasts: but out of his seriousness a wild beast still looks too — one not yet overcome!

He still stands there like a tiger about to spring; but I do not like these tensed souls, and my taste is unkind to all these withdrawn ones.

And you tell me, friends, that there is no arguing about taste and tasting? But all of life is an argument about taste and tasting!

Taste: that is the weight and the scales and the weigher, all at once; and woe to every living thing that wanted to live without arguing over weight and scales and weigher!

If he grew tired of his sublimity, this sublime one: only then would his beauty begin — and only then do I want to taste him and find him to my taste.

And only when he turns away from himself will he leap over his own shadow — and, truly! into *his* sun.

Far too long he sat in the shade, and the cheeks of the penitent of the spirit grew pale; he nearly starved on his expectations.

There is contempt in his eye still, and disgust hides around his mouth. He is resting now, it is true, but his rest has not yet lain down in the sun.

He should do as the bull does; and his happiness should smell of the earth, and not of contempt for the earth.

I would like to see him as a white bull, snorting and bellowing as he goes ahead of the plowshare: and his bellowing should still praise everything earthly!

His face is still dark; the shadow of the hand plays over it. The sense of his eye is still in shadow.

His deed itself is still the shadow over him: the hand darkens the one whose hand it is. He has not yet overcome his deed.

I do love the bull’s neck on him: but now I want to see the eye of the angel as well.

He must still unlearn his hero’s will, too: I want him to be one who is lifted, and not only one who is sublime: — the ether itself should lift him, the will-less one!

He conquered monsters, he solved riddles: but he should also absolve his monsters and riddles; he should still transform them into heavenly children.

His knowledge has not yet learned to smile and to be without jealousy; his flooding passion has not yet grown still in beauty.

Truly, his longing should fall silent and sink down not in satiety, but in beauty! Grace belongs to the generosity of the great-hearted.

His arm laid over his head: that is how the hero should rest, and that is how he should overcome even his resting.

But to the hero above all, the *beautiful* is the hardest thing of all. Beauty cannot be won by any violent will.

A little more, a little less: just this counts for much here, it counts for the most.

To stand with muscles slack and with the will unharnessed: that is the hardest thing for all of you, you sublime ones!

When power becomes gracious and comes down into the visible: beauty is what I call such a coming down.

And from no one do I want beauty as much as from you, you mighty one: let your goodness be your last overpowering of yourself.

I believe you capable of every evil: that is why I want the good from you.

Truly, I have often laughed at the weaklings who think themselves good because their paws are lame!

You shall strive after the virtue of the pillar: it grows ever more beautiful and more delicate, but inwardly harder and more load-bearing, the higher it rises.

Yes, you sublime one, one day you shall be beautiful too, and hold up the mirror to your own beauty.

Then your soul will shudder with divine desires; and there will be worship even in your vanity!

For this is the secret of the soul: only when the hero has abandoned it does there approach it, in a dream — the over-hero.

Thus spoke Zarathustra.
