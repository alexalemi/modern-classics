On the Flies of the Marketplace

Flee, my friend, into your solitude! I see you deafened by the noise of the great men and stung all over by the stings of the little ones.

Forest and rock know how to keep a dignified silence with you. Be like the tree again that you love, the broad-branched one: silent and listening, it hangs over the sea.

Where solitude ends, the marketplace begins; and where the marketplace begins, there begins the noise of the great actors too, and the buzzing of the poisonous flies.

In the world even the best things are still worth nothing without someone to stage them first: the people call these stagers great men.

The people understand little of what is great, that is: what creates. But they have a nose for all the stagers and actors of great things.

Around the inventors of new values the world turns: — it turns invisibly. But around the actors turn the people and fame: that is the way of the world.

The actor has spirit, but little conscience of the spirit. He always believes in whatever makes others believe most strongly — makes them believe in *him*!

Tomorrow he has a new faith, and the day after tomorrow a newer one. He has quick senses, like the people, and a changeable nose for the weather.

To bowl people over — that, to him, means to prove. To drive them wild — that, to him, means to convince. And blood counts with him as the best of all reasons.

A truth that slips only into fine ears he calls a lie and a nothing. Truly, he believes only in gods who make a great noise in the world!

The marketplace is full of solemn jesters — and the people boast of their great men! To them these are the masters of the hour.

But the hour presses them: so they press you. And they want a Yes or a No from you too. Woe, would you set your chair between For and Against?

Do not be jealous of these absolute and pushy ones, you lover of truth! Truth has never yet hung on the arm of an absolute man.

Because of these sudden ones, go back into your safety: only in the marketplace is one ambushed with Yes? or No?

All deep wells experience slowly: they must wait a long time before they know *what* has fallen into their depths.

Everything great happens far from the marketplace and from fame: the inventors of new values have always lived far from the marketplace and from fame.

Flee, my friend, into your solitude: I see you stung all over by poisonous flies. Flee to where a rough, strong wind blows!

Flee into your solitude! You have lived too close to the small and the wretched. Flee from their invisible revenge! Toward you they are nothing but revenge.

Do not raise your arm against them anymore! They are countless, and it is not your lot to be a fly swatter.

Countless are these small and wretched ones; and raindrops and weeds have already been the downfall of many a proud building.

You are not a stone, but already you have been hollowed out by many drops. Many drops will yet make you crack and burst apart.

I see you worn out by poisonous flies, I see you scratched bloody in a hundred places; and your pride will not even get angry.

They want blood from you in all innocence; their bloodless souls crave blood — and so they sting, in all innocence.

But you deep one, you suffer too deeply even from small wounds; and before you have even healed, the same poisonous worm has crawled over your hand.

You are too proud, to my mind, to kill these sweet-toothed nibblers. But take care that it does not become your doom to bear all their poisonous injustice!

They buzz around you with their praise, too: their praise is pushiness. They want to be near your skin and your blood.

They flatter you as one flatters a god or a devil; they whimper before you as before a god or a devil. What does it matter! They are flatterers and whimperers, and nothing more.

Often, too, they show themselves to you as lovable. But that has always been the shrewdness of cowards. Yes, cowards are shrewd!

They think a great deal about you with their narrow souls — you are always questionable to them! Everything that is much questioned becomes questionable.

They punish you for all your virtues. From the bottom of their hearts they forgive you only — your mistakes.

Because you are gentle and just in mind, you say: “They are not to blame for their small existence.” But their narrow souls think: “All great existence is to blame.”

Even when you are gentle with them, they still feel despised by you; and they repay your good turns with hidden ill turns.

Your wordless pride always goes against their taste; they rejoice if for once you are modest enough to be vain.

Whatever we recognize in a person, we also set alight in him. So beware of the small ones!

Before you they feel small, and their lowness smolders and glows against you in invisible revenge.

Did you not notice how often they fell silent when you came up to them, and how their strength went out of them like smoke from a dying fire?

Yes, my friend, you are the bad conscience of your neighbors: for they are unworthy of you. So they hate you and would like to suck your blood.

Your neighbors will always be poisonous flies; what is great in you — that itself must make them more poisonous and ever more fly-like.

Flee, my friend, into your solitude, and to where a rough, strong wind blows. It is not your lot to be a fly swatter. —

Thus spoke Zarathustra.
