On Priests

And once Zarathustra gave his disciples a sign and spoke these words to them:

“Here are priests: and even though they are my enemies, pass by them quietly, with your swords asleep!

Among them too there are heroes; many of them have suffered too much —: so they want to make others suffer.

They are bad enemies: nothing is more vengeful than their humility. And whoever attacks them easily soils himself.

But my blood is related to theirs; and I want my blood honored even in theirs.” —

And when they had passed by, pain seized Zarathustra; and he had not wrestled long with his pain when he began to speak like this:

These priests grieve me. They also go against my taste; but that matters least to me, since I have been among human beings.

But I suffer and have suffered with them: to me they are prisoners and marked men. The one they call Redeemer has put them in chains: —

In chains of false values and delusion-words! Ah, if only someone would redeem them from their Redeemer!

They once thought they had landed on an island when the sea was tossing them about; but look, it was a sleeping monster!

False values and delusion-words: these are the worst monsters for mortals — long does doom sleep and wait in them.

But at last it comes and wakes and eats and swallows whatever built itself huts on it.

Oh, just look at these huts that these priests have built themselves! Churches, they call their sweet-smelling caves.

Oh, this falsified light, this swampy air! Here, where the soul, up toward its height — may not fly!

Instead, their faith commands: “Up the stairs on your knees, you sinners!”

Truly, I would rather see even the shameless than the twisted eyes of their shame and devotion!

Who created such caves and stairs of penance for themselves? Was it not those who wanted to hide and were ashamed before the clear sky?

And only when the clear sky looks in again through broken roofs, and down on grass and red poppies by broken walls — will I turn my heart again to the places of this god.

They called God whatever opposed them and hurt them: and truly, there was much of the hero in their worship!

And they knew no other way to love their god than by nailing man to the cross!

They meant to live as corpses, they dressed their corpses in black; even from their speeches I still smell the foul spice of burial vaults.

And whoever lives near them lives near black ponds, out of which the toad sings its song with sweet profundity.

They would have to sing me better songs for me to learn to believe in their Redeemer: his disciples would have to look more redeemed to me!

I would like to see them naked: for beauty alone should preach penance. But whom will this muffled-up gloom ever persuade!

Truly, their redeemers themselves did not come from freedom and freedom’s seventh heaven! Truly, they themselves never walked on the carpets of knowledge!

The spirit of these redeemers was made of gaps; but into every gap they had put their delusion, their gap-filler, which they called God.

Their spirit had drowned in their pity, and when they swelled and overswelled with pity, a great folly always floated to the top.

Eagerly and with shouting they drove their herd over their footbridge: as if there were only one footbridge to the future! Truly, these shepherds too still belonged among the sheep!

These shepherds had small spirits and roomy souls: but, my brothers, what small countries even the roomiest souls have been until now!

They wrote signs of blood on the way they walked, and their folly taught that truth is proved with blood.

But blood is the worst witness to truth; blood poisons even the purest teaching into delusion and hatred of the heart.

And if someone walks through fire for his teaching — what does that prove! It is more, truly, when one’s own teaching comes out of one’s own burning!

A sultry heart and a cold head: where these meet, the roaring wind arises, the “Redeemer.”

Truly, there have been greater ones and higher-born ones than those the people call redeemers, these sweeping, roaring winds!

And you must be redeemed, my brothers, by still greater ones than all the redeemers were, if you want to find the way to freedom!

There has never yet been an overman. I have seen both of them naked, the greatest man and the smallest man: —

They are still all too similar to each other. Truly, even the greatest I found — all too human!

Thus spoke Zarathustra.
