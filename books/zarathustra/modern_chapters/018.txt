On Little Old and Young Women

“Why do you creep so timidly through the twilight, Zarathustra? And what are you hiding so carefully under your cloak?

Is it a treasure someone gave you? Or a child that was born to you? Or are you now walking the ways of thieves yourself, you friend of the wicked?” —

Truly, my brother! said Zarathustra, it is a treasure that was given to me: it is a little truth that I am carrying.

But it is as unruly as a young child; and if I do not hold its mouth shut, it screams far too loudly.

As I was going my way alone today, at the hour when the sun sinks, a little old woman met me and spoke like this to my soul:

“Zarathustra has said many things to us women too, but he has never spoken to us about woman.”

And I answered her: “About woman, one should speak only to men.”

“Speak to me too about woman,” she said; “I am old enough to forget it again at once.”

And I obliged the little old woman and spoke to her like this:

Everything about woman is a riddle, and everything about woman has one solution: it is called pregnancy.

For woman, man is a means: the end is always the child. But what is woman for man?

The real man wants two things: danger and play. That is why he wants woman, as the most dangerous plaything.

Man should be trained for war, and woman for the recreation of the warrior: everything else is folly.

Fruits that are all too sweet — the warrior does not like them. That is why he likes woman; even the sweetest woman is still bitter.

Woman understands children better than man does, but man is more childlike than woman.

In the real man a child is hidden: it wants to play. Come, you women, discover the child in man for me!

Let woman be a plaything, pure and fine, like a precious stone, lit by the virtues of a world that does not yet exist.

Let the ray of a star shine in your love! Let your hope be called: “May I give birth to the overman!”

Let there be courage in your love! With your love you shall go after the one who makes you afraid!

Let your honor be in your love! Otherwise woman understands little about honor. But let this be your honor: always to love more than you are loved, and never to be second.

Let man fear woman when she loves: then she makes every sacrifice, and every other thing counts as worthless to her.

Let man fear woman when she hates: for at the bottom of his soul man is only evil, but woman there is bad.

Whom does woman hate most? — Thus spoke the iron to the magnet: “I hate you most, because you attract me, but are not strong enough to pull me to you.”

Man’s happiness is called: I will. Woman’s happiness is called: he will.

“Look, just now the world has become perfect!” — so thinks every woman when she obeys out of whole love.

And woman must obey, and find a depth for her surface. Woman’s nature is surface, a restless, stormy skin over shallow water.

But man’s nature is deep; his current roars in underground caves: woman senses his power, but does not grasp it. —

Then the little old woman answered me: “Zarathustra has said many nice things, especially for those who are young enough for them.

It is strange: Zarathustra knows little about women, and yet he is right about them! Does this happen because with woman nothing is impossible?

And now take a little truth by way of thanks! After all, I am old enough for it!

Wrap it up and hold its mouth shut: otherwise it will scream far too loudly, this little truth.”

“Woman, give me your little truth!” I said. And the little old woman spoke like this:

“You are going to women? Do not forget the whip!” —

Thus spoke Zarathustra.
