On the Despisers of the Body

I want to say my word to the despisers of the body. I do not want them to learn differently and teach differently, but only to say farewell to their own bodies — and so fall silent.

“I am body and soul” — so the child speaks. And why should one not speak like the children?

But the awakened one, the one who knows, says: I am body entirely, and nothing besides; and soul is only a word for something about the body.

The body is a great reason, a multitude with one sense, a war and a peace, a herd and a shepherd.

Your little reason too, my brother, which you call “spirit,” is a tool of your body, a little tool and toy of your great reason.

“I,” you say, and you are proud of this word. But the greater thing, which you do not want to believe in, is your body and its great reason: it does not say “I,” but does “I.”

What the sense feels, what the spirit knows, never has its end in itself. But sense and spirit would like to persuade you that they are the end of all things: that is how vain they are.

Sense and spirit are tools and toys: behind them still lies the Self. The Self seeks with the eyes of the senses too; it listens with the ears of the spirit too.

The Self is always listening and seeking: it compares, compels, conquers, destroys. It rules, and is the ruler of the “I” as well.

Behind your thoughts and feelings, my brother, stands a mighty commander, an unknown sage — whose name is Self. He lives in your body; he is your body.

There is more reason in your body than in your best wisdom. And who knows, then, why your body needs precisely your best wisdom?

Your Self laughs at your “I” and its proud leaps. “What are these leaps and flights of thought to me?” it says to itself. “A detour to my purpose. I am the leading strings of the ‘I’ and the prompter of its ideas.”

The Self says to the “I”: “Feel pain here!” And then it suffers, and thinks about how it might suffer no more — and that is exactly why it *is meant* to think.

The Self says to the “I”: “Feel pleasure here!” Then it is glad, and thinks about how it might often be glad again — and that is exactly why it *is meant* to think.

I want to say a word to the despisers of the body. It is their esteeming that makes them despise. What is it that created esteeming and despising and value and will?

The creating Self created esteeming and despising for itself; it created pleasure and pain for itself. The creating body created the spirit for itself, as a hand of its will.

Even in your folly and contempt, you despisers of the body, you serve your Self. I tell you: your Self itself wants to die, and is turning away from life.

It can no longer do what it would most like to do: — to create beyond itself. That is what it would most like to do; that is its whole fervor.

But now it has grown too late for that: — so your Self wants to go under, you despisers of the body.

Your Self wants to go under, and that is why you became despisers of the body! For you can no longer create beyond yourselves.

And that is why you are now angry with life and the earth. An unconscious envy lies in the sidelong glance of your contempt.

I do not go your way, you despisers of the body! You are no bridges to the overman for me! —

Thus spoke Zarathustra.
