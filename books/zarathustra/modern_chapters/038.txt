On Immaculate Perception

When the moon rose yesterday, I imagined it wanted to give birth to a sun: so broad and pregnant did it lie on the horizon.

But it lied to me with its pregnancy; and I would sooner believe in the man in the moon than in the woman.

Of course, there is not much of a man in him either, this shy night-prowler. Truly, he wanders over the rooftops with a bad conscience.

For he is lustful and jealous, the monk in the moon, lustful for the earth and for all the joys of lovers.

No, I do not like him, this tomcat on the rooftops! Repulsive to me are all who creep around half-closed windows!

Pious and silent he wanders along on carpets of stars: — but I do not like any soft-treading feet of men on which not even a spur jingles.

Every honest man’s step speaks up; but the cat steals away over the floor. Look, catlike and dishonest the moon comes along. —

This parable I give to you, you sentimental hypocrites, to you, the “pure perceivers”! *I* call you — the lustful!

You too love the earth and what is earthly: I have guessed you well! — but there is shame in your love, and a bad conscience — you are like the moon!

Your spirit has been talked into despising what is earthly, but your bowels have not: and *they* are the strongest thing in you!

And now your spirit is ashamed of doing what your bowels want, and to escape its own shame it goes by sneaking paths and lying paths.

“That would be the highest thing for me” — so your lying spirit talks to itself — “to look at life without desire, and not like a dog with its tongue hanging out:

To be happy in looking, with a will that has died, without the grip and greed of selfishness — cold and ash-gray all over the body, but with drunken moon-eyes!”

“That would be the dearest thing to me” — so the seduced one seduces himself — “to love the earth the way the moon loves it, and to touch its beauty with the eye alone.

And this I would call the *immaculate* perception of all things: that I want nothing from things, except to be allowed to lie before them like a mirror with a hundred eyes.” —

Oh, you sentimental hypocrites, you lustful ones! You lack innocence in your desire: and so now you slander desiring!

Truly, you do not love the earth as creators, as begetters, as those who delight in becoming!

Where is innocence? Where there is the will to beget. And whoever wants to create beyond himself has, for me, the purest will.

Where is beauty? Where I *must will* with all my will; where I want to love and go under, so that an image does not remain only an image.

Loving and going under: these have rhymed for eternities. The will to love: that means being willing to die as well. That is how I speak to you cowards!

But now your unmanned leering wants to be called “contemplation”! And whatever lets itself be fingered by cowardly eyes is to be christened “beautiful”! Oh, you contaminators of noble names!

But this shall be your curse, you immaculate ones, you pure perceivers, that you will never give birth: even though you lie broad and pregnant on the horizon!

Truly, you fill your mouths with noble words: and we are supposed to believe that your hearts are overflowing, you liars?

But *my* words are humble, despised, crooked words: I gladly pick up what falls under the table at your meals.

With them I can still — tell hypocrites the truth! Yes, my fish bones, shells, and prickly leaves shall — tickle the noses of hypocrites!

There is always bad air around you and your meals: for your lustful thoughts, your lies and secrets, are in the air!

Just dare first to believe yourselves — yourselves and your bowels! Whoever does not believe himself always lies.

You have hung a god’s mask in front of yourselves, you “pure ones”: into a god’s mask your loathsome ringed worm has crawled.

Truly, you deceive, you “contemplative ones”! Zarathustra too was once the fool of your godlike skins; he did not guess the serpent coils they were stuffed with.

Once I imagined I saw a god’s soul playing in your games, you pure perceivers! Once I imagined there was no better art than your arts!

The distance hid from me the serpent filth and the foul smell: and that a lizard’s cunning was creeping around here, lustfully.

But I came *near* you: then the day came to me — and now it is coming to you — the moon’s love affair has come to an end!

Just look! There he stands, caught out and pale — before the dawn!

For already she is coming, the glowing one — *her* love for the earth is coming! Innocence and the creator’s desire are all the love of the sun!

Just look how impatiently she comes over the sea! Do you not feel the thirst and the hot breath of her love?

She wants to suck at the sea and drink its depth up to her height: and the desire of the sea rises with a thousand breasts.

It *wants* to be kissed and sucked by the thirst of the sun; it *wants* to become air and height and a footpath of light, and light itself!

Truly, like the sun I love life and all deep seas.

And this is what knowledge means *to me*: everything deep shall rise — up to my height!

Thus spoke Zarathustra.
