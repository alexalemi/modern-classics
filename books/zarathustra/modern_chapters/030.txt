On the Tarantulas

Look, this is the tarantula’s den! Do you want to see her yourself? Here hangs her web: touch it, so that it trembles.

Here she comes, willingly: welcome, tarantula! Black on your back sits your triangle, your emblem; and I know too what sits in your soul.

Revenge sits in your soul: wherever you bite, black scabs grow; with revenge your poison makes the soul whirl!

So I speak to you in a parable, you who make souls whirl, you preachers of *equality*! To me you are tarantulas, secretly vengeful ones!

But I will drag your hiding places into the light soon enough: that is why I laugh my laughter of the heights in your faces.

That is why I tear at your web, so that your rage may lure you out of your den of lies, and your revenge may leap out from behind your word “justice.”

For that man may be redeemed from revenge: that is for me the bridge to the highest hope, and a rainbow after long storms.

But the tarantulas, of course, want it otherwise. “What we call justice is precisely this: that the world be filled with the storms of our revenge” — so they talk with one another.

“We will take revenge on all who are not equal to us, and heap abuse on them” — so the tarantula hearts vow to one another.

“And ‘will to equality’ — that itself shall from now on be the name for virtue; and against everything that has power we will raise our outcry!”

You preachers of equality, it is the tyrant-madness of impotence that cries out of you like this for “equality”: your most secret tyrant-lusts disguise themselves like this in virtue-words!

Embittered conceit, pent-up envy, perhaps your fathers’ conceit and envy: it bursts out of you as a flame and a madness of revenge.

What the father kept silent about comes out speaking in the son; and I have often found the son to be the father’s secret laid bare.

They look like the inspired: but it is not the heart that inspires them — it is revenge. And when they grow refined and cold, it is not the spirit but envy that makes them refined and cold.

Their jealousy leads them onto the paths of thinkers too; and this is the mark of their jealousy — they always go too far: so that in the end their weariness has to lie down and sleep on the snow.

Revenge rings out of every one of their complaints, there is a wish to hurt in every one of their praises; and being judges seems like bliss to them.

But this is my advice to you, my friends: mistrust all in whom the urge to punish is powerful!

They are people of a bad kind and a bad descent; the hangman and the bloodhound look out of their faces.

Mistrust all those who talk a great deal about their justice! Truly, it is not only honey that their souls lack.

And when they call themselves “the good and the just,” do not forget that nothing stands between them and the Pharisee except — power!

My friends, I do not want to be mixed up with others or mistaken for them.

There are those who preach my teaching about life: and at the same time they are preachers of equality, and tarantulas.

That they speak up for life, even though they sit in their den, these poison spiders, turned away from life: the reason is that they want to hurt with it.

They want to hurt those who have power now: for among them the preaching of death is still most at home.

If it were otherwise, the tarantulas would teach otherwise: and it was precisely they who were once the best slanderers of the world and burners of heretics.

I do not want to be mixed up with these preachers of equality or mistaken for them. For this is how justice speaks *to me*: “People are not equal.”

Nor shall they become so! What would my love of the overman be, if I spoke otherwise?

On a thousand bridges and footpaths they shall throng toward the future, and ever more war and inequality shall be set between them: that is how my great love makes me speak!

They shall become inventors of images and ghosts in their hostilities, and with their images and ghosts they shall yet fight the highest fight against one another!

Good and evil, and rich and poor, and high and low, and all the names of values: they shall be weapons, and clanging marks of this, that life must overcome itself again and again!

Life itself wants to build itself up into the heights with pillars and steps: it wants to gaze into far distances and out toward blissful beauties — *that is why* it needs height!

And because it needs height, it needs steps, and the contradiction between the steps and those who climb them! Life wants to climb, and in climbing to overcome itself.

And just look at this, my friends! Here, where the tarantula’s den is, the ruins of an old temple rise up — just look at them with enlightened eyes!

Truly, whoever once piled his thoughts up toward the sky in stone here knew the secret of all life as well as the wisest!

That there is struggle and inequality even in beauty, and war for power and more-than-power: he teaches us that here in the plainest parable.

How divinely vault and arch break against each other here, in a wrestling match: how they strive against each other with light and shadow, these divine strivers —

Let us be enemies like this too, my friends, sure and beautiful! Let us strive divinely *against* one another! —

Woe! Now the tarantula has bitten me myself, my old enemy! Divinely sure and beautiful, she bit me on the finger!

“There must be punishment and justice” — so she thinks: “he shall not sing songs here in honor of enmity for nothing!”

Yes, she has taken her revenge! And woe! now with revenge she will make my soul whirl too!

But so that I do *not* whirl, my friends, tie me fast here to this pillar! I would rather be a pillar saint than a whirlwind of vengefulness!

Truly, Zarathustra is no twister and no whirlwind; and if he is a dancer, he is never a tarantula dancer! —

Thus spoke Zarathustra.
