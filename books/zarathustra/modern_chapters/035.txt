On Self-Overcoming

“Will to truth” — is that what you call it, you wisest ones, the thing that drives you and sets you burning?

A will to the thinkability of all that is: that is what *I* call your will!

You want first to *make* all that is thinkable: for you doubt, with a healthy mistrust, whether it is thinkable already.

But it shall bend and yield to you! So your will wants it. It shall become smooth and subject to the spirit, as the spirit’s mirror and reflection.

That is your whole will, you wisest ones, a will to power; and that holds even when you talk of good and evil and of valuations. You want to create the world before which you can kneel: that is your last hope and your intoxication.

The unwise, of course, the people — they are like the river on which a boat floats along: and in the boat sit the valuations, solemn and cloaked.

You set your will and your values on the river of becoming; what the people believe to be good and evil betrays to me an old will to power.

It was you, you wisest ones, who put such guests in this boat and gave them pomp and proud names — you and your ruling will!

Now the river carries your boat along: it *must* carry it. It matters little if the broken wave foams and angrily contradicts the keel!

It is not the river that is your danger and the end of your good and evil, you wisest ones: it is that will itself, the will to power — the unexhausted, begetting will of life.

But so that you may understand my word about good and evil, I will also tell you my word about life, and about the nature of all that lives.

I followed what lives; I walked the greatest and the smallest paths, so as to know its nature.

With a hundredfold mirror I caught its glance even when its mouth was shut, so that its eye might speak to me. And its eye spoke to me.

But wherever I found something living, there I also heard the talk of obedience. Everything living is something that obeys.

And this is the second thing: whoever cannot obey himself is commanded. That is the nature of what lives.

But this is the third thing I heard: that commanding is harder than obeying. And not only because the one who commands bears the burden of all who obey, and because this burden easily crushes him: —

An experiment and a risk appeared to me in all commanding; and whenever it commands, the living thing risks itself in doing so.

Yes, even when it commands itself: even then it must pay for its commanding. It must become judge and avenger and victim of its own law.

How does this happen! I asked myself. What persuades the living thing to obey and to command, and in commanding still to practice obedience?

Hear my word now, you wisest ones! Test it in earnest, whether I have crept into the very heart of life, and down to the roots of its heart!

Wherever I found something living, I found will to power; and even in the will of the one who serves I found the will to be master.

That the weaker should serve the stronger: to this its will persuades it, the will that wants to be master over what is weaker still. This one pleasure it will not do without.

And as the smaller gives itself up to the greater, so that it may have pleasure and power over the smallest: so even the greatest gives itself up, and for the sake of power stakes — its life.

That is the self-surrender of the greatest: that it is risk and danger, and a game of dice with death.

And where there are sacrifice and services and loving glances: there too is the will to be master. By secret paths the weaker steals into the castle, and right into the heart of the mightier — and there steals power.

And life itself told me this secret. Look, it said, I am that which must always overcome itself.

“Of course, you call it a will to procreation, or a drive toward a goal, toward the higher, the farther, the more manifold: but all of this is one thing and one secret.

I would rather go under than renounce this one thing; and truly, wherever there is going under and the falling of leaves, look, there life sacrifices itself — for power!

That I must be struggle and becoming and goal and the contradiction of goals: ah, whoever guesses my will surely guesses too on what *crooked* paths it must walk!

Whatever I create and however much I love it — soon I must be its opponent, and the opponent of my love: so my will wants it.

And you too, seeker of knowledge, are only a path and a footprint of my will: truly, my will to power walks even on the feet of your will to truth!

He certainly did not hit the truth who shot at it with the phrase ‘will to existence’: that will — does not exist!

For what does not exist cannot will; but what already exists, how could it still want to exist!

Only where there is life is there also will: not will to life, but — so I teach you — will to power!

The living esteem many things more highly than life itself; but out of the esteeming itself speaks — the will to power!” —

Thus life once taught me: and with this, you wisest ones, I will yet solve the riddle of your hearts.

Truly I tell you: good and evil that would last forever — there is no such thing! Out of itself it must overcome itself again and again.

With your values and your words of good and evil you exercise force, you esteemers of value: and this is your hidden love and the shining, trembling and overflowing of your soul.

But a stronger force grows out of your values, and a new overcoming: on it egg and eggshell break.

And whoever must be a creator in good and evil: truly, he must first be a destroyer and break values.

So the highest evil belongs to the highest goodness: but that is the creative goodness. —

Let us speak of it, you wisest ones, even if it is bad. To be silent is worse; all truths kept silent become poisonous.

And let everything that can break on our truths — break! There are many houses still to build!

Thus spoke Zarathustra.
