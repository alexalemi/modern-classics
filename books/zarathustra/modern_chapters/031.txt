On the Famous Wise Men

You have served the people and the people’s superstition, all you famous wise men! — and *not* the truth! And that is exactly why they paid you reverence.

And that is also why they put up with your unbelief: because it was a joke and a detour on the way to the people. That is how the master lets his slaves have their way, and even enjoys their high spirits.

But the one the people hate as the dogs hate a wolf: that is the free spirit, the enemy of fetters, the non-worshiper, the one who makes his home in the forests.

To hunt him out of his hiding place — the people have always called that a “sense of what is right”: against him they still set their sharpest-toothed dogs.

“For the truth is there: after all, the people are there! Woe, woe to those who seek!” — so it has rung out from the beginning.

You wanted to justify your people in their reverence: that you called the “will to truth,” you famous wise men!

And your heart always said to itself: “I came from the people: from there the voice of God came to me as well.”

Stiff-necked and shrewd, like the donkey, you have always been the people’s advocates.

And many a powerful man who wanted to fare well with the people harnessed in front of his horses — a little donkey, a famous wise man.

And now, you famous wise men, I wish you would at last throw off the lion’s skin entirely!

The skin of the beast of prey, the spotted, motley one, and the shaggy mane of the researcher, the seeker, the conqueror!

Ah, for me to learn to believe in your “truthfulness,” you would first have to break your reverent will.

Truthful — that is what I call the one who goes into godless deserts and has broken his reverent heart.

In the yellow sand, burned by the sun, he may well squint thirstily over at the islands rich in springs, where living things rest under dark trees.

But his thirst does not persuade him to become like these comfortable ones: for where there are oases, there are idols too.

Hungry, violent, solitary, godless: that is how the lion-will wants itself to be.

Free from the happiness of serfs, redeemed from gods and worship, fearless and fearsome, great and solitary: that is the will of the truthful.

The truthful, the free spirits, have always lived in the desert, as the desert’s masters; but in the cities live the well-fed, famous wise men — the draft animals.

For they always pull, as donkeys pull — the *people’s* cart!

Not that I am angry with them for that: but to me they remain servants and creatures in harness, even when their harness gleams with gold.

And often they were good servants, worthy of praise. For this is how virtue speaks: if you must be a servant, look for the one whom your service profits most!

“Your master’s spirit and virtue shall grow because you are his servant: so you yourself will grow along with his spirit and his virtue!”

And truly, you famous wise men, you servants of the people! You yourselves have grown along with the people’s spirit and virtue — and the people through you! I say this to your credit!

But to me you remain the people even in your virtues, the people with dull eyes — the people who do not know what *spirit* is!

Spirit is the life that itself cuts into life: by its own torment it increases its own knowledge — did you know that already?

And the spirit’s happiness is this: to be anointed and consecrated with tears as the sacrificial animal — did you know that already?

And the blindness of the blind man, and his seeking and groping, shall yet bear witness to the power of the sun he looked into — did you know that already?

And the seeker of knowledge shall learn to *build* with mountains! It is a small thing for the spirit to move mountains — did you know that already?

You know only the sparks of the spirit: but you do not see the anvil that it is, nor the cruelty of its hammer!

Truly, you do not know the spirit’s pride! But still less could you bear the spirit’s modesty, if it ever wanted to speak!

And never yet have you been allowed to throw your spirit into a pit of snow: you are not hot enough for that! And so you do not know the raptures of its coldness either.

But in everything you are too familiar with the spirit; and you have often turned wisdom into a poorhouse and a hospital for bad poets.

You are no eagles: so you have never experienced the happiness in the terror of the spirit either. And whoever is not a bird should not make his camp above abysses.

To me you are lukewarm: but all deep knowledge flows cold. Ice-cold are the innermost wells of the spirit: a refreshment to hot hands and to those who take things in hand.

There you stand, respectable and stiff and with straight backs, you famous wise men! — no strong wind and no strong will drives you.

Have you never seen a sail go over the sea, rounded and swelling and trembling before the fury of the wind?

Like the sail, trembling before the fury of the spirit, my wisdom goes over the sea — my wild wisdom!

But you servants of the people, you famous wise men — how *could* you go with me! —

Thus spoke Zarathustra.
