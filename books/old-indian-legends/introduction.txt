Zitkala-Ša was born in 1876 on the Yankton reservation in what is now South Dakota. Her mother was Yankton Dakota and her father was white. When she was eight, Quaker missionaries took her to a boarding school in Wabash, Indiana, and she spent most of the next fifteen years in white institutions: as a pupil, as a student at Earlham College, and as a teacher at the Carlisle Indian School in Pennsylvania. Zitkala-Ša, the name she wrote under, is Lakota for Red Bird. In 1900 the Atlantic Monthly printed three essays by her about her childhood and her schooling; they are collected in American Indian Stories, the companion to this volume.

Old Indian Legends came out in Boston in 1901, from Ginn & Company, a publisher of school books. It is a book for children, and the preface says which children: the little black-haired aborigine who heard these tales beside the night fire, and the blue-eyed little patriot who can hear them now. She had the stories from old Dakota story-tellers in both Dakotas, and when two tellings of a tale differed, she writes, each helped her restore what the other had lost.

Most of the legends are about Iktomi, the spider fairy, a trickster who dresses like a Dakota brave, is vain and greedy, and is usually caught in his own snare. The others bring in the badger and the bear, a rabbit, a turtle, and Iya, the Eater. Animals talk and keep house like people, and the old stone Inyan is prayed to as great-grandfather. A few Dakota exclamations, such as "Hoye!" and "Hinnu!", are left untranslated, as the book leaves them. She writes both teepee and wigwam.

The illustrations are by Angel De Cora, a Winnebago artist who studied at Smith College and under Howard Pyle, and who taught art at Carlisle from 1906. The title page adds her Winnebago name, Hinook-Mahiwi-Kilinaka.

This is a restored edition: Zitkala-Ša's words are unchanged. The Project Gutenberg transcription it is built from has no preface, none of De Cora's fourteen plates and none of their captions. This edition restores all three from a scan of the 1901 book, keeping the line printed under each plate and adding a description and alt text for each. It also adds modern typesetting, a proper ebook, and this introduction.
