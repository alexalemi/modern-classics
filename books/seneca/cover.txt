Manuel Domínguez Sánchez's *The Suicide of Seneca* (1871). The crop keeps the mourners: a man in a red cloak who has collapsed against the rim of a marble bath with his face buried in his arm, and a young man in a pale toga standing back against the wall, his hand at his chest. A brazier smokes behind them. When Nero ordered Seneca's death he opened his veins and died in a bath, three years after he withdrew from court, and these letters were written in the time between.
