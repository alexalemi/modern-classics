Drawn for this edition, in the manner of the Dover science paperbacks of the 1950s and 60s, after the example Spinoza gives in the note to Proposition 8 of Part II. Any two lines crossing inside a circle cut each other so that the rectangles made from their pieces are equal; draw lines through one point in every direction and you have infinitely many equal rectangles, two of them drawn here. Spinoza uses it to show how the ideas of things that do not exist can still be contained in the idea of God. The line at the top is his.
