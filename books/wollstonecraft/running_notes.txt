LOCKED VOCABULARY — caps spent, no re-gloss
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Each of these is glossed ONCE, at first use, and thereafter used plainly.

  sexual character        -> "a character that belongs to one's sex",
                             glossed once in the Introduction and then
                             used as "a character of her own sex" /
                             "a separate character for women". NEVER
                             left as "sexual character": the modern
                             reading is not merely wrong, it is
                             obscene, and it is the book's main term.
  sexual characteristics  -> "the qualities supposed to belong to women"
  sexual virtue           -> "a virtue belonging to one sex"
  sensibility             -> KEPT, glossed once at Chapter Four as the
                             capacity for feeling. It is a period term
                             of art, she defines it herself out of
                             Johnson's dictionary, and no modern word
                             covers it.
  manners                 -> KEPT where it means the moral texture of a
                             society (as burke/); "behaviour" only where
                             she plainly means conduct in company.
  accomplishments         -> KEPT, glossed once at the Introduction
                             (music, drawing, French, needlework).
  understanding           -> "mind" or "intellect"; "understanding"
                             where the faculty is the subject.
  the sex / my sex        -> "women" / "my own sex". "The sex" for women
                             is dead English and reads as a coy joke.
  fine ladies             -> KEPT.
  delicacy                -> "delicacy" where feeling, "modesty" only
                             where she plainly means the sexual sense
                             and the ambiguity is not the point.
  prejudice               -> "inherited opinion", "received opinion";
                             "prejudice" where she means it pejoratively.
  virtue                  -> KEPT throughout.
  condescension           -> "graciousness" / "stooping".
  libertine               -> KEPT.
  seraglio                -> "harem" (decided in 001, against the first
                             draft of this ledger, which said KEEP). She
                             uses it three times and always for the
                             image rather than the place; "seraglio" is
                             opaque now and the image is the whole point.
  Muhammadan              -> "Muslim". The WORD is modernised, the CLAIM
                             stands. See text_analysis section 6.
  the great               -> "the great" / "people of rank".
  bugbear                 -> KEPT.

DECISIONS LOG
---------------------------------------------------------------------
(appended as they are made, with the file that forced them)

--- 005, Chapter Three -----------------------------------------------
QUOTED SCRIPTURE IS MODERNISED, like all quoted prose (text_analysis
section 4), and unlike quoted verse. Two cases here, both caught by the
archaism sweep rather than by me: Job 38:11 ("thus far shalt thou go")
and the talents of Matthew 25 ("behold, thou gavest me a talent").
She quotes the King James wording, but she quotes it the way she quotes
Rousseau -- as somebody else's prose that her own sentence has to carry
-- and the augustine precedent governs: the book has ONE voice, and
re-archaising the quotations would put a frame round them that she did
not. This is a decision about a CLASS, so it is recorded here rather
than exempted phrase by phrase.
Contrast soap-bubbles, which kept Proverbs 23:31 in the King James
wording: there the verse is a set-piece Boys reads out, and nothing
depends on the reader following its argument.

CHECK FIX, 003. The archaism sweep now skips tab-indented lines, and
that is a structural exemption rather than the grimm exact-phrase kind,
with an argument: those blocks must be VERBATIM Milton, Dryden and
Pope, so an archaism inside one is the poet's by construction, and
check 3 independently pins every verse block's line count so the
exempted region cannot grow. The exact-phrase version was tried first
and was incomplete within one file -- Adam's eleven-line reply fired on
hast/thou/thy as soon as it was written. Proved by reintroducing "hath"
into ordinary prose and watching the sweep fail (the boethius rule).

--- 007, 012: THE APOSTROPHES TO GOD ----------------------------------
She breaks off three times to address God directly ("Gracious Creator
of the whole human race!"; "O my Father, have you...") and elsewhere
addresses the seduced woman, and her sisters, in the same second
person. augustine/ capitalises the divine pronouns FOR DISAMBIGUATION,
because Augustine switches addressee inside a paragraph with nothing to
mark it. Here every switch is signposted by a vocative, so the capitals
would buy nothing and would be a convention used four times in 85,000
words. LOWERCASE THROUGHOUT, and the thou-family goes as everywhere
else.

--- 014, 020: TWO RACIAL NOUNS, BOTH REPLACED BY THE RELATIONSHIP -----
The mill/burke precedent exactly: the Verne rule protects a dated CLAIM
that is part of the author's subject, and in both of these the claim
survives entire when the noun is replaced.
  014 "like the poor African slaves, to be subject to prejudices that
      brutalize them" -> "like the enslaved Africans". Her subject is
      that propriety brutalizes whoever it is imposed on; the
      comparison is the argument and stands.
  020 "the savage desire of admiration which the black heroes inherit
      from both their parents" -> "that enslaved people inherit from
      both their parents alike". Her subject here is that a love of
      ornament is HUMAN and not female -- which is why the phrase "from
      both their parents" is doing the work, and why it is worth
      spelling out as "alike". The sardonic epithet carries no part of
      that argument.
NOTHING ELSE IN THE BOOK IS IN THIS CLASS. Her abolitionist passages
stand entire and unhedged: "Is sugar always to be produced by human
blood?", the minister whose cold hand rivets the chains "by sanctioning
the abominable trade", and the whole slavery vocabulary she applies to
marriage.

--- 019: DATED CLAIMS KEPT, PER THE VERNE RULE ------------------------
"the Indians worship the devil" (Section One) and the two "Muhammadan"
passages of Chapter Two stand as her claims, with only the WORD
modernised in the second case. She is arguing about blind submission to
power and about Milton respectively, and both comparisons are hers.

--- CHAPTER FIVE: THE QUOTED PROSE IS MODERNISED ----------------------
This is the book's one departure from the lecture-volumes rule and the
reason is in text_analysis section 4. Chapter Five is 16,000 words of
Rousseau, Fordyce, Gregory and Chesterfield quoted at length and then
taken apart; leaving the charge in 1762 English while the reply is
modern would let the reader follow the reply and not the charge. Verse
stays verbatim (Milton, Dryden, Pope), and every attribution is kept.
NOTHING IS SOFTENED INSIDE THE QUOTATIONS -- Rousseau on women existing
to please, Gregory advising a daughter to conceal her learning and
feign poor health, Fordyce's "curst be the impious hand". They are the
evidence.
