AESCHYLUS: THE SEVEN TRAGEDIES
Translation strategy. From the GREEK (Perseus' text of Smyth's 1926
Loeb), with Smyth's facing English prose under reference/ as a per-file
crib ONLY.

READ FIRST: sophocles/text_analysis.txt -- this is the second volume of
the same shelf, the form ruling and the whole method carry over, and
this file records only what is DIFFERENT about Aeschylus. Then
odyssey/text_analysis.txt (from-the-Greek pipeline, the crib-is-a-Loeb
warning) and euclid-rivals/ (what a lost speaker tag does to a play).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHY THIS BOOK, and the measurement is NOT the Sophocles one
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Screened 2026-09-09 with screen.py:

    Morshead, House of Atreus (verse, 1881)   arch 32.46   verse 82%
    Buckley, PB + Seven (PROSE, 1849)         arch 22.92
    Murray, Agamemnon (rhymed verse, 1920)    arch 22.81
    Plumptre, Tragedies (verse, 1868)         arch 19.81
    Browning, Agamemnon (verse, 1877)         arch 17.17
    Smyth, Loeb (prose, 1926)                 arch  0.1    calq 9-11

    Sophocles (Campbell, verse)               arch 39.10   <- the record

So the Sophocles case holds for six of the seven public-domain
Aeschyluses: what a reader downloads is Victorian or Edwardian verse
whose archaism is the TRANSLATOR'S costume and not in the Greek. Even
Buckley's Bohn PROSE runs at 23 -- "thou" throughout -- and Browning's
famous version is not only archaic but word-for-word Greek syntax in
English, which is why it is unreadable by common consent.

BUT SMYTH MEASURES CLEAN, and the case has to be made honestly against
him, because he is the text this edition works from. It is the odyssey/
lesson again from the other side: Butler scored clean because he threw
the poem away; Smyth scores clean because he is a LOEB CRIB, written to
sit opposite the Greek and be checked against it word by word. "Here we
are, the faithful Council of the Persians, who have gone to the land of
Hellas, we who serve as warders of the royal abode" is not archaic. It
is also not English anyone would read for pleasure, and it is PROSE
THROUGHOUT -- the odes are paragraphs -- so the one formal fact about
these plays, that the chorus is singing, is invisible in him. His
clean score is the reason he is a good crib and no reason at all to
leave the plays where they are.

THE THIRD REASON is the one the Sophocles volume did not have to make:
Aeschylus is the HARDEST Greek of the three tragedians. Compound
epithets ("the many-golden seats", "the bronze-bitted horse"), sentences
that stack images three deep, and choral odes whose grammar scholars
still argue about. Every translator either flattens the images or keeps
them and loses the sentence. Smyth keeps them and loses the sentence.
The retelling's job is to keep the image AND make the sentence go.

2. THE FORM -- as sophocles/, derived from the source, with a caveat
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  SPOKEN scenes, iambic trimeter       -> MODERN PROSE
  SUNG odes, lyric metres, refrains    -> VERSE, tab-indented, unrhymed
  CHANTED anapaests, trochaic dialogue -> anapaests VERSE, trochees prose

THE CAVEAT: Perseus' Greek markup for Aeschylus is WORSE than for
Sophocles. Twelve times a <div> labelled for a choral stanza runs on
over the spoken scene after it (the whole Agamemnon-Clytemnestra scene,
the whole Io scene, the Nurse). prep.py's KIND_FIXES corrects them per
speech with a reason each, and asserts that every fix fires. What is
left in chapters/ is therefore the edition's ruling, and AGENTS FOLLOW
chapters/, NEVER THE CRIB and never their own sense of the metre. If a
[Sung] marker looks wrong, say so in the notes; do not silently set it
as prose. check.py asserts the modern file against prep.

AESCHYLUS IS MORE THAN HALF CHORUS. The Suppliants and The Persians are
essentially oratorios; the Agamemnon's parodos is 200 lines before
anyone speaks. This is the opposite balance from Sophocles and it makes
the verse setting the larger half of the work, not the smaller. The
odes must be READABLE VERSE, not a crib set in short lines: an image a
line, syntax a fourteen-year-old can follow aloud, and the strophe and
antistrophe matched in shape. A song heard once.

THE REFRAINS (ephymnia) are Aeschylus' own device and nobody else's:
the Suppliants' hymn, the Furies' binding song ("over our victim we
sing this song"), the Agamemnon kommos. A refrain is REPEATED VERBATIM
every time it recurs -- the nights/ rule for frame formulas. Perseus
prints some refrains twice (162a-167a) where editors restore them;
render each occurrence chapters/ prints.

3. VOICE
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Aeschylus is the grandest of the three and the least interested in
people as individuals; his characters are forces and his plots are
about whole houses over three generations. Do NOT make him small and
do NOT make him solemn. The register is BIG, PHYSICAL and CONCRETE:
the sea, fire on hilltops, a net, a robe, a lion cub in the house, a
snake at the breast. Every abstraction he uses (Justice, Ruin, Fear)
he immediately gives a body -- Justice "tips her scales", Ruin "is
born a child of Outrage". Keep those bodies.

  - THE IMAGES ARE THE ARGUMENT. Where Sophocles reasons, Aeschylus
    piles images until the pattern becomes visible: the net thrown over
    Troy becomes the robe thrown over Agamemnon becomes the net Orestes
    holds up in the Libation Bearers. Keep the same English word for
    the same image every time it recurs across the trilogy (see the
    ledger). A reader must be able to SEE the chain.
  - LONG SPEECHES, NOT STICHOMYTHIA. Aeschylus has far less one-line
    exchange than Sophocles and far more sixty-line set piece: the
    Messenger's Salamis, the Herald, Clytemnestra's beacons,
    Cassandra, Prometheus' catalogue of what he gave men. Paragraph
    them; keep them moving; never trim.
  - CLYTEMNESTRA is the greatest part in Greek tragedy and she is
    IRONIC from her first line to her last: everything she says to
    the Herald and to Agamemnon is true in a sense he cannot hear.
    Render the double meaning exactly, never wink at it, never
    flatten it into either the innocent or the guilty sense.
  - CASSANDRA sees three tenses at once. Her scene moves from lyric
    cries (sung) to lucid iambics (spoken); the shift IS the scene.
  - THE PERSIANS is the only surviving tragedy about a historical
    event eight years old, told from the ENEMY'S side with sympathy.
    Xerxes is not a villain; the play is a lament. The Messenger's
    account of Salamis is the earliest eyewitness-style battle
    narrative in literature and must be exact and fast.
  - THE FURIES are hideous and they are RIGHT: they have an argument
    about blood and the old laws, and Athena does not refute it, she
    buys them off. Never make them stupid or merely monstrous.
  - PROMETHEUS BOUND is almost static -- a bound god visited by a
    series of speakers -- and lives on the catalogue and the
    prophecy. Keep the geography of Io's wanderings entire.
  - THE PLAIN LINES ARE PLAIN (the hume rule): "the doer must suffer",
    "Zeus, whoever he is", "learning comes through suffering". Do not
    ornament them.

4. LOCKED VOCABULARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------
All of sophocles/text_analysis.txt section 4 carries over unchanged
(ate RUIN, hubris OUTRAGE, dike JUSTICE and lawsuit, philos the people
you owe, nomos law/custom, daimon not demon, Hades a name). Added:

  pathei mathos   "learning through suffering" -- Agamemnon 177, the
                  phrase the trilogy is built on. Render it plainly.
  drasanti pathein "the doer must suffer" (LB 313). Same form every
                  time the law of retaliation is stated.
  Erinyes         THE FURIES throughout; "the Kindly Ones" only where
                  the Eumenides text itself renames them.
  Dike            "Justice" capitalised where she acts as a person
                  (tips scales, comes late); lower case as a concept.
  Zeus teleios    "Zeus who brings things to their end"; the epithet
                  is the point.
  thymos/phren    heart/mind -- keep the body-words; Aeschylus locates
                  thought in organs and it is part of the physicality.
  barbaros        "barbarian" is KEPT in The Persians: the Persians
                  call themselves that (the word just means non-Greek
                  in 472 and the play uses it with sympathy). The
                  Verne rule; do not soften to "foreign".
  doulos/dmoe     SLAVE, never handmaid/servant, where the Greek has a
                  slave word (the odyssey ruling). "Servant" only for
                  Οἰκέτης/Θεράπαινα.
  Names: familiar forms -- Clytemnestra (not Klutaimnestra or Smyth's
  Clytaemestra), Cassandra, Aegisthus, Orestes, Electra, Eteocles,
  Polyneices, Xerxes, Darius, Atossa is NOT named (the Queen), Io,
  Oceanus, Hephaestus, Hermes, Pelasgus is the King (named once in the
  text at 251; tag stays "King"). Cult titles resolved to plain names as
  ovid/ does, except where the periphrasis is the point.

5. STAGE DIRECTIONS ARE SMYTH'S, NOT AESCHYLUS'
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The Greek carries none. Smyth's are sparse (4-8 per play) and ours to
write and modernise freely. Own paragraph in parentheses, factual,
never interpretive; long enough not to render as a heading
(check.py asks assemble.is_subheading itself). Ghosts rising, the
chariot, the Furies asleep, the robe held up: these are the ones a
reader needs.

6. THE VERNE RULE
--------------------------------------------------------------------
No editorial note anywhere in the volume. Translated in full, unhedged:
  - THE SUPPLIANTS' HATRED OF MARRIAGE and of their cousins, which the
    play half endorses; the King's decision to risk his city for fifty
    foreign women, argued out at length.
  - The PERSIANS' account of Greek victory, put in Persian mouths, and
    the Elders' litany of the dead by name -- the names are the point,
    all of them, every time.
  - AGAMEMNON'S sacrifice of his daughter, told by the chorus with her
    gag and her eyes; Clytemnestra exulting over the corpse "like rain
    from heaven on the sprouting corn"; her sexual claim on Aegisthus
    stated plainly.
  - THE NURSE'S SPEECH about Orestes' nappies (LB 749-760), which is
    comic and about a baby's bowels, and is kept exactly so.
  - ORESTES KILLING HIS MOTHER, on stage-adjacent ground, with her
    baring the breast that fed him; his flat "you killed the man you
    should not have; now suffer what you should not."
  - The Furies' account of what they do to the guilty, and Apollo's
    argument in the Eumenides that a mother is "no parent, only the
    nurse of the seed" -- the play's own argument, stated by a god,
    and it wins the case. Not softened, not footnoted.
  - The Eumenides' close, in which Athens is praised by its own poet at
    its own festival; the civic flattery stands.
  - Prometheus' contempt for Zeus and the Chorus' fear of it.

7. RATIO
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Greek verse -> English runs HIGHER here than in Sophocles. Smyth's
prose is 68,000 English against 40,335 Greek, 1.70 (Jebb ran 1.54 on
Sophocles), because Aeschylus' compound words each unpack into three
or four of ours. Target 1.55-1.80 per file; check.py 1.45-1.95;
verify --min-ratio 1.40 --max-ratio 1.95. UNDER 1.45 MEANS SUMMARISING,
which in a play means a speech has been trimmed or an ode compressed.
Re-derive the band from the finished files (the purgatorio rule) and
record it in running_notes.txt.
