Gakudo Yojinshu: Points to Watch in Studying the Way (1234)

One: You Must Raise the Thought of Awakening

The thought of awakening goes by many names, but they all point to one
mind. The great teacher Nagarjuna put it this way: the mind that simply
watches the world arise and pass away, the mind that observes
impermanence, is itself the thought of awakening. So let us take that
mind, for now, as the thought of awakening.

Truly, while you are watching how everything passes, the idea of a self
does not come up, and thoughts of name and gain do not get started. You
are frightened by how fast time is going, and so you practise the way
as if your hair had caught fire. You see how little your body and your
life can be relied on, and so you push yourself, like the buddha in the
old story who stood on one foot for seven days out of sheer devotion.
The sweetest singers in heaven could be praising you, and it would only
be the evening wind brushing your ear. The most beautiful women in all
the old stories could be standing in front of you, and they would be
morning dew across your eyes. Once you are free of the pull of sounds
and sights, you fall in naturally with what the mind of the way is
about.

In the past as now, there have been people who heard little and people
who saw little, and most of them fell into the pit of name and gain and
lost the life of the buddha way for good. It is pitiable. It is a
shame. You must know this. A man may read the finest scriptures, the
simple ones and the profound ones. He may handle and pass on the texts
of every school, the open teachings and the secret ones. But if he has
not thrown away name and gain, nobody should call that raising the
thought of awakening.

Some people say: the thought of awakening is the mind of unsurpassed
perfect awakening, and it has nothing to do with reputation and profit.
Some say: it is seeing three thousand worlds in a single thought. Some
say: it is the teaching in which not a single thought arises. Some say:
it is the mind that enters the realm of the buddhas.

People like this do not know what the thought of awakening is. They
slander it without meaning to. They are far, far away from the buddha
way.

Try turning and looking at yourself. Look at your own name and gain.
Look at the mind you have right now. Has it fused with the whole nature
of three thousand worlds in one thought? Has it realised the teaching
in which not one thought arises? Or is there nothing in it but the
craving for a name and the love of gain, and no mind of the way to be
found at all?

The saints of old who found the way and found the dharma had their
ways of mixing with the dust of the world, but they had no crooked
thoughts of name and gain. They did not even cling to the dharma. How
much less would they cling to the world.

The thought of awakening I am talking about is the one described
above: the mind that observes impermanence. It is nothing like what
those madmen are pointing at. The thought in which nothing arises, the
three thousand worlds in a single thought: those are wonderful
practices, but they come after the thought of awakening has been
raised. They are not to be treated carelessly. Only forget the self for
a while, and practise quietly. That is close kin to the thought of
awakening.

Every one of the sixty-two wrong views takes the self as its root. So
when the idea of a self comes up, sit quietly and look into it. What is
the root of everything inside and outside this body of mine, right now?
Body, hair and skin came from my parents, from two drops, one red and
one white. From beginning to end they are empty, so they are not the
self. Mind, intellect, consciousness and understanding are tied to the
length of one life. One breath in and one breath out, and in the end,
what are they? So they are not the self. There is nothing here or there
that can be held on to.

The deluded hold on. The awakened let go. And then people work out a
self that has no self, and cling to an arising that does not arise.
They do not do the practice of the buddha way, which can be done. They
do not cut off worldly feeling, which can be cut off. They are
disgusted by the real dharma and go looking for a false one. Is that
not a mistake?

Two: Having Seen and Heard the True Dharma, You Must Practise It

A loyal minister offers one word, and it can have the force to turn the
whole of heaven round. The buddhas and patriarchs give out a single
word, and there is nobody whose mind is not turned by it. But unless a
ruler is wise, there is no room in him for loyal advice. And unless a
man stands out from the crowd, there is no room in him for the words of
the buddhas. If the mind has not been turned, the flow of birth and
death has not been cut off. If loyal advice finds no room, the good
government of the country has not been put into practice.

Three: The Buddha Way Must Be Entered Through Practice

In the world they say: study, and a salary comes with it. The Buddha
said: practise, and realisation comes with it. Nobody has ever heard of
a man who got a salary without studying, or realisation without
practising. Your practice may come through faith or through
understanding; it may be sudden or gradual. Either way, you have to do
the practice before you can go beyond it into realisation. Your study
may be shallow or deep, quick or slow. Either way, you have to pile the
study up before you can draw a salary. None of this depends on whether
the king favours you, or whether luck is with you.

If a salary could be had without study, who would hand down the way in
which the old kings kept order and settled the world? If realisation
could be had without practice, who would understand the Buddha's
teaching about delusion and awakening? You should know that you set up
your practice right in the middle of delusion, and you get realisation
before awakening. Only then do you find out that the raft and the boat
were last night's dream, and you cut off for good the old view that
took a piece of rope for a snake.

This is not something the Buddha forces on you. It is your own
capacity, turning about.

And besides: what practice brings on is realisation. The treasure of
your own house does not come from outside. What realisation puts to
work is practice. How could the tracks across the ground of the mind be
turned back?

And yet, if you turn the eye of realisation round and look back at the
ground of practice, there is not a speck of dust in your eye, and what
you see is white clouds for ten thousand miles. If you lift the foot of
practice and try it against the steps of realisation, there is not a
mote of dust under it, and what you tread is the gap between heaven and
earth. At this point you take a step backwards, and jump into the
ground of the buddhas.

Written on the ninth day of the third month, the second year of Tenpuku.

Four: Do Not Practise the Buddha-Dharma With a Mind That Seeks Gain

In practising the buddha-dharma you must take the true instruction of
someone who has gone before you, and not use your own private ideas.
Besides, the buddha-dharma cannot be got with a mind, and it cannot be
got without a mind. If the mind you are working with does not agree
with the way, your body and mind will never be at peace. When body and
mind are not at peace, they are not at ease. When they are not at ease,
thorns grow up over the road to realisation.

So what kind of practice agrees with the way? A mind that does not pick
things up and put them down. A mind with no name and gain in it.

The practice of the buddha-dharma is not something you do for other
people. But in the world as it is now, the minds of the people who
practise it are far, far from the way. If something is admired, they
practise it, even knowing it is not the way. If something is not
respected and praised, they drop it and will not practise it, even
knowing it is the true way. How painful.

Try quieting your mind and looking into it. Is this way of behaving
the buddha-dharma or not? It is shameful. It is shameful. The eye of
the saints is on it.

A man who practises the buddha-dharma does not do it even for himself,
let alone for reputation and profit. He does it only for the
buddha-dharma's sake.

The compassion of the buddhas, their pity for living beings, is not for
their own sake and not for the sake of others. It is simply what the
buddha-dharma always is. Have you not seen it in small insects and
animals rearing their young? Body and mind put to hardship, the whole
business bitter labour, and in the end they raise them, and for the
parents there is nothing in it at all. And yet the compassion of a
creature thinking of its young is like that, even in something so
small. It is a picture of the buddhas thinking of living beings.

The wonderful dharma of the buddhas is not compassion alone. It appears
everywhere, through every gate, and this is the root of all of it.
Since we are already the children of the buddhas, why not fall in with
the way the buddhas do things?

A practitioner must not think of practising the buddha-dharma for his
own sake. He must not practise it for name and gain, or to get a
reward, or to get miraculous powers. He must practise the
buddha-dharma only for the buddha-dharma. That is the way.

Five: In Taking Up Zen and Studying the Way, You Must Seek a True Teacher

A man of old said: if the thought is not raised correctly, ten thousand
practices are all wasted. That is true. Whether your practice of the
way turns out right or wrong depends on your guide.

The student is like good timber. The teacher is like a carpenter.
However good the timber, without a good workman its beauty never comes
out. However crooked the wood, in skilful hands its excellence shows at
once. You can tell from this that whether the awakening is true or
false follows from whether the teacher is true or false.

But in our country there has never yet been a true teacher. How do I
know? Look at what they say and examine it, the way you would trace a
stream back to its source. The teachers of our country, from long ago,
have compiled books, instructed disciples, and given teaching to gods
and men. But their words are green. Their language is not ripe. They
have not reached the top of the ground of study, so how could they get
anywhere near realisation? They have only handed down written words and
made people recite names. Day and night they count another man's
treasure, and have not half a coin of their own. The blame for this
lies with the old teachers.

Some direct people to seek awakening outside the mind. Some teach
people to pray for rebirth in another land. Confusion starts here, and
crooked thinking sets up its office here.

Even if you are given good medicine, if nobody teaches you how to take
it, it makes you sicker than poison would. In our country, from of old,
there has been nobody, so to speak, to give out good medicine, and no
teacher to take the poison out of it. That is why the sickness of this
life is so hard to get rid of, and why we cannot escape old age and
death. It is all the fault of the teachers, and no fault of the
students at all.

Why do I say so? Because those who set up as teachers make people
abandon the root and chase the branches. Before they have got their own
understanding settled, they attend only to their own opinions, teach
others carelessly, and drag them down into wrong states. It is pitiable
that the teachers do not know they are in error themselves. So how are
the students going to work out what is right and what is wrong? It is a
sad thing.

In this remote little country the buddha-dharma has not spread, and a
true teacher has not appeared. If you want to study the unsurpassed
buddha way, you should go far away and consult those who know, in the
land of Song China. You should look, far off, for the living road that
lies outside the mind. If you cannot find a true teacher, it is better
not to study at all.

A true teacher is not a matter of being old and long established. He
is someone who has made the true dharma clear and received the seal of
a true teacher's confirmation. Book learning does not come first.
Clever understanding does not come first. He has a capacity beyond the
ordinary measure and a will that goes past the usual limits. He is not
held by his own views. He does not get stuck in his feelings and his
opinions. What he does and what he understands match. That is a true
teacher.

Six: What You Should Know in Taking Up Zen

Taking up Zen and studying the way is the great matter of a lifetime.
It must not be treated lightly, and certainly not casually. Men of old
cut off an arm for it, and cut off fingers. Those are the fine traces
they left in China. Long ago the Buddha left his house and gave up his
kingdom. Those are the tracks left by the practice of the way.

People now say: do the practice that is easy to do. That is badly said,
and it is thoroughly out of keeping with the buddha way. If that is the
spirit you bring to one thing, then even lying down is laziness. Be
lazy in one thing and you will be lazy in ten thousand. A man who
prefers what is easy is telling you himself that he is no vessel for
the way.

Besides: the dharma that is current in the world today is the dharma
that our great teacher Shakyamuni got only after immeasurable ages of
hard and bitter practice. If the source was like that, how could the
streams running out of it be easy?

You who love the way: do not set your mind on easy practice. If you
look for easy practice you will certainly not reach the real ground,
and you will certainly not arrive at the place where the treasure is.
The men of old had great capacity, and even they called it hard. You
should understand from that how deep and how great the buddha way is.
If the buddha way were easy by nature, the men of great capacity in the
past would not have called it hard to practise and hard to understand.

Compare the people of today with the people of old. We do not come to
one hair on nine oxen. With roots this shallow and knowledge this thin,
even if we drove ourselves and tried to do the hard practice, we could
not come up to what the ancients found easy to practise and easy to
understand.

So what is this dharma of easy understanding and easy practice that
people today like so much? It is not even a worldly dharma any more.
Nor is it the buddha-dharma. It does not even come up to the practice
of Mara the Evil One. It does not even come up to the practice of the
non-Buddhist schools, or the lesser Buddhist ones. You might call it
the far end of ordinary delusion. It makes a show of getting free, but
it is only more turning of the same wheel.

Look at it. To break your bones and crush your marrow: is that so hard?
Bringing the mind under control and handling it is harder. To keep the
long fast and the pure life: is that so hard? Bringing the body under
control and conducting it is harder. If grinding your bones to powder
were what counted, then plenty of people from of old could endure it,
and very few got the dharma. If keeping the fast were what counted,
then plenty have kept it from long ago, and very few woke up to the
way. That is because bringing the mind under control is so extremely
difficult.

Cleverness does not come first. Learning does not come first. Mind,
intellect and consciousness do not come first. Memory, imagination and
contemplation do not come first. Without using any of these at all, you
bring body and mind into order and enter the buddha way. Old Shakyamuni
said of Avalokiteshvara, the bodhisattva of compassion, that he entered
the stream and forgot what was known. That is what this means. The two
appearances, moving and still, plainly do not arise. That is what
bringing the mind into order looks like.

If the buddha way could be entered by cleverness and wide learning,
then the senior monk Shenxiu, the most learned man in his monastery,
would have been the one to do it. If the buddha way could be refused to
the plain-bodied and the lowly, then how would Huineng, the barefoot
rice-pounder who became the sixth patriarch, have dared? It is clear
from this that the transmission of the buddha way lies outside
cleverness and wide learning.

Search it out. Look, and take part. It does not matter that you are old
and failing. It does not matter that you are a child, or in your prime.
Zhaozhou began his practice past sixty, and he became the hero of the
patriarchs' seat. The girl Zheng studied a long time from the age of
thirteen, and she stood out in the whole monastery. The power of the
buddha-dharma appears according to whether you put yourself into it or
not. It divides according to whether you practise or do not. Those who
have long studied in the doctrinal schools, or who have old skill in
worldly books, may all come to the Zen gate. There are many examples.
Huisi of Nanyue was a man of many talents, and still he went to study
under Bodhidharma's line. Xuanjue of Yongjia was an outstanding man,
and he went to study under Huineng, and made the dharma clear and found
the way. That is the strength of studying with a teacher.

But when you put your questions to a master of the school, listen to
what the teacher says and do not match it against your own views. If
you match it against your own views you will not get the teacher's
dharma. When you study with a teacher and hear the dharma, clean out
body and mind, quiet your eyes and ears, and simply take in what the
teacher gives without mixing any other thought into it. Body and mind
as one thing, like water poured from one vessel into another. Do that,
and you get the teacher's dharma.

Now there are foolish, stupid people who either memorise texts or store
up what they have heard before, and put that alongside what the teacher
says. What they have then is only their own views and the old words,
and the teacher's words never fit them. There is another sort who put
their own views first and then open the scriptures, hold on to a phrase
or two, and take that for the buddha-dharma. Afterwards, when they go
to a clear-eyed teacher and a master of the school and hear the dharma,
if it matches their own views they call it right, and if it does not
agree with their old ideas they call it wrong. They do not know the
method of throwing off what is false. So how are they going to climb
the road back to what is true? They will still be deluded after ages as
many as the sands. It is greatly to be pitied.

Students should know that the buddha way lies outside thinking,
distinguishing, guessing, contemplating, perceiving and intellectual
understanding. If it lay inside those, then, since we have been in the
middle of them from birth and have always played with them, why do we
still not perceive the buddha way now? A person studying the way must
not use thinking and distinguishing and the rest. Take your own body
and examine it while you are carrying thinking and the rest about with
you, and this becomes clear as in a bright mirror.

The gate you go in by is fully known to a master of the school who has
got the dharma. It is not something the teachers of the written word
can reach.

Written on the Qingming day of the year of the horse, Tenpuku.

Seven: Those Who Want Release Must Take Up Zen

The buddha-dharma is superior to all the other ways, and that is why
people seek it. When the Buddha was in the world there were not two
teachings and not two teachers. The great teacher Shakyamuni drew
living beings in by unsurpassed awakening and nothing else. Since
Kashyapa received the treasury of the true dharma eye from him, there
have been twenty-eight generations in India and six in China, and on to
the founders of the five houses of Zen, each receiving it in direct
succession, with no break in it. So from the Putong era of the Liang
dynasty onwards, from the monks up to the kings and ministers, nobody
of any standing has failed to come over to it.

Truly, what you ought to love as superior is what is superior. Do not
be like the Duke of She, who loved dragons. He had dragons carved on
his pillars and painted on his walls, and when a real dragon heard of
it and came to visit, he ran away.

In China and the countries east of it, the net of doctrinal writings is
spread over the sea and lies all across the mountains. It lies across
the mountains, but it has no heart for the clouds. It is spread over
the sea, but it has a withered heart for the waves. Fools relish it, as
though a man picked up a fish's eye and took it for a pearl. The
deluded play with it, as though a man hid away a common stone and
honoured it as jade. Many fall into Mara's pit and injure themselves
again and again.

It is pitiable that in a remote and backward place a crooked wind blows
easily and the true dharma goes about with difficulty. Yet China has
already come over to the true dharma of the buddhas, while in our
country, and in Korea, the true dharma has not spread. Why is that?
Why? In Korea at least they hear the name of the true dharma. In our
country it has not even been heard. This is because the teachers who
went to China in the past all got stuck in the net of doctrine. They
brought back Buddhist books, but it is as if they had forgotten the
buddha-dharma. What was the good of it? In the end the work came to
nothing. And this was because they did not know how the study of the
way is actually done. It is pitiable: a whole human life spent in
labour for nothing.

Now, when you study the buddha way and first come in at the gate, you
hear the teaching of someone who knows and you practise as you are
taught. At that point there is something you should understand. The
dharma turns me, and I turn the dharma. When I am able to turn the
dharma, I am strong and the dharma is weak. When the dharma turns me in
its turn, the dharma is strong and I am weak. The buddha-dharma has
always had these two phases. Nobody who is not a true heir has ever
known it. Nobody who is not a patch-robed monk has so much as heard
the name of it. Without knowing how this actually works, the study of
the way cannot be sorted out, and how is right to be told from wrong?

People who take up Zen and study the way now have this handed to them
directly, and so they do not go wrong. There is no other gate. A person
who longs for the buddha way cannot come to know the true way except by
taking up Zen.

Eight: The Conduct of a Zen Monk

From the buddhas and the patriarchs down, it has been directly pointed
at and handed on one to one. Twenty-eight teachers in India, six in
China, and not a thread or a hair added, not one speck broken. The robe
came down to Huineng, and the dharma went round the worlds like grains
of sand. In those days the treasury of the true dharma eye flourished
in Tang China.

As for what this dharma is: you cannot grope for it, and you cannot go
looking for it. Where it is seen, knowing is lost. When it is got, it
goes beyond mind. Huineng lost his face at Huangmei, the night the
fifth patriarch handed him the robe. Huike cut off his arm at Shaolin
to show Bodhidharma he was in earnest. Huike got the marrow, turned his
mind over, and bought elegance. He made his bow, stepped back, and fell
into the advantage of it. And yet in mind and in body there is no
dwelling and no attachment, no staying and no getting stuck.

A monk asked Zhaozhou: does a dog have buddha-nature or not? Zhaozhou
said: no.

Can you take a measurement on top of that word "no"? Can you get stuck
on it? There is no handle to it anywhere. Please try letting go. Just
let go and look.

What are body and mind? What is your conduct? What is birth and death?
What is the buddha-dharma? What is the dharma of the world? Mountains
and rivers and the great earth, people and animals and houses: in the
end, what are they?

Look, and go on looking. Of themselves the two appearances, moving and
still, plainly do not arise. When they do not arise, that is not mere
blankness. There is nobody to confirm it, and a great many who mistake
it.

Those who take up Zen: you have got half the road. Do not refuse the
whole road. I pray you. I pray you.

Nine: You Must Practise Facing the Way

A man of stature who studies the way must first know whether he is
facing the way correctly or not. Shakyamuni, the tamer of men, sat
under the tree of awakening, saw the morning star, and all at once was
awakened to the unsurpassed way. What he was awakened to is not
something the wisest disciples can reach. The Buddha awoke by himself,
and buddha handed it on to buddha, and it has not been cut off down to
now. Can someone who has this awakening be anything but a buddha?

Facing the way means understanding the whole extent of the buddha way.
It means making clear what the buddha way looks like. The buddha way is
under everybody's heels. Let the way get in your way, let it stop you,
and it is clear where you are standing. Let awakening get in your way,
and the person is fully complete.

And so, even if you raise a complete understanding, you still come down
in a half awakening. That is the elegance of facing the way.

People studying the way now have not sorted out whether the way is open
or blocked, and they are very fond of having proofs. Who is it that
does not go wrong? Remember the parable of the rich man's son. He left
his father and ran off. He abandoned the treasure and went wandering.
Though he was the only son of a rich man, he spent a long time as a
hired labourer, a low sort of person. There are good reasons for it.

A person studying the way seeks to be stopped by the way. To be stopped
by the way is to lose the traces of awakening. Someone who practises
the buddha way must first have faith in the buddha way. Someone who has
faith in the buddha way must have faith in himself: that he is already
in the way, not confused, not upside down, with nothing to add and
nothing to take away, and without error. Raise a faith like this, make
a way like this clear, and practise accordingly. That is the ground of
studying the way.

The rule for doing it is to sit and cut off the root of the intellect,
so that it does not go down the road of intellectual understanding.
That is a skilful means for drawing in beginners. After that, dropping
away body and mind, and putting down both delusion and awakening, is
the second stage.

Generally speaking, people who have faith that they themselves are in
the buddha way are the hardest of all to find. If you have correct
faith that you are in the way, then naturally you will understand
whether the great way is open or blocked, and you will know how
delusion and awakening come about.

Try sitting and cutting off the root of the intellect. Eight or nine
out of ten will suddenly get to see the way.

Ten: Taking Hold of It Directly

In sorting out body and mind there are two ways: studying with a
teacher and hearing the dharma, and the effort of sitting. Hearing the
dharma moves about in your mind and consciousness. Sitting is a matter
of practice and realisation, on your left hand and your right. So in
entering the buddha way you must not throw either of them away when you
take hold.

Everybody has a body and a mind. In what they do there is bound to be
strength and weakness, boldness and dullness. It moves, and it takes a
form. To realise buddhahood directly with this body and this mind is
what taking hold means. Not to turn the body and mind you have always
had into something else, but simply to follow them into realisation,
is what is meant by directly. That is what is meant by taking hold.
Because you simply follow it, this is not the old view. Because you
simply take hold, this is not a new nest.
