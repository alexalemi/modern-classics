DŌGEN: THE PRACTICE INSTRUCTIONS
Translation strategy. From the KANBUN, with NO English crib.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT THIS EDITION IS, AND WHAT IT IS NOT
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Two complete works by Dōgen Kigen (1200-1253), founder of the Sōtō
school:

  普勸坐禪儀  Fukanzazengi, 1227      894 characters
    Written the year he came back from China. The instruction for
    zazen: what to do with the legs, the hands, the eyes, the breath,
    and the mind. It is the shortest important text in Zen.
  學道用心集  Gakudō Yōjinshū, 1234   4,342 characters, ten sections
    Ten pieces of advice to people taking up the practice, from
    "you must raise the thought of awakening" to "you must take hold
    of it directly."

SOURCE: Japanese Wikisource, transcribed and proofread to 100% from
承陽大師聖教全集 (Jōyō Daishi Shōgyō Zenshū), EIHEIJI'S OWN COLLECTED
EDITION OF 1909, NDL Digital Collections pid 823139 and 823141, marked
PDM. VERIFIED, not assumed: the NDL page images were read directly
against the transcription for the Gakudō Yōjinshū's first four
sections, and they agree character for character -- 「第二　見聞正法必可
修習事／右忠臣獻一言、數有囘天之力」 and the colophon 「天福二甲午三月
九日書」. Two witnesses.

THERE IS NO PUBLIC-DOMAIN ENGLISH DŌGEN. This is the central fact about
the edition and it is stated in the front matter rather than implied.
Every English Dōgen -- Nishijima & Cross, Tanahashi, Waddell & Abe,
Cleary, Kim -- is in copyright, and Gutenberg, Archive.org and
Wikisource return nothing. So unlike every other from-the-original
volume in this collection (Murray for Homer, Riley for Ovid, Longfellow
for Dante, Jebb for Sophocles, Symonds for Cellini), reference/ is
EMPTY, and the second witness the method depends on is absent.
What partly stands in for it: the text is short enough to work phrase
by phrase, and its cruxes are famous enough to have settled English
renderings to check against.

IT IS KANBUN -- classical Chinese written by a Japanese author. Not
Japanese. 「原夫。道本圓通、爭假修證。」 is Chinese syntax throughout.
This is the collection's first from-the-Chinese volume; sun-tzu/
modernises Giles's English precisely because it is not one.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE CHECK THAT CANNOT FIRE
--------------------------------------------------------------------
A WORD RATIO IS MEANINGLESS ON THIS BOOK. CJK text has no spaces, so
len(text.split()) is about 1 for a whole chapter, and verify.py's ratio
-- the collection's one mechanical guard against silent summarising --
would be inert while looking like coverage. That is worse than no check
(the nights lesson, learned there only after shipping it).
check.py therefore measures CHARACTERS of source against WORDS of
translation. Kanbun is extremely compressed. MEASURED, after guessing wrong: the
Fukanzazengi runs 894 characters to 1,120 English words, or 1.25 words
per character -- I had predicted 2.0-3.0, which would have set a band
no correct file could satisfy. Derive the band from the finished files
and pin it (the purgatorio rule); a file below it has been summarised,
which in a text this dense is very easy to do by accident.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
3. VOICE
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Dōgen is not vague and not soothing, and the English tradition has made
him both. The Fukanzazengi is a MANUAL: it tells you which foot goes on
which thigh, that the ears line up with the shoulders and the nose with
the navel, that the tongue rests on the palate and the eyes stay open.
Render the physical instructions as physical instructions -- plainly,
in the imperative, the way soap-bubbles/ renders Boys at the bench.
The philosophy is welded to the manual and must not be lifted out of
it. The most famous sentence in the book sits in the middle of a
posture instruction: 思量箇不思量底。不思量底如何思量。非思量。
"Think of not-thinking. How do you think of not-thinking? Beyond
thinking." Keep it as terse in English as it is in Chinese; every added
word is a loss.
READABILITY IS THE DELIVERABLE (Alex, 2026-09-02, after reading the
first draft: "I found it hard to read"). The persona is a scholar who
has spent a life on this text AND a storyteller retelling it, and the
storyteller half was missing. A faithful rendering that keeps Chinese
parallelism, carries every allusion as a bare name and every technical
term as a term is a crib, not a retelling. Fold the allusion into the
sentence; choose the plain word; give a paradox a handhold; write for
a bright fourteen-year-old reading aloud. See running_notes.txt for
the full diagnosis and the rule.
The Gakudō Yōjinshū is warmer and more argumentative -- Dōgen telling
students what to watch out for, often sharply, sometimes with contempt
for teachers he thinks are frauds. He quotes Nāgārjuna, he says 可哀可
惜 ("it is pitiable, it is a shame"), and he is completely certain.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. LOCKED VOCABULARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------
These are the terms the book turns on. The ledger is running_notes.txt.

  坐禪 zazen        SITTING. Keep "zazen" -- it is in English
                    dictionaries and Dōgen means one specific practice.
  辨道 bendō        "wholehearted practice of the way"; not "study".
  只管打坐          "just sitting" -- appears here as 唯務打坐.
  身心自然脱落      THE TEXT'S OWN FORM, checked and not remembered
                    (the epictetus rule). This entry was first written
                    as 身心脱落, the phrase Dōgen is famous for receiving
                    from Rujing -- but the Fukanzazengi has 自然 in the
                    middle of it, and a pin taken from memory could only
                    have been satisfied by a translation that dropped a
                    word Dōgen wrote. Rendered "body and mind will drop
                    away OF THEMSELVES".
  本來面目          "the original face". Kept; it is a koan phrase.
  非思量            "beyond thinking" -- NOT "not thinking", which is
                    the opposite of what the passage argues.
  公案現成          "the koan realised in the present"; do not render
                    公案 as "riddle".
  菩提心 bodaishin  "the thought of awakening" -- Gakudō Yōjinshū I
                    is entirely about what this does and does not mean.
  無常 mujō         "impermanence"; 觀無常 "to observe impermanence".
  正師              "a true teacher" -- section 5 is about how to tell.
  名利              "name and gain" -- fame and profit, Dōgen's
                    standing target. Never soften it.
  法執 / 世執       "clinging to the dharma" / "clinging to the world".
  參禪              "to take up Zen practice" -- not "meditate on".

--------------------------------------------------------------------
5. THE VERNE RULE
--------------------------------------------------------------------
No editorial note anywhere in the volume. Dōgen's claims stand as his:
that this practice is the only correct one and the others are mistaken;
that people who talk about the thought of awakening without having
raised it are "far, far from the buddha way"; his contempt for those
who study for reputation; the miraculous framing of the buddhas and
patriarchs; and the flat assertion that sitting IS awakening rather
than a means to it. He is a thirteenth-century Buddhist monk making
religious claims, and the edition renders them, not a modern
accommodation of them.
