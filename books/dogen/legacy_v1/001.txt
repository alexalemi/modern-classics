Gakudo Yojinshu: Points to Watch in Studying the Way (1234)

One: You Must Raise the Thought of Awakening

The thought of awakening goes by many names, and one mind is one of
them. The patriarch Nāgārjuna said: the mind that simply observes the
world arising and passing away, the mind that observes impermanence, is
also called the thought of awakening. So may we take that mind,
provisionally, as the thought of awakening?

Truly, when you are observing impermanence, the mind of self does not
come up, and thoughts of name and gain do not start. You are afraid of
how fast the light goes, and so you practise the way as though you were
putting out a fire in your hair. You look at how little your body and
your life can be relied on, and so you make an effort, like the man who
stood on tiptoe. Though you hear the praising voices of the kinnara and
the kalavinka, they are the evening wind brushing your ear. Though you
see the exquisite faces of Maoqiang and Xishi, they are the morning dew
across your eyes. Once you are free of the bindings of sound and form,
you naturally fall into agreement with what the mind of the way is
about.

In the past and now, there have been men who had heard little and men
who had seen little, and most of them have fallen into the pit of name
and gain and lost the life of the buddha way for good. It is pitiable.
It is a shame. You must know this. Though a man may read the fine
scriptures of the provisional and the real teachings, though he may
handle and pass on the texts of the exoteric and esoteric schools, if
he has not thrown away name and gain, this is not to be called raising
the thought of awakening.

Some say: the thought of awakening is the mind of unsurpassed perfect
awakening, and must not be tied to reputation and profit. Some say: it
is the contemplation of three thousand worlds in a single thought.
Some say: it is the dharma-gate in which not a single thought arises.
Some say: it is the mind that enters the realm of buddhas.

People like this do not know what the thought of awakening is. They
slander it carelessly. They are far, far away from the buddha way.

Try turning and looking at your own self, at your own name and gain, at
the mind you have now. Has it fused with the nature and the
characteristics of three thousand worlds in one thought? Has it
realised the dharma-gate in which not one thought arises? Or is there
nothing there but the deluded craving for name and the love of gain,
and no mind of the way to be had at all?

The saints of old who gained the way and gained the dharma had their
skilful means for mixing with the dust of the world, but they had no
crooked thoughts of name and gain. They did not even cling to the
dharma — how much less to the world.

The thought of awakening I am speaking of is the one described above:
the mind that observes impermanence. It is not at all what those madmen
are pointing at. The thought in which nothing arises, the
characteristics of the three thousand worlds — these are excellent
practices that come after the thought of awakening has been raised.
They are not to be treated carelessly. Only forget the self for a
while, and practise quietly: that is close kin to the thought of
awakening.

The sixty-two views all take the self as their root. So when the view
of a self comes up, sit quietly and look into it. What is the root of
everything inside and outside this body of mine, now? Body, hair and
skin came from my parents, from two drops, red and white; from
beginning to end they are empty, so they are not the self. Mind,
intellect, consciousness and understanding are tied to the length of a
life; one breath in and out, and in the end what are they? So they are
not the self. There is nothing here or there that can be held on to.

The deluded hold on. The awakened let go. And then people work out a
self that has no self, and hold on to an arising that does not arise;
they do not do the practice of the buddha way, which can be done, and
they do not cut off worldly feeling, which can be cut off; they are
disgusted by the real dharma and go looking for a false one. Is that
not a mistake?

Two: Having Seen and Heard the True Dharma, You Must Practise It

A loyal minister offers one word, and it can have the force of turning
heaven round. The buddhas and patriarchs give out a single word, and
there is nobody whose mind is not turned by it. But unless a ruler is
enlightened, there is no room in him for loyal advice; and unless a man
stands out from the crowd, there is no room in him for the words of the
buddhas. If the mind is not turned, the flow of birth and death has not
been cut off. If loyal advice finds no room, the virtuous government of
the country has not been put into practice.

Three: The Buddha Way Must Be Entered Through Practice

In the world they say: study, and the salary is in it. The Buddha said:
practise, and realisation is in it. Nobody has ever heard of a man who
got a salary without studying, or realisation without practising.
Whether your practice belongs to faith or to dharma, whether it is
sudden or gradual, you have to wait on practice before you go beyond
into realisation. Whether your study is shallow or deep, quick or slow,
you have to pile study up before you take a salary. This is not a
matter of whether the ruler favours you or not, or whether fortune is
with you or not.

If a salary could be had without study, who would hand down the way in
which the former kings ordered and settled the world? If realisation
could be had without practice, who would understand the Tathāgata's
dharma of delusion and awakening? You should know that you set up your
practice in the middle of delusion and get realisation before
awakening. Just then you first learn that the raft and the boat were
last night's dream, and you cut off for good the old view that took a
rope for a snake.

This is not something the Buddha forces on you. It is your own
capacity, turning about.

And besides: what practice brings on is realisation. The treasure of
your own house does not come from outside. What realisation puts to
work is practice. How could the tracks across the ground of the mind be
turned back?

And yet, if you turn the eye of realisation round and look back at the
ground of practice, there is not a speck of dust in the eye, and what
you will see is white clouds for ten thousand miles. If you lift the
foot of practice and try it against the steps of realisation, there is
not a mote of dust under it, and what you tread is the gap between
heaven and earth. At this point you step backwards, and jump into the
ground of the buddhas.

Written on the ninth day of the third month, the second year of Tenpuku.

Four: Do Not Practise the Buddha-Dharma With a Mind That Seeks Gain

In practising the buddha-dharma you must receive the true instruction
of one who has gone before you, and not use your own private ideas.
Besides, the buddha-dharma cannot be got with a mind, and it cannot be
got without a mind. If the mind you are working with does not agree
with the way, body and mind will never be at peace. When body and mind
are not at peace, they are not at ease. When they are not at ease,
thorns grow up over the way of realisation.

So how do you conduct a practice that agrees with the way? The mind
does not pick things up and put them down; the mind has no name and
gain in it.

The practice of the buddha-dharma is not something you do for other
people. In the world as it is now, the minds of people who practise the
buddha-dharma are far, far from the way. If something is admired, they
practise it, even knowing it is not the way. If it is not respected and
praised, they abandon it and will not practise it, even knowing it is
the true way. How painful.

Try quieting the mind and looking into it. Is this conduct of the mind
the buddha-dharma or not? It is shameful. It is shameful. The eye of
the saints is on it.

A man who practises the buddha-dharma does not do it even for himself,
let alone for reputation and profit. He does it only for the
buddha-dharma's sake.

The compassion of the buddhas, their pity for living beings, is not for
their own sake and not for the sake of others. It is simply what the
buddha-dharma always is. Do you not see it in small insects and
animals, rearing their young? Body and mind put to hardship, the whole
management of it bitter labour, and in the end they raise them — and
for the parents there is nothing in it at all. And still the compassion
of a creature thinking of its young is like that, even in something so
small. It resembles the buddhas thinking of living beings.

The wonderful dharma of the buddhas is not compassion alone. It appears
everywhere, through every gate, and this is the root of all of it.
Since we are already the children of buddhas, why not fall in with the
way the buddhas do things?

A practitioner must not think of practising the buddha-dharma for his
own sake. He must not practise it for name and gain, or to get a
reward, or to get miraculous powers. He must practise the
buddha-dharma only for the buddha-dharma. That is the way.

Five: In Taking Up Zen and Studying the Way, You Must Seek a True Teacher

A man of old said: if the raising of the thought is not correct, the
ten thousand practices are all wasted. That is true. Whether your
practice of the way is right or wrong depends on your guide.

The student is like good timber; the teacher is like a carpenter.
However good the timber, without a good workman its beauty never
appears. However crooked the wood, in skilful hands its excellence
shows at once. You can tell from this that whether the awakening is
true or false follows from whether the teacher is true or false.

But in our country there has never yet been a true teacher. How do I
know? Look at what they say and examine it — as you would trace a
stream to find its source. The teachers of our country from long ago
have compiled books, instructed disciples and given teaching to gods
and men. But their words are green; their language is not ripe. They
have not reached the top of the ground of study, so how could they get
anywhere near realisation? They have only handed down written words and
made people recite names. Day and night they count another man's
treasure, and have not half a coin of their own. The blame for this
lies with the old teachers.

Some direct people to seek awakening outside the mind. Some teach
people to pray for rebirth in another land. Confusion starts here, and
crooked thinking has its office here.

Even if you are given good medicine, if you are not taught how to take
it, it makes you iller than poison would. In our country from of old
there has been, as it were, nobody to give out good medicine, and no
teacher to take the poison out of it. That is why sickness in life is
hard to get rid of, and why we cannot escape old age and death. It is
all the fault of the teachers, and no fault of the students at all.

Why do I say so? Because those who set up as teachers make people
abandon the root and chase the branches. Before they have got their own
understanding settled, they attend only to their own opinions, and
teach others carelessly, and drag them down into wrong states. It is
pitiable that the teachers do not know they are in error themselves —
so how are the disciples going to work out what is right and wrong? It
is a sad thing.

In this remote little country the buddha-dharma has not spread, and a
true teacher has not appeared. If you want to study the unsurpassed
buddha way, you should go far off and consult those who know in the
land of Song. You should look, far away, for the living road that is
outside the mind. If you cannot get a true teacher, better not to study
at all.

A true teacher is not a question of being old and long-established.
He is one who has made the true dharma clear and received the seal of a
true teacher's confirmation. Letters do not come first; understanding
does not come first. He has a capacity outside the ordinary measure and
a will that goes past the usual limits; he is not held by his own
views; he does not get stuck in his feelings and his knowledge; his
conduct and his understanding match. That is a true teacher.

Six: What You Should Know in Taking Up Zen

Taking up Zen and studying the way is the great matter of a lifetime.
It must not be treated lightly, and certainly not casually. Men of old
cut off an arm and cut off fingers; those are fine traces left in
China. Long ago the Buddha left his house and gave up his country;
those are the tracks left by the practice of the way.

People now say: practise the practice that is easy to do. That is
badly said and thoroughly out of keeping with the buddha way. If you
attend to one thing only in that spirit, then even lying down is
laziness. Be lazy in one thing and you will be lazy in ten thousand. A
man who likes what is easy is telling you himself that he is no vessel
for the way.

Besides: the dharma that is current in the world today is the dharma
that our great teacher Shakyamuni got only after immeasurable ages of
difficult practice and bitter practice. If the source was like that,
how could the streams running out of it be easy?

You who love the way — do not set your mind on easy practice. If you
look for easy practice you will certainly not reach the real ground,
and you will certainly not arrive at the place where the treasure is.
Men of old had great capacity, and even they called it hard. You should
understand from that how deep and how great the buddha way is. If the
buddha way were easy to practise by its nature, the men of great
capacity in the past would not have called it hard to practise and hard
to understand.

Compare people now with the people of old: we do not come to one hair
of nine oxen. With these shallow roots and this thin knowledge, even if
we drove ourselves and tried to do the hard practice, we could not come
up to the easy practice and easy understanding of the ancients.

So what is this dharma of easy understanding and easy practice that
people now like so much? It is no longer a worldly dharma. Neither is
it the buddha-dharma. It does not even reach the practice of Māra the
Evil One. It does not even reach the practice of the outside ways or
the two vehicles. It might be called the extremity of ordinary
delusion. Though it makes a show of getting free, it is only endless
turning in the wheel again.

Look at it: to break your bones and crush your marrow — is that so
hard? Bringing the mind under control and handling it is harder. To
keep the long fast and the pure practice — is that so hard? Bringing
the body under control and conducting it is harder. If pulverising the
bones were what counted, there have been many who could endure it from
of old, and very few who got the dharma. If keeping the fast were what
counted, there have been many from long ago, and very few who awoke to
the way. This is because bringing the mind under control is so
extremely difficult.

Cleverness does not come first. Learned understanding does not come
first. Mind, intellect and consciousness do not come first. Memory,
imagination and contemplation do not come first. Without using any of
these at all, you bring body and mind into order and enter the buddha
way. Old Shakyamuni said: Avalokiteśvara entered the stream and forgot
what was known. That is the meaning of it. The two characteristics,
moving and still, clearly do not arise. That is the order of it.

If the buddha way could be entered by cleverness and wide
understanding, the senior monk Shenxiu would have been the man for it.
If the buddha way could be refused to the plain-bodied and the lowly,
how would the high patriarch of Caoxi have dared? It is clear from this
that the transmission of the dharma of the buddha way lies outside
cleverness and wide understanding.

Search it out, and look, and take part. It does not matter that you are
old and failing; it does not matter that you are a child or in your
prime. Zhaozhou began his practice past sixty, and he became the hero
of the patriarchs' seat. The girl Zheng studied a long time from
thirteen, and she stood out in the monastery. The power of the
buddha-dharma appears according to whether you put yourself into it or
not; it divides according to whether you practise or do not. Those who
have long studied in the doctrinal schools, or who have old skill in
worldly texts, may all come to the Zen gate. There are many examples.
Huisi of Nanyue was a man of many talents, and still he went to study
under Bodhidharma's line. Xuanjue of Yongjia was an outstanding man,
and he studied under the great mirror Huineng, and made the dharma
clear and got the way. This is the strength of studying with a teacher.

But when you put your questions to a master of the school, listen to
what the teacher says and do not match it against your own views. If
you match it against your own views you will not get the teacher's
dharma. When you study with a teacher and hear the dharma, clean out
body and mind, quiet the eyes and ears, and simply take in the
teacher's dharma without mixing any other thought into it. Body and
mind as one thing, like water poured from one vessel into another. If
you can do that, then you get the teacher's dharma.

Now there are foolish, stupid people who either memorise texts or store
up what they have heard before, and put that alongside the teacher's
dharma. In that case what they have is only their own views and the old
words, and the teacher's words never fit them. There is another sort
who put their own views first and then open the scriptures, and hold on
to a phrase or two and take that for the buddha-dharma. Afterwards,
when they go to a clear-eyed teacher and a master of the school and
hear the dharma, if it matches their own views they call it right, and
if it does not agree with their old ideas they call it wrong. They do
not know the method of throwing off what is false — so how are they
going to climb the road back to what is true? They will still be
deluded after ages as many as the sands. It is greatly to be pitied.

Students should know that the buddha way lies outside thinking,
discrimination, guessing, contemplation, perception and intellectual
understanding. If it lay within those, then since we have been in the
middle of them from birth and have always played with them, why do we
still not perceive the buddha way now? A person studying the way must
not use thinking and discrimination and the rest. Take your own body
and examine it while carrying thinking and the rest about with you, and
this becomes clear as in a bright mirror.

The gate you go in by is fully known to a master of the school who has
got the dharma. It is not something the teachers of the written word
can reach.

Written on the Qingming day of the year of the horse, Tenpuku.

Seven: Those Who Want Release Must Take Up Zen

The buddha-dharma is superior to all the other ways, and that is why
people seek it. When the Tathāgata was in the world there were not two
teachings at all and not two teachers at all. The great teacher
Shakyamuni drew living beings in by unsurpassed awakening and nothing
else. Since Kāśyapa received the treasury of the true dharma eye, there
have been twenty-eight generations in India and six in China, and on to
the patriarchs of the five houses, each receiving it in direct
succession, with no break in it. So from the Putong era of the Liang
onwards, from the monks up to the kings and ministers, there is nobody
of any standing who has not come over to it.

Truly, what you ought to love as superior is what is superior. You
should not be like the Duke of She, who loved dragons.

In China and the countries east of it, the net of doctrinal writings is
spread over the sea and lies all across the mountains. It lies across
the mountains but has no mind of the clouds; it is spread over the sea
but has a withered mind for the waves. Fools relish it — as though a
man picked up a fish's eye and took it for a pearl. The deluded play
with it — as though a man hid away a stone from Yan and honoured it as
jade. Many fall into the pit of Māra and injure themselves repeatedly.

It is pitiable that in a remote and backward place a crooked wind blows
easily and the true dharma goes about with difficulty. Yet China has
already come over to the true dharma of the buddhas, while in our
country and in Koryŏ the true dharma has not spread. Why is that? Why?
In Koryŏ at least they hear the name of the true dharma. In our country
it has not even been heard. This is because the teachers who went to
China in the past all got stuck in the doctrinal net. Though they
brought back buddhist books, it is as if they had forgotten the
buddha-dharma. What is the good of it? In the end the work of it came
to nothing. And this is because they did not know how the study of the
way is actually done. It is pitiable — a whole human life spent in
labour for nothing.

Now: when you study the buddha way and first come in at the gate, you
hear the teaching of one who knows and practise as you are taught. At
that time there is something you should understand, namely that the
dharma turns me, and I turn the dharma. When I am able to turn the
dharma, I am strong and the dharma is weak. When the dharma turns me in
its turn, the dharma is strong and I am weak. The buddha-dharma has
always had these two phases. Nobody who is not a true heir has ever
known it. Nobody who is not a patch-robed monk has so much as heard the
name of it. Without knowing how this actually works, the study of the
way cannot be sorted out, and how is right to be distinguished from
wrong?

People who take up Zen and study the way now have this handed to them
directly, and so they do not go wrong. There is no other gate. A person
who longs for the buddha way cannot come to know the true way except by
taking up Zen.

Eight: The Conduct of a Zen Monk

From the buddhas and the patriarchs down, it has been directly pointed
at and singly transmitted. Four and seven in the west, six generations
in the east — not a thread or a hair added, and not one speck broken.
The robe came down to Caoxi, and the dharma went round the worlds of
sand. At that time the treasury of the true dharma eye of the Tathāgata
flourished in the great Tang.

As for what this dharma is: you cannot grope for it and you cannot go
looking for it. Where it is seen, knowing is lost; when it is got, it
goes beyond mind. He lost his face at Huangmei. He cut off his arm at
Shaoshi. He got the marrow, turned his mind over, and bought elegance.
He made his bow, stepped back, and fell into the advantage of it. And
yet in mind and in body there is no dwelling and no attachment, no
staying and no getting stuck.

A monk asked Zhaozhou: does a dog have buddha-nature or not? Zhaozhou
said: no.

Can you take a measurement on top of that word "no"? Can you get stuck
on it? There is no handle to it anywhere. Please try letting go. Just
let go and look.

What are body and mind? What is your conduct? What is birth and death?
What is the buddha-dharma? What is the dharma of the world? Mountains
and rivers and the great earth, people and animals and houses — in the
end, what are they?

Look, and go on looking. Of themselves the two characteristics, moving
and still, clearly do not arise. When they do not arise, this is not
mere blankness. There is nobody to confirm it, and there are a great
many who mistake it.

Those who take up Zen — you have got half the road. Do not refuse the
whole road. I pray you. I pray you.

Nine: You Must Practise Facing the Way

A man of stature who studies the way must first know whether his facing
of the way is correct or not. Shakyamuni, the tamer, sat under the tree
of awakening, saw the morning star, and all at once was suddenly
awakened to the unsurpassed way. What he was awakened to is not
something the hearers and the solitary buddhas can reach. The Buddha
awoke by himself, and buddha handed it on to buddha, and it has not
been cut off down to now. Can one who has this awakening be anything
but a buddha?

Facing the way means understanding the whole extent of the buddha way.
It means making clear what the buddha way looks like. The buddha way is
under everybody's heels. Be obstructed by the way, and it is clear
where you are standing. Be obstructed by awakening, and the person is
fully complete.

And so, even if you raise a complete understanding, you still come down
in a half awakening — and that is the elegance of facing the way.

People studying the way now have not sorted out whether the way is open
or blocked, and are very fond of having proofs of it. Who is it that
does not go wrong? They leave their father and run off, they abandon
the treasure and go wandering. Though he was the only son of a rich
man, he spent a long time as a hired labourer, a low sort of person.
There are good reasons for it.

A person studying the way seeks to be obstructed by the way. To be
obstructed by the way is to lose the traces of awakening. One who
practises the buddha way must first have faith in the buddha way. One
who has faith in the buddha way must have faith in himself: that he is
originally in the way, not confused, not upside down, without increase
or decrease, and without error. Raise a faith like this, make a way
like this clear, and practise accordingly — that is the ground of
studying the way.

The rule for doing it is to sit and cut off the root of the intellect,
so that it does not go down the road of intellectual understanding.
That is a skilful means for drawing in beginners. After that, dropping
away body and mind, and putting down both delusion and awakening, is
the second stage.

Generally speaking, people who have faith that they themselves are in
the buddha way are the hardest of all to find. If you have correct
faith that you are in the way, then naturally you will understand
whether the great way is open or blocked, and you will know how
delusion and awakening come about.

Try sitting and cutting off the root of the intellect. Eight or nine
out of ten will suddenly get to see the way.

Ten: Taking Hold of It Directly

In sorting out body and mind there are two ways: studying with a
teacher and hearing the dharma, and the effort of sitting. Hearing the
dharma moves about in mind and consciousness. Sitting is a matter of
practice and realisation, right and left of you. So in entering the
buddha way you must not throw either of them away when you take hold.

Everybody has a body and a mind. In what they do there is bound to be
strength and weakness, boldness and dullness. It moves, and it takes a
form. To realise buddhahood directly with this body and this mind is
what taking hold means. Not to turn the body and mind you have always
had into something else, but simply to follow it into realisation, is
what is meant by directly. It is what is meant by taking hold. Because
you simply follow it, this is not the old view. Because you simply take
hold, this is not a new nest.
