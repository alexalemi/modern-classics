BOLTZMANN, "REISE EINES DEUTSCHEN PROFESSORS INS ELDORADO" -- STANDING BRIEF
Read this whole file, then running_notes.txt, before translating.

WHAT THIS WORK IS
Ludwig Boltzmann (1844-1906), the Austrian physicist, wrote this comic
travel essay about his 1905 trip from Vienna to California, where he taught
at the University of California's summer school in Berkeley. It closed his
collection Populäre Schriften (Leipzig, 1905). Public domain everywhere.
There is no public-domain English translation: you are translating from
the German, and your English is the only text readers will have.

THE VOICE
A "kleine Plauderei" -- a little chat. Boltzmann is funny, self-mocking,
digressive, warm, a Viennese who worries about his stomach, his luggage and
his beer; a great scientist who describes the machinery of an academic
encyclopedia with the same mock-seriousness as a hotel breakfast. Keep the
jokes working in English (rebuild a pun on the English words; never
explain a joke). Keep his quotations (Schiller, Goethe, songs) as quotations,
translated, verse as verse. Keep his occasional ENGLISH words (washing
rooms, etc.) as they are -- he used English on purpose. Food names: keep
the Viennese dish and gloss it lightly in the sentence the first time
(Geselchtes mit Knödel -> smoked pork with dumplings), never a footnote.

THE STANDARD
Faithful but not literal: every sentence, every aside, every name and
number, in the order he wrote them. Modern, natural English at an
eighth-grade-to-high-school reading level: break up long German sentences,
use contractions where they sound natural, no archaisms, no German word
order. Complete: never summarise, never skip. Target length is about the
same as the German (English usually runs 10-20% longer).

The OCR source has occasional garbage characters (KoQstantinopel,
Amerikay, ^, f, stray digits); read through them to the obvious word. If a
passage is genuinely unreadable, translate what is clear and mark it
[?] -- do not invent.
