George Caleb Bingham's *Stump Speaking* (1853). A candidate in a white coat leans from a wooden platform under a big tree, hands out, making his case to the crowd at his feet. Some of them grin, some listen hard, a man with a cane stands watching, and a boy in front is counting coins. It is the scene Tocqueville kept returning to, a people that argues out its own government in public, painted by a man from Missouri who had stood on such platforms himself.
