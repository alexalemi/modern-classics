John Constable's *Wivenhoe Park, Essex* (1816). A red-brick house stands among oaks across a still lake, with cows grazing in the foreground behind a rail fence and a gate. It is English land as Locke's readers knew it, owned, fenced and worked. Chapter five of the Second Treatise asks how a world given to everyone in common came to be divided up like this, and on what terms anyone may keep his share.
