Drawn for this edition, in the manner of the Dover science paperbacks of the 1950s and 60s, after the geometry lesson in the Meno. Socrates asks Meno's slave boy for a square twice the size of a given one. The boy first doubles the side, which makes it four times as big; the answer is the square built on the diagonal, made of four half-squares, here in blue. Socrates draws it all out of the boy by questions alone, and claims it shows that learning is remembering. The line at the top is his.
