Jacques-Louis David's *The Death of Socrates* (1787). Socrates sits on his prison bed, one hand reaching for the cup of hemlock and the other pointing upward, still talking. The young man handing him the cup turns away and covers his eyes; a friend leans against the wall with his arms raised, and the chains lie on the floor. It is the last scene of the Phaedo, the end of the four dialogues that open this book.
