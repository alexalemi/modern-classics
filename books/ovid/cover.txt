Nicolas Poussin's *Landscape during a Thunderstorm with Pyramus and Thisbe* (1651). Lightning splits a dark sky, a tree bends in the wind, and herdsmen and riders scatter across the fields below a hill town. The story of the title takes up only the bottom corner: Pyramus lies dead on the ground, and Thisbe runs towards him with her arms thrown up. Ovid often sets the same scale, a single person's ruin inside a world that goes on with its weather.
