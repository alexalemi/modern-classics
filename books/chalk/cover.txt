Drawn for this edition, in the manner of the Dover science paperbacks of the 1950s and 60s. It is the shell of Globigerina, a cluster of rounded chambers, each larger than the last and pricked with pores, the creature too small to see without a microscope of which, Huxley tells the working men of Norwich, some chalk consists almost entirely. The line at the top is where he turns his audience's attention to it.
