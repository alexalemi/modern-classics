Canto Two

	You there, in your little boat,
	who have followed, longing to listen,
	behind my ship as it goes singing on its way:

	turn back to see your own shores again.
	Do not put out on the open sea, for perhaps,
	losing me, you would be left astray.

	The water I am taking was never crossed before.
	Minerva fills my sail, Apollo steers,
	and the nine Muses show me the Bear to steer by.

	But you few others, who reached up early
	for the bread of angels, the food of truth,
	which men live on here and are never full of,

	you may well set your vessel out
	on the deep salt water, keeping in my furrow
	ahead of the water that closes level again.

	The Argonauts, those glorious men who crossed to Colchis,
	did not wonder as you will wonder,
	when they saw Jason ploughing with the fire-breathing oxen.

	The thirst we are born with and never lose,
	for the kingdom made in God's image, carried us
	almost as fast as you see the sky turn.

	Beatrice was looking upward, and I at her;
	and perhaps in the time an arrow takes
	to settle and fly and leave the notch,

	I saw myself arrived where a marvellous thing
	twisted my face towards it; and so she,
	from whom what I cared about could not be hidden,

	turned to me, as glad as she was beautiful,
	and said: "Set your mind on God with thanks,
	for he has brought us to the first star, the moon."

	It seemed to me that a cloud covered us,
	shining, dense, solid and polished,
	like a diamond struck by the sun.

	The eternal pearl took us inside itself
	as water takes in a ray of light
	and stays unbroken.

	If I was still a body (and down here we cannot conceive
	how one solid thing could let another through it,
	which must be so if body enters into body),

	that ought to kindle in us the longing all the more
	to see that essence in which one sees
	how our nature and God were joined.

	There we shall see what we hold by faith,
	not proved, but known of itself
	like the first truths a man believes without proof.

	I answered: "My lady, as devoutly
	as I can, I thank him
	who has taken me away from the mortal world.

	But tell me: what are the dark marks
	on this body, which down there on earth
	make people tell stories about Cain?"

	She smiled a little, and then: "If the opinion
	of mortals goes wrong," she said to me,
	"about things the key of the senses cannot unlock,

	surely the arrows of astonishment
	should not sting you now, since you see
	that reason, following the senses, has short wings.

	But tell me what you think about it yourself."
	And I: "The light and dark we see up here
	I believe are made by the moon being thin in some parts and dense in others."

	And she: "You will certainly see your belief
	sunk deep in the false, if you listen well
	to the argument I shall bring against it.

	The eighth sphere, the sphere of the fixed stars, shows you many lights,
	and in their quality and in their size
	you can see that they have different faces.

	If thin and dense were all there was to it,
	one single power would be in every star,
	shared out in larger and smaller amounts and nothing more.

	But different powers must be the fruits
	of different forming principles; and on your reasoning
	all of those but one would be wiped out.

	Again, if thinness were the cause
	of the dark patches you ask about, then either
	this planet would be starved of its matter

	right through in one part, or else, as a body
	has fat parts and lean, this one
	would be layered like the leaves of a book.

	If the first were so, it would show plainly
	in an eclipse of the sun, by the light
	shining through, as it does through anything else rare.

	That does not happen; so we must look
	at the other case, and if I can knock that down too,
	your opinion will be proved false.

	If the thin part does not go right through,
	there must be a boundary beyond which
	the dense part lets it go no further;

	and from there the sun's ray is thrown back,
	as colour comes back from a glass
	that has lead hidden behind it, a mirror.

	Now you will say that the ray shows itself
	darker there than in other parts
	because it is reflected from further back.

	Experiment can free you from this objection,
	if you ever try it;
	experiment is the spring your sciences flow from.

	You will take three mirrors; and set two of them
	the same distance from you, and let the third,
	further off, meet your eyes between the first two.

	Turned towards them, have a light behind your back
	which kindles the three mirrors
	and comes back to you struck off all of them.

	Although the more distant image does not stretch
	so far in size, you will see there
	that it must shine just as brightly.

	Now, as under the blows of the warm rays
	the ground under the snow is left bare
	of the colour and the cold it had before,

	so, now that your understanding is bared like that,
	I want to fill it with a light so living
	that it will tremble before your eyes.

	Inside the heaven of the divine peace
	a body wheels round, the outermost sphere, and in its power
	lies the being of everything it holds inside it.

	The next heaven, the one with so many stars,
	shares that being out among different things,
	distinct from it and held inside it.

	The other spheres, each in its own way,
	direct the differences they hold inside themselves
	towards their own ends, as seed is directed to its fruit.

	These organs of the world go on
	as you see now, from rank to rank,
	taking from above and working below.

	Watch carefully now how I go
	through this place to the truth you long for,
	so that afterwards you can cross this ford on your own.

	The motion and the power of the holy circles
	must be breathed into them by the blessed movers,
	as the hammer's craft comes from the smith;

	and the heaven that so many lights make beautiful
	takes its image from the deep mind that turns it
	and becomes the seal that stamps that image on.

	And as the soul inside your dust
	is worked out through different limbs, shaped
	to different faculties,

	so the angelic intelligence unfolds its own goodness,
	multiplied, through the stars,
	while it turns on its own oneness.

	A different power makes a different alloy
	with each precious star-body it brings to life,
	and is bound into it as life is bound into you.

	Because of the glad nature it comes from,
	the power mixed through the body shines
	as gladness does through a living eye.

	This is what makes one light differ
	from another, not dense and thin:
	this is the forming principle that produces,

	according to its own goodness, the dim and the clear."
