# Passages the Paradiso is not allowed to lose, written FROM the finished
# translation and not from memory (the epictetus rule). Almost every
# Paradiso phrase an English reader knows is somebody's TRANSLATION rather
# than Dante, so a pin taken from memory could only be satisfied by a
# rendering that had drifted to another translator's wording.
#
# Several are pinned UNCHANGED because the line is already plain in the
# Italian and any improvement would be damage (the hume rule).

# I.1-3 -- the opening, and the thesis of the whole cantica: one light,
# received unequally.
The glory of him who moves all things

# I.70 -- "trasumanar". Dante coins a verb and then says the coinage
# cannot be explained; the rendering must keep the refusal, not paraphrase
# it away.
To pass beyond the human cannot be told

# III.85 -- Piccarda, and the most quoted line in the cantica. The comma
# and the colon are part of the decision: the sentence is an answer, and
# the sea follows it as its proof.
And in his will is our peace:

# XV.28-30 -- Cacciaguida greets his descendant in Latin. It stays Latin:
# it is the one place in the poem where an ancestor speaks the language of
# the Rome he served, and Dante prints it untranslated on purpose.
O sanguis meus, o superinfusa

# XVII.58-60 -- the exile prophecy. Pinned unchanged; the plainness is
# the whole force of it.
You will find out how salt another man's bread
going down and up another man's stairs

# XXII.151 -- "l'aiuola", the little threshing floor. Earth seen from the
# fixed stars, and the same phrase returns in XXVII.
The little threshing floor that makes us so fierce

# XXIV.64-65 -- Dante recites Hebrews 11:1 to Saint Peter as his
# definition of faith. It has to read as a formula being recited.
faith is the substance of things hoped for

# XXV.1-3 -- the exile's hope of the laurel, and the most personal thing
# Dante ever wrote about his own book.
If it ever happens that the sacred poem
so that it has made me lean for many years

# XXXI.79-81 -- the farewell to Beatrice, which is the last thing he says
# to her in the whole Comedy.
You have drawn me from slavery into freedom

# XXXIII.1 -- Bernard's prayer. The paradox is the line.
Virgin Mother, daughter of your son

# XXXIII.85-87 -- the vision of the universe bound into one volume.
bound with love into one volume

# XXXIII.145 -- the last line of the Divine Comedy.
by the love that moves the sun and the other stars.
