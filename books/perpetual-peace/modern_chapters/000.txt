Preface

A Dutch innkeeper once hung out a sign that read *To Perpetual Peace*, and painted a graveyard on it. Whether the joke was aimed at *human beings* in general, or at the heads of state in particular, who can never get enough of war, or perhaps only at the philosophers who dream that sweet dream, we can leave undecided. But the author of what follows does ask for one condition. The practical politician likes to look down, with great self-satisfaction, on the political theorist as a schoolroom pedant whose ideas, empty of real content, pose no danger to the state, since the state has to proceed from the lessons of experience; the theorist can be allowed to knock down all eleven pins with a single bowl, in his head where it costs nothing, without the *worldly-wise* statesman needing to pay the slightest attention. Very well: if the two ever quarrel, the statesman must be consistent and must not scent a danger to the state behind opinions that the theorist has ventured at random and spoken aloud in public. With this saving clause (*clausula salvatoria*), the author hereby expressly protects himself, in due and proper form, against every malicious interpretation.
