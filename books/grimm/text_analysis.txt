GRIMM'S HOUSEHOLD TALES — TRANSLATION STRATEGY
Margaret Hunt's 1884 English, retold. All 200 tales + 10 legends.


WHY THIS BOOK, AND WHAT THE WORK ACTUALLY IS
============================================

Measured against every other public-domain English Grimm on one scale:

    Hunt 1884 (complete, 210)   288k words   14.4 archaic/1k   13.0 thou/1k
    Taylor & Edwardes (~60)     102k words    2.6              1.5
    Lucy Crane 1882 (~45)        89k words    2.4              2.2
    Grimm's Fairy Stories (~23)  51k words    2.6              1.8

Hunt is a SIX-FOLD outlier among Grimm translators. That is the whole
situation, and it has two halves:

1. HER ARCHAISM IS ONE DECISION, MADE 3,729 TIMES. 90% of all archaic
   tokens in her text are the thou-family (thou/thee/thy/art/hast/wilt/
   shalt/dost/canst), and 99% of those sit INSIDE QUOTATION MARKS. She
   rendered German *du* — the ordinary intimate second person, what a wife
   calls her husband and a frog calls a child — as English *thou*, which
   was already biblical in 1884. Her narration is plain and often lovely;
   it is the dialogue that wears the costume.

   NONE OF THIS IS IN THE GERMAN. That is the same case as Burton's
   Nights and it is the strongest case this project has: we are not
   removing the centuries between a reader and an author, we are removing
   a translator's costume.

2. THE PLAIN RIVALS ARE ALL SELECTIONS. Hunt's is the ONLY COMPLETE
   English Grimm. At least 120 of her 200 tales exist in no plain
   public-domain English at all. So the deliverable is not "a readable
   Grimm" — that exists three times over — but THE COMPLETE GRIMM IN
   MODERN ENGLISH, WHICH DOES NOT EXIST. Completeness is the point, and
   it is why the famous sixty are in here alongside the unknown hundred
   and forty.


THE CENTRAL RULE
================

TURN EVERY *thou* INTO *you*, AND LET THE SENTENCE RESETTLE.

This is not a find-and-replace. "What ails thee, King's daughter? Thou
weepest so that even a stone would show pity" becomes "What is the matter,
princess? You are crying so hard that even a stone would feel sorry for
you." The verb goes with the pronoun (hast -> have, dost -> do, wilt ->
will, art -> are), and so does the word order and often the vocabulary
around it.

    Hunt:  "Be quiet, and do not weep," answered the frog, "I can help
           thee, but what wilt thou give me if I bring thy plaything up
           again?"
    Here:  "Don't cry," said the frog. "I can help you — but what will you
           give me if I bring your plaything up again?"

    Hunt:  "O, thou fool!" said she, "Then we must all four die of hunger,
           thou mayest as well plane the planks for our coffins."
    Here:  "You fool," she said. "Then all four of us will starve, and you
           may as well start planing the boards for our coffins."

The second is the test case for the whole book. In German a wife is
scolding her husband. In Hunt she sounds like a prophet.


WHAT ELSE COMES OFF
===================

- GERMAN CALQUES that are not English. "Hard by a great forest dwelt a
  poor wood-cutter" -> "A poor woodcutter lived at the edge of a great
  forest." "He had little to bite and to break" (wenig zu beißen und zu
  brechen) -> "he had little enough to eat."
- VICTORIAN VOCABULARY: "when she was dull" (= bored), "would not suffer
  any one to do her injury" (= allow), "it might discover us" (= give us
  away), "wench", "bedgown", "presently" where it means "soon".
- INVERSION AND "SAID SHE". "said she" -> "she said"; "Then said the
  King" -> "Then the King said".
- LONG COMMA-CHAINS. Hunt reproduces the German's habit of stringing
  clauses with "and ... and ... and". Some of that is the oral voice and
  STAYS (see below); some is just under-punctuated and becomes sentences.

WHAT STAYS
==========

- THE ORAL VOICE. These were told aloud. Short sentences, plain words,
  repetition, and the storyteller's rhythm are the artifact, not a defect.
  Do NOT smooth "Once upon a time" openings into literary prose, and do
  NOT vary the wording of a repeated formula — repetition is how the tales
  work. The fisherman's wife's demands escalate in the SAME words each
  time; keep them the same.
- THE RHYMES. Every tale that has one keeps it, AS VERSE, tab-indented,
  and rhymed in English. They are the memorable heart of the tales:
      "Rumpelstiltskin is my name"
      "Mirror, mirror on the wall"
      "Flounder, flounder in the sea"
      "Blow, blow, little breeze, / let Kürdchen's hat go flying"
  A rhyme that does not rhyme has been mistranslated. Rework the English
  until it scans and chimes; a slightly freer sense is the right trade.
- THE CRUELTY. Grimm is violent and the violence is not decorative: the
  stepmother dances in red-hot iron shoes, the pigeons peck out the
  stepsisters' eyes, the juniper tree's boy is beheaded by the lid of a
  chest and cooked into a stew his father eats. RENDER IT PLAINLY AND IN
  FULL. No softening, no heightening, no editorial flinch. The tales are
  frightening on purpose and they were told to children on purpose.
- NAMES. Hansel, Gretel, Rapunzel, Rumpelstiltskin, Briar-Rose,
  Little Red-Cap, Allerleirauh, Fundevogel, Frau Trude, Herr Korbes,
  Mother Holle. Hunt's German-derived names stay; they are the tales'
  own. "Grethel" -> "Gretel" throughout (spelling, not name).
- THE PIETY. God, the Devil, saints, Our Lady and heaven are everywhere,
  and the ten Children's Legends are frankly devotional. Rendered
  straight, as the tellers meant them.


THE HARD CASES
==============

- ANTISEMITIC TALES. Three tales turn on a Jew as victim of a cruel
  trick played for laughs: "The Jew Among Thorns" (110), "The Good
  Bargain" (7), and "The Bright Sun Brings It to Light" (115). These are
  not incidental slurs but whole plots, and 110 ends with the Jew hanged.
  THIS IS A DECISION FOR ALEX, NOT FOR THE TRANSLATOR, and it is asked
  BEFORE those files are written. The nights/ precedent gives the test —
  does the reader lose information or gain a false one? — and gives both
  answers: file 026 kept entire for its symmetry, file 058's libel cut
  because there was no symmetry to protect. Here there is none either.
  Options are: translate in full as the Verne rule (the tales are
  historical evidence of what was told to German children); translate
  with a short editor's note; or omit, which breaks completeness — the
  one thing this edition exists to provide.
- "THE GIRL WITHOUT HANDS" (31) and other tales of incest, mutilation and
  child-murder. These stay in full. They are why the collection matters.

CONVENTIONS
===========

- Markup-free, as always. Tab indent = verse. ALL-CAPS = heading, so
  never write an all-caps line. No asterisks, no underscores.
- EVERY FILE BEGINS WITH ITS FIRST TALE'S TITLE, exactly as the manifest
  has it, on its own line. assemble.py splits the file on those title
  lines and DISCARDS anything before the first one.
- Each subsequent tale in the file starts with its exact title on its own
  line. A title that drifts by one character loses its own section and
  welds its tale onto the tale before it.
- Ratio target 0.90-1.15 per file. Hunt's English is already about the
  right length; the thou-work is a substitution, not an expansion.
