Book Thirteen: The Days of Creation

(Part 1 of 4)

I call on You, my God, my mercy, who created me and did not forget me when I forgot You. I call You into my soul, which You are preparing for Yourself by the longing You breathe into her. Do not abandon me now that I am calling on You, when You went ahead of me before I called, and pressed me with all sorts of repeated calls so that I might hear You from far off, and turn round, and call on You who were calling after me. For You, Lord, wiped out all my bad deserts, so as not to pay back into my hands what I fell away from You with; and You went ahead of all my good deserts, so as to pay back the work of Your own hands, with which You made me. Because before I existed, You existed; and I was nothing that You could grant existence to. And yet here I am, out of Your goodness, which came before everything You have made me of and made me into. For You had no need of me, and I am no such good thing as to be of help to You, my Lord and God: not by serving You as though You would grow tired of working, or as though Your power would be less if my service were missing; not by cultivating Your service as if it were a piece of land that must lie uncultivated unless I cultivated You. I serve and worship You so that I may receive well-being from You, from whom it comes that I have a being capable of well-being at all.

For it is out of the fullness of Your goodness that Your creation exists: so that a good which could bring You no profit at all, and was not out of You, in case it should be equal to You, might still exist, since it could be made by You. What claim on You did heaven and earth have, which You made in the beginning? Let those spiritual and bodily natures which You made in Your wisdom say what claim they had on You to depend on You — even in that state of theirs, just begun and formless, whether spiritual or bodily, ready to fall away into an unrestrained liberty and a far-off unlikeness to You. The spiritual, though without form, is superior to the bodily even when the bodily is formed; and the bodily, though without form, is better than it would be if it were altogether nothing. And they depend on Your Word as formless things, unless by that same Word they are brought back to Your unity, given form, and made all very good by You, the one sovereign good. What claim did they have on You even to be formless, when they would not have been even that except from You?

What claim did bodily matter have on You even to be invisible and without form? It would not have been even that if You had not made it; and therefore, because it did not exist, it could have no claim on You to be made. Or how could the just-begun spiritual creature have had a claim on You even to ebb and flow darkly like the deep, unlike You, unless it had been turned by the same Word to the one by whom it was created, and been so lit by him as to become light — not equally with him, but conformed to the form which is equal to You? For as in a body, existing is not the same thing as being beautiful, since otherwise it could not be ugly, so likewise for a created spirit, living is not the same thing as living wisely, since otherwise it would be wise unchangeably. But it is good for it always to hold fast to You, in case the light it has gained by turning to You is lost by turning away from You, and it slips back into a life like the dark deep. For we ourselves also, who as to the soul are a spiritual creature, once turned away from You our light, were in that life darkness; and we still labour among the remains of our darkness, until in Your only Son we become Your righteousness, like the mountains of God. For we have been Your judgements, which are like the great deep.

What You said at the beginning of creation, Let there be light, and there was light, I take, not unsuitably, to be about the spiritual creature: because there was already a kind of life for You to illuminate. But just as it had no claim on You for a life that could be enlightened, so neither, now that it existed, had it any claim to be enlightened. For its formless state could not be pleasing to You unless it became light — and became light not by simply existing but by looking at the illuminating light and holding fast to it. So that its living, and living happily, it owes to nothing but Your grace, having been turned by a change for the better towards the one who cannot be changed for worse or better, which is what You alone are, because You alone simply are. For You it is not one thing to live and another to live blessedly, since You are Your own blessedness.

What then could be lacking to Your good, which You Yourself are, even if these things had either never existed or remained without form — these things which You made not out of any lack but out of the fullness of Your goodness, holding them in and turning them into form, not as though Your joy were completed by them? For to You, being perfect, their imperfection is displeasing; and so they were perfected by You and please You — not as though You were imperfect and were to be perfected by their perfecting. For Your good Spirit was borne over the waters and not borne up by them, as if he rested on them. Those on whom Your good Spirit is said to rest, he causes to rest in himself. But Your incorruptible and unchangeable will, in itself all-sufficient for itself, was borne over that life You had created, for which living is not the same as living happily, since it lives even while ebbing and flowing in its own darkness. And it remains for it to be turned to him by whom it was made, and to live more and more by the fountain of life, and in his light to see light, and be perfected and enlightened and made beautiful.

Now the Trinity appears to me in a glass darkly, which is You, my God — because You, Father, in him who is the beginning of our wisdom, which is Your wisdom, born of Yourself, equal to You and co-eternal, that is, in Your Son, created heaven and earth. We have now said much about the heaven of heavens, and about the earth invisible and without form, and about the dark deep, with reference to the wandering instability of its spiritual deformity, unless it had been turned towards him from whom it had the degree of life it then had, and by his enlightening became a beautiful life, and the heaven of that heaven which was afterwards set between water and water. And under the name of God I now took the Father, who made these things, and under the name of Beginning the Son, in whom he made these things; and believing, as I did, in my God as Trinity, I searched further in his holy words — and there was Your Spirit moving over the waters. There is the Trinity, my God: Father and Son and Holy Spirit, creator of all creation.

But what was the reason, truth-speaking light? To You I lift up my heart; let it not teach me empty things; scatter its darkness. Tell me, I beg You, by our mother, love — tell me the reason, I beg You, why after the mention of heaven, and of the earth invisible and without form, and darkness upon the deep, Your Scripture should only then mention Your Spirit. Was it because it was fitting that knowledge of him should be conveyed as being "borne above", and this could not be said unless the thing over which Your Spirit may be understood to have been borne were mentioned first? For he was not borne above the Father or the Son, and could not rightly be said to be borne above if he were borne over nothing. So first the thing had to be mentioned that he might be borne over, and then he, whom it was fitting to speak of only as being borne. But why was it not fitting for knowledge of him to be conveyed otherwise than as being borne above?

Let anyone who can, follow Your apostle here with his understanding, where he says: Because Your love is poured into our hearts by the Holy Spirit who is given to us; and where, on the subject of spiritual gifts, he teaches and shows us a more excellent way of love; and where he bends his knee to You for us, so that we may know the surpassing knowledge of the love of Christ. And that is why from the beginning he was borne surpassingly above the waters. Whom shall I say this to? And how am I to speak of the weight of evil desires, pulling down to the steep abyss, and of how love raises us up again by Your Spirit, who was borne above the waters? Whom shall I say it to, and how? For it is not in space that we sink and rise. What could be more like, and yet what less? They are affections, they are loves: the uncleanness of our spirit flowing away downwards under the load of cares, and the holiness of Yours raising us up by love of an untroubled rest, so that we may lift our hearts to You, where Your Spirit is borne above the waters, and come to that surpassing rest when our soul has passed through the waters that give no footing.

Angels fell away, and the soul of man fell away, and so marked out the abyss in that dark depth, ready for the whole spiritual creation, if You had not said from the beginning, Let there be light — and there was light, and every obedient intelligence of Your heavenly city held fast to You and rested in Your Spirit, who is borne unchangeably over everything changeable. Otherwise even the heaven of heavens would have been in itself a dark deep; but now it is light in the Lord. For even in that miserable restlessness of the spirits who fell away and discovered their own darkness when stripped of the clothing of Your light, You reveal well enough how noble a thing You made the reasoning creature: nothing less than You will be enough to give it a happy rest, and so not even itself. For You, our God, will lighten our darkness; from You our garment of light rises; and then our darkness shall be as noonday. Give Yourself to me, my God, restore Yourself to me. I love, and if that is too little, let me love more strongly. I cannot measure so as to know how much love is still lacking in me, before my life may run into Your embrace and never turn away until it is hidden in the hidden place of Your presence. This only I know: that it goes badly with me except in You — not only outside myself but inside myself too; and all abundance that is not my God is emptiness to me.

But was neither the Father nor the Son borne above the waters? If this means in space, like a body, then neither was the Holy Spirit. But if it means the unchangeable surpassingness of divinity above everything changeable, then Father and Son and Holy Spirit were all borne above the waters. Why then is it said of Your Spirit only? Why is it said of him alone, as though he had been in a place, who is not in a place, and of whom alone it is written that he is Your gift? In Your gift we rest; there we enjoy You. Our rest is our place. Love lifts us there, and Your good Spirit lifts our lowliness up from the gates of death. In Your good pleasure is our peace. A body strives by its own weight towards its own place. Weight does not pull only downwards, but towards a thing's own place. Fire tends upwards, a stone downwards. They are driven by their own weight; they look for their own places. Oil poured under water is lifted above the water; water poured on oil sinks below the oil. They are driven by their own weights to look for their own places. When they are out of their order they are restless; put back in order, they are at rest. My weight is my love; by it I am carried wherever I am carried. By Your gift we are set alight and carried upwards; we glow inwardly and go forward. We climb Your paths that are in our heart, and sing a song of ascents; we glow inwardly with Your fire, with Your good fire, and we go, because we are going upwards to the peace of Jerusalem — for I was glad when they said to me, We will go up to the house of the Lord. There Your good pleasure has placed us, so that we may want nothing else but to stay there for ever.

Blessed creature, which, being itself other than You, has known no other condition than this: that as soon as it was made it was, without any interval, carried aloft by Your gift, which is borne above everything changeable — carried aloft by that call in which You said, Let there be light, and there was light. Whereas in us this happened at different times, in that we were darkness and are made light; but of that creature it is only said what it would have been if it had not been enlightened. And this is said as though it had been unsettled and dark before, so that the cause by which it was made otherwise might appear: namely that being turned to the unfailing light it became light. Let anyone who can, understand this; let him ask You for it. Why should he trouble me, as though I could enlighten any man who comes into this world?

Which of us takes in the almighty Trinity? And yet which of us does not speak of it, if it is indeed that we are speaking of? Rare is the soul that, while speaking of it, knows what it is speaking of. And people argue and struggle, and without peace nobody sees that vision. I wish people would consider these three things that are in themselves. These three are indeed something quite other than the Trinity; I only say where they may practise on themselves, and there test and feel how far off they are. The three I mean are: to be, to know, and to will. For I am, and I know, and I will. I am knowing and willing; and I know myself to be and to will; and I will to be and to know. In these three, then, let anyone who can make out how inseparable a life there is — one life, one mind, one essence; and how inseparable a distinction there is, and yet a distinction. Surely a man has it in front of him; let him look into himself and see, and tell me. But when he discovers something and can say something about these three, let him not therefore think he has found the unchangeable one who is above them, who is unchangeably, and knows unchangeably, and wills unchangeably. And whether because of these three there is also a Trinity in God, or whether all three are in each, so that the three belong to each, or whether both at once, wonderfully, simply and yet manifoldly, itself a boundary to itself within itself, yet unbounded, by which it is and is known to itself and is enough for itself, unchangeably the selfsame by the abundant greatness of its unity — who can readily conceive this? Who could express it in any way at all? Who would pronounce on it rashly?

Go on with your confession, my faith; say to the Lord your God: Holy, holy, holy, Lord my God, in Your name we have been baptised — Father, Son and Holy Spirit; in Your name we baptise — Father, Son and Holy Spirit. Because among us too, in his Christ, God made heaven and earth, namely the spiritual and the unspiritual people of his church. And our earth, before it received the form of doctrine, was invisible and without form, and we were covered with the darkness of ignorance. For You corrected man for his wrongdoing, and Your judgements were like the great deep to him. But because Your Spirit was borne above the waters, Your mercy did not abandon our misery, and You said, Let there be light — Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand. Repent: let there be light. And because our soul was troubled within us, we remembered You, Lord, from the land of Jordan, and that mountain equal to Yourself but made small for our sakes; and our darkness displeased us, and we turned to You, and there was light. And we were once darkness, and are now light in the Lord.

But so far by faith and not by sight; for we are saved by hope, and hope that is seen is not hope. So far deep still calls to deep, but now in the voice of Your waterfalls. So far the man who says, I could not speak to you as spiritual but as unspiritual — even he does not yet think he has taken hold of it, and forgets the things that are behind and reaches forward to the things ahead, and groans under his burden, and his soul thirsts for the living God as the deer for the water-brooks, and says, When shall I come? — wanting to be clothed with his house from heaven; and calls to this lower deep, saying, Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind; and, Do not be children in understanding, but in malice be children, so that in understanding you may be grown up; and, Foolish Galatians, who has bewitched you? But now no longer in his own voice, but in Yours, who sent Your Spirit from above through him who went up on high and opened the floodgates of his gifts, so that the force of his streams might gladden the city of God. It is for him that this friend of the bridegroom sighs, having now the first fruits of the Spirit laid up with him, and yet still groaning inwardly, waiting for the adoption, that is, the redemption of his body. For him he sighs, being a member of the bride; for him he is jealous, being a friend of the bridegroom; for him and not for himself he is jealous, because it is in the voice of Your waterfalls and not in his own voice that he calls to that other depth, over which, being jealous, he is afraid — in case, as the serpent tricked Eve by his cunning, so their minds should be corrupted from the purity that is in our bridegroom, Your only Son. What a light of beauty that will be, when we shall see him as he is, and those tears have passed away which have been my food day and night, while they say to me every day, Where is Your God now?
