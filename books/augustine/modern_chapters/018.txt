Book Nine: Baptism, Ostia, and the Death of Monica

(Part 1 of 3)

Lord, I am Your servant; I am Your servant and the son of Your handmaid. You have broken my chains apart. I will offer You the sacrifice of praise. Let my heart and my tongue praise You, and let all my bones say, Lord, who is like You? Let them say it, and answer me, and say to my soul, I am your salvation. Who am I, and what am I? What evil has there not been, either in my actions, or, if not in my actions, in my words, or, if not in my words, in my will? But You, Lord, are good and merciful, and Your right hand looked into the depth of my death and emptied the pit of corruption out of the bottom of my heart. And this whole gift of Yours was to make me refuse what I willed and will what You willed. But through all those years, out of what low and deep recess was my free will called out in a moment, so that I could put my neck under Your easy yoke and my shoulders under Your light burden, Christ Jesus, my helper and my redeemer? How sweet it suddenly became to go without the sweetness of those toys! What I had dreaded losing was now a joy to let go. For You threw them out of me, true and highest sweetness. You threw them out, and came in Yourself in their place: sweeter than any pleasure, though not to flesh and blood; brighter than any light, and more hidden than any depth; higher than any honour, but not to those who are high in their own opinion. Now my soul was free of the biting cares of canvassing and acquiring, and of wallowing in filth, and of scratching the itch of lust. And I talked to You like an infant learning to speak — to my brightness, my riches, my health, the Lord my God.

And I decided, in Your sight, not to tear my tongue's service noisily away from the market in words, but to withdraw it quietly: so that young men who were not students of Your law or of Your peace, but of lying nonsense and legal skirmishing, should no longer buy weapons for their madness at my mouth. And very conveniently, there were only a few days left before the vintage holidays, and I decided to see them out and then resign in the ordinary way — and having been bought by You, never to go back on sale again. So our plan was known to You, and to men, apart from our own friends, it was not. For we had agreed among ourselves not to let it out to anybody; although, as we climbed up out of the valley of tears singing that song of ascents, You had given us sharp arrows and burning coals against the subtle tongue that appears to be advising us and would thwart us instead, and would eat us up out of love, as it eats its dinner.

You had pierced our hearts with Your love, and we carried Your words fixed in our insides, so to speak; and the examples of Your servants, whom You had turned from black to bright and from dead to living, all piled up together in the storehouse of our thoughts, kindled and burned up our heavy sluggishness so that we should not sink into the pit. They set us on fire so fiercely that all the blasts of subtle tongues from people opposing us could only inflame us further, not put us out. Still, since for the sake of Your name, which You have made holy across the earth, this vow and purpose of ours might well find people to praise it, it seemed like showing off not to wait for the holidays that were now so close, but to quit early from a public profession that everybody could see. Then everyone looking at what I had done, and noticing how near the vintage was that I had wanted to anticipate, would talk about me a great deal, as if I had wanted to look like somebody important. And what good would it have done me for people to speculate and argue about my intentions, and for what was good in us to be spoken of as evil?

There was another thing that had worried me at first. That very summer my lungs had begun to give way under too much literary work, so that I breathed deeply with difficulty and had a pain in my chest showing they were damaged, and I could not manage any full or extended speaking. This had worried me, because it nearly forced me to lay down the burden of teaching out of necessity, or at least to interrupt it if I could be cured and recover. But once the full desire for leisure — so that I might see that You are the Lord — had risen in me and settled there, then, my God, You know, I actually began to be glad that I had this secondary excuse, and a genuine one, that might soften the offence taken by people who, for their sons' sake, wished me never to have the freedom of Your sons. So, full of that gladness, I saw the interval out; it may have been some twenty days, and they were endured like a man. Endured, because the greed that used to carry part of this heavy business had left me, and I was on my own, and would have been overwhelmed if patience had not taken its place. Perhaps some of Your servants, my brothers, will say I sinned in this, in that with my heart wholly set on Your service I allowed myself to sit even one hour in the chair of lies. I shall not argue about it. But have You not, most merciful Lord, pardoned and remitted this sin too, along with my other most horrible and deadly sins, in the holy water?

Verecundus was worn down with worry about this blessedness of ours, because he saw that being held back by ties that bound him tightly, he would be separated from us. He was not yet a Christian himself, though his wife was one of the faithful; and this, more rigidly than any other chain, held him back from the journey we had now begun. He would not, he said, be a Christian on any other terms than the ones he could not manage. Even so, he courteously offered to let us stay at his country house as long as we were there. You will reward him, Lord, in the resurrection of the just, seeing that You have already given him the portion of the righteous. For although, while we were away and he was in Rome, he was seized by bodily illness, and being made a Christian and one of the faithful in the course of it, left this life — even so, You had mercy not only on him but on us, so that we should not be tortured by unbearable grief in remembering our friend's exceptional kindness to us and being unable to count him among Your flock. Thanks be to You, our God: we are Yours. Your promptings and consolations tell us so. Faithful to Your promises, You are now repaying Verecundus for his country house at Cassiciacum, where we rested in You from the fever of the world, with the eternal freshness of Your paradise; because You have forgiven him his sins upon earth, in that rich mountain, the mountain that yields milk, Your own mountain.

He had sorrow at that time; Nebridius had joy. For although he too, not being a Christian yet, had fallen into the pit of that most destructive error and believed the flesh of Your Son to be a phantom, he came out of it and believed as we did — not yet furnished with any of Your church's sacraments, but a most passionate searcher after truth. Not long after our conversion and rebirth by Your baptism, when he too was a faithful member of the Catholic Church and was serving You in perfect chastity and self-control among his own people in Africa, his whole household having been made Christian through him first, You released him from the flesh; and now he lives in Abraham's bosom. Whatever it is that this bosom means, there lives my Nebridius, my sweet friend, and Your son, Lord, adopted from being a freedman: there he lives. What other place could there be for such a soul? There he lives, in the place he used to ask me so many questions about, poor inexperienced man that I was. Now he does not put his ear to my mouth, but his spiritual mouth to Your fountain, and drinks as much wisdom as he can take in, in proportion to his thirst, endlessly happy. And I do not think he is so drunk on it that he forgets me, seeing that You, Lord, whom he drinks, are mindful of us. So that was our state then, comforting Verecundus, who was grieving, as far as friendship allowed, that our conversion had gone the way it did, and urging him to become faithful according to his own condition, that is, of a married man; and waiting for Nebridius to follow us, which he was on the very point of doing, so close was he. And so those days rolled by at last — they seemed long and many, because of the love I had for the untroubled freedom in which I would be able to sing to You from the marrow of my bones: My heart has said to You, I have sought Your face; Your face, Lord, will I seek.

Now the day came when I was actually to be free of my professorship of rhetoric, as in my mind I already was. And it was done. You rescued my tongue, as You had already rescued my heart. And I blessed You, rejoicing, and went off with all my household to the villa. What I did there in writing, which was by now enlisted in Your service — though still panting from the school of pride, as though in a breathing space — my books can testify: both what I debated with others and what I debated with myself alone in Your presence. And what I debated with Nebridius, who was away, my letters testify. And when shall I have time to recount all Your great benefits to us at that time, especially since I am hurrying on to still greater mercies? For my memory calls me back, and it is a pleasure to me, Lord, to confess to You by what inward goads You tamed me, and how You levelled me, lowering the mountains and hills of my high imaginings, straightening my crookedness and smoothing my rough roads. And how You also brought the brother of my heart, Alypius, under the name of Your only-begotten, our Lord and saviour Jesus Christ — a name he would not at first agree to have put in our writings, preferring that they should smell of the lofty cedars of the schools, which the Lord has now brought down, rather than of the wholesome herbs of the church, the antidote against serpents.

In what tones I spoke to You, my God, when I read the Psalms of David, those faithful songs and sounds of devotion which leave no room for a swollen spirit — still a catechumen, and a beginner in Your real love, resting at that villa with Alypius, another catechumen, and my mother staying close by us, in a woman's clothes with a man's faith, with the calm of age, a mother's love, a Christian's devotion. What tones I used to You in those Psalms, and how they set me alight towards You, and made me burn to recite them, if I could, through the whole world, against the pride of the human race! And yet they are sung through the whole world, and nobody can hide from Your heat.

With what violent and bitter sorrow I was angry at the Manichees — and then again pitied them, because they did not know those sacraments, those medicines, and were raging against the antidote that could have cured their raving. How I wished they had been somewhere near me then, without my knowing they were there, and could have watched my face and heard my words when I read the fourth Psalm in that time of rest, and could have seen how that Psalm worked on me: When I called, the God of my righteousness heard me; in trouble You gave me room. Have mercy on me, Lord, and hear my prayer. If only they could hear what I said over those words, without my knowing they heard, in case they thought I was saying it for their benefit! Because in truth I would not have said the same things, or said them the same way, if I had known they could see and hear me; and if I had said them, they would not have taken them the way they came out when I spoke by myself and for myself in front of You, out of the natural feelings of my soul.

I trembled with fear and then caught fire with hope and with delight in Your mercy, Father; and all of it came out through my eyes and my voice, when Your good Spirit turned to us and said, Sons of men, how long will you be slow of heart? Why do you love emptiness and go looking for lies? For I had loved emptiness and gone looking for lies. And You, Lord, had already magnified Your holy one, raising him from the dead and setting him at Your right hand, from where he was to send his promise from on high, the comforter, the Spirit of truth. And he had already sent him, and I did not know it. He had sent him, because he was now magnified, rising again from the dead and going up into heaven. Until then, the Spirit was not yet given, because Jesus was not yet glorified. And the prophet cries out, How long will you be slow of heart? Why do you love emptiness and go looking for lies? Know this: that the Lord has magnified his holy one. He cries out, How long? He cries out, Know this. And I, for all that time, not knowing, loved emptiness and looked for lies. So I heard and trembled, because it was spoken to people like the one I remembered being. In the phantoms I had taken for truths there was emptiness and lying; and I said a great deal aloud, earnestly and forcefully, in the bitterness of my memory. If only the people who still love emptiness and go looking for lies had heard it! Perhaps they would have been troubled and vomited it up, and You would have heard them when they cried out to You; for he died a real death in the flesh for us, who now pleads with You on our behalf.

I read on: Be angry, and do not sin. And how I was moved, my God, having now learned to be angry with myself over the past, so as not to sin in the future! And justly angry, since it was not some other nature belonging to a people of darkness that had sinned for me, as the people say who are not angry with themselves, and store up wrath against the day of wrath and of the revelation of Your just judgement. And my good things were now not outside me, and not looked for with the eyes of the flesh in the sun of this world; for those who want joy from outside soon grow empty and spend themselves on things seen and temporary, and in their starving thoughts they lick at the very shadows. If only they were worn out with their hunger and said, Who will show us good things? And we would say, and they would hear: The light of Your face is stamped on us. For we are not the light that lights every man; we are lit by You, so that having once been darkness we may be light in You. If only they could see the eternal light within, which, having tasted it, I was sorry I could not show them, so long as they kept bringing me their heart in their eyes, roving abroad away from You while they said, Who will show us good things? For there, where I was angry with myself in my own room, where I was inwardly stung, where I had made my sacrifice, killing my old self and beginning the plan of a new life, putting my trust in You — there You had begun to grow sweet to me, and had put gladness in my heart. And I cried out as I read it outwardly, finding it inwardly. And I did not want to be multiplied with worldly goods, wasting time and being wasted by time, when in Your eternal simple being I had other corn and wine and oil.

And with a loud cry of my heart I cried out at the next verse: peace! and the Selfsame! What was he saying — I will lie down and sleep? For who will stop us, when what is written comes to pass, Death is swallowed up in victory? And You are supremely the Selfsame, who do not change; and in You is a rest that forgets all labour, since there is nobody else with You, and we are not to go looking for all those other things that are not what You are. But You, Lord, alone have made me live in hope. I read, and caught fire; and I could not find what to do about those deaf and dead people, of whom I had been one myself — a pestilent creature, a bitter and blind bawler against those writings that are sweetened with the honey of heaven and lit with Your own light. And I was eaten up with zeal against the enemies of this Scripture.

When shall I recall everything that happened in those holidays? And yet I have not forgotten, and I will not pass over, the severity of Your whip and the wonderful speed of Your mercy. You tormented me then with toothache; and when it had reached the point where I could not speak, it came into my heart to ask all the friends who were there to pray to You for me, the God of every kind of health. And I wrote this on wax and gave it to them to read. And the moment we had knelt down in humble devotion, the pain went away. But what pain it had been, and how did it go? I was frightened, my Lord and my God, for I had never experienced anything like it since infancy. And the power of Your nod sank deep into me; and rejoicing in faith I praised Your name. And that faith would not let me be easy about my past sins, which were not yet forgiven me by Your baptism.
