Drawn for this edition, in the manner of the Dover science paperbacks of the 1950s and 60s. A small cube stands beside one ten times as tall, its face ruled into a hundred squares. It is the arithmetic of Haldane's giant sixty feet high: ten times a man's height, a hundred times the cross-section of his bones, a thousand times his weight, so that every square inch of giant bone must carry ten times the load, and he breaks his thigh at his first step.
