STANDING INSTRUCTIONS: PAGE-READING RACHEL CARSON'S "CONSERVATION IN ACTION" BOOKLETS

You are proofreading a restored edition: the exact text of five illustrated
US Fish and Wildlife Service booklets (1947-1950) by Rachel Carson, a US
government publication in the public domain. You read each assigned page
from its IMAGE and write what is printed. Nothing is modernized, nothing
corrected, nothing summarized.

FILES (repo root /home/alemi/projects/modern-classics/carson/):
  _src/pages/{book}-{NN}.png     the page image (150 dpi). READ THIS. It is the authority.
  _src/draft/{book}-{NN}.txt     an OCR draft of the same page: a crib only. It has
                                 misreadings, joins the two columns wrongly, and
                                 mixes captions into the text. Never trust it over the image.
  proof/{book}-{NN}.txt          WHAT YOU WRITE, one file per page.

WHAT TO WRITE in proof/{book}-{NN}.txt:

Line 1: "# {book} {NN}: KIND" where KIND is one of
  cover, inside-cover, title, text, map, plate-only, blank, back-cover.

Then, in READING ORDER (left column top to bottom, then right column top to
bottom; a full-width block where it falls), each paragraph as one line,
paragraphs separated by one blank line. Exact words, spelling, punctuation and
capitals as printed, with these conventions:
  - Italics as *word*. Book titles, Latin names, and the like.
  - The opening words of a section are often set in SMALL CAPITALS ("THE
    AMERICAN BRANT, a small goose"). Write them in normal sentence case
    ("The American brant, a small goose"). Do NOT write ALL CAPS for small caps.
  - Join words hyphenated at line ends (an end-of-line break of "migra-/tion"
    is "migration"), but keep a hyphen that belongs to the word ("well-known").
    If unsure, keep the hyphen and add the note "?hyphen" in the REPORT.
  - Use curly quotes “ ” ‘ ’ and real dashes — as printed.
  - If the page begins in the middle of a paragraph from the previous page,
    start that first paragraph with the marker "⟨cont⟩ ". If the page ends in
    the middle of a paragraph, end its last paragraph with " ⟨cont⟩".
  - A section heading printed on its own line (rare): write it on its own line
    preceded by "## ".
  - A SECTION BREAK -- extra white space followed by a paragraph opening in
    small capitals -- gets a line "* * *" before that paragraph (and only
    then; an ordinary paragraph indent is not a break). A small-caps opening
    at the very top of the text is not a break.
  - Omit: page numbers, running heads, library stamps and call numbers
    ("GOVT. I 49.36", "LIBRARY", shelf marks), and printers' furniture.

ILLUSTRATIONS. Every drawing, photograph, map or decoration gets its own line,
placed in the reading order where it sits on the page (between the paragraphs
nearest it):
  [PLATE x0 y0 x1 y1 | kind | caption]
  - x0 y0 x1 y1 are the illustration's bounding box as FRACTIONS of the page
    image's width and height (0.00 = left/top, 1.00 = right/bottom), two
    decimals, generous rather than tight (include the whole drawing, not the
    surrounding text).
  - kind: drawing, photo, map, or ornament.
  - caption: the PRINTED caption or legend, exactly, if there is one (map
    legends included); otherwise leave it empty after the bar. Do not invent one.
  - Text printed INSIDE a map or drawing (place names, arrows) is not body text:
    leave it out of the paragraphs.

COVER, TITLE, INSIDE COVER, BACK COVER pages: transcribe their printed words
as plain lines (title, series number, author, illustrators, imprint, price),
and mark their illustrations with PLATE lines like any other.

DO NOT:
  - delete a printed word because it looks wrong, repeated or ungrammatical. If
    you think the print has an error, write what is printed and note it in the
    REPORT as "?print: ...".
  - "fix" spelling, modernize, or complete a broken sentence across a page
    except with the ⟨cont⟩ markers.
  - read heavy ink as bold. These booklets use roman, italic and small caps only;
    captions may be in a heavier or italic face: write them as plain text.
  - invent anything you cannot see. If a word is illegible write [?] and report it.

CHECK YOUR WORK, for each page, before moving on:
  python3 /home/alemi/projects/modern-classics/carson/pagecheck.py {book} {NN}
It lists draft words missing from your proof (often a dropped line or a
caption you left out) and proof words not in the draft (often your typo, or a
draft misreading you rightly fixed). Look at the image for every item and fix
your proof where the image says so.

REPORT (your final reply): for each page, one line: "{book} {NN}: KIND, n
paragraphs, m plates" plus any "?print", "?hyphen", "[?]" notes, and anything
you were unsure of. Keep it short.
