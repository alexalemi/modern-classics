Chapter 6: The Three Things He Has Forgotten

"First, then, will you let me test the state of your mind with a few questions, so that I can work out how to begin the cure?"

"Ask whatever you like," I said, "and I will answer."

Then she said: "This world of ours — do you think it is run at random and by accident, or do you believe there is some rational guidance in it?"

"No," I said, "there is no way I could think that motions as fixed as these are settled by random chance. I know that God the creator presides over his own work, and the day will never come that drives me off holding on to the truth of that."

"Yes," she said, "you said so just now in your song, when you complained that human beings alone had no share in the divine care. About everything else you were unshaken in believing that reason governs it. And yet I am astonished that you have fallen ill while holding that opinion so firmly. But let us probe deeper: something or other is missing, I think. Tell me — since you have no doubt that God governs the world, do you see by what means he rules it?"

"I hardly understand what you mean," I said, "let alone answer the question."

"Was I not right that something is missing, through which, as through a gap in the ramparts, disease has got in and unsettled your mind? But tell me: do you remember what universal end everything in nature is aimed at?"

"I heard it once," I said, "but grief has blunted my memory."

"And yet you know where everything came from."

"Yes, I know that," I said, "and I have already answered: from God."

"Then how is it possible that you do not know what the end of existence is, when you understand its source and origin? Still, these disturbances of mind have the power to shake a man's footing; they cannot pull him up and root him out of himself altogether. But answer this too, if you would: do you remember that you are a human being?"

"How could I not?" I said.

"Then can you say what a human being is?"

"Is your question whether I know myself to be a creature that has reason and dies? I do acknowledge myself to be that."

Then she said: "Do you know yourself to be anything else?"

"No."

"Now," she said, "I know another cause of your illness, and a serious one. You have stopped knowing what you yourself are. So now I have found out both the causes of your sickness and the means of restoring your health. It is because forgetting yourself has bewildered your mind that you have wept over yourself as an exile stripped of what was his; it is because you do not know the end of existence that you think loathsome and wicked men are happy and powerful; and it is because you have forgotten by what means the earth is governed that you think Fortune's changes ebb and flow with no guiding hand to hold them in.

"These are serious enough to cause not just illness but death. But thanks to the author of our health, the light of nature has not left you completely. In your sound judgement about the world's government — in your believing it subject not to the random drift of chance but to divine reason — we have the spark from which your recovery can be hoped for. So do not be afraid: from these weak embers the vital heat will be kindled in you again. But since it is not yet time for strong remedies, and since the mind is plainly so made that when it throws off true opinions it immediately puts on false ones, from which rises a cloud of confusion that spoils its proper sight, I shall now try to break up these mists with a mild and soothing application, so that the darkness of misleading passion may be scattered and you may come to make out the brightness of the true light."

Song VII: The Perturbations of Passion

	Stars give no light
		through the black night
	when the clouds close.
	And the whipped wave,
		if the winds rage
	over the sea —
	once as serene
		as the bright day —
	is fouled and spoiled
	by the storm's spite,
		and shows itself
	clouded and dirty.
	Often the clear stream
		straying downhill
	towards the sea
	is blocked and held
		by some tumbled slab
	of fallen rock.
	So: do you want
		to make out the truth
	clear and plain,
	and hold to the road
		steadily,
	and not turn off it?
	Then let no joy,
		no hope, no fear
	come near you;
	drive grief away.
		The mind these rule
	is chained and blind.
