Chapter 8: The One Service Bad Fortune Does

"But so that you do not think I am waging an implacable war against Fortune, I admit there is a time when the deceitful goddess does men good — I mean when she shows herself, uncovers her face, and admits what she really is. Perhaps you do not follow me yet. What I am trying to say is a strange thing, and that is why I can hardly find words to make my meaning clear.

"I truly believe that Bad Fortune is more use to men than Good Fortune. Good Fortune, when she wears the look of happiness and seems to be at her most affectionate, is always lying; Bad Fortune is always truthful, since by changing she shows how inconstant she is. The one deceives, the other teaches. The one chains up the minds of those who enjoy her favour with the appearance of a good that is not there; the other sets them free by letting them know how fragile happiness is. So you may see the one fickle, shifting like the wind, and always self-deceived; and the other sober, alert and watchful, precisely because adversity has disciplined her.

"And finally, Good Fortune with her enticements drags men far away from the real good; Bad Fortune often hauls them back to the real good with grappling irons. Again — do you think it a small favour that this cruel, this hateful Fortune has shown you the hearts of your faithful friends? The other one hid the faces of the true friends and the false alike; but in leaving, she has taken away *her* friends and left you *yours*. What would you not have paid for that service in the fullness of your prosperity, when you thought yourself a lucky man? So stop looking for the wealth you have lost, since in true friends you have found the most precious riches there are."

Song VIII: Love Is Lord of All

		Why are nature's changes bound
	to a fixed and ordered round?
	What has bent to a treaty of peace
	every element that was at war?
	Why does the rose-coloured morning
	rise carried on the sun's chariot?
	Why should the moon rule the night,
	led by the evening star's light?
	What is the power that holds
	the restless sea in its place,
	that keeps it inside fixed limits
	and stops it flooding over the earth?
	Love it is that holds the chains,
	Love that rules over sea and land;
	Love — who else but sovereign Love? —
	Love, high lord in heaven above.
	Yet if he relaxed his care,
	everything that is now knit so close
	in sweet love and holy peace
	would not stop fighting any more,
	but with the rough shock and jar of strife
	would wreck the fair fabric of the world.
		Love unites tribes and nations
	by the sacred rites of a fair treaty;
	Love makes the bonds of marriage holy
	with affection's softest ties;
	Love lays down, as is right,
	faithful laws for true companions.
	Love, all-sovereign Love — then
	you are blessed, you sons of men,
	if the love that rules the sky
	is enthroned high in your hearts.
