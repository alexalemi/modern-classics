FORM (settled before file 000; see text_analysis.txt section 2)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
- Spoken: modern prose under a "Name." tag. Sung: TAB-INDENTED unrhymed
  verse. Follow chapters/'s [Sung]/[Spoken] markers exactly. They come
  from prep.classify, which decides from the SYLLABLE COUNT (the metre)
  with Perseus' labels as tie-breaker, because those labels are wrong
  in ~380 places for Euripides. If a marker still looks wrong, REPORT
  it in your notes; never silently override it.
- Trochaic tetrameter (long lines, 15-16 syllables; common in the late
  plays -- Ion, Orestes, Iphigenia at Aulis, the Bacchae) is SPOKEN.
  Anapaests and every lyric metre are VERSE.
- Speaker tags are the closed set in check.py SPEAKERS. Euripides has
  MANY nameless parts: "Nurse.", "Tutor.", "Servant." (Θεράπων),
  "Maid." (Θεράπαινα), "Old Man." (Πρέσβυς/Πρεσβύτης), "Old Woman.",
  "Child."/"Children.", "Farmer." (the Autourgos of Electra),
  "Herdsman.", "Charioteer.", "Phrygian.", "Priestess.", "Shepherd.",
  "Messenger."/"Second Messenger.", "Herald.", "Huntsmen." (Hippolytus'
  companions), "Argive Soldiers." (IA), "Half-Chorus."/"First
  Half-Chorus."/"Second Half-Chorus.", and in Cyclops 635-641 "First
  Satyr." to "Fourth Satyr.". Lyssa is "Madness."; the Dioscuri are
  "Castor." (he speaks for both). Two consecutive tags for one speaker
  are KEPT where the Greek has two speeches.
- Each play is its own divider and its own chapter; titles as the
  manifest gives them: "Alcestis", "Medea", "The Children of Heracles",
  "Hippolytus", "Andromache", "Hecuba", "The Suppliants", "Electra",
  "Heracles", "The Trojan Women", "Iphigenia among the Taurians", "Ion",
  "Helen", "The Phoenician Women", "Orestes", "The Bacchae", "Iphigenia
  at Aulis", "Cyclops", "Rhesus".

- THE CLASSIFIER'S RESIDUAL, recorded because it recurs and was accepted
  rather than fixed: DOCHMIACS ARE LONG LINES. The lyric rule needs a
  majority of lines under eleven syllables, and a dochmiac stretch
  inside an "episode" div (Hecuba 684-692, Ion 763-799, Phoenician
  Women 293-300, Orestes 1295-1300) does not meet it, so those passages
  are set as prose. Four agents reported it independently, which is what
  makes it a known residual and not a surprise. Fixing it would need a
  second metrical signal (dochmiac has a characteristic shape, not a
  characteristic length); not attempted.
- THE ONE-LINER COMPROMISE, likewise accepted: a trimeter one-liner
  inside a choral lyric is tabbed as verse unless the run around it has
  already been decided spoken. Reported by the Andromache, Ion, Helen,
  Orestes and Suppliants agents. It reads well and keeps the exchange
  visually one thing.

Rhesus (056) -- COMPLETE, and it found A GENERAL TRAP worth keeping:
  A ONE-WORD ANSWER THAT IS A CHARACTER'S NAME IS A PHANTOM SPEAKER TAG.
  The password is "Apollo", and "Apollo." alone on a line is both a hit
  for the phantom-tag grep AND a member of check.py's closed NAMES set,
  so it would have been counted as a speech by the god. Give any such
  password, oath or one-word reply a carrier sentence: "The word is
  Apollo." Applies to any play whose cast shares a name with a thing
  people say.
  Also: the Muse's curse names Diomedes and Odysseus alongside their
  patronymics ("Curse Diomedes, who came out of Oeneus's house"),
  because a curse is unusable if the reader cannot tell who is cursed;
  bare patronymics stand elsewhere. "the girls of the springs" for the
  Parthenoi (never "the Maidens" -- banned word).
