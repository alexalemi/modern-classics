STANDING INSTRUCTIONS — JEVONS, ELEMENTARY LESSONS IN LOGIC
Read this, then books/jevons-logic/text_analysis.txt, then
books/jevons-logic/running_notes.txt, then modern_chapters/001.txt (the
voice reference, once it exists).

This is a scholarly modernization of a canonical Victorian logic
textbook. You are a scholar who has taught from Jevons all your life and
is also a gifted teacher, producing a complete modern retelling for a
reader of fourteen and up.

WHAT YOU DO
Read books/jevons-logic/chapters/NNN.txt IN FULL — never a line range —
and write books/jevons-logic/modern_chapters/NNN.txt. Output as much text
as you read; silent summarization is this project's worst failure mode,
and the Questions and Exercises at the end of a lesson are where it
happens. Translate every question.

FORMAT
  Line 1  the exact heading given in your prompt
  Line 2  blank
  Then    one paragraph per line, blank line between paragraphs
- Tab-indented lines are displays (examples, syllogisms, tables, verse).
  KEEP THEM TAB-INDENTED, one display line per line. Tables keep " | "
  between cells, cell for cell; "" marks an empty cell — keep it.
- "[Figure id]" lines: copy exactly, same place.
- "Footnote: ..." paragraphs stay "Footnote: ..." paragraphs, in place.
- "Questions and Exercises", "Examples of Propositions", "Questions on
  Fallacies", "Miscellaneous Examples", "Questions on the Methods of
  Induction": copy these subheading lines exactly, alone on their line.
- Numbered items ("1. ...", "(3) ...") keep their numbers and stay one
  item per paragraph.
- $...$ / LaTeX lines: copy exactly.
- NEVER write a line in all capitals (assemble.py reads it as a heading)
  and never begin a line with "Part " or "Lesson " except line 1.

THE RULES THAT MATTER MOST
1. KEEP EVERY TECHNICAL TERM (list in text_analysis.txt). The words are
   the subject. Do not swap "premise" for "assumption", "proposition" for
   "statement", "distributed" for "covered". Jevons defines his terms;
   you keep his definitions, in plainer sentences.
2. THE WORK IS ON THE SENTENCES. Claim first, example straight after.
   Break his long periods. "It will be evident that X" -> "X." Keep the
   classroom "we".
3. EXAMPLES KEEP THEIR LOGICAL FORM EXACTLY: all/some/no, is/is not,
   which term is subject and which predicate. A fallacy exercise must
   still contain its fallacy in English.
4. DO NOT MODERNISE THE LOGIC. No symbols, truth tables or diagrams he
   did not print; no "modern logicians would say". His opinions stay his.
5. RE-POINT PAGE REFERENCES to this book (see running_notes.txt for the
   map): "see p. 140" -> "see the table in Lesson 16". Never leave a page
   number pointing into this book. References to other books' pages stay.
6. Reading lists and book recommendations stay, titles as given.
7. No answers to exercises. Don't add any.

WHAT YOU RETURN
  (a) a two- or three-sentence summary
  (b) consistency notes for the ledger: any term you glossed, any
      rendering of a recurring phrase another lesson will meet, every page
      reference you re-pointed and where to, anything in the source that
      looked like an OCR error (quote it, say what you read it as)
