Canto Twenty-Eight

	Who could ever, even in free prose,
	tell fully of the blood and the wounds
	that I saw now, however many times he told it?

	Every tongue would certainly fall short,
	because of our speech, and our minds,
	which have too little room to take in so much.

	If all the people were gathered together again
	who once, on the fated ground
	of Apulia, grieved for their own blood

	because of the Trojans, and because of the long war
	that made such a high spoil of rings,
	as Livy writes, who does not err,

	together with those who felt the pain of blows
	from standing against Robert Guiscard;
	and the others whose bones are still being collected

	at Ceperano, where every Apulian
	turned liar, and over there at Tagliacozzo,
	where old Alardo won without weapons;

	and if one showed his pierced limb and another his cut-off one,
	it would be nothing beside
	the filthy manner of the ninth pouch.

	No cask that has lost its middle stave or its end board
	gapes as I saw one man gape,
	split open from the chin down to where we fart.

	His guts were hanging between his legs;
	you could see his organs, and the miserable sack
	that makes shit out of what is swallowed.

	While I fasten myself on seeing all of him,
	he looked at me and opened his chest with his hands,
	saying: "Now look how I split myself!

	Look how mangled Muhammad is!
	In front of me Ali goes weeping,
	cleft in the face from the chin to the forelock.

	And all the others you see here
	were sowers of scandal and of schism
	while they lived, and that is why they are split like this.

	There is a devil back here who trims us
	so cruelly, putting every one of this batch
	back to the edge of the sword

	each time we have gone round the grieving road;
	because the wounds close up again
	before anyone comes back in front of him.

	But who are you, loitering there on the ridge,
	perhaps to put off going to the punishment
	that has been judged on your own accusations?"

	"Death has not reached him yet, and no guilt brings him
	to be tormented," my master answered;
	"but to give him a full experience of it,

	it falls to me, who am dead, to lead him
	down here through Hell, from circle to circle;
	and this is true, as I am speaking to you."

	There were more than a hundred who, when they heard him,
	stopped in the ditch to look at me
	in astonishment, forgetting their torture.

	"Then tell Fra Dolcino to arm himself,
	you who will perhaps see the sun before long,
	if he does not want to follow me here soon —

	to arm himself with provisions, so that pressure of snow
	does not bring the victory to the man from Novara,
	who would not find it easy to win otherwise."

	Muhammad said these words to me
	after he had lifted one foot to go;
	then he stretched it to the ground and left.

	Another, who had his throat pierced through
	and his nose cut off up to the eyebrows,
	and had no more than a single ear,

	having stopped with the others to stare in astonishment,
	opened his windpipe before the others —
	it was crimson on every side outside —

	and said: "O you whom no guilt condemns,
	and whom I saw up in the Italian country,
	unless too much likeness is deceiving me,

	remember Pier da Medicina,
	if you ever go back to see the sweet plain
	that slopes down from Vercelli to Marcabò.

	And let the two best men of Fano know,
	Messer Guido, and Angiolello too,
	that if seeing ahead here is not empty,

	they will be thrown out of their vessel
	and drowned in a sack near La Cattolica
	through the treachery of a wicked tyrant.

	Between the islands of Cyprus and Majorca
	Neptune never saw so great a crime,
	not from pirates, nor from the people of Argos.

	That traitor who sees out of one eye only,
	and holds the town that one who is here with me
	wishes he had never set eyes on,

	will get them to come and parley with him;
	then he will act so that they will need
	neither vow nor prayer against the wind off Focara."

	And I to him: "Show me and explain,
	if you want me to carry news of you up there,
	who it is who found the sight so bitter."

	Then he put his hand on the jaw
	of one of his companions and opened his mouth,
	shouting: "This is the man, and he does not speak.

	This one, when he was driven out, drowned the doubting
	in Caesar, saying that a man who is ready
	always suffers loss by waiting."

	Oh, how dismayed he looked to me,
	with his tongue cut out of his gullet —
	Curio, who had been so bold in speech!

	And one who had both his hands cut off,
	lifting the stumps through the dark air
	so that the blood made his face filthy,

	shouted: "You will remember Mosca too,
	who said — alas! — 'A thing done has an end,'
	which was a bad seed for the Tuscan people."

	And I added: "And death to your own family";
	at which he, piling grief on grief,
	went off like a person miserable and mad.

	But I stayed to look at the crowd,
	and I saw a thing I would be afraid
	to tell by itself, without more evidence;

	except that conscience gives me confidence —
	the good companion that makes a man free
	under the breastplate of feeling himself clean.

	I certainly saw, and it still seems that I see it,
	a trunk without a head walking along
	as the others of that miserable flock walked;

	and it held the cut-off head by the hair,
	swinging it in its hand like a lantern;
	and the head looked at us and said: "Oh, me!"

	It was making a lamp of itself for itself,
	and they were two in one and one in two;
	how that can be, he knows who so governs.

	When it was right at the foot of the bridge
	it lifted its arm high with the whole head,
	to bring its words nearer to us,

	which were: "Now see the miserable punishment,
	you who go breathing, looking at the dead:
	see whether any is as great as this.

	And so that you may carry news of me,
	know that I am Bertran de Born, the one
	who gave the young king his bad counsel.

	I made the father and the son rebels against each other;
	Ahithophel did no more to Absalom
	and to David with his wicked goadings.

	Because I divided people who were so joined,
	I carry my brain divided — alas —
	from its beginning, which is in this trunk.

	So the counter-suffering is observed in me."
