# Passages that must survive near-verbatim into modern_chapters/.
#
# CHECKED AGAINST THE PREPARED ITALIAN, not recalled (the epictetus
# rule), which matters more here than in most books: the Inferno is
# quoted constantly in English, and almost every phrase everybody knows
# is somebody's TRANSLATION rather than Dante. "Abandon all hope, ye who
# enter here" has a "here" that is not in the line; "in the middle of
# the journey of our life" is one of five current versions; and the
# famous last words of Canto Three are six words in Italian and vary by
# a word in every English edition ever printed. A pin written from
# memory could only be satisfied by a translation that had drifted to
# somebody else's wording.
#
# The pins are therefore written from THIS edition's own decisions,
# which prep.py and check.py cannot see: the line divisions, the choice
# of plain modern English over ornament, and the handful of places
# where a famous line is famous because it is plain.

# --- Canto One: the opening, which everybody knows and nobody agrees on
Midway through the journey of our life
for the straight way had been lost

# --- Canto Three: the gate ---------------------------------------------
Abandon all hope, you who enter
here every cowardice has to die
# Virgil's definition of the damned, and the whole book's thesis
who have lost the good of the intellect

# --- Canto Four: Aristotle, unnamed and unmistakable --------------------
the master of those who know

# --- Canto Five: Francesca ---------------------------------------------
# The three anaphoric Loves, which have to stay three and stay parallel
Love, which is quickly caught in a noble heart
Love, which lets nobody who is loved off from loving
Love led us to a single death
There is no greater pain
than to remember a happy time
# the Galeotto line, rendered for sense: the reader has to feel the
# accusation, and no modern reader knows who Gallehault was
The book was our go-between
that day we read no further in it
And I fell as a dead body falls

# --- Canto Nine: the address to the reader ------------------------------
look at the teaching that is hidden
under the veil of these strange verses

# --- Canto Thirteen -----------------------------------------------------
I made a gallows for myself out of my own house

# --- Canto Fifteen: Brunetto, and the line the whole canto is for -------
you taught me how a man makes himself eternal

# --- Canto Twenty-One: the last line, which must not be tidied ----------
and he had made a trumpet of his arse

# --- Canto Twenty-Four --------------------------------------------------
sitting on down
brings nobody to fame

# --- Canto Twenty-Six: Ulysses ------------------------------------------
you were not made to live like brutes
but to follow virtue and knowledge

# --- Canto Twenty-Eight: the term the whole structure of Hell rests on --
So the counter-suffering is observed in me

# --- Canto Thirty-Three: Ugolino ----------------------------------------
# THE PIN EXISTS TO KEEP THE AMBIGUITY. Dante's line can mean that
# hunger finished what grief could not, or that he ate them; he refuses
# to say, and seven centuries have argued about it. Any rendering that
# settles the question is wrong, whichever way it settles it.
Then fasting had more power than grief

# --- Canto Thirty-Four: the last line of the book -----------------------
And from there we came out to see the stars again
