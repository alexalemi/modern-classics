Chapter 30: What to Have Ready When Things Get Hard

When you are going in to see some great man, remember that somebody else is watching what happens from above, and that it is him you ought to please rather than the other. So the one who watches from above asks you:

"In the schools, what did you use to say about exile and chains and death and disgrace?"

I used to say they are indifferent — neither good nor bad.

"And what do you say about them now? Have they changed at all?"

No.

"Have you changed, then?"

No.

"Tell me, then: what things are indifferent?"

The things outside the will.

"And tell me what follows from that."

That the things outside the will are nothing to me.

"Tell me about the good, too. What was your view?"

A will such as it ought to be, and the right use of impressions.

"And the end — what is it?"

To follow you.

"Do you still say this now?"

I say the same now.

Then go in to the great man boldly, remembering these things, and you will see what a young man who has studied them looks like among men who have not. For my part I imagine you will be thinking something like this: why do we make such great and elaborate preparations for nothing? Is this the thing men call power? Is this the antechamber? These the chamberlains? These the armed guards? Was it for this that I listened to so many lectures? All this is nothing — and I had been preparing myself as though for something great.
