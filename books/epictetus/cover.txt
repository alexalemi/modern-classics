Jean-Léon Gérôme's *Diogenes* (1860). The Cynic sits in the mouth of the big clay jar he lived in, half dressed and barefoot on a bed of straw, trimming the lamp he carried in daylight looking for an honest man; two dogs sit beside him. Diogenes is the one Epictetus points to whenever he needs to show what a free man looks like: a man who owned nothing anyone could take from him.
