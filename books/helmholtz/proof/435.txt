The limiting conditions are
	1) that \(z = 0\) along the legs of the angle, that is, \(z = 0\), when \(y = \pm x \cdot \tan \eta\).
	2) that \(z = 0\), when \(x = y = 0\), that is, at the vertex of the angle, and finally
	3) that \(z\) is finite, when \(x\) is infinite.

The further development of the problem will shew how these two last limiting equations, which suffice for our purpose, may be replaced by certain determinate curves acting as fixed boundaries between the legs of the angle (p. 411c, d).

¶ By putting \(x = \xi \cdot \sqrt{P}\) and \(y = \upsilon \cdot \sqrt{Q}\) the equation (1) may be reduced to the better known form \[Z + \frac{d^2z}{d\xi^2} + \frac{d^2z}{d\upsilon^2} = \mu \cdot \frac{d^2z}{dt^2} \tag{1a}\] which is the equation of motion for a membrane stretched uniformly in all directions, \(\xi\) and \(\upsilon\) being the rectangular co-ordinates on its surface.

For this notation the limiting conditions become
	1) \(z = 0\) for \(\upsilon = \pm \xi \cdot \sqrt{\frac{P}{Q}} \cdot \tan \eta\),
	2) \(z = 0\) for \(\xi = \upsilon = 0\),
	3) \(z\) finite, for \(\xi = \infty\).

The transformed problem consequently differs from the original merely in having a uniformly stretched membrane, and a different amount of angle, which ¶ we will represent by \(2\epsilon\).

Since in the applications which we have to make of the result, \(P\) will be very small in comparison with \(Q\), the angle \(\epsilon\) for the transformed membrane will also be very small, and upon this circumstance mainly depend the analytical difficulties of the problem.

After these preliminary remarks, we proceed to the analytical treatment of the equations (1) and (1a) by introducing polar co-ordinates, assuming \[\left.\begin{aligned} x &= \xi \cdot \sqrt{P} = r \cdot \sqrt{P} \cdot \cos \omega \\ y &= \upsilon \cdot \sqrt{Q} = r \cdot \sqrt{Q} \cdot \sin \omega \end{aligned}\right\} \tag{1b}\]

The equations (1) and (1a) then take the form \[\frac{d^2z}{dr^2} + \frac{1}{r} \cdot \frac{dz}{dr} + \frac{1}{r^2} \cdot \frac{d^2z}{d\omega^2} = \mu \cdot \frac{d^2z}{dt^2} - Z \tag{1c}\]

¶ The limiting conditions are now, that
	1) \(z = 0\), when \(\omega = \pm \epsilon\), and hence \(\tan \epsilon = \sqrt{\frac{P}{Q}} \cdot \tan \eta\),
	2) \(z = 0\) for \(r = 0\),
	3) \(z\) is finite, when \(r\) is infinite.

As regards the nature of the force \(Z\), we shall assume that it consists of two parts; the *first* depending on the friction, which we may put \(= - \nu \cdot \frac{dz}{dt}\), where is a positive real constant; the *second*, depending on a periodically variable pressure exerted by the surrounding medium on the membrane, uniformly over its whole surface.
