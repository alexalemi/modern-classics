This confusion is not entirely obviated by adding the tone *c*, and the consequence is the chord of the Seventh *d* | *f* + *a₁* - *c* is usually employed only in alternation with the chord of the dominant Seventh in the cadence, where it distinguishes *C* major from *C* minor.⁎ But the addition of the tone *b₁* to the triad *d* | *f* + *a₁* [as *b₁* - *d* | *f* + *a₁*] is characteristic, because this last can at most be confused with *b₁* | *d₁* - *f* + *a₁*, which belongs to *A₁* minor. The chord *b₁* - *d* | *f* + *a₁*, however, sounds comparatively harsh in every position for which *a₁* is not the highest note, and hence its application is very limited. It is often united with the chord of the dominant Seventh as a chord of the Ninth, thus *g* + *b₁* - *d'* | *f'* + *a₁'*, in which *g* and *a₁'* must remain the extreme tones. More upon this hereafter.

In the key of *C* minor, the triad *d* | *f* - *a¹*♭ would, in just intonation, be characteristic, but yet it is easily confused with other chords. Thus ¶ \[\begin{aligned} &d \underset{\tfrac{32}{27}}{\mid} f \underset{\tfrac{6}{5}}{-} a^1\flat \text{ [in cents } d \text{ 294 } f \text{ 316 } a^1\flat\text{] belongs to } C \text{ minor} \\ &d_1 \underset{\tfrac{6}{5}}{-} f \underset{\tfrac{32}{27}}{\mid} a\flat \text{ [in cents } d \text{ 316 } f \text{ 294 } a\flat\text{] to } E\flat \text{ major and } E\flat \text{ minor} \\ &d \underset{\tfrac{6}{5}}{-} f^1 \underset{\tfrac{75}{64}}{\ldots} g_1\sharp \text{ [in cents } d \text{ 316 } f^1 \text{ 274 } g_1\sharp\text{] to } A \text{ minor} \\ &d^1 \underset{\tfrac{75}{64}}{\ldots} e_1\sharp \underset{\tfrac{6}{5}}{-} g\sharp \text{ [in cents } d^1 \text{ 274 } e_1\sharp \text{ 316 } g\sharp\text{] to } F\sharp \text{ minor.} \end{aligned}\]

The addition of the tone *C* in the first chord of the Seventh above, thus *d* | *f* - *a¹*♭ + *c*, would decisively exclude the key *F*♯ minor only, and the addition of the tone *b₁* (which in tempered intonation is confused with *b* or *c¹*♭) would also ¶ readily be adapted to all the above keys. Thus altered it becomes the chord *b₁* - *d* | *f* - *a¹*♭, and is called *the chord of the diminished Seventh*, which on keyed instruments appears as a series of minor Thirds. In reality a Pythagorean minor Third or else an acute augmented Second separates the normal minor Thirds, thus: \[\begin{gathered} b_1 \underset{\tfrac{6}{5}}{-} d \underset{\tfrac{32}{27}}{\mid} f \underset{\tfrac{6}{5}}{-} a^1\flat \underset{\tfrac{75}{64}}{\quad} b_1 \underset{\tfrac{6}{5}}{-} d \underset{\tfrac{32}{27}}{\mid} f \underset{\tfrac{6}{5}}{-} a^1\flat \underset{\tfrac{75}{64}}{\ldots} b_1 \\ \text{[In cents: 316 294 316 274 316 294 316 274]} \end{gathered}\]

Since the three intervals 6/5, 32/27, and 75/64 [cents 316, 294, 274] differ but very slightly [by 20, 22, 42 cents respectively], they are readily confused,† and we obtain the following, nearly identical, series of tones: ¶ \[\begin{aligned} &b_1 \underset{\tfrac{6}{5}}{-} d \underset{\tfrac{32}{27}}{\mid} f \underset{\tfrac{6}{5}}{-} a^1\flat \underset{\tfrac{75}{64}}{\ldots} b_1 \text{ [in cents } b_1 \text{ 316 } d \text{ 294 } f \text{ 316 } a_1\flat \text{ 274 } b_1\text{] in } C \text{ minor} \\ &b \underset{\tfrac{32}{27}}{\mid} d \underset{\tfrac{6}{5}}{-} f^1 \underset{\tfrac{75}{64}}{\ldots} g_1\sharp \underset{\tfrac{6}{5}}{-} b \text{ [in cents } b \text{ 294 } d \text{ 316 } f^1 \text{ 274 } g_1\sharp \text{ 316 } b\text{] in } A \text{ minor} \\ &b \underset{\tfrac{6}{5}}{-} d^1 \underset{\tfrac{75}{64}}{\ldots} e_1\sharp \underset{\tfrac{6}{5}}{-} g\sharp \underset{\tfrac{32}{27}}{\mid} b \text{ [in cents } b \text{ 316 } d^1 \text{ 274 } e_1\sharp \text{ 316 } g\sharp \text{ 294 } b\text{] in } F\sharp \text{ minor} \\ &c^1\flat \underset{\tfrac{75}{74}}{\ldots} d_1 \underset{\tfrac{6}{5}}{-} f \underset{\tfrac{32}{27}}{\mid} a\flat \underset{\tfrac{6}{5}}{-} c^1\flat \text{ [in cents } c^1\flat \text{ 274 } d_1 \text{ 316 } f \text{ 294 } a\flat \text{ 316 } c^1\flat\text{] in } E\flat \text{ minor.} \end{aligned}\]

Footnote: † [It is quite impossible to confuse them in the just intonation of any harmonic intervals, but they are absolutely identified in equally tempered intonation as 300 cents, and hence in all written music they are treated as identical.
