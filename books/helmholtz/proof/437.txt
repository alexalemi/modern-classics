It is therefore preferable for our present purpose to obtain the expression for \(s\) in the form of definite integrals.

Let \(\phi\) and \(\psi\) denote the following pair of integrals:— \[\left.\begin{aligned} \psi &= \int_0^{\frac{1}{2}\pi} e^{-\mathrm{i}\kappa r \cdot \sin t} \cdot \sin mht \cdot dt \\ \phi &= \int_1^{\infty} u^{-mh-1} \cdot e^{-\frac{1}{2} \cdot \mathrm{i}\kappa r \cdot \left(u + \frac{1}{u}\right)} \cdot du \end{aligned}\right\} \tag{4}\] where ¶ \[\kappa = \sqrt{(\mu n^2 - \mathrm{i} n \nu)} \tag{4a}\] and the sign of the root is so chosen that the possible part of \(\mathrm{i}\kappa\) is positive.

Then \[s_m = \frac{4A}{\pi\kappa^2} \cdot (mh \cdot \psi + mh \cdot \phi \cdot \cos \tfrac{1}{2} mh\pi - 1 \tag{4b}\] which is the required expression for \(s_m\).

To shew that the expression in (4b) really satisfies the equation (3b), substitute this value for \(s_m\) in that equation, and in differentiating under the integral signs of \(\psi\) and \(\phi\), use partial integration to eliminate the factors \(\cos t\) and \(\left(u - \frac{1}{u}\right)\) which appear under the integral signs.

For \(r = 0\) we find ¶ \[\begin{aligned} \psi &= \int_0^{\frac{1}{2}\pi} \sin mht \cdot dt = \frac{1}{mh} \cdot \left(1 - \cos \frac{mh\pi}{2}\right) \\ \phi &= \int_1^{\infty} \frac{du}{u^{mh+1}} = \frac{1}{mh} \end{aligned}\] and hence \(s_m = 0\).

For \(r = \infty\), we have \(\phi = \psi = 0\), and hence \[s_m = - \frac{4A}{\pi\kappa^2}.\]

Hence the function \(s_m\) also satisfies the two limiting conditions, which have been already shewn to be sufficient to determine it.

The equation (4b) may be used to determine the value of \(s_m\) when \(P\), the tension of the membrane in the direction \(x\), is infinitesimal. In this case, as (1b) shews, \(r\) must be the infinite; as also \(h\), of which the value is ¶ \[h = \frac{\pi \cdot \sqrt{Q}}{2 \cdot \sqrt{P} \cdot \tan \eta}.\]

Hence putting \[r = h\rho\] \(\rho\) will be the finite, namely \[\rho = \frac{2x \cdot \tan \eta}{\pi \cdot \sqrt{Q}}.\]

It is easily seen that under these circumstances \(mh\phi\) will \(= 0\).
