Assume for abbreviation \[\left. \begin{array}{r} \frac{pT}{f^2 - Mm)^2} = \tan k \\ (f^2 - Mm^2)^2 + p^2T^2 = C^2 \end{array} \right\} \ldots\ldots \tag{8b}\]

Then the values of \(F\) and \(G\) will be as follows: \[\left. \begin{array}{l} F = - \frac{A}{2} \cdot \frac{C^2 \cdot \sin 2\,(pl + k) + g^4m^2 \cdot \sin 2pl}{C^2 \cdot \sin^2 (pl + k) + g^4m^2 \cdot \sin^2 pl} \\ G = - A\, \frac{Cmg^2 \cdot \sin k}{C^2 \cdot \sin^2 (pl + k) + g^4m^2 \cdot \sin^2 pl} \end{array} \right\} \ldots\ldots \tag{8c}\]

Putting the amplitude of the vibration of the extremity of the string which rests upon the bridge = \(V\), equation (7) becomes ¶ \[V^2 = [F \cdot \sin pl + A \cdot \cos pl]^2 + G^2 \cdot \sin^2 pl,\] and on putting in this equation the values of \(F\) and \(G\) from (8c) we find \[V = \frac{AC \cdot \sin k}{\surd\, [C^2 \cdot \sin^2 (pl + k) + g^4m^2 \cdot \sin {}^2pl]} \ldots\ldots \tag{9}\]

The numerator in this expression is independent of the length of the string. Any alteration of its length therefore affects the denominator only. Under the radical sign is the sum of two squares, which can never = 0, because \(m\), \(g\), \(p\), \(T\), and hence \(k\), can never = 0. The coefficient of the resistance of air, \(g\), must certainly be considered as infinitesimal. Hence the denominator is a maximum, and \(V\) is a minimum, when \(\sin (pl + k) = 0\), or when \[pl = \nu\pi - k \ldots\ldots \tag{9a}\] ¶ where \(\nu\) is any whole number. The maximum value of \(V\) is \[V_M = \frac{AC}{g^2m}.\]

Hence, other circumstances being the same, this maximum value increases as \(g\), the coefficient of the resistance of the air, decreases, and as \(C\) increases. To ascertain the circumstances on which the magnitude of \(C\) depends, put for \(p^2\) in the second of the equations (8b), which defines the meaning of \(C\), its value from (7a), and also put \(n^2 = \frac{f^2}{M}\); this gives \[C^2 = M^2 \cdot (n^2 - m^2)^2 + T\mu m^2.\]

Now \(n\) is the number of vibrations which the bridge would perform in \(2\pi\) seconds, under the influence of the elastic sounding board alone, without the string and the resistance of the air; and \(m\) is the same number of vibrations for ¶ the tuning-fork. Hence the maximum value of \(V\) can now be written \[V_M = \frac{A}{g^2} \surd \left[ M^2 \cdot \left(1 - \frac{n^2}{m^2}\right)^2 + T\mu \right]\] in which everything is reduced to the weights \(M\), \(T\), \(\eta\) and the magnitude of the interval \(1 - \frac{n}{m}\).

If \(m > n\), which is usually the case, it is advantageous to make the weight of the bridge \(M\), rather large. Hence I have had it constructed of a plate of copper. If \(M\) is very large, \(k\) will be very small in consequence of (8b), and then the equation (9a) shews that the various tones of greatest resonance approach all the more nearly to those which correspond with the series of simple whole numbers. The heavier the bridge the sharper the boundaries of the tones of the string.

Observe that the rules here given for the influence of the bridge hold only for the assumption that the string is excited by a tuning-fork, and not, so far as this investigation extends, for other cases.
