Hence, if \(\psi\) is a continuous function, \[\frac{d}{dt}(y + xi) = A\kappa \int_{-\infty}^{t} \psi_s \cdot e^{\kappa(t-s)} \cdot ds + A\psi_t \tag{6c}\] \[\frac{d^2}{dt^2}(y + xi) = A\kappa^2 \int_{-\infty}^{t} \psi_s \cdot e^{\kappa(t-s)} \cdot ds + A\kappa\psi_t + A\frac{d\psi}{dt} \tag{6d}\] Then multiplying (6) by \(a^2\), (6c) by \(b^2\), (6d) by \(m\), and adding, and taking account of (6a), we obtain the following equation between the imaginary parts of the respective expressions, ¶ \[m\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + b^2\frac{dx}{dt} + \alpha^2x = mA\beta\psi_t.\] Then assuming \[A = \frac{1}{\beta m}\] equation (6) gives a value for \(x\) which satisfies the differential equation in \(s\), and is finite for all values of the time, namely \[x = \frac{1}{\beta m}\int_{-\infty}^{t} \psi_s \cdot e^{-\alpha(t-s)} \cdot \sin \beta(t - s) \cdot ds.\] That is, \(x\) is shewn to be a sum of superposed expiring oscillations, of which the initial time is \(s\), and the initial amplitude \(\frac{\psi_s}{\beta m}\), and every moment preceding the point of time \(t\) contributes to the result. But this contribution vanishes for those parts of the motion which were excited for a long time before the moment ¶ considered, that is, for those for which the exponent \(\alpha\) \((t-s)\) is a large number, and the motion therefore depends at every instant only on those forces \(\psi\) which have acted a short time previously.

If the action of the force \(\psi\) takes place only during a limited time from \(t_0\) to \(t_1\), then \(x\) of the equation (6d) will not be = 0 except up to the time \(t_0\), after which it will differ from nothing, and after \(t_1\) the motion will be that of simply expiring vibrations. Also the magnitude of \(x\) will depend upon how often large positive values of \(\psi\) occur at the same time as large positive values of \(\sin \beta t\), and negative with negative. The value of \(x\) will be comparatively the greatest when \(\psi\) and \(\sin \beta t\) change their sign nearly at the same time.

If \(\psi_t\) has had a constant value \(p\) from \(t = t_0\) to \(t = t_1\), then \[x = H e^{-\alpha(t-t_1)} \sin [\beta(t - t_1) + \epsilon - \eta]\] on putting \[k \cos \eta = -\alpha, \quad k \sin \eta = \beta\] ¶ \[H \cos \epsilon = \frac{p}{\beta mk} [1 - e^{-\alpha(t_1-t_0)} \cos \beta(t_1 - t_0)]\] \[H \sin \epsilon = \frac{p}{\beta mk} e^{-\alpha(t_1-t_0)} \cdot \sin \beta(t_1 - t_0).\] If we suppose \(k\) to have the positive value of \(\sqrt{(\alpha^2 + \beta^2)}\), \(\eta\) will be an obtuse angle. If we give \(H\) the sign of the pressure \(p\), then the angle \(\epsilon\), which lies between \(+ \frac{1}{2}\pi\) and \(- \frac{1}{2}\pi\), will have the same sign as \(\sin \beta(t_1 - t_0)\). In this case the expression for \(x\) represents expiring vibrations, of which the initial amplitude (putting \(\tau\) = the length of the action = \(t_1 - t_0\)) has the value \[H = \frac{p}{\beta mk} \sqrt{(1 - 2 e^{-\alpha\tau} \cos \beta\tau + e^{-2\alpha\tau})}.\] This is a maximum for different values of \(\tau\), when \(\cos (\beta\tau + \eta) = \cos \eta \cdot e^{-\alpha\tau}\), or, for small values of \(\alpha\) and \(\tau\), when \(\beta\tau\) approximately contains an uneven number
