PILLOW PROBLEMS — TRANSLATION STRATEGY
Lewis Carroll (C. L. Dodgson), Curiosa Mathematica Part II, 4th ed. 1895
Source: Project Gutenberg #79080 (the illustrated HTML edition)


1. WHAT THIS BOOK IS, AND WHY IT IS WORTH DOING

Seventy-two mathematical problems, every one of them worked out in his
head, in bed, in the dark, before a diagram was drawn or a word written
down. Questions in Chapter I, Answers in Chapter II, Solutions in
Chapter III, sharing one numbering.

The premise sells it, but the Introduction is the reason to publish it.
Carroll explains that he does not recommend mathematics as a cure for
insomnia — he says plainly he has never suffered from it, and changed
the title from "sleepless nights" to "wakeful hours" because kind
friends had written to sympathise with an illness he did not have. What
he offers it as is a remedy for "the harassing thoughts that are apt to
invade a wholly-unoccupied mind". Then he says why, and drops his guard:

  "there are sceptical thoughts, which seem for the moment to uproot
  the firmest faith; there are blasphemous thoughts, which dart unbidden
  into the most reverent souls; there are unholy thoughts, which
  torture, with their hateful presence, the fancy that would fain be
  pure."

That is a devout, scrupulous, frightened man telling you what he does at
three in the morning, and it is the most naked page he ever printed. It
governs the register of the whole front matter. DO NOT modernise it into
the language of mental health — no "intrusive thoughts", no "anxiety",
no clinical vocabulary at all. He is not describing a diagnosis, he is
describing a night. Keep his cadence, keep "unholy", keep the biblical
"unclean spirit" who came back with seven others because he found the
chamber "swept and garnished" (Matthew 12), and let the reader meet him
as he was.


2. THE DECISION THAT GOVERNS THE MATHEMATICS

MODERNISE THE NOTATION. Decided by Alex before a line was written, and
DELIBERATELY THE OPPOSITE of the ruling for symbolic-logic/. There,
Carroll's terminology was kept untouched because the words WERE the
machine — Retinend and Eliminand are parts he built and the system does
not run without them. Here the notation is incidental to the argument,
and the argument is what the reader came for. So:

  - his factorial, a vertical bar with the number underlined, is "3!"
  - his mid-height decimal point is a full stop: ·5 becomes 0.5
  - "&c." becomes "etc.", "i. e." closes up to "i.e."
  - fractions set as text, x/y, with brackets where they are needed

All of this is done mechanically by tex.py before the translation sees
it, so the translator's job is the PROSE around the mathematics. Read
tex.py's docstring before touching a formula: the four traps there (the
decimal point that is not a multiplication sign, the multiplication dot
that is not a decimal point, the vinculum that is a bracket, and the row
break welded to an ampersand) each produce plausible, readable, wrong
arithmetic.

WHAT IS *NOT* MODERNISED: his mathematical vocabulary and his notation
for the objects themselves. Tetragon stays a Tetragon. He capitalises
Triangle, Square, Problem, Solid, Points; keep it, as in symbolic-logic
— it is how he marks a term of art. Q.E.F. stays. His letters for
points and his angle names stay exactly as printed, because the diagrams
carry the same letters and a reader with the plate in front of them must
be able to follow.


3. TWO THINGS THE TRANSCRIBER DID, WHICH ARE NOT CARROLL

Both are stated in the Gutenberg transcriber's note and both matter:

  - CARROLL USED PRIVATE SYMBOLS FOR SINE AND COSINE — ⫙ and ⌓, derived
    from the old theory in which sines and cosines were actual lines.
    The transcription replaced them with "sin" and "cos" throughout. His
    Preface to the Fourth Edition explains the symbols and shows the
    diagram they come from, and that passage is KEPT: it is a lovely
    piece of mathematical history and the only place the symbols now
    survive. Translate it so a reader understands that the body of the
    book no longer uses them.
  - 'a' AND GREEK ALPHA ARE INDISTINGUISHABLE IN THE PRINTED BOOK, and
    the transcriber "corrected certain expressions to avoid potential
    mathematical errors". So some symbol assignments are HIS judgement,
    not Carroll's page. This cuts against the tangled-tale rule (suspect
    the transcription before the author): here the transcription has
    already been silently edited, and where an a/α reading looks wrong
    the transcriber is as likely to be the cause as the compositor.


4. CARROLL'S OWN ERRATA ARE PART OF THE BOOK

The Introduction ends with him listing his own mistakes, one by one,
with the flat cheerfulness of a man who does not mind being wrong in
public: "In No. 55, I put 'tan' for 'sin'." "Apparently I was under the
delusion that 'a sin B sin C' was the same thing as 'sin A·bc'!" KEEP
EVERY ONE, and keep the closing hope that a reader will find further
mistakes and enjoy feeling superior to him. Do not silently correct the
solutions to match — Chapter III already contains the corrected working;
the list is a confession, not an erratum slip.

ONE REAL MISPRINT TO FIX: in the Preface to the Fourth Edition, "b² +
c² - a² must be greater than O" sets a capital letter O where the
figure 0 belongs. Correct it silently.


5. VOICE

Three registers, and they must stay apart.

  - THE PREFACES AND INTRODUCTION: conversational, self-deprecating,
    intermittently very moving. This is where the work is. Long
    Victorian periods to unstack, and jokes that must survive — the
    child who cannot win a penny by not thinking of strawberry-jam, "A
    poor thing, Sir, but mine own!", the "Transcendental Probabilities"
    boast and its follow-up, "Is not Life itself a Paradox?"
  - THE QUESTIONS: terse, formal, and often funny in one clause ("A bag
    contains one counter, known to be either white or black"). Do not
    pad them. Each ends with the date he solved it, in the margin.
  - THE SOLUTIONS: working, not exposition. Short connective phrases
    between formulas — "Hence", "Also", "Similarly", "which answers
    (1)". Leave them short. The temptation is to explain his steps; do
    not. He is showing what he did in the dark, and the compression is
    the point.


6. RATIO

Expect close to 1:1, as with the other English-to-English volumes;
verify with --min-ratio 0.85 --max-ratio 1.3. Note that the word counts
are dominated by mathematics that passes through untouched, so the prose
ratio matters more than the number: check the Introduction on its own.

AND THE CHECK THAT MATTERS MOST IS NOT THE RATIO. verify.py cannot see
arithmetic. Run a numeric-token set difference per file (the fleming
recipe) and read the derivations against the Answers in Chapter II —
2,436 formulas came through a converter, and a converter that is wrong
is wrong in a way that reads perfectly well.
