Drawn for this edition, in the manner of the Dover science paperbacks of the 1950s and 60s. It is Carroll's figure for Problem 58, the chance that three points taken at random on a plane make an obtuse-angled triangle: AB is the longest side, the third point must fall inside the pointed arch drawn on it, and if it falls inside the semicircle the triangle is obtuse. Carroll worked such problems out in his head in bed, without paper, which is why the figure stands against a night sky.
