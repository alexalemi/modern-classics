John William Waterhouse's *A Tale from the Decameron* (1916). Young women in long gowns sit on the grass of a walled garden near a stone fountain, listening. One wears a small gold crown; at the right a young man in red leans toward them with his hand raised, in the middle of a story. It is the frame of Boccaccio's book: the company out of the city, the day's crowned queen, and someone telling a tale.
