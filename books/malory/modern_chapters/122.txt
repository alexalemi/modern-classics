Chapter 3: How Beaumains asked for the battle, and how it was granted him, and how he asked to be knighted by Sir Lancelot

At these words Beaumains came before the king, while the damsel was still there, and said, "Sir king, God thank you, I have been in your kitchen this twelvemonth, and have had my full keep, and now I will ask the two gifts that are still owing."

"Ask, and I will stand by it," said the king.

"Sir, these shall be my two gifts. First, that you will grant me this damsel's adventure, for it belongs to me."

"You shall have it," said the king. "I grant it to you."

"Then, sir, this is the other gift: that you will order Lancelot du Lake to make me a knight, for I will be made a knight by him and by no one else. And when I have gone, I beg you to let him ride after me, and make me a knight when I ask him."

"All this shall be done," said the king.

"Shame on you!" said the damsel. "Am I to have no one but your kitchen page?"

Then she was angry, and took her horse and left. And with that someone came to Beaumains and told him his horse and armour had come for him. And there was the dwarf, come with everything he needed, of the richest kind; and the whole court marvelled greatly where all that gear had come from. So when he was armed, there were only a few men as handsome as he was. And so he came into the hall and took his leave of King Arthur, and Sir Gawain, and Sir Lancelot, and begged Lancelot to hurry after him; and so he left, and rode after the damsel.
