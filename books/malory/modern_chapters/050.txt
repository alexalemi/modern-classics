Chapter 4: How Sir Tor was recognised as the son of King Pellinore, and how Gawain was made a knight

So in the morning King Pellinore came to the court of King Arthur, who was overjoyed to see him, and told him about Tor, how he was his son, and how he had made him a knight at the request of the cowherd. When Pellinore looked at Tor, he was very pleased with him. So the king made Gawain a knight, but Tor was the first he made at the feast.

"What is the reason," said King Arthur, "that there are two places empty among the seats?"

"Sir," said Merlin, "no man shall sit in those places but those who are of the most honour. But in the Siege Perilous, the Perilous Seat, no man shall sit but one, and if anyone is bold enough to try it he will be destroyed; and he who sits there shall have no equal."

And with that Merlin took King Pellinore by the hand, and at the place next to the two seats and the Siege Perilous he said, in the hearing of all, "This is your place, and of anyone here you are the most worthy to sit in it."

At that Sir Gawain sat in great envy, and told his brother Gaheris, "That knight over there is given great honour, which grieves me badly, for he killed our father King Lot. So I will kill him," said Gawain, "with a sword that was sent to me that is exceedingly sharp."

"You shall not do that," said Gaheris, "not now. For at this time I am only a squire, and when I am made a knight I will be avenged on him. And so, brother, it is best that you wait until another time, when we can get him out of the court; for if we did it now we would disturb this high feast."

"Gladly," said Gawain, "as you wish."
