Chapter 23: How Sir Pelleas stopped loving Ettard through the Damsel of the Lake, whom he loved ever after

"Sir Pelleas, sir knight," said the Damsel of the Lake, "take your horse and come away with me out of this country, and you shall love a lady who will love you."

"Gladly," said Sir Pelleas, "for this Lady Ettard has done me great insult and shame." And there he told her the whole story from beginning to end, and how he had meant never to get up again until he was dead. "And now God has sent me such grace that I hate her as much as ever I loved her, thanks be to our Lord Jesus!"

"Thank me," said the Damsel of the Lake.

At once Sir Pelleas armed himself, and took his horse, and ordered his men to bring his tents and his belongings after him to wherever the Damsel of the Lake should direct. So the Lady Ettard died of sorrow, and the Damsel of the Lake gave Sir Pelleas joy, and they loved each other all the days of their lives.
