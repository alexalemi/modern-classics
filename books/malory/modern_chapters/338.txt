Chapter 11: How a servant of Sir Aglovale's was killed, and what vengeance Sir Aglovale and Sir Percival took for it

And when she had come to, she sent a squire after them with plenty of money for expenses. And so when the squire had caught up with them, they would not let him ride with them, but sent him home again to comfort their mother, asking her humbly for her blessing. And so night overtook this squire, and by misfortune he happened to come to a castle where a baron lived. And so when the squire had come into the castle, the lord asked him where he came from, and whom he served.

"My lord," said the squire, "I serve a good knight who is called Sir Aglovale." The squire said it with good intentions, thinking he would be better treated for Sir Aglovale's sake than if he had said he served the queen, Aglovale's mother.

"Well, fellow," said the lord of that castle, "for Sir Aglovale's sake you'll get a bad lodging. Sir Aglovale killed my brother, and so you'll die, as part payment."

And then that lord commanded his men to take him away and kill him; and so they did, and dragged him out of the castle, and there they killed him without mercy.

Just then, in the morning, Sir Aglovale and Sir Percival came riding by a churchyard, where men and women were busy, and looking at the dead squire, and they meant to bury him.

"What is it there," said Sir Aglovale, "that you are staring at so hard?"

A good man stepped forward and said, "Fair knight, here lies a squire who was shamefully killed last night."

"How was he killed, my good fellow?" said Sir Aglovale.

"My fair sir," said the man, "the lord of this castle lodged this squire last night; and because he said he was the servant of a good knight who is with King Arthur, whose name is Sir Aglovale, the lord ordered him killed, and that is why he is dead."

"Many thanks," said Sir Aglovale, "and you shall see his death avenged swiftly; for I am that same knight for whose sake this squire was killed."

Then Sir Aglovale called Sir Percival to him, and told him to dismount quickly; and so they both dismounted, and handed their horses over to their men, and so they went on foot into the castle. And as soon as they were inside the castle gate, Sir Aglovale told the porter, "Go to your lord and tell him that I am Sir Aglovale, for whose sake this squire was killed last night."

At once the porter told this to his lord, whose name was Goodewin. At once he armed himself, and then he came into the courtyard and said, "Which of you is Sir Aglovale?"

"Here I am," said Aglovale. "Why did you kill my mother's squire last night?"

"I killed him because of you," said Sir Goodewin, "for you killed my brother, Sir Gawdelin."

"As for your brother," said Sir Aglovale, "I admit it, I killed him, for he was a false knight and a betrayer of ladies and of good knights. And for the death of my squire you shall die."

"I defy you," said Sir Goodewin.

Then they laid into each other as fiercely as two lions, and Sir Percival fought with all the rest who would fight. And within a while Sir Percival had killed all who would stand against him; for Sir Percival dealt his strokes so roughly that no man dared face him. And within a while Sir Aglovale had Sir Goodewin on the ground, and there he unlaced his helmet and struck off his head. And then they left and took their horses; and then they had the dead squire carried to a priory, and there they buried him.
