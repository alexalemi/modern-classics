Chapter 43: How Sir Archade accused Sir Palomides of treason, and how Sir Palomides slew him

When Palomides had taken off his armour, he asked for lodging for himself and the damsel. At once the high prince ordered them to be lodged. And he was hardly in his lodging when a knight came there called Archade, who was the brother of Goneries, whom Palomides had killed before in the damsel's quarrel. And this knight, Archade, called Sir Palomides a traitor, and accused him of his brother's death.

"With the high prince's leave," said Palomides, "I will answer you."

When Sir Galahalt understood their quarrel, he told them to go to dinner: "And as soon as you have dined, see that each knight is ready in the field."

So when they had dined they both armed themselves, and took their horses; and the queen, and the prince, and Sir Lancelot were seated to watch them. And so they let their horses run, and there Sir Palomides carried Archade on his spear over his horse's tail. And then Palomides got down and drew his sword, but Sir Archade could not get up; and there Sir Palomides tore off his helm and struck off his head. Then the high prince and Queen Guinevere went to supper. Then King Bagdemagus sent his son Meliagaunce away, so that Sir Lancelot would not meet him; for he hated Sir Lancelot, and Sir Lancelot did not know it.
