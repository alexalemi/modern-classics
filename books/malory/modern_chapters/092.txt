Chapter 3: How King Arthur held a parliament at York, and how he arranged for the realm to be governed in his absence

Now we leave Lucius the Emperor and speak of King Arthur, who commanded all the men of his household to be ready at the octave of St. Hilary, a week after the saint's day, to hold a parliament at York. And at that parliament it was decided to seize all the ships of the land, and to be ready within fifteen days at Sandwich. And there he explained to his army how he meant to conquer the Empire, which he ought to have by right. And there he appointed two governors of this realm, that is to say, Sir Baudwin of Britain, to give the best counsel, and Sir Constantine, son of Sir Cador of Cornwall, who after the death of Arthur was king of this realm. And in the presence of all his lords he handed over to them the rule of the realm, and Guinevere his queen. At this Sir Lancelot was angry, for he left Sir Tristram with King Mark for the love of Isolde the Fair. Then Queen Guinevere grieved greatly at the departure of her lord and the others, and fainted so that the ladies carried her into her room. So the king departed with his great army, leaving the queen and the realm in the keeping of Sir Baudwin and Constantine. And when he was on his horse he said in a loud voice, "If I die on this expedition, it is my will that Sir Constantine shall be my heir, and crowned king of this realm, as the next of my blood."

And afterwards he departed and put to sea at Sandwich with all his army, with a great multitude of ships, galleys, small boats and great warships, sailing on the sea.
