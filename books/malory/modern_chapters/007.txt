Chapter 7: How King Arthur was crowned, and how he appointed officers

And at the feast of Pentecost all kinds of men who wished to try pulled at the sword, but none could succeed but Arthur, and he pulled it out before all the lords and common people who were there. At this all the common people cried out together, "We will have Arthur for our king! We will put him off no longer, for we all see that it is God's will that he shall be our king, and whoever holds out against it, we will kill him."

And with that they all knelt at once, both rich and poor, and begged Arthur's pardon for having put him off so long. And Arthur forgave them, and took the sword in both his hands, and offered it on the altar where the Archbishop was, and so he was made a knight by the best man who was there. And so the coronation was held at once. And there he swore to his lords and the common people to be a true king, and to stand by true justice from then on, all the days of his life. Then too he made all the lords who held land from the crown come in and do their service as they ought to do. And many complaints were made to Sir Arthur of great wrongs done since the death of King Uther, of many lands that had been taken from lords, knights, ladies and gentlemen. So King Arthur had the lands given back to those who owned them.

When this was done, and the king had settled all the country around London, then he made Sir Kay seneschal of England; and Sir Baudwin of Britain was made constable; and Sir Ulfius was made chamberlain; and Sir Brastias was made warden, to keep watch on the north from the Trent onwards, for at that time it was for the most part the king's enemies. But within a few years Arthur won all the north, Scotland, and all that were under their rule. Part of Wales also held out against Arthur, but he overcame them all, as he did the rest, through his own noble prowess and that of his knights of the Round Table.
