Chapter 26: How news came to Arthur that King Rience had overcome eleven kings, and how he wanted Arthur's beard to trim his cloak

Meanwhile a messenger came from King Rience of North Wales, and he was king of all Ireland too, and of many islands. And this was his message, greeting King Arthur in this way: saying that King Rience had defeated and overcome eleven kings, and every one of them did him homage, and this was how: they gave him their beards, flayed clean off, as much as there was of them. And so the messenger had come for King Arthur's beard. For King Rience had trimmed a cloak with kings' beards, and there was one place lacking on the cloak; and so he sent for his beard, or else he would invade his lands, and burn and kill, and never stop until he had the head and the beard.

"Well," said Arthur, "you have delivered your message, which is the most insulting and ignorant message that any man ever heard sent to a king. Also, you can see that my beard is still very young to make a trimming out of. But tell your king this: I owe him no homage, and none of my forefathers did; but before long he shall do me homage on both his knees, or else he shall lose his head, on my life. For this is the most shameful message that I ever heard of. I can see your king has never yet met a man of honour. But tell him I will have his head unless he does me homage."

Then the messenger left.

"Now, is there anyone here," said Arthur, "who knows King Rience?"

Then a knight called Naram answered, "Sir, I know the king well. He is an exceedingly good fighting man, as few alive are, and an exceedingly proud man. And sir, have no doubt he will make war on you with a mighty army."

"Well," said Arthur, "I will deal with him before long."
