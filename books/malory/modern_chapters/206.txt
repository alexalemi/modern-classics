Chapter 11: How Sir Tristram met Sir Lamorak de Galis, and how they fought, and afterwards agreed never to fight each other again

"Now, fair knight," he said, "my name is Sir Tristram de Liones."

"Sir," said the other knight, "and my name is Sir Lamorak de Galis."

"Ah, Sir Lamorak," said Sir Tristram, "well met! Now remember the insult you did me by sending the horn to King Mark's court, meaning to have my lady the queen, Isolde the Fair, killed or dishonoured. And so know this," said Sir Tristram, "one of us will die before we part."

"Sir," said Sir Lamorak, "remember that we were together on the Isle of Servage, and at that time you promised me great friendship."

Then Sir Tristram would make no more delay, but laid into Sir Lamorak; and so they fought a long time, until each was weary of the other. Then Sir Tristram said to Sir Lamorak, "In all my life I never met a knight so strong and with such staying power as you. And so," said Sir Tristram, "it would be a pity if either of us should come to harm here."

"Sir," said Sir Lamorak, "for your renown and your name, I want you to have the honour of this battle, and so I will yield to you."

And with that he took his sword by the point to yield himself.

"No," said Sir Tristram, "you shall not do that, for I know very well that you make this offer out of your courtesy, more than from any fear or dread you have of me."

And with that Sir Tristram offered him his sword and said, "Sir Lamorak, as an overcome knight I yield to you, as to a man of the noblest prowess I ever met."

"No," said Sir Lamorak, "I will do you a courtesy. I ask that we swear together that neither of us shall ever fight the other after this day."

And with that Sir Tristram and Sir Lamorak swore that neither of them would ever fight against the other, not for good fortune nor for bad.
