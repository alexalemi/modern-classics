Chapter 34: How Anglides gave the bloodstained doublet to her son Alisander on the day he was made a knight, and the charge that came with it

Then one day Bellangere the Constable came to Anglides and said, "Madam, it is time my lord Alisander was made a knight, for he is an exceedingly strong young man."

"Sir," she said, "I wish he were made a knight; but then I must give him the heaviest charge that ever a sinful mother gave to her child."

"Do as you please," said Bellangere, "and I will give him notice that he is to be made a knight. Now it would be well done for him to be made a knight on Our Lady's Day in Lent."

"So be it," said Anglides, "and I beg you to make ready for it."

So the Constable came to Alisander, and told him that he was to be made a knight on Our Lady's Day in Lent.

"I thank God," said Alisander. "This is the best news that ever came to me."

Then the Constable chose twenty of the sons of the greatest gentlemen, and the best-born men of the country, who were to be made knights on the same day that Alisander was made a knight. So on that same day, when Alisander and his twenty companions were made knights, at the offering of the mass Anglides came to her son and said this: "O fair sweet son, I charge you on my blessing, and by the high order of chivalry that you take on yourself here this day, that you understand what I am going to say, and the charge I am going to lay on you."

With that she pulled out a bloody doublet and a bloody shirt, all stained with old blood. When Alisander saw this he started back and turned pale, and said, "Fair mother, what can this mean?"

"I will tell you, fair son. This was your own father's doublet and shirt, which he wore on the very day that he was killed."

And there she told him why and for what reason, and how for his goodness King Mark killed him with his dagger before her own eyes. "And so this shall be the charge that I give you."
