Chapter 16: How Sir Lionel slew Sir Colgrevance, and how afterwards he meant to slay Sir Bors

Then Colgrevance called out again and again to Sir Bors, "Why will you let me die here for your sake? If it pleases you that I die for you, it will please me all the better, to save a worthy man."

At that word Sir Lionel struck the helm off his head. Then Colgrevance saw that he could not escape; then he said, "Fair sweet Jesus, have mercy on my soul for what I have done wrong; and may the sorrow my heart suffers for doing good, and the charity I would have done here, be a relief of penance to me for my soul's health."

At these words Lionel struck him so hard that he bore him to the ground. Having killed Colgrevance, he ran at his brother like a fiend, and gave him such a stroke that he made him stagger. And Bors, who was full of humility, begged him for God's love to leave off this battle, "For if it happened, fair brother, that I killed you or you me, we would both die in that sin."

"May God never help me if I have any mercy on you, if I can get the upper hand."

Then Bors drew his sword, weeping, and said, "Fair brother, God knows my intent. Ah, fair brother, you have done great evil this day to kill such a holy priest, who never did any wrong. You have also killed a noble knight, and one of our fellows. And you know well that I am not much afraid of you, but I fear the wrath of God; and this is an unnatural war, so may God show a miracle upon us both. Now God have mercy on me, though I defend my life against my brother."

With that Bors lifted his hand and would have struck his brother.
