Chapter 15: How Balin fought King Pellam, and how his sword broke, and how he got a spear with which he struck the Dolorous Stroke

At once all the knights rose from the table to set upon Balin, and King Pellam himself rose up fiercely, and said, "Knight, have you killed my brother? You will die for it before you leave."

"Well," said Balin, "do it yourself, then."

"Yes," said King Pellam, "no man shall fight you but me myself, for the love of my brother."

Then King Pellam caught up a grim weapon in his hand and struck fiercely at Balin. But Balin put his sword between his head and the stroke, and with that his sword burst in pieces. And when Balin was weaponless he ran into a chamber to look for some weapon, and so from chamber to chamber, and he could find no weapon, and King Pellam was always after him. And at last he came into a chamber that was marvellously and richly furnished, with a bed hung with cloth of gold, the richest that could be imagined, and someone lying in it. And beside it stood a table of pure gold, with four pillars of silver holding up the table, and on the table stood a marvellous spear, strangely made. And when Balin saw that spear, he took it in his hand and turned to King Pellam, and struck him exceedingly hard with that spear, so that King Pellam fell down in a faint. And with that the castle's roof and walls broke and fell to the ground, and Balin fell down so that he could not stir hand or foot. And so the greater part of the castle, which had fallen down through that Dolorous Stroke, lay on top of Pellam and Balin for three days.
