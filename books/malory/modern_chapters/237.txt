Chapter 42: How Sir Tristram took the shield with him, and how he slew Morgan le Fay's lover

So then Sir Tristram took his leave of the queen, and took the shield with him. Then came the knight who kept Queen Morgan le Fay as his lover (his name was Sir Hemison), and he made himself ready to follow Sir Tristram.

"Fair friend," said Morgan, "don't ride after that knight, for you will win no honour from him."

"Shame on him, the coward," said Sir Hemison, "for I never knew a good knight to come out of Cornwall, except Sir Tristram de Liones."

"And what if this is he?" she said.

"No, no," he said, "he is with Isolde the Fair, and this is only some foolish knight."

"Alas, my fair friend, you will find him the best knight you ever met, for I know him better than you do."

"For your sake," said Sir Hemison, "I will kill him."

"Ah, fair friend," said the queen, "I am sorry that you will follow that knight, for I am badly afraid for your coming back."

With this the knight rode on his way mad with rage, and he rode after Sir Tristram as fast as if he were being chased by knights. When Sir Tristram heard a knight coming after him so fast, he turned about, and saw a knight coming at him. And when he came near Sir Tristram he cried aloud, "Sir knight, defend yourself against me!"

Then they rushed together as if it were thunder, and Sir Hemison broke his spear on Sir Tristram, but his armour was so good that he could not hurt him. And Sir Tristram struck him harder, and ran him through the body, and he fell over his horse's crupper. Then Sir Tristram turned to do more with his sword, but he saw so much blood running from him that it seemed to him he was likely to die. And so he left him, and came to a fine manor belonging to an old knight, and there Sir Tristram lodged.
