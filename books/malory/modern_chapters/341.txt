Chapter 14: How by a miracle they were both healed when the holy vessel, the Holy Grail, came to them

Then they both grieved beyond measure.

"This will do no good," said Sir Percival.

And then he knelt down and prayed devoutly to Almighty Jesus, for he was one of the best knights in the world at that time, and the one in whom the true faith was strongest. Just then the holy vessel of the Holy Grail came by, with every kind of sweetness and fragrance; but they could not clearly see who carried that vessel, though Sir Percival had a glimpse of the vessel and of the maiden who carried it, for he was a perfectly pure virgin himself. And immediately they were both as whole in skin and limb as they had ever been in their lives. Then they gave thanks to God with great humility.

"O Jesus," said Sir Percival, "what can this mean, that we are healed like this, when just now we were at the point of dying?"

"I know very well," said Sir Ector, "what it is. It is a holy vessel that is carried by a maiden, and in it is part of the holy blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, blessed may he be. But it cannot be seen," said Sir Ector, "except by a perfect man."

"God help me," said Sir Percival, "I saw a damsel, as I thought, all in white, with a vessel in both her hands, and immediately I was whole."

So then they took their horses and their armour, and mended their armour where it was broken as well as they could; and so they mounted their horses, and rode along talking together. And there Sir Ector de Maris told Sir Percival how he had looked for his brother, Sir Lancelot, a long time, and could never hear any news of him: "I have been in many strange adventures on this quest." And so each told the other of their adventures.
