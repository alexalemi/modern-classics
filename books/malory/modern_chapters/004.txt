Chapter 4: Of the death of King Uther Pendragon

Then, within two years, King Uther fell sick of a great illness. And meanwhile his enemies encroached on his lands, and fought a great battle against his men, and killed many of his people.

"Sir," said Merlin, "you cannot lie here as you are doing, for you must go to the field even if you ride in a horse-litter. For you will never get the better of your enemies unless you are there in person, and then you will have the victory."

So it was done as Merlin had planned, and they carried the king out in a horse-litter with a great army against his enemies. And at St. Albans a great army from the North met the king. And that day Sir Ulfius and Sir Brastias did great deeds of arms, and King Uther's men overcame the northern army and killed many people, and put the rest to flight. And then the king returned to London, and rejoiced greatly in his victory. And then he fell desperately sick, so that for three days and three nights he could not speak. At this all the barons grieved greatly, and asked Merlin what would be the best thing to do.

"There is no other remedy," said Merlin, "but God will have His will. But see that all you barons are before King Uther tomorrow, and God and I will make him speak."

So in the morning all the barons came before the king with Merlin. Then Merlin said aloud to King Uther, "Sir, shall your son Arthur be king of this realm after your days, with all that belongs to it?"

Then Uther Pendragon turned over and said, in the hearing of them all, "I give him God's blessing and mine, and bid him pray for my soul, and claim the crown rightfully and honourably, on pain of losing my blessing."

And with that he gave up the ghost, and then he was buried as befitted a king. And the queen, the fair Igraine, grieved greatly, and so did all the barons.
