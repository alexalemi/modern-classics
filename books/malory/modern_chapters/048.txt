Chapter 2: How the Knights of the Round Table were established and their seats blessed by the Bishop of Canterbury

When King Arthur heard of the coming of Guinevere and the hundred knights with the Round Table, then King Arthur rejoiced greatly at her coming and at that rich present, and said openly, "This fair lady is exceedingly welcome to me, for I have loved her long, and so there is nothing so dear to me. And these knights with the Round Table please me more than very great riches."

And in all haste the king had the marriage and the coronation arranged in the most honourable way that could be devised.

"Now, Merlin," said King Arthur, "go and find me fifty knights in all this land who are of the most prowess and honour."

Within a short time Merlin had found knights enough to fill twenty-eight places, but he could find no more. Then the Bishop of Canterbury was fetched, and he blessed the seats with great royal splendour and devotion, and there seated the twenty-eight knights in their places. And when this was done Merlin said, "Fair sirs, you must all rise and come to King Arthur to do him homage; he will be the more willing to support you."

And so they rose and did their homage. And when they had gone, Merlin found on every seat letters of gold that told the names of the knights who had sat there. But two seats were empty.

And so at once young Gawain came and asked the king for a gift.

"Ask," said the king, "and I will grant it you."

"Sir, I ask that you will make me a knight on the same day that you marry fair Guinevere."

"I will do it gladly," said King Arthur, "and do you all the honour I can, for I must by right: you are my nephew, my sister's son."
