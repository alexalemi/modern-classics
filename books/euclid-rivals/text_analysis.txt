EUCLID AND HIS MODERN RIVALS — TRANSLATION STRATEGY
Lewis Carroll (C. L. Dodgson), 1879; second edition 1885
Source: Archive.org, two scans. See source_notes.txt for the assessment
and for what prep already fixes.


1. WHAT THE BOOK IS

A farce in four Acts about geometry textbooks, and it is much funnier
than that sounds. Minos, a college examiner, sits up at midnight marking
papers, his hair "radiating in all directions, like the second Corollary
of Euc. I. 32". The ghost of Euclid appears. Then Herr Niemand — Mr
Nobody, "the phantom of a German professor" — arrives to speak on behalf
of each modern rival textbook in turn, thirteen of them, and is
demolished one at a time with perfectly straight-faced courtesy.

It is a real argument conducted as a joke: what is a proof FOR, what may
be assumed, and what does a sequence of theorems owe the beginner who
has to learn it. Carroll is defending Euclid not because he is
old but because his order is honest about what depends on what.


2. THE VOICE, AND THE THING TO PROTECT

Two registers, and the comedy lives in the gap between them.

  - MINOS is exhausted, sardonic and unfailingly polite. He says
    "refreshingly cool!" about an undergraduate assuming the proposition
    he is meant to be proving, and gives a fallacy "that peculiar mark
    which cricketers call a duck's egg, and thermometers zero."
  - NIEMAND is earnest, German, and doomed. He defends every manual with
    complete good faith and is destroyed by having his own words read
    back to him.

Keep the courtesy. The book is never sneering: Minos apologises while
demolishing. That politeness IS the joke, and flattening it into modern
sarcasm would kill the book.

STAGE DIRECTIONS ARE PART OF THE COMEDY and must be kept — the knocking
heard, the pages turned over, the ghost who "vanishes with a sigh". They
are italic in the printed book; prep keeps them as their own paragraphs.

SPEAKER TAGS ARE TITLE-CASE, NEVER ALL-CAPS (the galileo trap:
assemble.py reserves all-caps lines for section headings). prep already
writes them out in full — "Minos.", "Euclid.", "Niemand." — rather than
Carroll's abbreviations, since a modern reader has no reason to know
that "Nie." is a person.


3. WHAT PREP HAS ALREADY DONE, AND WHAT IT DELIBERATELY HAS NOT

DONE: all 1,068 speaker tags resolved structurally (speakers.py); the
1885 OCR repaired against the 1879 where a dictionary says the other
reading is better and no numeral changes (repair.py); running heads,
signature marks and the duplicated opening page removed; the Appendices
sent to reference/appendices.txt as a crib.

NOT DONE, AND ON PURPOSE — THE GEOMETRICAL LABELS ARE STILL AS OCR'D.
repair.py refuses to touch any span containing two or more capitals in a
row, because a dictionary will always prefer a word to a label and the
result is catastrophic: "EF" became "IF", "EGB" became "12GB", and
"AGH, GHD," became "AGE, GET),". Those letters name points on a printed
diagram. The guard is right, but it means the labels arrive damaged —
"A Gil and EGB", "GIID", "thin;^", "Qgg" — and they can only be repaired
BY READING THE SENTENCE AGAINST ITS FIGURE. Do that. A wrong label is
the exact analogue of tyndall/'s figure-id collisions: it reads
perfectly and it is wrong.


4. THE THINGS THAT WILL STILL BE WRONG

- REAL-WORD OCR ERRORS. "lie" for "he", "Kerens" for "Here's", "Koman"
  for "Roman" (that one is fixed; the others are not). A dictionary
  cannot see these, and neither can any check in this repo. They are
  found by reading, which is what the translation pass is.
- SCENE NUMBERING. The Acts are reliable (they are set in 19-point and
  prep keys on the size). The Scene headings are not all present in the
  scan, so prep numbers them sequentially as it meets them, and the
  result does not always match the book. THE AUTHORITY IS THE BOOK'S OWN
  "ARGUMENT OF DRAMA" in the front matter, which lists every Act, Scene
  and § with its subject; check the manifest titles against it before
  assembling.
- THE 19 TABLES and 20 PLATES are marked, not built. Every table must be
  checked against the page image (thompson/'s rule: normalise() once ate
  three rows out of a table and moved the word ratio by nothing), and
  the plates must be cut from the JP2 scans with replate.py's method —
  grow each side until it reaches whitespace, then map darkness to alpha
  so the paper goes and the ink survives.


5. THE APPENDICES ARE A CRIB, NOT A TRANSLATION

Alex's ruling, and the right one. They are ~11,000 words of dense
cross-reference tables — lists of Euclid's propositions against a dozen
rival manuals, by section and page. They are apparatus for a Victorian
schoolmaster checking his textbook, not reading matter, and no modern
reader will follow a reference to page 262 of Cuthbertson. They sit in
reference/appendices.txt and are drawn on only where the body text
depends on one (the bunyan/ treatment of Offor's commentator notes).


6. RATIO

Expect close to 1:1, as with the other English-to-English volumes; verify
with --min-ratio 0.85 --max-ratio 1.3. But note that the source is OCR:
the word counts include its damage, so the ratio is a weaker check here
than usual and the numeric-token diff matters more.
