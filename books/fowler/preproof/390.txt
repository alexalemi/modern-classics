4. Number & person after neither ee.nor. If both subjects are singular & in the third person, the onl need is to remember that the ver must be singular & not plural. This is often forgotten; the OED quotes, from Johnson, Neither search nor labour are necessary, &, from Ruskin, Neither painting nor fighting feed men, where is & feeds are undoubtealy required. The right course is not to indulge in bad grammar ourselves & then plead that better men

+ like Johnson & Ruskin have done it before us, but to follow what is now the accepted as well as the logical rule. Complications occur when, owing to a difference in number or person between the subject of the netther member & that of the nor member, the same verbform or pronoun or possessive adjective does not fit both: Neither you nor I (was?, were?) chosen; Neither you nor I (is?, am?, are?) the right person; Neither eyes nor nose (does its?, do their?) work; Neither employer nor hands will say what (they want?, he wants?). The wise man, in writing, evades these problems by rejecting all the alternatives—any of which may set up friction between him & his reader—& putting the thing in some other shape; & in speaking, which does not allow time for paraphrase, he takes risks with equanimity & says what instinct dictates. But, as instinct is directed largely by habit, it is well to eschew habitually the clearly wrong forms (such as Neither chapter nor verse are given) & the clearly provocative ones (such as Neither husband nor wife ts competent to act without his consort).
