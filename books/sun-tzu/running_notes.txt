DECISIONS LOG
---------------------------------------------------------------------

--- 001: FIGURES STAY FIGURES IN A NUMERICAL NOTE. Tu Mu's gloss turns
on 3/10 against 7/10, and spelling both out (which I did first) loses
the contrast the note exists for. The numeric check caught it.

--- 004: THE SHIP VICTORY. I set her name in italics, the modern
convention; Giles used quotation marks. Reverted to his — the emphasis
check compares span CONTENT, and an added span is as much a drift as a
dropped one.

--- 006: SCHOLARLY LOCATORS ARE DATA. I dropped "chap. 1 ad init.",
"year 10, § 1" and "(chap. 4)" from citations, and spelled "ch. 1" out
as "the first chapter". All restored. This is the burke lesson exactly:
there the numeric diff earned its keep by catching a dropped "(see
pp. 222, 241)", and it earned it again here.

--- 007 AND 009: GILES'S TWO ETHNIC GENERALISATIONS, DECIDED
DIFFERENTLY FROM EACH OTHER, and the split is principled.
  - Chapter Ten's opening note reads "a strange lack of logical
    perception is shown in the Chinaman's unquestioning acceptance of
    glaring cross-divisions". THE CLAIM STANDS as his — he is judging
    the Chinese commentators' logic, that judgement is part of his
    commentary's character, and this edition's whole framing is that we
    are reading an Edwardian Englishman. But the NOUN goes: "Chinese
    writers" names the same people and carries no part of the claim, so
    the reader loses no information and gains no false one. This is the
    mill "planters and negroes" precedent exactly.
  - Chapter Eight's "some of which would occur only to the Oriental
    mind" is KEPT ENTIRE. Here the dated category IS the claim — he is
    asserting that these particular stratagems are characteristically
    Eastern — and replacing the term would put a different claim in his
    mouth. That is the Verne rule, and it is why the two are not
    handled alike: swap a slur WORD for the neutral name of the same
    people; never swap out the CLAIM.
No note anywhere in the volume for either.

--- 010 AND 011: GILES'S PAGE REFERENCES TO HIS OWN EDITION. There are
five, and they are meaningless in a reflowable page. Three RESOLVE and
were rewritten to point where the reader can actually go: "(See p. 57.)"
is Chao She's march, Chapter Seven, verse 4, note; "p. 90" is T'ien Tan
with the oxen, Chapter Nine, verse 24, note; "(See p. 132.)" is Pan
Ch'ao before Yarkand, Chapter Eleven, verse 36, note. ONE DOES NOT: "one
of his most brilliant battles, already alluded to on p. 28" points into
Giles's own introduction, which this edition does not carry, so the
locator is dropped -- and the battle is then described in full in the
same sentence, which is why nothing is lost. All five are entered in
check.py's NUM_DROPPED with the reason beside them; the counted numeric
diff caught every one of them, which is what that check is for.

--- 011: THE BARBARIANS OF THE HOU HAN SHU ARE KEPT. Giles quotes the
history at length and its Chinese court calls the Hsiung-nu "the
Northern barbarians"; Pan Ch'ao uses the word again in his own speech in
chapter Twelve. This is the Verne rule and not the nights "blackamoor"
case: the word is not a slur standing in for a people already named, it
is the Chinese state's political category for everyone outside it, and
the whole point of the anecdote is a small kingdom choosing which of two
powers to submit to. Giles's own bracketed gloss ("the mortal enemies of
the Chinese") stays with it.

--- 011: *débandade* GOES, and it is the one emphasis span in the book
deliberately dropped. Giles italicises transliterated Chinese and real
technical terms, and those are locked; this is a French word he reached
for where English has one, inside a Chinese general's reported speech,
and modern English never took it up. A dated WORD, not a dated claim.
Rendered "stampede", and registered in check.py's EMPH_DELTA so that the
parity check still guards every other span.

--- 012 AND 013: FOUR TRANSMISSION TYPOS in Gutenberg's transcription,
each of which reads perfectly and says the wrong thing, so none is
protected by the Verne rule: "kits them together" for KNITS (the San
Lueh on how a prince holds an army), "Sun Tzu's agreement is certainly
ingenious" for ARGUMENT, "reliance on spies, while production of great
results" for PRODUCTIVE, and "Lo Shang ... march out all his best
troops" for MARCHED. Corrected silently.
