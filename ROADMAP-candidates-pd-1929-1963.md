# Candidates: US books of 1931–1963 that were registered but not renewed

Made 2026-10-05 with `copyright_check.py` (see its docstring for sources and
caveats). Works published up to 1930 are already public domain in the US by
age; works published from 1964 on were renewed automatically. Between those
dates, a US book whose copyright was registered and **not renewed in its
28th year** is in the public domain in the US. This list is the search for
such books that would suit the collection.

**Method.** I drew 393 titles from memory (popular science, history,
philosophy, economics, law, essays, craft, magic and games, notable
translations, and a few literary works) and ran every one through the tool.
Every result below was checked by the tool, and every NOT RENEWED result
was also checked by hand: a phrase search of each title across *all* renewal
years 1950–2001 (including the full-text field), plus a search by author
name in the renewal window. The totals were:

| Verdict | Titles |
|---|---|
| RENEWED (ruled out) | 315 |
| NOT RENEWED (likely US public domain) | 61 |
| NOT FOUND IN REGISTRATIONS (unresolved) | 15 |
| Out of range (1929–30, already PD) | 2 |

Most famous titles were renewed. Publishers renewed what was selling, so the
books that lapsed are mostly second-tier titles, first editions that a later
revised edition replaced, or books from small or institutional publishers.

**What "NOT RENEWED" means here.** It means the NYPL transcriptions show a
registration and no matching renewal. That makes the book **likely** US
public domain. It is not a clearance. Before any work starts, a human must
still confirm:

1. **The edition.** Many entries below are first editions whose later revised
   editions *were* renewed (marked "later ed. renewed"). Only the text of the
   unrenewed edition is free, so the scan has to be of that printing.
2. **The printed notice.** The notice should match the registration, with no
   earlier publication (for example a magazine serialization) that was renewed
   separately.
3. **Foreign status (URAA).** A work first published outside the US was
   restored on 1 Jan 1996 if it was still protected at home, even if it was
   never renewed. A foreign author is fine when the book was *first*
   published in the US, but that has to be established for each book.
4. **Separately owned parts.** Forewords, illustrations, photographs and
   translations can each have their own owner.
5. **The Copyright Office's own records.** These are authoritative and the
   transcriptions are not. An OCR error can hide a renewal, so for the top
   picks, run a search at publicrecords.copyright.gov (1978+) or check the
   CCE scans on Archive.org.

Renewal window searched: the tool looks for a renewal **by original
registration number in every renewal year from 1950 to 2001**, and **by
fuzzy title and author in registration year +26 to +29**. The statutory
window is +27/+28. "Window" in the tables below is the statutory window.
