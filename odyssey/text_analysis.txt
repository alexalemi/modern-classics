HOMER'S ODYSSEY -- TRANSLATION STRATEGY
Scholarly modernization of a canonical text, from the Greek.


1. WHAT THIS EDITION IS, SAID PLAINLY

From the GREEK: Perseus' canonical-greekLit, the text of the 1919 Loeb
(Allen), 24 books and 12,107 lines.  A. T. Murray's 1919 Loeb English sits
under reference/ as a per-book crib with the Greek line numbers marked in
it, and is a crib only -- it is never the thing being modernised.  That
distinction matters and is the opposite of sun-tzu/, where nobody here
reads classical Chinese and the page says so in the subtitle.  Here the
subtitle can say "from the Greek" because it is true.

RETOLD AS PROSE (Alex's ruling).  Say so; do not imply a verse fidelity
the edition does not have.  Homer's line is a six-beat hexameter and there
is no rhyme to abandon, so verse was possible -- it was declined in favour
of what a modern reader of a novel-length narrative actually wants.

NO ORIGINAL-TEXT COMPANION PAGE.  Perseus' markup is CC BY-SA 4.0 and
Tufts asserts copyright over the digital library; the 1919 text underneath
is public domain by age.  Our retelling is new writing and is unaffected,
but shipping the Greek as a companion edition would be redistributing
their marked-up text.  The Comedy shipped no companion page either.


2. THE COST OF PROSE, AND WHAT PAYS IT BACK

The three cantiche were guarded by exact line and tercet parity, which is
what caught the only real defect in the Paradiso.  Prose has no lines, so
that check is gone and something has to stand in its place.

WHAT STANDS IN ITS PLACE IS SPEECH PARITY.  Perseus tags every speech in
the poem <q>: 674 of them, 570 spoken and 104 quoted inside another
speech.  The translation renders the first in double quotes and the second
in single quotes -- the convention ovid/ already uses -- so check.py can
count both, per file, against what prep.py recorded.

A LOST SPEECH BOUNDARY IS THIS BOOK'S SILENT SUMMARISATION.  Two speeches
welded into one, or a speech dissolved into narration, leaves every word
present, in order, reading perfectly, with the word ratio unmoved.  It is
the euclid-rivals defect (a misattributed speech reads perfectly and
argues the opposite) and the cellini one (a dropped chapter label welds
two chapters and moves nothing).

AND IT IS WEAKER THAN LINE PARITY, WHICH MUST BE SAID.  It can see a
speech that vanished; it cannot see four verses of narration that did.
The word ratio is the only guard on those, which is why the bounds are
tight (1.30-1.80, against Murray's 1.53) and why a file under 1.30 is to
be read rather than explained.


3. THE FOUR BOOKS WHERE THE MARKUP CANNOT BE TRUSTED

Books 9-12 are Odysseus telling his own story to the Phaeacians -- one
speech of 4,145 words in book 10 alone.  THE OVID RULE GOVERNS: a long
embedded tale renders as PRIMARY NARRATION, with no enclosing quotation
marks round the whole thing, and speeches inside it are double-quoted like
any other.  Nothing else is readable.

But Perseus' nesting is inconsistent across exactly these four books:

   book  9   one top-level <q>, lines 2-566            consistent
   book 10   one top-level <q>, lines 1-574            consistent
   book 11   seven: the tale, the Phaeacian intermezzo
             at 336-376, then the tale resumed         consistent
   book 12   ELEVEN, and FLATTENED -- Circe at 116-141
             and the Sirens at 184-191 are speeches
             inside the tale but are tagged top-level,
             and 352-453 opens "ὣς ἔφατʼ Εὐρύλοχος",
             which is narration and not a speech

So for those four files the counts are hand-calibrated against the Greek
at translation time, and until they are, check.py PRINTS that it is not
checking them.  A check that silently passes on four of twenty-five files
is worse than none, because it is counted as coverage (the nights lesson).


4. THE CRIB IS 1919 AND THE SWEEP IS AIMED AT YOU

Murray writes "thou hast", "thee", "thy", "aye", "verily", "sore
afflicted", "wroth".  With him open beside you all day the drift is into
HIS English -- exactly what happened in cellini/, where the archaism sweep
fired eight times across the book on one word ("whereupon"), Symonds'
habit and nobody's speech, and every firing was mine.  check.py sweeps for
the thou-family and its neighbours.  Exemptions go in by EXACT PHRASE and
never by loosening the pattern (the grimm rule), and ARCHAIC_OK is empty
and should stay that way.


5. VOICE

It is an adventure story told aloud, and it was composed to be heard.
Keep it moving.  The register is plain, concrete and fast -- Homer's
similes are about laundry, olive trees, a farmer's dog, an octopus pulled
off a rock -- and the poem is far less solemn than its reputation.

FOUR REGISTERS, KEPT APART:
  - TELEMACHUS' BOOKS (1-4): a boy of twenty who has never been listened
    to, learning to speak in public and doing it badly at first.  His
    nerves are the point; do not make him competent too early.
  - THE PHAEACIAN BOOKS (5-8, 13): court comedy and hospitality, with
    Nausicaa's episode the most charming thing in the poem and Odysseus
    at his most calculating inside it.
  - THE APOLOGUE (9-12): a man telling adventure stories to an audience
    he is working.  He is a superb and self-serving narrator; keep the
    performance audible.
  - ITHACA (13-24): patient, tense, domestic and cruel by turns.  The
    disguised beggar sequence is the best sustained writing in the poem
    and depends on the reader knowing what the characters do not.

FORMULAS ARE THE POEM'S MACHINERY AND ARE LOCKED.  "Rosy-fingered dawn",
"wine-dark sea", "much-enduring Odysseus", "grey-eyed Athena",
"circumspect Penelope", "winged words".  They repeat because the form is
oral, and a reader meets them a hundred times and must recognise each one
(the grimm Dummling rule and the nights frame-formula rule).  Lock them in
running_notes.txt on the first file that uses them and never vary them for
elegance.  Do NOT, however, print every epithet every time the Greek has
one: English prose cannot carry that density.  The rule is that a given
epithet always renders the SAME WAY when it renders at all.

THE VERNE RULE, and this poem needs it stated in advance:
  - The slaughter of the suitors, and then the hanging of the twelve
    serving women, in full and unsoftened.  Telemachus calls it a clean
    death being too good for them, and the text does not editorialise.
    Neither do we, and there is no note.
  - Slavery is everywhere in this household and is simply the world of the
    poem: Eumaeus was a stolen prince, Eurycleia was bought.  Render the
    relationships exactly, do not soften "slave" into "servant" where the
    Greek is explicit, and add nothing.
  - The gods behave badly, Athena's favouritism is open, and Odysseus lies
    constantly, including to his father.  Keep all of it.
  - Odysseus and Calypso: he sleeps with her nightly for seven years while
    weeping on the shore by day for home.  Both halves stand.


6. NAMES AND THINGS

Familiar English forms: Odysseus (never Ulysses), Penelope, Telemachus,
Athena, Circe, Calypso, Nausicaa, Alcinous, Eumaeus, Laertes, Antinous,
Eurymachus, Polyphemus, Poseidon, Nestor, Menelaus, Helen.
Patronymics resolve to the plain name where they are just a name, and are
kept where Homer is making a point of the parentage.
Cult titles and periphrasis resolve as in ovid/ ("the Earth-shaker" ->
Poseidon), EXCEPT where the periphrasis is doing work in the sentence.
Keep the Greek measures and objects as they are; do not modernise a
"talent of gold" into a weight or a "krater" into a bowl-shaped nothing.
