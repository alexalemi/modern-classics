LOCKED FORMULAS -- set in Book One, never varied for elegance

These repeat because the poem is oral, and a reader meets each of them
dozens of times.  A given epithet always renders the SAME WAY when it
renders at all (the grimm Dummling rule).  It need not render every time
the Greek has one -- English prose cannot carry that density -- but it
may never render two ways.

VOCABULARY DECISIONS
  - "Achaeans", "Argives", "Danaans" all kept as Homer uses them; they
    are the same people under three names and the variation is his.
  - δμῳαί / δμῶες are SLAVES and are called slaves.  Murray writes
    "handmaids" and "maid-servants" throughout; that is his 1919
    politeness, not Homer's word, and softening it would hide what the
    household is.  See the Verne-rule note in text_analysis.
  - Greek measures and objects stay: a talent of gold, a hecatomb, a
    krater.  Do not convert or generalise them away.
  - "the immortals", "the blessed gods", "the gods who live for ever"
    all occur; keep the variation, it is in the Greek.


THE CRIB IS 1919 AND THE SWEEP IS AIMED AT YOU

Murray writes thou/thee/thy, "aye", "verily", "wroth", "sore", "haply",
"I ween", "methinks".  Working with him open all day pulls the prose
towards him -- the cellini pattern exactly, where the archaism sweep
fired eight times on one word of Symonds' and every firing was mine.
check.py sweeps for the whole family.  ARCHAIC_OK is EMPTY and should
stay empty: exemptions go in by exact phrase or not at all (grimm).


SPEECH PARITY IS THE CHECK THAT MATTERS

Prose has no line count, so the guard against silent loss is the number
of speeches, counted per file against Perseus' <q> markup.  Two things
it catches, both of which read perfectly and move the word ratio by
almost nothing -- verified on Book One by reintroducing each defect and
watching the check fail (the boethius rule):
  - two speeches WELDED into one, by dropping a close-and-reopen;
  - a speech DISSOLVED into narration ("she told him not to wait").
CONVENTION, and check.py depends on it:
  - a speech is in double quotes;
  - a speech that runs over a paragraph break REOPENS its quote at the
    new paragraph and closes only at the end;
  - a speech quoted inside another speech is in single quotes;
  - books 9-12 are Odysseus' own narration and carry NO enclosing
    quotes at all (the ovid rule) -- see text_analysis section 3, and
    note that check.py cannot verify those four files until their
    counts are hand-calibrated, and says so when it runs.


PER-BOOK NOTES

Book 1 (000.txt) -- 3,175 Greek words -> 4,624 English, ratio 1.46, 20
  speeches.  Voice set here.  Telemachus is twenty and has never been
  listened to; his first speech to the suitors is brave and slightly
  too loud, and Antinous mocks him for exactly that, so the bravado has
  to be audible or the reply does not land.  Athena-as-Mentes is brisk
  and practical.  Zeus opens the poem complaining that mortals blame
  the gods for what they bring on themselves -- keep it conversational,
  it is an old man's grievance, not a pronouncement.
  Eurycleia is bought "for the price of twenty oxen" and Laertes never
  slept with her because he feared his wife's anger: rendered flat and
  entire, no softening (the Verne rule).
