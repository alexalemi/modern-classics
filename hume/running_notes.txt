THE GOVERNING RULE OF THIS BOOK

IF A SENTENCE IS ALREADY CLEAR, LEAVE IT ALONE.

Hume's archaism score is 0.47 per 1,000 words. He is a great stylist and
most of what he wrote needs no help. The work is concentrated in:
  - the section-opening throat-clearing (usually deletable outright);
  - the periodic sentences that set up a distinction before making it;
  - the technical vocabulary, which is glossed once and then used.
Everything else is left standing. A file in which every sentence has
been rewritten has been damaged, however defensible each rewrite is.

THE ITALICS

202 *marker* spans came through prep from the source's <em>. They are
argument, not decoration — the terms being defined, the propositions
under examination, the two definitions of cause. KEEP EVERY ONE, on the
same words. If a rewrite would break an italicised run in half, do not
do the rewrite. DO NOT ADD emphasis Hume did not have.

This is the first book that depends on the emphasis rendering, so it is
also the first place a bug in it would show. The pipeline is otherwise
markup-free: no other asterisks or underscores, no all-caps lines, tab
indent only for verse or a table.

STRUCTURE — CHECK THE MANIFEST BEFORE WRITING ANY FILE

Section Eight is THREE files. Sections Four, Five, Seven, Ten, Eleven
and Twelve are TWO. Sections One, Two, Three, Six and Nine are one each.
A multi-part file is: heading line, "(Part n of k)", blank, body. Parts
2+ never re-introduce the section; non-final parts never conclude it.
This is the quixote trap and it is invisible to verify.py — a wrong
heading on a part-2 file is silently swallowed by strip_front.

Hume's own subdivisions are already in the files as "Part One" / "Part
Two" on their own lines. WORD FORM, never "Part I:" — the descartes
trap. Leave them exactly as prep wrote them.


THE VERNE RULE, and the places it bites

  Section Ten (miracles) entire, including the remarks on the credulity
  of ignorant and barbarous nations — load-bearing in the argument about
  testimony, which is why they cannot be quietly dropped. Section Eleven
  keeps Hume's frame of a friend arguing in Epicurus' person. Section
  Eight's compatibilism including the passages tracing a criminal's sins
  to God. No notes, no hedges, no wink anywhere in the book.

SENSITIVE PASSAGE — SECTION TWO (decided at file 001)

Source: "A Laplander or Negro has no notion of the relish of wine."
Rendered: "A Laplander or an African has no notion of the taste of
wine."

THE WORD IS CUT AND THE EXAMPLE IS KEPT ENTIRE. This is NOT the Verne
rule and the distinction matters, because the Verne rule governs
almost everything else in this book. The Verne rule protects a dated
CLAIM an author makes as part of his subject — Section Ten's remarks
about the credulity of barbarous nations are load-bearing in the
argument about testimony and therefore stand untouched, as does
everything in Sections Eight and Eleven.

Here Hume's subject is the origin of ideas, and his claim is that a
person who has never tasted wine cannot imagine the taste. He needs two
peoples who live where no vines grow; the argument is about the absence
of an EXPERIENCE and says nothing whatever about the people. So the
noun carries no part of the argument, which is exactly the test: the
reader loses no information and gains no false one. It is the same
class as "the vulgar" -> "ordinary people", a dated WORD in a book
whose dated CLAIMS are all preserved.

Precedent: the nights/ Aladdin ruling (keep the character, cut the
libel) and the bunyan/ Flatterer (a phrase that in modern English names
a person's race, where the original named no race at all). Compare the
nights/ decision to KEEP "the Land of the Blacks", which is real
geography and whose deletion would cost the reader something. Nothing
is lost here.

"Laplander" is kept. It is a place-term rather than a slur, it is the
parallel Hume built the sentence on, and replacing both halves would
rewrite the sentence rather than correct a word in it.

NOTE FOR SECTION TEN: Hume's notorious footnote on race is in "Of
National Characters", a different essay, and is NOT in this book. Do
not import it, and do not let it colour the handling of Section Ten,
which must be rendered on its own terms.
