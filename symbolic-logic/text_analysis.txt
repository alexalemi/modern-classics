SYMBOLIC LOGIC, PART I — TRANSLATION STRATEGY
Lewis Carroll (Charles Dodgson), fourth edition, 1896
Source: Project Gutenberg #28696 (the illustrated HTML edition)


1. THE DECISION THAT GOVERNS EVERYTHING ELSE

KEEP CARROLL'S TERMINOLOGY. All of it.

This was the one question that had to be settled before a line was
written, because it is not the usual case. Elsewhere in this project the
rule is that dated CLAIMS are kept as the author's and dated WORDS are
modernised silently — Faraday reasons with "carbonic acid" so we keep it
and gloss the modern name once; Fleming's "carbonic oxide" becomes carbon
monoxide without comment. That rule does not reach this book, because
here THE WORDS ARE THE SYSTEM. "Retinend" and "Eliminand" are not old
names for something logic now calls another thing; they are parts Carroll
built, and the machine does not run without them.

So the work here is on SYNTAX AND SIGNPOSTING, not vocabulary. Unstack
his sentences, cut the throat-clearing, make the steps of an argument
visible — and leave his nouns exactly where he put them.

LOCKED, and pinned in must_contain.txt:

  Proposition            Premiss / Premisses  (HIS spelling — never
  Proposition of Existence                     "premise/premises")
  Proposition of Relation
  Universe of Discourse, and his abbreviation "Univ."
  Subject, Predicate, Copula
  Entity, Nullity
  Retinend, Eliminand / Eliminands
  Syllogism, Sorites (plural Soriteses, which is also his)
  Fallacy
  Biliteral Diagram, Triliteral Diagram
  Cell, Compartment, Half, Counter
  North, South, East, West, Inner, Outer

That last row matters more than it looks. Carroll's diagrams are squares
with a geography, and he reasons in it out loud — "there is at least one
Thing in the Inner portion of the North Half". Those are coordinates, not
decoration. Do not translate them into "top left" or anything else.

WHERE A TERM IS GENUINELY OPAQUE, gloss it ONCE, at first use, inside his
own sentence — never a footnote, never a second time. A reader who has
been told once what a Retinend is can read the remaining two hundred
pages; a reader reminded every chapter will stop.


2. WHAT ACTUALLY BLOCKS A MODERN READER

Not the vocabulary, once it is glossed. Three other things:

  - THE SENTENCES. Carroll writes long, heavily-subordinated periods with
    the operative clause buried in the middle. This is where nearly all
    the work is.
  - THE CAPITALISATION. He capitalises every technical noun, which to a
    modern eye reads as shouting. KEEP IT. It is how he marks a term of
    art, and stripping it makes his prose harder, not easier, because you
    can no longer see where a definition is being used.
  - HIS ADDRESS TO THE READER. He is funny, and constantly aside to the
    student — "the Reader is entreated to...". Keep the jokes. This is
    the same man who wrote Alice, and the humour is the reason a
    fourteen-year-old could get through it.

(A NOTE ON A LINE THAT IS NOT HERE: the "hard WORDS, not hard IDEAS"
sentence that states this project's thesis so exactly belongs to
Silvanus Thompson, in thompson/, not to Carroll. Do not go looking for
it in this book and do not import it. What Carroll offers instead is
"To Learners", which is better for being practical: begin at the
beginning, do not dip, do not go on until you understand, and work every
example. That address is the tone of the whole book and must keep its
jokes — including the digression about why the rich uncle is always in
India.)


3. THE DIAGRAMS ARE THE NOTATION

314 of them, and a reader who loses them loses the book. Each arrives as
[Figure N: caption] carrying Carroll's own alt text as the caption seed.
MODERNISE that caption, keeping his phrasing as the first clause and
extending it with what the diagram actually shows — the caption is also
the epub's alt text, and for this book that means it is the only thing a
reader using a screen reader gets.

A caption here must say WHICH CELLS ARE MARKED AND WITH WHAT. "Diagram
representing all x are y" is a start, not a caption.


4. TABLES, ASIDES, CROSS-REFERENCES

108 tables arrive as indented blocks with cells joined by " | ". Keep
them as blocks; do not turn a table into prose. CHECK EVERY TABLE THAT
CLAIMS TO BE COMPUTED — the fleming rule, and A Tangled Tale has just
shown what happens when a transcription silently corrupts an author's
own arithmetic.

130 bracketed asides are Carroll teaching in a lower voice. Keep them
bracketed and keep them distinct from the running argument.

509 print-page cross-references have ALREADY been rewritten by prep.py to
name the section they point at. Do not add page numbers back.


5. RATIO

Expect close to 1:1, as with the other English-to-English volumes;
verify with --min-ratio 0.85 --max-ratio 1.3. Unstacking a period
expands; keeping every technical term contracts nothing. The Examples,
Answers and Solutions books are long lists and will sit lowest.
