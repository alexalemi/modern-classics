# Passages that must survive near-verbatim into modern_chapters/.
#
# CHECKED AGAINST THE PREPARED TEXT, not recalled (the epictetus rule),
# and this book punishes memory harder than most: "swift as the wind",
# "the victorious warrior wins first and then goes to war" and "the
# sheathed sword" are all famous Sun Tzu — and none of them is in Giles.
# They belong to later translations. A pin written from memory here
# could only be satisfied by a translation that had drifted to somebody
# else's wording.
#
# Most are pinned UNCHANGED. Giles's aphorisms are already short and
# already modern; they are the reason the book is quoted, and the hume
# rule applies to them exactly (if a sentence is already clear, leave it
# alone). What needs work in this book is the CONNECTIVE prose and the
# commentary, not the maxims.

# --- Chapter One ------------------------------------------------------
All warfare is based on deception
Attack him where he is unprepared
Let your plans be dark and impenetrable as night

# --- Chapter Two ------------------------------------------------------
# his case against long wars, and the sentence the whole chapter is for
There is no instance of a country having benefited from prolonged warfare

# --- Chapter Three: the most quoted argument in the book --------------
supreme excellence consists in breaking the enemy's resistance without fighting
He will win who knows when to fight and when not to fight
# the ending, pinned in full because it is the passage everyone knows
If you know the enemy and know yourself, you need not fear the result of a hundred battles

# --- Chapter Six ------------------------------------------------------
# the water figure, which is the book's governing image
Water shapes its course according to the nature of the ground
