DON QUIXOTE — RUNNING NOTES
===========================
The consistency ledger. Everything here is settled; do not re-decide it.
Strategy and register live in text_analysis.txt.

FILE FORMAT
-----------
Line 1 of every modern file is the title, exactly as it stands in
manifest.json:
    Chapter 12: Of What a Goatherd Told Those Who Were With Don Quixote
For a split chapter, line 2 is "(Part 2 of 3)". Front-matter files carry
their English title alone.

CHAPTER TITLES live in titles.json, keyed "1-1" .. "2-74" for chapters and
by the Spanish heading for front matter. prep.py reads it, so the manifest
and the file agree and cannot drift. WRITE THE TITLE IN BOTH PLACES.
The titles are jokes. Translate them with the same relish as the text.

VERSE is tab-indented, one line per line, as everywhere else in this
project. The source marks none of it; prep detects it by line length, so
89 blocks arrive already indented. Keep the stanza breaks — the source runs
whole poems together and breaking them up is an improvement, so verse-block
counts will NOT match between source and translation and are not a check.

LOCKED VOCABULARY
-----------------
ínsula          -> "isle", ALWAYS, never "island". Cervantes has Quixote and
                   Sancho say ínsula where ordinary Spanish says isla; it is
                   a chivalric affectation and half the comedy of Sancho's
                   governorship in Part Two depends on his using a word he
                   does not quite understand. "Isle" carries the same slight
                   archaic strangeness in English. Ormsby flattens it to
                   "island" and the joke goes.
vuestra merced  -> "your grace" in Quixote's mouth and in formal speech;
                   Sancho's slurred "vuesa merced" gets the same, since his
                   comedy is in the proverbs, not here.
caballero andante -> "knight errant", never "wandering knight".
hidalgo         -> "gentleman" (the first sentence). Not "hidalgo": the
                   whole point is that he is minor gentry with a lance and
                   no money, which "gentleman" carries and the loanword does
                   not.
escudero        -> "squire".
duelos y quebrantos -> "'griefs and groans' — bacon and eggs". The Spanish
                   name is a joke and the dish needs explaining; three words
                   does both, and no footnote is possible.
Cide Hamete Benengeli — keep. The narrator's Arab "author" is load-bearing.

NAMES. Familiar English forms where they exist: Amadís of Gaul, Palmerín of
England, the Knight of Phoebus, Roland (for Roldán) but Orlando where
Cervantes means the Italian poem's hero, Ganelon, Trebizond, Bucephalus.
Spanish names keep their accents: Rocinante, Dulcinea, Sansón Carrasco,
Cardenio, Dorotea, Zoraida.

LATIN QUOTATIONS are kept verbatim, in the form Cervantes printed them, with
a plain English rendering immediately after in square brackets. The joke in
the Prologue is that the tags are banalities dressed as learning, and that
only lands if the reader can see what they say.

TEXTUAL DECISIONS
-----------------
"por Hepila famosa" (Prologue-adjacent, chapter 1, of Amadís adding his
country's name) is a 1605 misprint and means nothing. Read with the modern
editions as "por hacerla famosa" and translated "in order to make it
famous". Recorded here because it is an emendation, not a translation.

THE PRINTING PAPERWORK is not translated and is in reference/licences.txt —
Tasa, errata certificates, royal Privilegio, three Aprobaciones, 2,782
words. Both prologues and both dedications ARE translated.

REGISTER — THE ONE THING THAT MATTERS MOST
------------------------------------------
Don Quixote and Sancho must not sound alike. Quixote is formal, courtly and
elaborate, and genuinely eloquent when he is on a subject he understands;
his language goes absurd only in the chivalric fits, and there the absurdity
must read AS absurdity. Sancho is plain, fast, physical and shrewd. If both
come out in the same modern middle register, the book stops working, and
that is exactly what the Victorian translations did to it.

SANCHO'S PROVERBS: translate for the FUNCTION. Use a real English proverb
where one does the same work; invent an English-sounding one where none
does. Never render the Spanish literally — a literal proverb is not a
proverb, it is a sentence about a goat. Keep the avalanches at full length
(the length is the joke) and keep the misfires misfiring.

DECIDED IN FILES 013-021 (chapters 13-19)
-----------------------------------------
CERVANTES' OWN INCONSISTENCIES — the rule, now that three have come up.
Reconcile a slip a reader could only take for OUR error; keep a slip that
is part of the book's texture.
  - RECONCILED. Chapter 15 calls the carriers "gallegos" in the body but
    "yangueses" in its own title and everywhere afterwards; all of them are
    now Yanguesan carriers. Their ponies stay Galician — that is the breed,
    not the men. Alifanfaron is printed Alifanfaron / Alefanfaron /
    Alifanfuon within four pages of chapter 18; normalised. A reader has
    only the English in front of them and cannot hear that the seam is
    Cervantes'.
  - KEPT. Sancho's wife changes name (Juana Gutierrez / Mari Gutierrez /
    Juana Panza / Teresa), the saddlebags are lost and then reappear, and
    the ass is stolen and returns. These are famous and Cervantes himself
    answers for two of them in Part Two. Do NOT tidy them.

THE EPITHET IS LOCKED: "the Knight of the Sorrowful Countenance" (coined,
file 021). It is the phrase English has kept and it is pinned in
must_contain.txt. "Countenance" is above the reading level of the rest of
the book and stands anyway — Sancho glosses it on the spot ("your worship
really does have the worst-looking face I have ever seen"), so the joke
carries even for a reader who does not know the word. It recurs ~30 times
from here on; do not paraphrase it, ever.

SANCHO'S MISHEARINGS need an English mishearing, not a footnote. Locked:
"the balm of Fierabras" -> Sancho's "that drink of Fiery Brass" (feo Blas).
Pedro's "cripse"/"sterrile" and the sorrow/Sarah pair are in files 013-014.
Build each one out of the English word, never out of the Spanish.

TWO PUNS CARRIED WHOLE, both in file 021: the bachelor's "you have turned
me from straight to crooked" (enderezar tuertos / quedar tuerto), and the
sugared-almond pebbles of chapter 18, where "peladilla" is both a smooth
brook stone and a sweet, so the second stone arrives as "another almond".

NO MARKUP IN THE TEXT FILES. Book titles inside the prose are plain — no
asterisks, no underscores. The pipeline is markup-free by design and both
renderers would ship the characters literally. Structure comes only from
convention: tab indent = verse or table, ALL CAPS = heading (so never write
one), [Figure N: ...] = plate (unused in this book).

must_contain.txt WRITTEN at file 021, covering the opening sentence, the
coined names, the windmills, Marcela's two sentences and the two running
gags. Add to it as each famous passage lands.

SOURCE DEFECT (found reading file 023, chapter 21). Gutenberg's Spanish
#2000 has a stray token welded to the end of a sentence: "me hizo vomitar
las asaduras.along". It is the ONLY one in the whole book — checked with a
sweep over chapters/ — and prep.py did not introduce it. Left in
chapters/ so no boundary moves; simply not translated. Same class as the
Faraday note-16 misprint: a mechanical check cannot see it, and only
reading against the sense finds it.

SENSITIVE PASSAGES, DECIDED (files 030-031, chapters 28-29)
-----------------------------------------------------------
DOROTEA'S STORY is a seduction under a false promise of marriage,
followed by an attempted rape by her own servant. Rendered at full
length, plainly, with gravity and without a single word of
titillation — the Ovid rule. Her speech to Don Fernando ("I am your
vassal, but not your slave") is the moral centre of the episode and
gets the plainest, hardest English in the file. Nothing is cut and
nothing is softened; the shame she feels is HERS and is left as she
states it, because the whole point of the episode is that the reader
sees she is blameless and she does not.

SANCHO AND THE SLAVE TRADE (file 031). Told his master's new kingdom
lies in Guinea, Sancho works out that he can ship his black vassals to
Spain and sell them for cash, and buy himself a title with the
proceeds. It is the ugliest thing Sancho says in the book. TRANSLATED
IN FULL, plainly, with no editorial wink and no note — the Verne rule
and the Bunyan/Ball precedent. Cervantes gives it to Sancho as one of
his greedy daydreams, in the same breath as the empire and the
countship, and a modern reader needs to see exactly what a decent,
funny, ordinary man in 1605 took for granted. Softening it would
protect the reader from the book.

ANOTHER SLIP RECONCILED: in chapter 29 Cardenio addresses Dorotea as
"Teodora" once, mid-speech, a page after learning her name is Dorotea.
It is a printer's error for "senora" and is rendered "madam".

SPLIT-CHAPTER BUG, CAUGHT AT FILE 037 — READ THE MANIFEST, NOT THE PROSE
------------------------------------------------------------------------
This book has THREE split chapters, not one file per chapter throughout:
    035+036 = chapter 33   (the Curioso impertinente, part 1)
    037+038 = chapter 34   (the Curioso impertinente, part 2)
    045+046 = chapter 41   (the captive's story)
Everything else is 1 of 1. I had been titling every file as its own
chapter and got 036 and 037 wrong — 036 headed "Chapter 34" when it is
chapter 33's second half, and 037 headed "Chapter 34 (Part 2)" when it is
chapter 34's FIRST half.

WHAT WOULD HAVE SHIPPED, AND WHY NOTHING WOULD HAVE CAUGHT IT:
assemble.strip_front() drops the first non-blank line of EVERY file in a
group, part 1 and part 2 alike, and takes the heading from the part-1 file
only. So the wrong line in 036 would have been swallowed in silence, and
chapter 34 would have gone to the page and the epub titled
"Chapter 34 (Part 2): ... Is Carried Further". verify.py check 3 only
asserts that a "(Part n of k)" marker, IF PRESENT, sits in the first three
lines; a MISSING marker and a WRONG heading are both invisible to it, and
the word ratio and must_contain do not move at all.
FORMAT, now correct in all three files: heading line, then "(Part n of k)"
on its own line, then a blank line, then the body. Parts 2+ repeat the
same heading text and never re-introduce the chapter in the prose; a
non-final part never concludes it.
CHECK THE MANIFEST FOR "of" > 1 BEFORE WRITING ANY FILE.

LEONELA'S ABC (file 037) is an alphabet of a good lover's virtues, and the
Spanish letters do not survive translation. Rebuilt as a real English A-Z:
appreciative, blameless, chivalrous, devoted, enamoured, firm, gallant,
honourable, illustrious, just, kind, loyal, manly, noble, open-hearted,
princely, quick-witted, rich, "and the S's they talk about", tactful,
veracious. Cervantes' three jokes at the end all survive intact in
English: X is refused as a harsh letter, Y is said to have been covered by
I, and Z is "zealous guardian of your honour".
