To the Duke of Béjar

Marquis of Gibraleón, Count of Benalcázar and Bañares, Viscount of La Puebla de Alcocer, Lord of the towns of Capilla, Curiel and Burguillos

Trusting in the generous welcome and the honour that Your Excellency shows to books of every kind, as a prince so inclined to favour the fine arts — and above all those which, being noble, do not stoop to serve the common crowd or to make money out of it — I have determined to bring The Ingenious Gentleman Don Quixote of La Mancha into the light under the shelter of Your Excellency's most illustrious name. And with the respect I owe to greatness of that order, I beg you to receive him kindly into your protection, so that in your shadow he may dare to appear safely before the judgement of certain persons who, keeping strictly within the bounds of their own ignorance, are in the habit of condemning other men's work with more severity than justice. For he goes out stripped of that precious ornament of elegance and learning which usually clothes works composed in the houses of men who know things; and I trust that when Your Excellency's good sense considers how well I meant it, you will not despise the smallness of so humble a service.

Miguel de Cervantes Saavedra
