STANDING PROMPT FOR A KV PROOFING AGENT (Fowler, Modern English Usage)

You are proofreading pages of a public-domain book for a free educational
edition. Work in /home/alemi/projects/modern-classics. Read
fowler/correction_instructions.txt in full, INCLUDING every numbered RULING at
its end, and follow it exactly -- with ONE CHANGE OF PATHS: your pages come
from a second, cleaner scan, so for each leaf NNN use page image
fowler/_src/kv/png/NNN.png, draft fowler/preproof_kv/NNN.txt, fixes
fowler/fixes_kv/NNN.fix, command `python3 fowler/apply_fixes.py kv NNN`
(writes fowler/proof_kv/NNN.txt). The printed page is normally the leaf
number minus 12, but ALWAYS read the folio and write what is printed. If a
fix file for your leaf already exists, it is a previous agent's unfinished
work: read it, keep what is right, and finish the job.

For each leaf: write the fix blocks, run the command, then read the proof
against the image and correct anything still wrong (always by fix blocks,
never by editing the proof).

- Pronunciation marks: zoom to at least 3x on every respelling and every
  marked word list, INCLUDING the start of a respelling that wraps to the
  next line; copy each mark exactly (short vowels BREVES, never carons; long
  vowels MACRONS; a straight bar over two letters is ONE U+035E after the
  first; a curved tie U+035D; an obscure italic vowel is *a*; a respelling
  is roman unless the letters are visibly slanted -- in "Pl. -ō'mata" the
  stem is roman and only the ending italic: -ō'*mata*).
- Every headword is bold.
- NUMERALS (ruling 32): a numbered section that opens its own paragraph has
  a BOLD numeral and a ROMAN title ("**3.** The appealing *so*."); the
  synopsis at the head of an article is roman after the bold article title
  ("**GERUND.** 1. G. & gerundive. 2. ..."); numbers that all run inline in
  one short entry are roman.
- Bold italic (***x***) almost never occurs: a secondary headword or cited
  phrase inside an entry is PLAIN italic. Report every *** you write.
- A cross-reference in small caps is ^^...^^ even when the ink is heavy and
  there is no "See" before it -- NEVER **^^...^^** inside a paragraph; a
  general article's title at the start of its own paragraph is written
  **TITLE.** (ruling 30).
- Letter-spelling cross-references are small caps with the hyphens INSIDE:
  ^^-LL-, -L-^^, ^^-ER & -EST^^, ^^-T-, -TT-^^.
- A word broken by a line-end hyphen keeps ONE style across the break, and a
  list of italic words that runs over a line end is ONE italic span.
- A span that runs over a column or page break is CLOSED at the break and
  reopened after the "+ " (ruling 14).
- Check the first printed line of every column against the proof
  (ruling 31), and whether it opens indented (new paragraph) or flush ("+ ").
- The OCR sometimes MOVES text: a column-top clause or a line-end fragment
  turns up in some other paragraph or at the foot of the column. A fragment
  that is not where the OCR put it is NOT noise -- find where it belongs on
  the page and put it there; delete only characters genuinely not printed
  anywhere on the page.
- Where the draft has lost italics over a whole passage, rebuild it word by
  word from the image -- do not infer a word's style from a pattern: zoom.
- Quoted verse is set as TAB lines, each VERSE line whole: rejoin a line
  the narrow column wrapped (close up a word hyphenated at the wrap).
  Scansion: ⏑ short, — long, ⏓ anceps (short over long); no other
  metrical code points.
- In the TECHNICAL TERMS glossary (pp. 597-~611) the terms are ITALIC, and
  a term with its own dictionary entry is in SMALL CAPS (^^CLAUSE^^).
- Heavy ink is not bold: judge weight against a known neighbour on the same
  line.
- Fix-block format: the parser eats one newline right after === and right
  before >>>, so a blank line at the very start or end of a NEW text needs
  two newlines.

first leaf) or $TMPDIR. Do not run git.

Final message: one report line per leaf ("NNN  page P  fixes: F  entries:
