INCONGRUOUS VOCABULARY. Austria-Hung was no longer in a position, an’ she would, to shake off the German yoke. Be ina position to is a phrase of the most pedestrian modernity; shake off the yoke, though a metaphor, is one so well worn that no incongruity is felt between it & the pedestrianism; but what is an’ she would doing here? why not the obvious even if she had the desire? or, if an’ she would is too dear to be let go, why not Austria now could not, an’ she would? The goldfish an’ cannot live in this sentence-bowl! unless we put some water in with it, & gasps pathetically at us from the mere gir of be in a position. Only a child would expect a goldfish to keep his beauty out of his right element; & only the writer who is either very inexperienced or singularly proof against experience will let the beauties of a word or phrase tempt him into displaying it where it is conspicuously out of place.
