OCR CORRECTION PASS: H. W. Fowler, A Dictionary of Modern English Usage (1926)

PUBLIC DOMAIN. Fowler's Dictionary was published by the Clarendon Press in
1926; works of 1926 entered the United States public domain on 1 January
2022, and Fowler died in 1933, so it is also free in the United Kingdom and
wherever the term is life plus seventy years. The page images are a scan of
the 1926 printing, openly available at
archive.org/details/a-dictionary-of-modern-english-usage. This is a free,
non-commercial, educational RESTORED EDITION for the Modern Classics project
(github.com/alexalemi/modern-classics): the author's text unchanged, in
clean markup, with a proper ebook. (The 1965 Gowers revision is a different,
copyrighted book and is NOT what these pages are.)

WHAT YOU DO. Each page already has a machine draft made from the OCR:
fowler/preproof/NNN.txt. You do NOT retype the page. You compare the draft
with the page image, fowler/_src/png/NNN.png, and write only the
CORRECTIONS, as fix blocks, into fowler/fixes/NNN.fix. Then run

    python3 fowler/apply_fixes.py NNN

which applies the fixes and writes fowler/proof/NNN.txt. It refuses a block
whose OLD text is not found exactly once; fix the block and rerun. Then READ
proof/NNN.txt once against the image and fix anything still wrong.

FIX BLOCKS, one per correction, in reading order:

    <<<
    exact text now in the draft
    ===
    what it should be
    >>>

- HARD LIMIT: no block's NEW text longer than about 300 characters (two or
  three printed lines). A paragraph with many errors takes SEVERAL SMALL
  BLOCKS, never one replacement of the whole. Most of the draft's words are
  right; your job is the ones that are not, and the markup.
- Keep each OLD SHORT: the wrong word with just enough neighbours to be
  unique. Markup is a fix too: "dislocate. See" -> "**dislocate.** See".
- A line the OCR dropped entirely, or a span it destroyed: insert or
  replace it with a block (still within the size limit; several if needed).

THE TARGET FORMAT (what proof/NNN.txt must be when you are done)

- FIRST LINE "page: N" with the printed page number (roman in the front
  matter), or "page: none"; then a blank line. The draft's number comes from
  a page map: CHECK it against the folio printed at the top of the page.
- The page is TWO COLUMNS: the whole left column first, then the right. The
  running head (two guide words and the folio) is NOT transcribed.
- One paragraph per line; a blank line between paragraphs. An entry is a
  paragraph; so is each numbered section of a long entry when the print
  starts it on a new indented line. Join a word broken at a line end, and
  keep the hyphen only in a real compound (to-day, well-known).
- A paragraph that continues from the previous column or the previous page
  starts "+ " (the left column's first paragraph when the page opens
  mid-entry; the right column's first paragraph when it continues the left
  column's last). The draft guesses these; CHECK every one.
- Spelling, capitals and punctuation EXACTLY as printed: Fowler's "&",
  "to-day", "shew" if printed, his spacing of "e.g." and "i.e.". Do not
  modernise or correct. An obvious misprint is transcribed as printed and
  listed in your report. ASCII " and '; dashes as —.
- The printer's space before ; : ? ! is closed up ("tone:", "said;").

MARKUP (the only markup there is)
- **bold** for bold type: every HEADWORD at the start of an entry
  ("**dislocate.** See ...", "**disposal, disposition.** In some ..."), and
  any other bold the page prints. The bold span covers exactly the bold
  letters; its punctuation goes inside if printed bold.
- ^^SMALL CAPITALS^^ for words in small capitals. In this book they are the
  CROSS-REFERENCES to other articles ("see ^^JUDGEMENT^^", "a ^^NEEDLESS
  VARIANT^^", "see ^^-ABLE^^ 4"): write the letters as capitals, exactly as
  they read, inside the carets, with anything else printed with them
  (hyphens, parentheses, numbers) outside unless it is itself small
  capitals.
- *italic* for italic type: the illustrative quotations (Fowler prints his
  examples in italic, separated by "/"), words discussed as words, foreign
  words, titles. The italic span must not begin or end with a space, and an
  italic letter glued to a number stays roman ("sense 2a").
- PRONUNCIATION exactly as printed: the stress mark after a stressed
  syllable as ASCII ' ("di'sputabl"), and the vowel marks as the Unicode
  letters (ā ē ī ō ū, ă ĕ ĭ ŏ ŭ, ȧ, ü ...). If a mark has no Unicode form,
  describe it in the report and write [?] in the text.
- Greek, French, German and Latin as printed, with their accents.
- A letter-section heading (the large "B" that opens the B entries) is its
  own paragraph: just the letter.
- A FOOTNOTE (rare): its own paragraph at the end of the file, "Footnote: "
  then the mark and text; the mark stays in the body; an asterisk mark is
  written ⁎. Continued from the previous page: "Footnote+ ".
- A TABLE or set-out list: TAB rows, cells separated by " | ". Lines of
  verse or set-off lines: each printed line on its own line starting with a
  TAB.

Read the WHOLE page image, both columns, top to bottom. Enlarge crops (PIL,
into YOUR OWN subfolder of the scratchpad directory) wherever the type is
hard to read, especially for italic spans, small capitals, and pronunciation
marks. Keep scratch files in your own subfolder; change no files but your
own fixes/NNN.fix and proof/NNN.txt.

REPORT (final message, SHORT, never quoting page text):
  NNN  page <n>  fixes: <count>  entries: <count>
then misprints kept, anything uncertain, and anything odd.

RULINGS FROM THE PILOT (these override anything above that disagrees)
1. PAGE FURNITURE AT THE FOOT is not transcribed: the printer's signature
   letter ("B") and the printer's job number ("1351" on p. 1) are not the
   folio. THE DRAFT'S "page:" NUMBER COMES FROM A PAGE MAP THAT IS OFTEN
   WRONG (it runs two ahead by leaf 020): ALWAYS read the folio at the top
   of the page and write that. Only p. 1 prints none; there write "page: 1".
2. The display title over both columns on p. 1 ("A Dictionary of English
   Usage") is not transcribed: the edition supplies its own title.
3. Bold section numbers: mark "1.", "2." as **bold** only where they are
   visibly in the headword's weight; if the whole page prints heavily and you
   cannot tell, compare with the headword and with ordinary numbers in the
   text, and say in the report what you decided.
4. DAMAGED TYPE IS NOT A MISPRINT. Where a worn or broken letter prints as
   another ("docs" with a broken e for "does", "rcjecting"), transcribe the
   letter the printer set, the intended word. A misprint is a WRONG letter
   printed cleanly; list only those as misprints kept.
5. A stray folio or other page furniture the OCR planted in the middle of a
   column (a bare "386" between two lines of an entry) is deleted.
6. Lettered subsections run inline ("A. ... B. ...") stay in their
   paragraph; only a subsection the print starts on a new indented line is a
   paragraph of its own.
7. Curly quotes in the draft are made ASCII by apply_fixes.py itself: never
   edit proof/NNN.txt directly, since every correction must be a fix block
   (re-applying the fixes rebuilds the file).
8. A paragraph that continues from the left column into the right (same
   page) is written as two lines, the second starting "+ ": the prep joins
   them. Do the same across a page turn.
9. SUFFIX AND PREFIX CROSS-REFERENCES are small capitals too (^^-ABLE^^,
   ^^-EDLY^^, ^^-ER^^ & ^^-EST^^, ^^-OR^^): at this scan's resolution the
   step between the larger first letter and the rest is faint, so crop and
   compare letter heights before leaving any such suffix as plain capitals.
10. A TIE OVER A PAIR OF VOWELS in a pronunciation is one of TWO marks,
   and you copy the one printed (Fowler's Key to Pronunciation distinguishes
   them): a CURVED tie (the short oo of "book") is the combining double
   breve U+035D, "o͝o"; a STRAIGHT bar (the long oo of "food") is the
   combining double macron U+035E, "o͞o". Either goes after the first letter.
11. ROMAN INSIDE ITALIC: Fowler often prints the word or phrase he is
   pointing at in ROMAN inside an italic example ("*Hence the* loss *of*
   ..."). Close the italic before it and reopen after it, exactly where the
   print changes. Zoom on every example; do not italicise a whole example
   by default.
12. The raised-o numero sign ("No" with a small raised o) is written N°.
13. WHAT IS BOLD, AND WHAT IS NOT. The scan's inking varies from page to
   page and some pages print heavily throughout: that is NOT bold. Bold in
   this book is only (a) an entry's headword; (b) a general article's title
   and the synopsis of its sections that follows it ("**ANALOGY. 1. As a
   logical resource. 2. ...**"); (c) a section's own title at the start of
   its paragraph ("**1. As a logical resource.** The meaning ..."), and a
   bold section number. Running prose, word lists and examples are NEVER
   bold, however dark the page: compare against the headword on the same
   page, which is always clearly heavier.
14. A SPAN THAT RUNS OVER A COLUMN OR PAGE BREAK (italic, bold, small
   capitals): close it at the end of the column or leaf and open it again at
   the start of the "+ " continuation, so every leaf balances on its own
   ("... a. to cause ill-*" | "+ *health*"). The prep joins the two halves
   into one span.
15. THE FIGURE 3 IS FLAT-TOPPED IN THIS TYPE and, inked heavily, looks like
   an 8 at scan resolution. An 8 has a ROUND top; a 3 has a flat top bar.
   Zoom on EVERY 3 or 8 (section numbers, cross-reference numbers such as
   "^^REGARD^^ 3", dates, figures) and decide by the top. A section sequence
   "1., 2., 8." is always 3. Never list 8-for-3 as a misprint.
16. IN A PRONUNCIATION, AN OBSCURE (unstressed, neutral) VOWEL IS PRINTED
   ITALIC: "kăt*a*m*a*ră'n", "ăn*a*krŏ'nĭk". Mark each such letter as its
   own *italic* span; the marked vowels (ă, ē ...) stay roman.
17. THE SHORT-VOWEL MARK IS A BREVE (ă ĕ ĭ ŏ ŭ, a round cup), NEVER A CARON
   (ǎ ě ǐ ǒ ǔ, a pointed v): Fowler's type has no carons.
