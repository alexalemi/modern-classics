lay & lie. 1. Verbs. To lay is transitive only (=put to rest), & makes laid; to lie is intransitive only (=be at or come to rest), & makes lay, lain, never laid. But confusion even between the words lay & lie themselves is very common in uneducated talk; & still commoner, sometimes making its way into print, is the use of laid, which belongs to the verb to lay only, for lay the past tense, & lain the p.p., of lie (we laid out on the grass, & could have laid there all day). 2. Nouns. Lie & lay are both used in the senses configuration of ground, direction or position in which something lies. Neither has a long established history behind it; the OED has only one quotation earlier than the nineteenth century, & that is for lie (the proper lye of the land, 1692); dlie seems also the more reasonable form, lay having issued perhaps from sailors’ & rustic talk, in which the verbs are not kept distinct.
