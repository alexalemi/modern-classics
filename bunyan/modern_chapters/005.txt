The House of the Interpreter

Then he went on until he came to the house of the Interpreter, where he knocked over and over. At last somebody came to the door and asked who was there.

Christian. Sir, here is a traveller, who was told by a friend of the master of this house to call in here for his own good. I would like to speak with the master of the house.

So he called the master of the house, who after a little while came to Christian and asked him what he wanted.

Christian. Sir, said Christian, I am a man who has come from the City of Destruction, and I am going to Mount Zion. I was told by the man who stands at the gate at the head of this road that if I called here you would show me excellent things — things that would help me on my journey.

Interpreter. Then the Interpreter said: Come in. I will show you what will do you good.

So he told his servant to light a candle, and told Christian to follow him. He took him into a private room and told his servant to open a door; and when he had done it, Christian saw hanging on the wall the picture of a very solemn person. This was how it looked. Its eyes were lifted up to heaven, it had the best of books in its hand, the law of truth was written on its lips, and the world was behind its back. It stood as though it were pleading with people, and a crown of gold hung over its head.

Christian. Then Christian said: What does this mean?

Interpreter. The man in this picture is one in a thousand. He can father children, go through the pains of labour with them, and nurse them himself once they are born. As for seeing him with his eyes lifted to heaven, the best of books in his hand, and the law of truth written on his lips — that is to show you that his work is to know dark and difficult things and open them up for sinners; which is why you also see him standing as though he were pleading with people. And as for seeing the world thrown down behind him and a crown hanging over his head, that is to show you that because he thinks nothing of the things that are here and now, out of love for his Master's service, he is certain to have glory for his reward in the world that comes next.

Now, said the Interpreter, I have shown you this picture first because the man in it is the only man the Lord of the place you are going to has authorized to be your guide in all the hard places you may meet on the road. So take good notice of what I have shown you, and keep firmly in mind what you have seen — in case on your journey you run into people who claim to be leading you right, when their road goes down to death.

Then he took him by the hand and led him into a very large sitting room that was full of dust, because it had never been swept. After they had looked at it for a while, the Interpreter called for a man to sweep it. And when the man began to sweep, the dust flew about so thickly that Christian was nearly choked by it. Then the Interpreter said to a young woman standing by: Fetch the water and sprinkle the room. And when she had done it, the room was swept and cleaned easily and well.

Christian. Then Christian said: What does this mean?

Interpreter. The Interpreter answered: This room is the heart of a man who has never been made holy by the sweet grace of the Gospel. The dust is his original sin and the corruption inside him, which has spoiled the whole man. The one who started sweeping is the Law; and the one who brought the water and sprinkled it is the Gospel.

Now, as for what you saw — that as soon as the first began to sweep, the dust flew about so much that he could not get the room clean, and you were nearly choked by it — that is to show you that the Law, instead of cleaning sin out of the heart by its own working, actually stirs sin up, gives it strength and makes it grow in the soul, at the very moment it exposes sin and forbids it. Because the Law does not give the power to master it.

And again, as you saw the young woman sprinkle the room with water, after which it was cleaned easily and well — that is to show you that when the Gospel comes into the heart with its sweet and precious influence, then, I say, just as you saw her lay the dust by sprinkling the floor with water, so is sin beaten and mastered, and the soul made clean through faith in the Gospel, and so made fit for the King of glory to live in.

I saw further, in my dream, that the Interpreter took him by the hand and brought him into a little room where two small children were sitting, each in his own chair. The elder was called Passion, and the other Patience. Passion seemed very dissatisfied, but Patience was perfectly calm. So Christian asked what the reason was for Passion's dissatisfaction. The Interpreter answered: The one who looks after them wants him to wait for his best things until the beginning of next year — but he wants everything now. Patience, on the other hand, is willing to wait.

Then I saw somebody come to Passion and bring him a bag of treasure, and pour it out at his feet; and he picked it up and gloated over it, and laughed Patience to scorn into the bargain. But I watched only a little while, and he had squandered the lot, and had nothing left but rags.

Christian. Then Christian said to the Interpreter: Explain this to me more fully.

Interpreter. So he said: These two boys stand for something. Passion stands for the people of this world, and Patience for the people of the world that is coming. Because, as you see here, Passion wants everything now, this year — that is to say, in this world. So it is with the people of this world: they must have all their good things now, and cannot wait until next year, that is, until the next world, for what is coming to them. The proverb "a bird in the hand is worth two in the bush" carries more weight with them than everything God has said about the good of the world to come. But as you saw that Passion very quickly squandered it all away and had nothing left but rags, so it will be with all such people at the end of this world.

Christian. Then Christian said: Now I see that Patience has the better wisdom, and for a number of reasons. First, because he waits for the best things. Second, because he will still have his glory when the other has nothing but rags.

Interpreter. No — you may add another. The glory of the next world will never wear out, but these things are gone in a moment. So Passion had far less reason to laugh at Patience for having his good things first than Patience will have to laugh at Passion for having his best things last. Because first has to give way to last, since last must still have its turn coming; but last gives way to nothing, because there is nothing after it to follow on. So the man who gets his share first is bound to have a time when he spends it; but the man who gets his share last has it lastingly. That is why it is said to the rich man in the story, "In your lifetime you received your good things, and Lazarus likewise received evil things; but now he is comforted, and you are tormented."

Christian. Then I see it is not best to be greedy for the things that are here now, but to wait for the things to come.

Interpreter. You are quite right. "For the things which are seen are temporary; but the things which are not seen are eternal." But even so — because present things and our bodily appetites are such close neighbours to one another, and because things to come and our bodily senses are such total strangers to one another — that is why the first two make friends so quickly, and why the distance between the second two keeps up.

Then I saw in my dream that the Interpreter took Christian by the hand and led him to a place where there was a fire burning against a wall, and somebody standing beside it constantly throwing a great deal of water on it to put it out; and yet the fire burned higher and hotter.

Then Christian said: What does this mean?

The Interpreter answered: This fire is the work of grace going on in the heart. The one throwing water on it, to douse it and put it out, is the Devil. But as for why you see the fire burning higher and hotter in spite of him — you shall see the reason for that too.

So he took him round to the back of the wall, where he saw a man with a jar of oil in his hand, which he was also constantly pouring — but secretly — into the fire.

Then Christian said: What does this mean?

The Interpreter answered: This is Christ, who continually keeps up, with the oil of his grace, the work already begun in the heart; and by that means, whatever the Devil can do, the souls of his people keep their grace still. And as for your seeing that the man stood behind the wall to keep the fire going, that is to teach you that it is hard for someone under temptation to see how this work of grace is being kept up in the soul.

I saw too that the Interpreter took him by the hand again and led him to a pleasant place where a magnificent palace had been built, beautiful to look at; and Christian was delighted at the sight of it. He also saw certain people walking about on the top of it, dressed all in gold.

Then Christian said: May we go in there?

Then the Interpreter took him and led him up towards the door of the palace; and there at the door stood a great crowd of men who clearly wanted to go in but did not dare. There was also a man sitting a little way off from the door, at a table, with a book and his inkpot in front of him, to take down the name of anyone who went in. And Christian saw that in the doorway there stood a number of men in armour to guard it, who were determined to do as much harm and damage as they could to anybody who tried to enter.

Now Christian was rather taken aback. At last, when every man had shrunk back for fear of the armed guards, Christian saw a man with a very determined look on his face come up to the man sitting there to write, and say, "Put my name down, sir." And when he had done it, Christian saw the man draw his sword, put a helmet on his head, and rush at the door and the armed men, who fell on him with murderous force. But the man was not discouraged in the least, and set about cutting and hacking most fiercely. So after he had taken many wounds and given many to those who were trying to keep him out, he cut his way straight through the lot of them and pushed forward into the palace — at which a delighted shout was heard from those inside, from the very people who were walking on the top of the palace, saying:

	Come in, come in;
	eternal glory you shall win.

So he went in, and was dressed in the same clothes as they were. Then Christian smiled and said: I really think I know what this means.

Now, said Christian, let me go on from here.

No, wait, said the Interpreter, until I have shown you a little more; and after that you shall go on your way.

So he took him by the hand again and led him into a very dark room, where a man sat in an iron cage.

Now the man, to look at, seemed extremely sad. He sat with his eyes on the ground and his hands folded together, and he sighed as though his heart would break. Then Christian said: What does this mean? At which the Interpreter told him to talk to the man himself.

So Christian said to the man: What are you?

The man answered: I am what I once was not.

Christian. What were you once?

The man. I was once a fine and flourishing believer, so I looked in my own eyes and in other people's too. I was once, as I thought, well set for the Celestial City, and I used to feel real joy at the thought of getting there.

Christian. Well — but what are you now?

The man. I am now a man of despair, and I am shut up inside it as I am shut up in this iron cage. I cannot get out. Oh, now I cannot get out!

Christian. But how did you come to be in this state?

The man. I stopped keeping watch and staying sober. I gave my appetites their head. I sinned against the light of the Word and against the goodness of God. I have grieved the Spirit, and he is gone. I taunted the Devil, and he has come to me. I have provoked God to anger, and he has left me. I have hardened my own heart so far that I cannot repent.

Then Christian said to the Interpreter: But is there no hope for a man like this? Ask him, said the Interpreter. No, said Christian, please, sir — you ask him.

Interpreter. Then the Interpreter said: Is there no hope? Must you be kept in this iron cage of despair?

The man. No. None at all.

Interpreter. Why, the Son of the Blessed One is full of pity.

The man. I have crucified him all over again, for myself. I have despised his person. I have despised his righteousness. I have "counted his blood an unholy thing." I have "insulted the Spirit of grace." So I have shut myself out of every promise, and there is nothing left for me now but threats — terrible threats, terrifying threats of certain judgment and burning fury, which will devour me as an enemy.

Interpreter. And what did you bring yourself into this state for?

The man. For the appetites, the pleasures and the profits of this world, in the enjoyment of which I promised myself a great deal of delight at the time. But now every one of those same things bites me and gnaws me like a burning worm.

Interpreter. But can you not repent now, and turn back?

The man. God has refused me repentance. His Word gives me no encouragement to believe. He himself has shut me up in this iron cage, and all the men in the world could not let me out. Oh, eternity, eternity! How am I going to face the misery I must meet with in eternity?

Interpreter. Then the Interpreter said to Christian: Let this man's misery stay in your memory, and be a permanent warning to you.

Christian. Well, said Christian, this is terrifying. God help me to keep watch and stay sober, and to pray that I may keep clear of whatever brought this man to such misery. Sir, is it not time for me to go on my way now?

Interpreter. Wait until I have shown you one more thing, and then you shall go on your way.

So he took Christian by the hand again and led him into a bedroom where somebody was getting out of bed; and as he put his clothes on, he was shaking and trembling. Then Christian said: Why is this man trembling like that? The Interpreter told the man to give Christian the reason for it himself.

So he began, and said: Last night, while I was asleep, I dreamed — and the sky went utterly black, and there was thunder and lightning in the most frightening way, so that it threw me into an agony. Then I looked up in my dream and saw the clouds driving past at an extraordinary speed, and I heard a great blast of a trumpet, and I also saw a man sitting on a cloud, attended by the thousands of heaven. They were all in flames of fire, and the sky itself was burning.

Then I heard a voice saying, "Arise, you dead, and come to judgment." And with that the rocks split, the graves opened, and the dead inside them came out. Some of them were tremendously glad and looked upward; and some tried to hide themselves under the mountains.

Then I saw the man who sat on the cloud open the book and tell the world to come near. But because of a fierce flame that came out from in front of him, there was a good distance kept between him and them, as there is between the judge and the prisoners in the dock. And I heard it announced to those attending the man who sat on the cloud, "Gather together the weeds, the chaff and the stubble, and throw them into the burning lake." And with that, the bottomless pit opened, just about where I was standing, and out of its mouth came thick smoke and coals of fire, with hideous noises.

It was also said to the same people, "Gather my wheat into the barn." And with that I saw many of them snatched up and carried away into the clouds — but I was left behind. I tried to hide myself too, but I could not, because the man who sat on the cloud kept his eye on me the whole time. My sins came into my mind as well, and my conscience accused me from every side. And at that I woke up.

Christian. But what was it about the sight that frightened you so much?

The man. Why, I thought that the day of judgment had come, and that I was not ready for it. But this frightened me most: that the angels gathered up a number of people and left me behind — and that the pit of hell opened its mouth just where I was standing. My conscience tormented me too; and, as I thought, the Judge kept his eye on me the whole time, with anger showing in his face.

Then the Interpreter said to Christian: Have you thought about all these things?

Christian. Yes — and they leave me hoping and afraid at once.

Interpreter. Well, keep all of them in your mind, so that they may be like a spur in your side to drive you forward on the road you have to travel.

Then Christian began to tighten his belt and get himself ready for the journey. And the Interpreter said: May the Comforter be with you always, good Christian, to guide you on the road that leads to the City.

So Christian went on his way, saying:

	Here I have seen things rare and worth the knowing,
	things pleasant, things to dread, things to keep me going
	steady in what I have set my hand to do.
	So let me think them over, and think them through,
	and understand what they were shown me for —
	and thank you, good Interpreter, once more.
