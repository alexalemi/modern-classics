Lewis Carroll was the pen name of Charles Lutwidge Dodgson (1832–1898), a mathematics lecturer at Christ Church, Oxford. On 4 July 1862 he and a friend rowed the three daughters of the Dean of Christ Church up the Thames, and he told them a story about the middle one, Alice Liddell. At her request he wrote it down, and he later expanded it into this book, which Macmillan published in 1865 with illustrations by John Tenniel. The poem at the front of the book, "All in the golden afternoon", is about that trip. Prima, Secunda and Tertia are the three sisters.

Alice follows a White Rabbit down a hole and spends the rest of the book changing size and arguing with the creatures she meets. Almost every one of them contradicts her, corrects her grammar or tells her she is wrong. Much of the comedy is logic taken literally, and the rest is parody. Nearly all of the verse, "How doth the little crocodile", "You are old, Father William", "Twinkle, twinkle, little bat", parodies poems Victorian children had to learn by heart, most of them now forgotten. The Mouse's tale in Chapter III is printed in the shape of a tail, a pun on "tale" that Alice misunderstands.

This edition is not the first. In 1907 the book's British copyright ran out, several publishers brought out new illustrated editions that year, and Heinemann's, with pictures by Arthur Rackham, is the best known of them. Rackham drew thirteen colour plates for it, the frontispiece among them, and fourteen pen drawings, and the cover, endpapers and title page carry his designs. Austin Dobson's proem was written for this edition. It concedes that nothing "can part you now from Tenniel" and welcomes Rackham anyway, as the illustrator of Rip Van Winkle.

This is a restored edition: Carroll's words are unchanged. It is built from the Project Gutenberg transcription of the 1907 edition and keeps all of Rackham's pictures in the places they occupy there. The colour plates are reproduced from full-size scans of the 1907 plates, several times larger than the transcription's. The decorated initial that opens each chapter is set here as an ordinary letter. The poems keep their stanzas and indents, and the Mouse's tale keeps its shape. The chapter titles are those of the printed Contents. The edition also adds a description of every picture, modern typesetting, a proper ebook, and this introduction.
