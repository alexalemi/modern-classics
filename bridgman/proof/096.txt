The conditions are exceedingly complicated, and would be difficult to formulate in precise mathematical terms, but we perhaps may imagine it done by some sort of a super-being. The important thing to notice is that no dimensional constants appear in the equation of hydrodynamics if the ordinary mechanical units in terms of mass, length, and time are used, so that the result will involve only the measurable physical variables. The variables are the resistance to the motion, the velocity of motion, the shape of the body, which we may suppose specified by some absolute dimension and the ratio to it of certain other lengths (as, for instance, the shape of an ellipsoid may be specified by the length of the longest axis and the ratio to this axis of the other axes) and the constants of the fluid, which are its density, viscosity, and compressibility, the latter of which we may specify by giving the velocity of sound in the fluid. We suppose that gravity does not enter the results, that is, the body is in uniform motion at a constant level, so that no work is done by the gravitational forces. The formulation of the problem is now as follows.

	*Name of Quantity.* | *Symbol.* | *Dimensional Formula.*
	Resistance, | \(R\) | \(M L T^{-2}\)
	Velocity, | \(v\) | \(L T^{-1}\)
	Absolute dimension, | \(l\) | \(L\)
	Density of fluid, | \(d\) | \(M L^{-3}\)
	Viscosity of fluid, | \(\mu\) | \(M L^{-1} T^{-1}\)
	Velocity of sound in the fluid, | \(v'\) | \(L T^{-1}\)
	Shape factors, fixing shape of body, | \(r_1\), \(r_2\), etc. | \(0\)

We have here six variables, not counting the shape factors, which may have any number depending on the geometrical complexity of the body, so that there are three dimensionless products exclusive of the shape factors, which are already dimensionless. One of these three dimensionless products is obvious on inspection, and is \(v'/v\). We have to find the other dimensionless products in the way best adapted to this particular problem. Since we are interested in the resistance to the motion, we choose this as the term with unit exponent in one of the products, so that we may write the result with \(R\) standing alone on the left-hand side of the equation.
