On the Higher Man

I

When I came to people for the first time, I committed the hermit’s folly, the great folly: I set myself up in the marketplace.

And when I spoke to all, I spoke to none. But in the evening tightrope walkers were my companions, and corpses; and I myself was almost a corpse.

But with the new morning a new truth came to me: then I learned to say, “What do the marketplace and the mob and the mob’s noise and the mob’s long ears matter to me!”

You higher men, learn this from me: in the marketplace no one believes in higher men. And if you want to speak there, very well! But the mob blinks: “We are all equal.”

“You higher men” — so the mob blinks — “there are no higher men, we are all equal, man is man, before God — we are all equal!”

Before God! — But now this God has died. And before the mob we do not want to be equal. You higher men, go away from the marketplace!

II

Before God! — But now this God has died! You higher men, this God was your greatest danger.

Only since he has been lying in the grave have you risen again. Only now does the great noon come, only now does the higher man become — master!

Did you understand this word, oh my brothers? You are frightened: do your hearts grow dizzy? Does the abyss yawn for you here? Does the hellhound yelp at you here?

Well then! Up with you! You higher men! Only now is the mountain of man’s future in labor. God died: now *we* want — the overman to live.

III

The most careful ask today: “How is man to be preserved?” But Zarathustra asks, as the only one and the first: “How is man to be *overcome*?”

The overman lies close to my heart, *he* is my first and only one — and *not* man: not the neighbor, not the poorest, not the one who suffers most, not the best —

Oh my brothers, what I can love in man is that he is a going over and a going under. And in you too there is much that makes me love and hope.

That you despised, you higher men, that makes me hope. For the great despisers are the great reverers.

That you despaired, there is much to honor in that. For you did not learn how to submit, you did not learn the small prudences.

For today the small people have become master: they all preach submission and modesty and prudence and diligence and consideration and the long and-so-on of the small virtues.

Whatever is of woman’s kind, whatever comes from the serf’s kind, and especially the mob-hodgepodge: *that* now wants to become master of all human destiny — oh disgust! Disgust! Disgust!

*That* asks and asks and never grows tired: “How does man preserve himself, best, longest, most pleasantly?” With that — they are the masters of today.

Overcome these masters of today for me, oh my brothers — these small people: *they* are the overman’s greatest danger!

Overcome for me, you higher men, the small virtues, the small prudences, the grain-of-sand considerations, the anthill’s crawling clutter, the wretched contentment, the “happiness of the greatest number” —!

And despair rather than submit. And, truly, I love you for this, that you do not know how to live today, you higher men! For that is how *you* live — best!

IV

Do you have courage, oh my brothers? Are you brave-hearted? *Not* courage before witnesses, but the courage of hermits and eagles, which not even a god watches anymore?

Cold souls, mules, the blind, the drunken I do not call brave-hearted. He has heart who knows fear but *masters* fear; who sees the abyss, but with *pride*.

Whoever sees the abyss, but with an eagle’s eyes; whoever *grasps* the abyss with an eagle’s talons: he has courage. —

V

“Man is evil” — so all the wisest told me, for my comfort. Ah, if only it is still true today! For evil is man’s best strength.

“Man must become better and more evil” — so *I* teach. The most evil is necessary for the overman’s best.

It may have been good for that preacher of the small people that he suffered and bore the sin of man. But I rejoice in great sin as my great *comfort*. —

But such things are not said for long ears. Not every word belongs in every snout, either. These are fine, faraway things: sheep’s hooves shall not grab at them!

VI

You higher men, do you think I am here to make good what you made bad?

Or that from now on I wanted to make softer beds for you sufferers? Or to show you restless ones, you who have strayed, you who have climbed astray, new and easier footpaths?

No! No! Three times no! Ever more, ever better ones of your kind shall perish — for you shall have it ever worse and harder. Only so —

— only so does man grow up to *that* height where the lightning strikes him and breaks him: high enough for the lightning!

My mind and my longing go out toward the few, the long, the far: what would your small, many, short misery matter to me!

You do not yet suffer enough for me! For you suffer from yourselves; you have not yet suffered *from man*. You would be lying if you said otherwise! None of you suffers from what I suffered. —

VII

It is not enough for me that the lightning no longer does harm. I do not want to conduct it away: it shall learn — to work for *me*. —

My wisdom has long been gathering like a cloud; it grows stiller and darker. So does every wisdom that is *one day* to give birth to lightning. —

To these people of today I do not want to be *light*, nor to be called light. *Them* — I want to blind: lightning of my wisdom! Put out their eyes!

VIII

Will nothing beyond your ability: there is a wicked falseness in those who will beyond their ability.

Especially when they will great things! For they awaken mistrust of great things, these subtle counterfeiters and actors: —

— until at last they are false even before themselves, squint-eyed, whitewashed worm-rot, cloaked in strong words, in shop-window virtues, in glittering false works.

Be well on your guard there, you higher men! For nothing today is more precious to me, and rarer, than honesty.

Does this today not belong to the mob? But the mob does not know what is great, what is small, what is straight and honest: it is innocently crooked, it always lies.

IX

Have a good mistrust today, you higher men, you brave-hearted ones! You openhearted ones! And keep your reasons secret! For this today belongs to the mob.

What the mob once learned to believe without reasons, who could, with reasons — overturn that for it?

And in the marketplace one persuades with gestures. But reasons make the mob mistrustful.

And if truth ever did win a victory there, then ask yourselves with good mistrust: “What strong error fought for it?”

Beware of the scholars too! They hate you: for they are barren! They have cold, dried-up eyes; before them every bird lies plucked.

Such people boast that they do not lie: but being powerless to lie is still far from being love of truth. Beware!

Freedom from fever is still far from being knowledge! I do not believe in spirits that have gone cold. Whoever cannot lie does not know what truth is.

X

If you want to rise high, use your own legs! Do not let yourselves be *carried* up; do not sit on other people’s backs and heads!

But you have mounted a horse? You are riding briskly up to your goal now? Well then, my friend! But your lame foot is riding on the horse too!

When you are at your goal, when you leap down from your horse: right on your *height*, you higher man — you will stumble!

XI

You creators, you higher men! One is pregnant only with one’s own child.

Do not let anyone talk you into anything, or talk anything into you! Who, then, is *your* neighbor? And even if you act “for the neighbor” — you still do not create for him!

Unlearn this “for” for me, you creators: your very virtue wants you to do nothing with “for” and “in order to” and “because.” You shall seal your ears against these false little words.

“For the neighbor” is the virtue only of small people: there they say “like with like” and “one hand washes the other”: — they have neither the right nor the strength for *your* self-interest!

In your self-interest, you creators, is the caution and the foresight of the pregnant! What no one has yet seen with eyes, the fruit: that is what your whole love shelters and spares and feeds.

Where your whole love is, with your child, there is your whole virtue too! Your work, your will is *your* “neighbor”: do not let anyone talk you into false values!

XII

You creators, you higher men! Whoever must give birth is sick; but whoever has given birth is unclean.

Ask the women: one does not give birth because it is a pleasure. Pain makes hens and poets cackle.

You creators, there is much that is unclean in you. That is because you had to be mothers.

A new child: oh, how much new filth came into the world too! Step aside! And whoever has given birth shall wash his soul clean!

XIII

Do not be virtuous beyond your strength! And want nothing of yourselves that goes against probability!

Walk in the footsteps where your fathers’ virtue already walked! How would you climb high if your fathers’ will did not climb with you?

But whoever wants to be a firstling, let him see to it that he does not also become a lastling! And where the vices of your fathers are, there you should not want to pass for saints!

Someone whose fathers kept company with women and strong wines and wild boars: what would it be if he wanted chastity of himself?

It would be a folly! Truly, it seems a great deal to me for such a man if he is the husband of one or two or three women.

And if he founded monasteries and wrote over the door, “The way to holiness,” — I would still say: what for! It is a new folly!

He has founded himself a house of correction and a house of flight: much good may it do him! But I do not believe in it.

In solitude whatever one brings into it grows, the inner brute too. For this reason solitude is inadvisable for many.

Has there been anything filthier on earth so far than the saints of the desert? *Around them* not only was the devil loose — but the swine too.

XIV

Shy, ashamed, clumsy, like a tiger whose leap has failed: that, you higher men, is how I have often seen you slink aside. A *throw* had failed you.

But, you dice-players, what does it matter! You did not learn to play and to mock as one must play and mock! Are we not always sitting at a great table of mocking and playing?

And if great things failed you, does that make you yourselves — failures? And if you yourselves are failures, does that make man — a failure? But if man is a failure: well then! Up with you!

XV

The higher a thing’s kind, the more rarely it succeeds. You higher men here, have you not all — failed?

Take heart, what does it matter! How much is still possible! Learn to laugh at yourselves, as one must laugh!

And what wonder that you failed and half succeeded, you half-broken ones! Is it not — man’s *future* that presses and jostles in you?

Man’s farthest, deepest, star-highest, his tremendous strength: does not all of that foam against itself in your pot?

What wonder that many a pot breaks! Learn to laugh at yourselves, as one must laugh! You higher men, oh, how much is still possible!

And truly, how much has already succeeded! How rich this earth is in small, good, perfect things, in things that turned out well!

Set small, good, perfect things around you, you higher men! Their golden ripeness heals the heart. What is perfect teaches hope.

XVI

What has been the greatest sin here on earth so far? Was it not the word of the one who said, “Woe to those who laugh here!”

Did he find no reasons to laugh on the earth itself? Then he searched badly. Even a child finds reasons here.

He — did not love enough: otherwise he would have loved us too, the laughing ones! But he hated and jeered at us; he promised us weeping and gnashing of teeth.

Must one curse right away where one does not love? That — seems to me bad taste. But that is what he did, this unconditional one. He came from the mob.

And he himself simply did not love enough: otherwise he would have been less angry that people did not love him. All great love does not *want* love: — it wants more.

Get out of the way of all such unconditional ones! They are a poor, sick kind, a mob-kind: they look on this life with ill will, they have the evil eye for this earth.

Get out of the way of all such unconditional ones! They have heavy feet and sultry hearts: — they do not know how to dance. How could the earth be light to such as these!

XVII

All good things come near their goal crookedly. Like cats they arch their backs, they purr inwardly at their coming happiness — all good things laugh.

A person’s stride betrays whether he is already walking on *his own* path: so watch me walk! But whoever comes near his goal, dances.

And, truly, I have not become a statue, nor do I stand there yet, stiff, stolid, stony, a pillar; I love running fast.

And even if there are bogs and thick gloom on earth: whoever has light feet runs on over the mud and dances as on swept ice.

Lift up your hearts, my brothers, high! higher! And do not forget your legs either! Lift up your legs too, you good dancers, and better still: stand on your heads as well!

XVIII

This crown of the laughing one, this rose-wreath crown: I myself set this crown on my head, I myself have canonized my laughter. I found no one else strong enough for it today.

Zarathustra the dancer, Zarathustra the light one, who beckons with his wings, one ready for flight, beckoning to all birds, ready and set, a blissfully lighthearted one: —

Zarathustra the soothsayer, Zarathustra the sooth-laugher, no impatient one, no unconditional one, one who loves leaps and side-leaps; I myself set this crown on my head!

XIX

Lift up your hearts, my brothers, high! higher! And do not forget your legs either! Lift up your legs too, you good dancers, and better still: stand on your heads as well!

There are heavy beasts in happiness too; there are clubfoots from the very beginning. Strangely they toil away, like an elephant toiling to stand on its head.

But better to be foolish with happiness than foolish with misfortune, better to dance clumsily than to walk lamely. So learn this wisdom of mine from me: even the worst thing has two good reverse sides, —

— even the worst thing has good legs for dancing: so learn from me, you higher men, how to stand yourselves on your proper legs!

So unlearn your moping and all mob-sadness! Oh, how sad even the mob’s clowns seem to me today! But this today belongs to the mob.

XX

Do as the wind does when it rushes out of its mountain caves: it wants to dance to its own piping, and the seas tremble and leap under its footsteps.

The one who gives donkeys wings, who milks lionesses, praised be this good, unruly spirit, which comes like a storm wind to all of today and to all the mob, —

— which is the enemy of thistle-heads and fiddle-heads and of all withered leaves and weeds: praised be this wild, good, free storm-spirit, which dances on bogs and glooms as if on meadows!

Which hates the mob’s consumptive curs and all the botched and gloomy brood: praised be this spirit of all free spirits, the laughing storm, which blows dust into the eyes of all the black-sighted and the sore-blighted!

You higher men, the worst thing about you is this: none of you has learned to dance as one must dance — to dance beyond yourselves! What does it matter that you failed!

How much is still possible! So *learn* to laugh beyond yourselves! Lift up your hearts, you good dancers, high! higher! And do not forget good laughter either!

This crown of the laughing one, this rose-wreath crown: to you, my brothers, I throw this crown! I have canonized laughter; you higher men, *learn* — to laugh!
