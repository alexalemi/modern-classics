The Night Song

It is night: now all the leaping fountains speak more loudly. And my soul too is a leaping fountain.

It is night: only now do all the songs of lovers awake. And my soul too is the song of a lover.

Something unstilled, unstillable is in me; it wants to be heard. A craving for love is in me, and it speaks the very language of love.

I am light: ah, that I were night! But this is my solitude, that I am girded round with light.

Ah, that I were dark and of the night! How I would suck at the breasts of light!

And I would bless even you, you little twinkling stars and glowworms up there! — and be blessed in your gifts of light.

But I live in my own light; I drink back into myself the flames that break out of me.

I do not know the happiness of those who take; and I have often dreamed that stealing must be more blessed still than taking.

This is my poverty, that my hand never rests from giving; this is my envy, that I see waiting eyes and the lit-up nights of longing.

Oh, the misery of all who give! Oh, the eclipse of my sun! Oh, the craving to crave! Oh, the ravenous hunger in fullness!

They take from me: but do I still touch their soul? There is a gulf between giving and taking; and the smallest gulf is the last to be bridged.

A hunger grows out of my beauty: I would like to hurt those I shine for, I would like to rob those I have given gifts to: — that is how I hunger for malice.

Pulling back my hand when another hand already reaches out to it; hesitating like the waterfall that hesitates even as it plunges: — that is how I hunger for malice.

Such revenge my fullness devises; such spite wells up out of my solitude.

My happiness in giving died in giving; my virtue grew weary of itself in its overflow!

Whoever always gives is in danger of losing his shame; whoever always hands out has calluses on hand and heart from sheer handing out.

My eye no longer overflows at the shame of those who beg; my hand has grown too hard to feel the trembling of hands that have been filled.

Where have the tears of my eye gone, and the down of my heart? Oh, the solitude of all who give! Oh, the silence of all who shine!

Many suns circle in empty space: to everything that is dark they speak with their light — to me they are silent.

Oh, this is the hostility of light toward what shines: without mercy it travels its paths.

Unjust in its deepest heart toward what shines, cold toward suns — so travels every sun.

Like a storm the suns fly along their paths: that is their traveling. They follow their own relentless will: that is their coldness.

Oh, it is only you, you dark ones, you creatures of the night, who make warmth out of what shines! Oh, only you drink milk and comfort from the udders of light!

Ah, there is ice around me, my hand is burned by what is icy! Ah, there is thirst in me that pines for your thirst!

It is night: ah, that I must be light! And thirst for what belongs to the night! And solitude!

It is night: now my longing breaks out of me like a spring — I long to speak.

It is night: now all the leaping fountains speak more loudly. And my soul too is a leaping fountain.

It is night: only now do all the songs of lovers awake. And my soul too is the song of a lover. —

Thus sang Zarathustra.
