On the New Idol

Somewhere there are still peoples and herds, but not where we are, my brothers: here there are states.

State? What is that? Well then! Now open your ears to me, for now I will tell you my word about the death of peoples.

State is the name of the coldest of all cold monsters. Coldly it lies, too; and this lie crawls out of its mouth: “I, the state, am the people.”

It is a lie! It was creators who created the peoples and hung a faith and a love over them: that is how they served life.

It is destroyers who set traps for the many and call them the state: they hang a sword and a hundred cravings over them.

Where there is still a people, it does not understand the state, and hates it as the evil eye and as a sin against customs and laws.

This sign I give you: every people speaks its own tongue of good and evil, which its neighbor does not understand. It invented its own language for itself in its customs and laws.

But the state lies in every tongue of good and evil; and whatever it says, it lies — and whatever it has, it has stolen.

Everything about it is false; it bites with stolen teeth, this biter. Even its bowels are false.

A confusion of tongues of good and evil: this sign I give you as the sign of the state. Truly, this sign points to the will to death! Truly, it beckons to the preachers of death!

Far too many are born: the state was invented for the superfluous!

Just look how it lures them to itself, the many-too-many! How it gulps them down, and chews them, and chews them again like cud!

“On earth there is nothing greater than I: I am the ordering finger of God” — so roars the monster. And it is not only the long-eared and the short-sighted who sink to their knees!

Ah, even in you, you great souls, it whispers its gloomy lies! Ah, it finds out the rich hearts that like to squander themselves!

Yes, it finds you out too, you conquerors of the old God! You grew weary in the struggle, and now your weariness serves even the new idol!

Heroes and honorable men it would like to set up around itself, the new idol! It loves to bask in the sunshine of good consciences — the cold monster!

It will give *you* everything, if *you* worship it, the new idol: that is how it buys the luster of your virtue and the look in your proud eyes.

It wants to use you as bait for the many-too-many! Yes, a hellish trick has been invented here, a horse of death, jingling in the finery of divine honors!

Yes, a dying for the many has been invented here, which praises itself as life: truly, a service dear to the hearts of all preachers of death!

State I call it, where all are poison-drinkers, the good and the bad: state, where all lose themselves, the good and the bad: state, where the slow suicide of all — is called “life.”

Just look at these superfluous ones! They steal the works of the inventors and the treasures of the wise: culture is what they call their theft — and everything turns to sickness and misery for them!

Just look at these superfluous ones! They are always sick; they vomit their bile and call it a newspaper. They devour one another and cannot even digest themselves.

Just look at these superfluous ones! They acquire riches and become poorer by them. They want power, and first of all the crowbar of power, a lot of money — these men of no means!

Watch them climb, these nimble apes! They climb over one another and so drag each other down into the mud and the depths.

They all want to get to the throne: that is their madness — as if happiness sat on the throne! Often it is mud that sits on the throne — and often the throne sits on mud, too.

Madmen they all are to me, and climbing apes, and overheated. Their idol smells foul to me, the cold monster: they all smell foul to me together, these idol-worshipers.

My brothers, do you want to suffocate in the fumes of their mouths and their cravings? Better to smash the windows and leap out into the open air!

Get out of the way of the bad smell! Go away from the idol-worship of the superfluous!

Get out of the way of the bad smell! Go away from the smoke of these human sacrifices!

The earth still stands open to great souls, even now. Many places are still empty for those who live alone and those who live alone together, places where the scent of quiet seas drifts around.

A free life still stands open to great souls. Truly, whoever possesses little is all the less possessed: praised be a little poverty!

There, where the state ends, there the human being who is not superfluous first begins: there begins the song of the necessary, the one and only, irreplaceable tune.

There, where the state *ends* — look over there, my brothers! Do you not see it, the rainbow and the bridges of the overman? —

Thus spoke Zarathustra.
