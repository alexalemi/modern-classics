On the Thousand and One Goals

Zarathustra saw many lands and many peoples: and so he discovered the good and evil of many peoples. Zarathustra found no greater power on earth than good and evil.

No people could live without first esteeming; but if it wants to preserve itself, it must not esteem as its neighbor esteems.

Much that one people called good, another called scorn and disgrace: so I found it. Much I found called evil here, and there decked out in purple honors.

Never did one neighbor understand another: his soul always marveled at his neighbor’s madness and wickedness.

A tablet of goods hangs over every people. Look, it is the tablet of its overcomings; look, it is the voice of its will to power.

Whatever it finds hard, it counts as praiseworthy; whatever is indispensable and hard is called good; and whatever frees it even from the direst need, the rare thing, the hardest of all — that it praises as holy.

Whatever makes it rule and conquer and shine, to the horror and envy of its neighbor: that it counts as the high, the first, the measure, the meaning of all things.

Truly, my brother, once you have recognized a people’s need and land and sky and neighbor, you can surely guess the law of its overcomings, and why it climbs this ladder toward its hope.

“You shall always be the first and stand out above the others: your jealous soul shall love no one, except the friend” — this made the soul of a Greek tremble: and so he walked his path of greatness.

“To speak the truth and to be good with bow and arrow” — this seemed at once dear and hard to that people from whom my name comes — the name that is at once dear and hard to me.

“To honor father and mother and to do their will down to the root of the soul”: this tablet of overcoming another people hung up over itself, and became mighty and eternal by it.

“To practice loyalty, and for loyalty’s sake to stake honor and blood even on wicked and dangerous causes”: by teaching itself this, another people mastered itself, and by mastering itself this way it became pregnant and heavy with great hopes.

Truly, human beings gave themselves all their good and evil. Truly, they did not take it, they did not find it, it did not fall to them as a voice from heaven.

It was man who first put values into things, to preserve himself — he was the first to create a meaning for things, a human meaning! That is why he calls himself “man”, which means: the esteemer.

Esteeming is creating: hear it, you creators! Esteeming itself is the treasure and the jewel of all esteemed things.

Only through esteeming is there value: and without esteeming, the nut of existence would be hollow. Hear it, you creators!

A change of values — that is a change of the creators. Whoever must be a creator always destroys.

At first the creators were peoples, and only later individuals; truly, the individual himself is still the youngest creation.

Peoples once hung a tablet of the good over themselves. Love that wants to rule and love that wants to obey created such tablets together.

The pleasure in the herd is older than the pleasure in the “I”: and as long as the good conscience is called herd, only the bad conscience says: I.

Truly, the cunning “I”, the loveless one, that wants its own advantage in the advantage of the many: that is not the origin of the herd, but its going under.

It was always lovers and creators who created good and evil. The fire of love glows in the names of all the virtues, and the fire of wrath.

Zarathustra saw many lands and many peoples: Zarathustra found no greater power on earth than the works of the lovers: “good” and “evil” is their name.

Truly, the power of this praising and blaming is a monster. Tell me, brothers, who will subdue it for me? Tell me, who will throw the fetter over the thousand necks of this beast?

There have been a thousand goals so far, for there have been a thousand peoples. Only the fetter for the thousand necks is still missing, the one goal is missing. Humanity still has no goal.

But tell me, my brothers: if humanity still lacks a goal, is it not also still lacking — humanity itself? —

Thus spoke Zarathustra.
