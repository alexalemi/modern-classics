On the Great Longing

Oh my soul, I taught you to say “today” as you say “once” and “formerly,” and to dance your round dance over every Here and There and Yonder.

Oh my soul, I redeemed you from every corner; I swept the dust, the spiders, and the twilight off you.

Oh my soul, I washed the petty shame and the corner-virtue off you and persuaded you to stand naked before the eyes of the sun.

With the storm that is called “spirit” I blew across your heaving sea; I blew all the clouds away; I even strangled the strangler that is called “sin.”

Oh my soul, I gave you the right to say No like the storm, and to say Yes as the open sky says Yes: still as light you stand, and now you walk through denying storms.

Oh my soul, I gave you back your freedom over what is created and what is uncreated: and who knows, as you know it, the lust of what is to come?

Oh my soul, I taught you the contempt that does not come like a gnawing worm, the great, the loving contempt that loves most where it most despises.

Oh my soul, I taught you to persuade so well that you persuade the very grounds to come to you: like the sun, which persuades even the sea up to its own height.

Oh my soul, I took from you all obeying, all bending of the knee, and all saying “Lord”; I myself gave you the names “Turning of Need” and “Destiny.”

Oh my soul, I gave you new names and many-colored playthings; I called you “Destiny” and “Circle of Circles” and “Umbilical Cord of Time” and “Azure Bell.”

Oh my soul, I gave your soil all wisdom to drink, all the new wines, and also all the strong wines of wisdom, old beyond memory.

Oh my soul, I poured every sun on you, and every night and every silence and every longing: — and you grew up for me like a vine.

Oh my soul, overrich and heavy you stand there now, a vine with swelling udders and crowded clusters of brown golden grapes: —

— crowded and pressed by your happiness, waiting out of overabundance, and still bashful about your waiting.

Oh my soul, there is now no soul anywhere that is more loving and more embracing and more encompassing! Where would future and past be closer together than in you?

Oh my soul, I have given you everything, and all my hands have grown empty on you: — and now! Now you say to me, smiling and full of melancholy: “Which of us has to give thanks? —

— does the giver not have to give thanks that the receiver received? Is giving not a need? Is receiving not — mercy?” —

Oh my soul, I understand the smile of your melancholy: your overwealth itself is now stretching out longing hands!

Your fullness gazes out over roaring seas and seeks and waits; the longing of overfullness gazes out of the smiling sky of your eyes!

And truly, oh my soul! Who could see your smile and not melt into tears? The angels themselves melt into tears at the overgoodness of your smile.

It is your goodness and overgoodness that will not complain and weep: and yet, oh my soul, your smile longs for tears, and your trembling mouth for sobs.

“Is not all weeping a complaining? And all complaining an accusing?” So you speak to yourself, and that is why, oh my soul, you would rather smile than pour out your sorrow —

— pour out in rushing tears all your sorrow over your fullness, and over all the vine’s urgent craving for the vintner and the vintner’s knife!

But if you will not weep, will not weep out your purple melancholy, then you will have to *sing*, oh my soul! — Look, I myself am smiling as I foretell this to you:

— sing, with a roaring song, until all the seas grow still to listen to your longing —

— until over still, longing seas the boat floats, the golden wonder, around whose gold all good, bad, and wondrous things leap: —

— many animals too, great and small, and everything that has light, wondrous feet, so that it can run on violet-blue paths —

— toward the golden wonder, the boat that comes of its own will, and toward its master: but that is the vintner, who waits with his diamond knife —

— your great deliverer, oh my soul, the nameless one — — for whom only future songs will find names! And truly, your breath is already fragrant with future songs —

— already you glow and dream, already you drink thirstily at all the deep, ringing wells of comfort, already your melancholy rests in the bliss of future songs! —

Oh my soul, now I have given you everything, even my last, and all my hands have grown empty on you: — *that I told you to sing*, look, that was my last!

That I told you to sing — speak now, speak: *which* of us now has to — give thanks? — But better still: sing to me, sing, oh my soul! And let me give thanks! —

Thus spoke Zarathustra.
