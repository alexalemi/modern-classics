The Grave Song

“There is the island of graves, the silent one; there too are the graves of my youth. There I will carry an evergreen wreath of life.”

Resolving this in my heart, I sailed over the sea. —

Oh you visions and apparitions of my youth! Oh, all you glances of love, you divine twinklings of an eye! How quickly you died on me! I remember you today as I remember my dead.

From you, my dearest dead, a sweet scent comes to me, a scent that loosens the heart and the tears. Truly, it shakes and loosens the heart of the one who sails alone.

I am still the richest and the most to be envied — I, the most solitary! For I *had* you after all, and you have me still: tell me, to whom did such rosy apples fall from the tree as fell to me?

I am still the heir of your love and the earth it was left to, blooming in your memory with motley, wild-growing virtues, oh you most beloved!

Ah, we were made to stay close to each other, you sweet strange wonders; and you did not come to me and my desire like timid birds — no, but as the trusting to the one who trusts!

Yes, made for faithfulness, like me, and for tender eternities: must I now name you after your faithlessness, you divine glances and twinklings: I have not yet learned any other name.

Truly, you died on me too quickly, you fugitives. Yet you did not flee from me, nor did I flee from you: we are innocent toward each other in our faithlessness.

To kill *me*, they strangled you, you songbirds of my hopes! Yes, at you, my dearest ones, malice always shot its arrows — to hit my heart!

And it hit! For you were always what was dearest to my heart, my possession and what possessed me: *that* is why you had to die young, and far too early!

At the most vulnerable thing I had they shot the arrow: that was you, whose skin is like down, and still more like the smile that dies at a glance!

But this word I will speak to my enemies: what is all the murder of men compared with what you did to me!

You did me a greater evil than any murder of men; you took from me what can never be brought back: — this is how I speak to you, my enemies!

For you murdered the visions of my youth and my dearest wonders! You took my playmates from me, the blessed spirits! To their memory I lay down this wreath and this curse.

This curse on you, my enemies! For you made what was eternal in me short, as a tone breaks off in a cold night! It came to me only as a gleam of divine eyes — as a twinkling of an eye!

Thus spoke my purity once, in a good hour: “All beings shall be divine to me.”

Then you fell upon me with filthy ghosts; ah, where has that good hour fled now!

“All days shall be holy to me” — so the wisdom of my youth once spoke: truly, the words of a joyful wisdom!

But then you enemies stole my nights from me and sold them to sleepless torment: ah, where has that joyful wisdom fled now?

Once I longed for lucky omens from the birds: then you led an owl-monster across my path, a repulsive one. Ah, where did my tender longing flee then?

Once I vowed to renounce all disgust: then you turned my near ones and my neighbors into boils. Ah, where did my noblest vow flee then?

Once I walked blessed paths like a blind man: then you threw filth on the blind man’s path: and now the old footpath of the blind disgusts him.

And when I did my hardest thing and celebrated the victory of my overcomings: then you made those who loved me cry out that I was hurting them most of all.

Truly, that was always your doing: you made my best honey bitter for me, and the labor of my best bees.

To my charity you always sent the most insolent beggars; around my pity you always crowded the incurably shameless. In this way you wounded my virtue in its faith.

And if I laid down my holiest thing as an offering, at once your “piety” set its fatter gifts beside it: so that my holiest thing was smothered in the smoke of your fat.

And once I wanted to dance as I had never danced before: I wanted to dance away over all the heavens. Then you talked my dearest singer over to your side.

And now he struck up a dreadful, muffled tune; ah, he droned it into my ears like a gloomy horn!

Murderous singer, tool of malice, most innocent one! I was already standing ready for the best dance: then with your tones you murdered my rapture!

Only in the dance do I know how to speak the parable of the highest things: — and now my highest parable remained unspoken in my limbs!

Unspoken and unredeemed my highest hope remained! And all the visions and consolations of my youth died on me!

How did I ever bear it? How did I get over and overcome such wounds? How did my soul rise again from these graves?

Yes, there is something in me that cannot be wounded, cannot be buried, something that bursts rocks apart: it is called *my will*. It strides silent and unchanged through the years.

It wants to walk its own way on my feet, my old will; hard-hearted is its temper, and it cannot be wounded.

I cannot be wounded except in my heel. You live there still and are the same as ever, most patient one! Still you broke out through every grave!

In you lives still what was unredeemed in my youth; and as life and youth you sit here, hoping, on yellow ruins of graves.

Yes, you are still the shatterer of all graves for me: Hail to you, my will! And only where there are graves are there resurrections. —

Thus sang Zarathustra. —
