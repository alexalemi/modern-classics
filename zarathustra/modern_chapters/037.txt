On the Land of Culture

I flew too far into the future: a horror came over me.

And when I looked around me, look! time was my only contemporary.

Then I fled backward, homeward — and faster and faster: and so I came to you, you people of the present, and into the land of culture.

For the first time I brought an eye for you along, and good desire: truly, I came with longing in my heart.

But what happened to me? Frightened as I was — I had to laugh! Never had my eye seen anything so speckled with color!

I laughed and laughed, while my foot was still trembling, and my heart too: “Why, this is the home of all the paint pots!” — I said.

Painted with fifty splotches on your faces and limbs: that is how you sat there, to my amazement, you people of the present!

And with fifty mirrors around you, flattering your play of colors and repeating it after you!

Truly, you could wear no better mask, you people of the present, than your own face! Who could ever — *recognize* you!

Written all over with the signs of the past, and those signs painted over with new signs: that is how well you have hidden yourselves from all interpreters of signs!

And even if one is a searcher of the innermost parts: who still believes that you have any innermost parts! You seem to be baked out of paint and scraps of glued paper.

All ages and peoples look out motley from your veils; all customs and faiths speak motley from your gestures.

Whoever took away your veils and wraps and colors and gestures would have just enough left over to scare the birds.

Truly, I myself am the scared bird that once saw you naked and without color; and I flew away when the skeleton beckoned to me with love.

I would rather be a day laborer in the underworld, among the shades of what once was! — why, even the dwellers of the underworld are plumper and fuller than you!

This, yes, this is bitterness to my bowels: that I can stand you neither naked nor clothed, you people of the present!

Everything unhomely in the future, and whatever ever made strayed birds shudder, is truly homelier and cozier than your “reality.”

For this is how you talk: “We are wholly real, and without faith or superstition”: that is how you puff out your chests — ah, and without even having chests!

Yes, how could you be *able* to believe, you speckled ones! — you who are paintings of everything that was ever believed!

You are walking refutations of faith itself, and the breaking of every thought’s limbs. *Unbelievable*: that is what *I* call you, you real ones!

All the ages chatter against one another in your spirits; and the dreams and chatter of all the ages were more real still than your waking is!

You are barren: *that is why* you lack faith. But whoever had to create always had his truth-dreams and star-signs too — and believed in believing! —

You are half-open gates at which gravediggers wait. And this is *your* reality: “Everything deserves to perish.”

Ah, how you stand there before me, you barren ones, how lean in the ribs! And many of you have surely noticed it yourselves.

And he said: “Did some god perhaps steal something from me secretly while I slept? Truly, enough to form himself a little woman from it!

Amazing is the poverty of my ribs!” So many a person of the present has already spoken.

Yes, you make me laugh, you people of the present! And especially when you marvel at yourselves!

And woe to me if I could not laugh at your marveling, and had to drink down everything repulsive in your bowls!

But as it is, I will take you more lightly, since I have something *heavy* to carry; and what does it matter to me if beetles and winged worms settle on my bundle as well!

Truly, it shall not grow any heavier for me on that account! And it is not from you, you people of the present, that my great weariness shall come. — Ah, where shall I climb now with my longing! From every mountain I look out for fatherlands and motherlands.

But I have found a home nowhere: I am unsettled in every settlement and a departure at every gate.

Strangers to me, and a mockery, are the people of the present, to whom my heart drove me not long ago; and I am driven out of fatherlands and motherlands.

So now I love only my *children’s land*, the undiscovered one, in the farthest sea: I bid my sails search and search for it.

To my children I will make up for being the child of my fathers: and to all the future — for *this* present!

Thus spoke Zarathustra.
