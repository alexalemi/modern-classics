Chapter 14: That God Watches Everything

Somebody asked him how a man could become convinced that everything he does is under God's eye. He answered:

Do you not think that all things are bound together into one?

"I do," the man replied.

Well, do you not think that earthly things are naturally in agreement and in union with heavenly ones?

"I do."

And how else, if not at God's command, do plants flower when He tells them to flower? When He tells them to put out shoots, do they not shoot? When He tells them to bear fruit, how else do they bear it? When He tells the fruit to ripen, does it not ripen? When He tells them to let the fruit fall, how else do they let it fall? When to drop their leaves, do they not drop them? And when He tells them to fold themselves up and stay quiet and rest, how else do they stay quiet and rest? And how else, as the moon waxes and wanes and the sun draws near and goes away again, do we see such great changes and reversals in the things of the earth?

Now if plants and our bodies are so tied and united to the whole, are our souls not far more so? And if our souls are tied to God and in contact with Him as parts and portions of Him, does God not feel every movement of these parts as a movement of His own, born along with Him?

Consider: you are able to think about the divine administration and about all things divine, and at the same time about human affairs; to be moved by ten thousand things at once in your senses and your understanding; to assent to some, to dissent from others, and about others again to suspend judgment. You hold in your soul so many impressions of so many different things, and, moved by them, you arrive at notions like the ones first impressed on you; you keep hold of numerous arts and the memory of ten thousand things. And is God not able to oversee everything, and to be present with everything, and to receive from everything a certain communication?

The sun can light up so large a part of the whole, and leave so little unlit — only the part the earth's shadow covers. And He who made the sun itself, and keeps it going round, and who is a small part of Him compared with the whole — can He not perceive everything?

"But I cannot take all of this in at once," the man may say.

And who told you that you have power equal to Zeus? All the same, He has set beside every man a guardian, each man's own guardian spirit, and put him in that spirit's charge — a guardian who never sleeps and is never deceived. To what better or more careful guardian could He have entrusted each of us?

So when you have shut the doors and made it dark inside, remember never to say that you are alone. You are not: God is within, and your guardian spirit is within, and what need have they of light to see what you are doing?

To this God you ought to swear an oath, just as soldiers swear to Caesar. But they, who are hired for pay, swear to put Caesar's safety above everything. And you, who have received so many and such great favours — will you not swear? Or having sworn, will you not stand by it? And what will you swear? Never to disobey, never to bring charges, never to find fault with anything He has given, and never to do or suffer unwillingly anything that must be. Is this oath like the soldiers'? They swear to put no man above Caesar; here men swear to put themselves above everything.
