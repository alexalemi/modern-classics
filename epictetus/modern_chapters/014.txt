Chapter 13: How to Do Everything in a Way That Pleases the Gods

Somebody asked how a man might eat in a way that pleases the gods. He answered:

If he can eat justly and contentedly, evenly, moderately and in good order, will that not please the gods as well? And when you have asked for hot water and the slave has not heard you, or has heard and brought it only lukewarm, or is not even to be found in the house — then not to be irritated, not to burst with temper, is that not pleasing to the gods?

"But how is a man to put up with people like that?"

Slave yourself — will you not put up with your own brother, who has Zeus for his ancestor, who is like a son sprung from the same seed and the same descent from above? And if you have been put in some higher position, are you going to make a tyrant of yourself straight away? Will you not remember who you are, and whom you are ruling? That they are your kinsmen, brothers by nature, the offspring of Zeus?

"But I bought them. They did not buy me."

Do you see which way you are looking? Toward the earth, toward the pit, toward these wretched laws of dead men — and not toward the laws of the gods.
