Chapter 11: More Miscellaneous Remarks

There are certain penalties fixed, as if by law, for men who disobey the divine administration. Whoever thinks anything good except what depends on the will — let him envy, let him crave, let him flatter, let him be thrown about. Whoever thinks anything else evil — let him grieve, let him lament, let him weep, let him be unhappy. And yet, punished as severely as that, we cannot stop.

Remember what the poet says about the stranger:

	Stranger, I must not — not even if a worse man comes.

This can be applied to a father as well: I must not, even if a worse man than you should come, treat a father unworthily; for all fathers come from Zeus the father. And the same about a brother, for all come from Zeus who presides over kinsmen. And so in the other relations of life we shall find Zeus watching.
