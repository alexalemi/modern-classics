Chapter 18: Why No News Should Disturb You

When something is reported to you that is of a kind to disturb you, have this principle ready: the news is about nothing within the power of your will.

Can any man report to you that you have formed a bad judgment, or had a bad desire? Not at all. But perhaps he will report that somebody has died. What is that to you? He may report that somebody is speaking ill of you. What is that to you? Or that your father is plotting something. Against whom? Against your will? How could he? Against your poor body, then, and your little property? You are perfectly safe: it is not against you.

"But the judge declares that you have committed an act of irreverence." And did the judges not make the same declaration about Socrates? Does it concern you that the judge has made the declaration? No. Then why trouble yourself about it any longer?

Your father has a certain duty, and if he does not carry it out he loses the character of a father, of a man with natural affection, of a gentle man. Do not wish him to lose anything else on that account. For a man never does wrong in one thing and suffers in another.

And on your side, it is your duty to make your defence firmly, with self-respect, and without anger; and if you do not, you too lose the character of a son, of a man of decent behaviour, of a generous man.

Well then, is the judge out of danger? No — he is in equal danger. So why are you still afraid of his verdict? What have you to do with somebody else's evil? Making a bad defence is your own evil; guard against that alone. Being condemned or not condemned is another man's act, and so it is another man's evil.

Somebody threatens you. Me? No. He blames you. Let him see to how he manages his own affairs. He is going to condemn you unjustly. He is a wretched man.
