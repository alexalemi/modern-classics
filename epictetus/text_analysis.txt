TEXT ANALYSIS — The Discourses (Epictetus, recorded by Arrian, c. 108 AD)
Source: George Long's translation (1877), from Standard Ebooks'
epictetus_discourses_george-long. The complete four books, 95 chapters,
119k words. Gutenberg has only a selection (#10661).

READ enchiridion/text_analysis.txt AND enchiridion/running_notes.txt
FIRST. The Enchiridion is an abridgement of THIS book — Arrian compiled
it out of these lectures — so the two editions must agree, and the
Enchiridion's locked vocabulary governs here without renegotiation.


WHY THIS BOOK, AND WHY THE USUAL TEST SAYS NO
=============================================
This project normally justifies a retelling by archaism density. On
that measure Long FAILS, and not narrowly:

    Bunyan (source)                  13.11 archaisms per 1,000 words
    Hunt's Grimm                     12.34
    Long's Discourses                 1.00
    Jacobs' English Fairy Tales       0.90   <- STRUCK from the roadmap
                                              in 2026-08 as too clean

Mean sentence 22.8 words, 19% over 35 — unremarkable. By every number
this project has ever used to choose a book, Long is fine and should be
left alone.

He is not fine, and the reason is a class this project has not met.
Long translates the Greek word by word, and the words he picks are
ordinary modern English that means something ELSE:

    phantasia    -> "appearance"     (it is an impression, what shows up
                                      in the mind, not how a thing looks)
    prohairesis  -> "will"           (it is the faculty of choosing, the
                                      part of you that assents — not
                                      willpower and not volition)
    dogma        -> "opinion"        (a settled judgment you live by, not
                                      a view you happen to hold)
    dunamis      -> "faculty"        (a capacity; the English word now
                                      says "department of a university")
    prolepsis    -> "precognition"   (a preconception, the rough idea of
                                      "good" everyone starts with)
    ataraxia     -> "perturbation"   (in the negative: freedom from being
                                      thrown, i.e. peace of mind)
    apatheia     -> "indifference"   (not not-caring; not being ruled by
                                      what you cannot control)

Those seven run to 4.99 per 1,000 words — five times his archaism rate.
A reader trips on "thou hast" and knows to look it up. A reader meets
"the right use of appearances" and reads straight past it having
understood nothing, or worse, having understood the opposite: that
Stoicism is about how things look. It is the bunyan justification —
the book does not obstruct, it MISLEADS — but sharper, because here the
misleading words are the technical vocabulary of the argument itself.
Every one of them is load-bearing and every one of them recurs hundreds
of times.

THIS IS A THIRD JUSTIFICATION CLASS, and worth naming for the roadmap:
  1. HARD — Burton, Leviathan. Archaism you can feel.
  2. FALSE FRIENDS — Bunyan, Grimm. Ordinary words that have moved
     ("conversation" = conduct, "want" = lack, "prevent" = go before).
  3. TECHNICAL CALQUE — this book. A translator renders a foreign
     term with an English word that looks transparent and is not, and
     then uses it consistently for 119,000 words.
Class 3 is invisible to the archaism measure by construction. Do not
conclude from Long's 1.00 that other philosophical translations are
safe; measure the technical vocabulary separately.

THE SECOND REASON, AND IT IS THE COMMERCIAL ONE: everyone has read the
Enchiridion and almost nobody has read the Discourses. The Enchiridion
is fifty numbered rules with the arguments taken out. This is the man
himself, in a room, being interrupted.


WHAT THIS IS
============
Not a book. Arrian's shorthand notes of an actual classroom in
Nicopolis, published without Epictetus' involvement — he says so
himself in the dedicatory letter that opens this edition, insisting he
did not write them and did not make them public. What survives is four
of an original eight books.

Epictetus was born enslaved in Phrygia, owned in Rome by Epaphroditus
(a freedman of Nero's), lame in one leg, freed at some point after
Nero's death, expelled from Rome with the other philosophers by
Domitian around AD 93, and taught for the rest of his life in a Greek
provincial town to the sons of the Roman upper class. He owned almost
nothing and had no wife and no children. Everything in the tone
follows from that: he is teaching privilege to people who have it,
about the one thing he had and they did not.


STYLE AND TONE OF THE ORIGINAL
==============================
- IT IS SPEECH, AND MUST SOUND LIKE SPEECH. The Greek form is the
  diatribe — the informal lecture — and the whole book is a man talking
  out loud: starting a sentence again, answering a heckler, breaking off
  to address a student by name, losing patience. Long flattens all of
  this into level Victorian prose. Restoring it is most of the job.
- THE IMAGINED OBJECTOR is everywhere and Long punctuates him badly.
  "But my nose runs. — What have you hands for, slave, if not to wipe
  it?" Set these as real exchanges. The reader must always know who is
  speaking; where Long leaves it ambiguous, decide and commit.
- HE IS FUNNY, and the comedy is load-bearing: the philosopher who
  weeps over spilled oil, the man who wants his beard admired, the
  athlete who won't train, Epictetus asking a student whether he came
  to be flattered or to be improved. Play it straight and it lands.
- HOMELY, PHYSICAL EXAMPLES, always: the tunic, the lamp stolen in the
  night, the ass and the pack-saddle, the ship at anchor and the
  shellfish gathered on shore, the fig that ripens in its own time,
  the smoke that means it is time to leave the room. Keep every one
  concrete. Never abstract an image into the point it illustrates.
- SECOND PERSON, IMPERATIVE, RELENTLESS — as in the Enchiridion, but
  with the arguments left in.
- CONTRAST WITH THE ENCHIRIDION, and do not flatten them together.
  The Enchiridion is a drill book: compressed, aphoristic, no working
  shown. The Discourses are the classroom the drills came out of, and
  are therefore LOOSER, warmer, more argumentative and much funnier.
  A reader who knows the handbook should meet a recognisable voice
  doing something different, not the handbook padded out.


LOCKED VOCABULARY — INHERITED, NOT RENEGOTIATED
===============================================
From enchiridion/running_notes.txt. Caps spent; do not re-gloss.
  - "up to us / not up to us" for the core distinction
  - "impression" for phantasia (Long: appearance, semblance)
  - "judgments" (or "views" for rhythm) for dogmata
  - "desire and avoidance" as the recurring pair
  - "in line with nature" for "conformable to nature"
  - "your will" / "what you choose" for prohairesis
  - "make progress" for prokopto (Long: proficiency, improvement)
  - "blocked / blocks" for hindered, impediment
  - "given back" (not "restored") for the give-it-back language
  - "slave" where the meaning is doulos, not "servant"

NEW, and locked here because the Discourses need terms the handbook
never had to name:
  - "preconception" for prolepsis (Long: precognition). NOT
    "presupposition" and not "innate idea".
  - "peace of mind" for ataraxia / "freedom from perturbation".
    NOTE the Enchiridion locked "peace and tranquility" for its own
    §12 pair; that stays as it is, and this is the general term.
  - "capacity" or plainly "what you can do" for dunamis, NOT "faculty"
    — except "the rational faculty" in I.1, where the phrase is
    Epictetus defining his own term and is glossed once on the spot.
  - "assent" kept, and glossed once, at its first real use: saying yes
    to an impression. It is the hinge of the whole psychology and there
    is no plainer English word that does the work.
  - "the governing part" for hegemonikon, glossed once as the mind's
    command centre. NOT "ruling faculty".
  - "externals" kept — Long's word, and it has entered English.
  - "indifferent things" kept, glossed once at II.6: things that are
    neither good nor bad in themselves.
  - "proper acts" / "what is fitting" for kathekon, NOT "duties" alone,
    where the argument turns on the technical sense.
  - "the Cynics" and "the Cynic life" for Long's "Cynism".
  - God / the gods / Zeus / Providence: keep exactly as the source has
    them, singular or plural as he says it. Do NOT Christianise and do
    NOT scrub. Epictetus slides between "God", "the gods" and "Zeus"
    inside a paragraph and means to.


CHALLENGES SPECIFIC TO THIS TEXT
================================
- LONG'S PARENTHETICAL GLOSSES. He writes "confidence (courage)",
  "tranquillity (freedom from perturbation)", "proficiency (progress)"
  — two shots at a word he could not settle. Pick one and drop the
  parenthesis. Keeping both is the single easiest way to make this
  edition read like the one it replaces.
- HIS TITLES ARE ALREADY FIXED, all 95 of them, in prep.py's TITLES
  table (the quixote rule). Use the heading exactly as the manifest
  gives it. Do not re-title while translating.
- THE ARGUMENTS ARE REAL ARGUMENTS and several are technical Stoic
  logic (I.7 on hypotheticals, the chapters on syllogisms). Do not
  smooth a step out of an argument to make a sentence flow. Where Long
  is obscure because the Greek is obscure, consult reference/notes.txt
  — Long usually says so there, often in the form "I am not sure that I
  have understood rightly."
- QUOTATIONS ARE VERBATIM AND VERSE STAYS VERSE. Homer, Euripides,
  Hesiod and Cleanthes are quoted throughout; prep has set them
  tab-indented with their attributions. The Cleanthes prayer appears
  twice here and once in our Enchiridion, and MUST match the locked
  Enchiridion rendering exactly: "Lead me, Zeus, and you too, Destiny,".
- THE ONE DRAMA QUOTATION (II.22, Polynices and Eteocles from
  Euripides' Phoenissae) is set as tab-indented speaker lines. Speaker
  tags are Title Case, never all-caps — assemble.py reads an all-caps
  line as a section heading (the galileo lesson).
- LONG'S 795 ENDNOTES ARE NOT TRANSLATED. They are in reference/
  notes.txt as a crib (the bunyan/Offor precedent). They are a Victorian
  translator's apparatus — citations to Schweighauser, Greek textual
  argument, and his own doubts — not Epictetus. Draw on them only where
  a modern reader genuinely cannot follow, and prefer an inline half-
  clause to a bracketed note. Aim for very few notes in the whole book.
- LONG'S INTRODUCTION IS KEPT AND TRANSLATED, in HIS register: a
  Victorian scholar's preface, plainer and more distant than Epictetus,
  and it is where the reader learns who Arrian was and how the text
  survived. Do not import the classroom warmth into it. (The candle/
  rule for Crookes's notes.)


SENSITIVE CONTENT — scholarly modernization of a canonical text
===============================================================
The Verne rule throughout: these are HIS claims, rendered as his,
unhedged and unsoftened, with no editorial wink.
- SLAVERY IS EVERYWHERE, and it is not incidental: Epictetus was
  himself enslaved, he addresses students who own people, and his
  central metaphor is that the free man may be a slave and the slave
  free. "Slave" where the meaning is doulos, plainly, with no
  euphemism. He also uses "slave" as an insult to a free student
  ("What have you hands for, slave?") and that jolt is deliberate —
  keep it. Do not soften the passages where enslaved people are
  examples of property alongside a farm or a horse; that is the world
  he is arguing inside, and the argument only lands if it is visible.
- HIS OWN DISABILITY: he was lame, and says so lightly ("Lameness is
  an impediment to the leg, but not to the will"). Keep the lightness.
- SUICIDE, repeatedly and approvingly — "the door is open", "the house
  is smoky and I leave it". This is a real and serious Stoic position
  argued at length (I.9, I.24, I.25, II.15, III.13). Translate it in
  full and in his voice. Do NOT add a modern note or a helpline; that
  would be the wink the Verne rule forbids and would misrepresent a
  philosophical argument as a symptom. Note that he also argues AGAINST
  leaving without reason, and forbids it in I.9 — keep both sides at
  full strength.
- WOMEN, and the ancient norms around them: II.4 (the adulterer),
  III.1, the Encheiridion-style remarks on women being valued for
  appearance, and III.22 on the Cynic's marriage. Render the argument
  as his argument, in a neutral historical register.
- THE BODY, bluntly: bathing, defecation, sex, the beard, snot. Long is
  prim about it. Epictetus is not, and the plainness is part of the
  philosophy — the body is externals. Do not restore Long's primness.


FAMOUS PASSAGES — locked renderings (see must_contain.txt)
==========================================================
- I.1: "Some things are up to us, and some are not." (matching the
  Enchiridion's locked opening.)
- I.1, on the tyrant: "I will fetter you." / "What are you saying? Me?
  You will fetter my leg, but not even Zeus can conquer my will."
- I.16, the closing: if he were a nightingale he would sing as a
  nightingale, a swan as a swan; being a rational creature, he must
  sing hymns to God — "this is my work; I do it, and I will not desert
  this post as long as I am allowed to keep it."
- I.24: "Remember that the door is open."
- I.25, the smoky room: "Has anyone made smoke in the house? If it is
  moderate, I will stay; if too much, I go out. For you must remember
  and hold to this, that the door is open."
- II.5: "the ship at anchor" and the shells and roots gathered on
  shore — the passenger who must drop everything and run when the
  captain calls, and who never lets go of his attention.
- III.22: the Cynic as "a scout sent from God to men" — and the
  Cynic's answer that he has no house, no wife, no children, and no
  slave, and is happier than any of them.
- IV.1 opening: "He is free who lives as he wishes to live."
- The Cleanthes prayer, exactly as the Enchiridion has it:
  "Lead me, Zeus, and you too, Destiny,"


RATIO
=====
EN -> EN modernization, as with the Royal Institution lectures and
bunyan: unstacking Long's Latinate syntax costs about what cutting his
throat-clearing saves. Expect ~0.95-1.05.
    python3 verify.py epictetus --min-ratio 0.85 --max-ratio 1.3
Under 0.90 on a file means a step of an argument has been dropped, not
that the prose got tighter. This is a book of arguments; the low bound
is the one that matters.
