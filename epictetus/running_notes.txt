EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES — RUNNING NOTES (the consistency ledger)
Read text_analysis.txt first, and enchiridion/running_notes.txt before
that. This file records what is LOCKED. The orchestrator updates it
between batches; it is not edited while translating a file.


THE INHERITANCE
===============
The Enchiridion is an abridgement of this book, it is already published,
and its ledger governs here. Caps spent, no re-gloss:

  up to us / not up to us        the core distinction
  impression                     phantasia (Long: appearance, semblance)
  judgments (or views)           dogmata
  desire and avoidance           the recurring pair
  in line with nature            "conformable to nature"
  your will / what you choose    prohairesis
  make progress                  prokopto (Long: proficiency)
  blocked / blocks               hindered, impediment
  given back                     the give-it-back language
  slave                          doulos — never "servant"
  puffed up                      elated

Tone calibration inherited: tough, plain-spoken coach; bracing, never
preachy, never self-help-smooth; second person imperative.

AND THE ONE PLACE THE INHERITANCE MUST BEND. The Enchiridion is a drill
book and reads like one. This is the classroom it came out of, so the
register is LOOSER, warmer, more argumentative and much funnier. Same
man, more air. A reader who knows the handbook should recognise the
voice and notice it is doing something different.


LOCKED HERE (new terms the handbook never had to name)
======================================================
  preconception       prolepsis (Long: precognition)
  peace of mind       ataraxia / "freedom from perturbation"
  capacity            dunamis — NOT "faculty", except "the rational
                      faculty" at I.1, which Epictetus is defining
  assent              kept, glossed ONCE at first real use: saying yes
                      to an impression
  the governing part  hegemonikon, glossed once as the mind's command
                      centre. NOT "ruling faculty"
  externals           kept (Long's word, and it is English now)
  indifferent things  kept, glossed once at II.6
  proper acts /       kathekon, where the technical sense is in play
    what is fitting
  the Cynics /        Long's "Cynism"
    the Cynic life

God / the gods / Zeus / Providence: exactly as the source has them,
singular or plural as he says it, sliding between them inside a
paragraph as he does. No Christianising, no scrubbing.


CONVENTIONS
===========
1. THE HEADING IS FIXED. Every file opens with the exact heading the
   manifest gives it ("Chapter 12: On Training"), then a blank line,
   then the translation. All 95 titles were decided in advance in
   prep.py's TITLES table. Do not re-title while translating.
2. Multi-part files (III.22, III.24, IV.1): heading line, then
   "(Part n of k)", then the body. Parts 2+ never re-introduce the
   chapter; non-final parts never conclude it.
3. MARKUP-FREE. No asterisks, no underscores, no markdown. Tab indent
   = verse or a speaker table; ALL CAPS = heading, so never write an
   all-caps line. Book and play titles go in plain text.
4. Verse stays tab-indented, one line per line, with its attribution
   as a final tab-indented line ("\t— Iliad, i 526.").
5. Drama speaker tags are Title Case, never all-caps.
6. Long's parenthetical double-shots — "confidence (courage)",
   "tranquillity (freedom from perturbation)" — are resolved to one
   word. Keeping both is the fastest way to sound like the translation
   we are replacing.


DECIDED IN ADVANCE
==================
SUICIDE. Argued at length and approvingly in I.9, I.24, I.25, II.15 and
III.13 — "the door is open", the smoky room. Translated in full, in his
voice, with no modern note and no helpline: that would be the wink the
Verne rule forbids, and would recast a philosophical position as a
symptom. He also forbids leaving without reason (I.9, where he stops a
class that has talked itself into it) — keep BOTH sides at full
strength, because the pair is the actual doctrine.

SLAVERY. Not incidental and not a metaphor only: he was enslaved, his
students owned people, and enslaved people appear in his examples as
property beside a farm or a horse. Rendered plainly, "slave" for
doulos, no euphemism, nothing cut. His use of "slave" as an insult to a
free student is deliberate and stays.

THE BODY. Long is prim; Epictetus is not, and the plainness is the
philosophy. Do not restore Long's primness.


LOG — decisions made while translating
======================================

FILE 002 (I.1), THE VOICE FILE. Ratio 0.96. Locked by it:

- THE OBJECTOR IS QUOTED; EPICTETUS IS NOT. Long runs both sides
  together in one undifferentiated paragraph, which is where most of
  the book's difficulty actually lives — the reader cannot tell who is
  talking. So: the heckler, the student and the tyrant get quotation
  marks and a paragraph of their own; Epictetus answers unquoted, also
  on his own line. "I will throw you in prison." / My poor body, you
  mean. Unambiguous, and it puts the comic timing back.
  Use it ONLY for genuine exchanges. His own rhetorical questions
  answered in the same breath stay inline ("I have to die. Do I have to
  die groaning as well?") — that is one voice, not two.
- "capacity" for dunamis throughout; "the rational faculty" appears
  once, at its definition, flagged as a name being given ("Reason — the
  rational faculty, to give it its name"), and never again.
- "blocked" for every one of Long's hindered / impediment / hindrance.
  It has to be the same word each time: the claim that nothing can
  block your will is the book's thesis and it recurs hundreds of times.
- Diminutives KEPT and are affectionate-dismissive, not cute: "this
  little body of yours", "this little property of yours", "my poor
  body". They are Epictetus' habit of shrinking externals by naming
  them small.
- "my good man" for Long's vocatives to a student. Not "my friend"
  (too warm), not "sir" (too formal).
- Gods plural and Zeus singular both stand exactly as the source has
  them, including inside one paragraph.
- SHORT LINES MUST END IN A STOP. "To exile." not "To exile" —
  assemble.is_subheading rejects a line ending in ".;:,—", so a
  terminal period is the difference between dialogue and a spurious
  section heading. This bit ball/ (68 headings) in the other
  direction; here it bites the dialogue.

PINNED IN THIS FILE (must_contain): "Some things are up to us" — placed
where the doctrine is actually stated, which in I.1 is the paragraph on
what the gods put in our power, not the opening line as in the
Enchiridion. And "not even Zeus can conquer my will".


FILES 000, 001, 003, 004. Ratios 0.90, 0.94, 0.94, 0.98.

- LONG'S INTRODUCTION IS IN HIS OWN REGISTER, not Epictetus'. A
  Victorian scholar: careful, dry, first person, personally modest.
  His "This was rather a great undertaking for an old man, who is now
  past seventy-six" and "I hope I have not made many blunders. I do
  not suppose I have made none" are the best things in it and are
  kept exactly. The candle/ rule for Crookes's notes.
- FOUR SOURCE SLIPS CORRECTED SILENTLY in the introduction, all of
  them transcription rather than Long: "Denionax" -> Demonax,
  "Jean Jacques Gamier" -> Garnier (the rn/m confusion), "Johann
  Johann Schweighauser" -> one Johann, "6 volums" -> six volumes.
  None is a claim of Long's, so the Verne rule does not reach them.
- GREEK IS ROMANISED in the introduction. The titles arrive as
  Ἐπικτήτου διατριβαί and Ἐγχειρίδιον; set as "Diatribai, the
  Discourses" and "the Encheiridion, or Manual". A reader of a plain-
  text edition should not have to step over an alphabet to learn what
  the book is called. Epictetus' own text has no Greek in it.
- "Mrs. Elizabeth Carter" -> "Elizabeth Carter", then "Carter", as any
  other scholar would be cited. Long's respect for her survives in
  what he says about her, which is warm and is kept in full.
- THIRD-PARTY DIALOGUE IS QUOTED ON BOTH SIDES (Agrippinus and Florus,
  Priscus and Vespasian, the athlete and his brother). The
  quoted-objector/unquoted-Epictetus rule from file 002 applies only
  where Epictetus himself is one of the two voices.
- A SPEECH IN WHICH ONE MAN PLAYS BOTH PARTS keeps its em dashes
  inside the quotation ("— But if I don't act in the tragedy, my head
  comes off. — Then go and act. I will not."). Agrippinus is
  ventriloquising Florus, and breaking it into separate quoted
  paragraphs would hand half his speech to another man.
- THE CHAMBER POT AND THE ATHLETE'S GENITALS are rendered plainly.
  Long writes "his private parts were amputated"; the plainness is the
  philosophy (the body is externals) and his primness is not restored.
- "puffed up" reused from the Enchiridion ledger at I.3.

CHECKED MECHANICALLY AFTER EVERY BATCH, and all clean so far:
assemble.is_subheading run over each finished file — not an
approximation of it, the function itself — since this book is mostly
dialogue and short lines, which is precisely what it has misread in
six other books.
