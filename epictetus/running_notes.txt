THE INHERITANCE
===============
The Enchiridion is an abridgement of this book, it is already published,
and its ledger governs here. Caps spent, no re-gloss:

  up to us / not up to us        the core distinction
  impression                     phantasia (Long: appearance, semblance)
  judgments (or views)           dogmata
  desire and avoidance           the recurring pair
  in line with nature            "conformable to nature"
  your will / what you choose    prohairesis
  make progress                  prokopto (Long: proficiency)
  blocked / blocks               hindered, impediment
  given back                     the give-it-back language
  slave                          doulos — never "servant"
  puffed up                      elated

Tone calibration inherited: tough, plain-spoken coach; bracing, never
preachy, never self-help-smooth; second person imperative.

AND THE ONE PLACE THE INHERITANCE MUST BEND. The Enchiridion is a drill
book and reads like one. This is the classroom it came out of, so the
register is LOOSER, warmer, more argumentative and much funnier. Same
man, more air. A reader who knows the handbook should recognise the
voice and notice it is doing something different.


LOCKED HERE (new terms the handbook never had to name)
======================================================
  preconception       prolepsis (Long: precognition)
  peace of mind       ataraxia / "freedom from perturbation"
  capacity            dunamis — NOT "faculty", except "the rational
                      faculty" at I.1, which Epictetus is defining
  assent              kept, glossed ONCE at first real use: saying yes
                      to an impression
  the governing part  hegemonikon, glossed once as the mind's command
                      centre. NOT "ruling faculty"
  externals           kept (Long's word, and it is English now)
  indifferent things  kept, glossed once at II.6
  proper acts /       kathekon, where the technical sense is in play
    what is fitting
  the Cynics /        Long's "Cynism"
    the Cynic life

God / the gods / Zeus / Providence: exactly as the source has them,
singular or plural as he says it, sliding between them inside a
paragraph as he does. No Christianising, no scrubbing.


CONVENTIONS
===========
1. THE HEADING IS FIXED. Every file opens with the exact heading the
   manifest gives it ("Chapter 12: On Training"), then a blank line,
   then the translation. All 95 titles were decided in advance in
   prep.py's TITLES table. Do not re-title while translating.
2. Multi-part files (III.22, III.24, IV.1): heading line, then
   "(Part n of k)", then the body. Parts 2+ never re-introduce the
   chapter; non-final parts never conclude it.
3. MARKUP-FREE. No asterisks, no underscores, no markdown. Tab indent
   = verse or a speaker table; ALL CAPS = heading, so never write an
   all-caps line. Book and play titles go in plain text.
4. Verse stays tab-indented, one line per line, with its attribution
   as a final tab-indented line ("\t— Iliad, i 526.").
5. Drama speaker tags are Title Case, never all-caps.
6. Long's parenthetical double-shots — "confidence (courage)",
   "tranquillity (freedom from perturbation)" — are resolved to one
   word. Keeping both is the fastest way to sound like the translation
   we are replacing.


DECIDED IN ADVANCE
==================
SUICIDE. Argued at length and approvingly in I.9, I.24, I.25, II.15 and
III.13 — "the door is open", the smoky room. Translated in full, in his
voice, with no modern note and no helpline: that would be the wink the
Verne rule forbids, and would recast a philosophical position as a
symptom. He also forbids leaving without reason (I.9, where he stops a
class that has talked itself into it) — keep BOTH sides at full
strength, because the pair is the actual doctrine.

SLAVERY. Not incidental and not a metaphor only: he was enslaved, his
students owned people, and enslaved people appear in his examples as
property beside a farm or a horse. Rendered plainly, "slave" for
doulos, no euphemism, nothing cut. His use of "slave" as an insult to a
free student is deliberate and stays.

THE BODY. Long is prim; Epictetus is not, and the plainness is the
philosophy. Do not restore Long's primness.
