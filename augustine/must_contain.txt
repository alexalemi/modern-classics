# The passages that must survive near-verbatim. Every one of these was
# copied from augustine/chapters/ and then rendered into the modern form
# the translation is REQUIRED to use -- the locked wording is repeated in
# running_notes.txt so no agent has to guess at it.
#
# The lesson these encode (from epictetus/, where a pin was written from
# memory and merged two of Epictetus' distinct terms into a phrase that
# is in neither the Greek nor Long): PIN FROM THE TEXT, then decide the
# modern form deliberately. Never write a pin from recollection of what
# the famous quotation sounds like.
#
# Matching is case- and whitespace-insensitive, so capitalisation of the
# divine pronoun is not at risk here. Keep the pins SHORT -- a pin long
# enough to constrain a whole sentence stops being a check on the famous
# phrase and becomes a check on the translator's syntax.

# Book One, the opening. The most quoted sentence Augustine wrote.
# Source: "Thou madest us for Thyself, and our heart is restless, until
# it repose in Thee."
you made us for yourself
our heart is restless until it rests in you

# Book Two, the pear tree -- the theft that is the book's whole argument
# about why people do wrong things for no reason at all.
# Source: "A pear tree there was near our vineyard, laden with fruit,
# tempting neither for colour nor taste."
pear tree
tempting neither in colour nor in taste

# Book Three, on his own taste for tragedy in Carthage.
cauldron of unholy loves

# Book Three, the bishop to Monica, refusing to argue with her son. The
# most famous sentence about her in the book, and the one she treated
# as having come out of heaven.
# Source: "Go thy ways and God bless thee, for it is not possible that
# the son of these tears should perish."
it is not possible that the son of these tears should perish

# Book Six. Augustine's account of the woman he dismissed, in his own
# terms and no one else's.
torn and wounded and bleeding

# Book Eight, the prayer he now knows was a fraud -- and which he tells
# as a joke against himself.
# Source: "Give me chastity and continency, only not yet."
give me chastity and self-control, but not yet

# Book Eight, the garden at Milan. The chanting voice next door.
take up and read

# Book Nine, Monica at Ostia, told she will be buried far from home.
# Source: "...any where; let not the care for that any way disquiet
# you: this only I request, that you would remember me at the Lord's
# altar, wherever you be."
lay this body anywhere
remember me at the altar of the Lord

# Book Ten. The other most-quoted sentence, and the reason the book is
# still read outside theology.
# Source: "Too late loved I Thee, O Thou Beauty of ancient days, yet
# ever new! too late I loved Thee! And behold, Thou wert within, and I
# abroad..."
late have I loved you
beauty so old and so new
you were within me, and I was outside

# Book Thirteen. One of the most quoted sentences Augustine wrote, and
# the reason his account of love is an account of physics.
# Source: "My weight, is my love; thereby am I borne, whithersoever I
# am borne."
my weight is my love

# Book Eleven, on time. The most famous confession of ignorance in
# philosophy.
# Source: "What then is time? If no one asks me, I know: if I wish to
# explain it to one that asketh, I know not."
if no one asks me, I know
