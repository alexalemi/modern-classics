Fukanzazengi: Universal Recommendation for Zazen (1227)

Now, when you look into it: the way is basically complete and reaches
everywhere. What could it have to do with practising, or with getting
anywhere? The vehicle carries itself freely. Why spend any effort on
it? The whole of it is far beyond the dust of the world. Who could
believe in a method for wiping the dust away? It is never apart from
where you are standing right now. What is the use of going off
somewhere to practise?

And yet. Let there be a hair's breadth of difference and it is the gap
between heaven and earth. Let liking and disliking so much as start up,
and you lose the mind of it in the confusion.

Suppose you are proud of your understanding and rich in realisation, so
that you have got hold of a flash of insight; suppose you have found
the way and cleared your mind, and your ambition goes up to strike the
sky; suppose you are strolling about in the borderlands of the entrance
to it. You will still be missing, more or less, the living road that
leads out.

Look at the one in the Jeta Grove, who was born knowing. You can still
see the marks of the six years he sat there. Look at the one at
Shaolin, who passed the seal of the mind on. The report of the nine
years he spent facing a wall is still going about. The old saints were
like that. Why will people now not do the work?

So: stop chasing after words and running down phrases. Learn the step
backwards, the one that turns your light inward and lets it shine on
you. Body and mind will drop away of themselves, and your original face
will be there in front of you. If you want to get that, get on with
that.

For sitting, a quiet room is best. Eat and drink moderately. Put down
all your involvements. Let the ten thousand things rest. Do not think
good or evil. Do not concern yourself with right or wrong. Stop the
wheels of mind, intellect and consciousness turning; stop the
measurements of memory, imagination and contemplation. Have no design
on becoming a buddha. This has nothing whatever to do with sitting or
lying down.

At your usual sitting place, spread a thick mat and put a round cushion
on top of it. Sit either in the full lotus or the half lotus. For the
full lotus, first put your right foot on your left thigh, then your
left foot on your right thigh. For the half lotus, simply press your
left foot onto your right thigh. Loosen your robe and belt and get them
straight and even.

Then put your right hand on your left foot, and your left palm on your
right palm, with the tips of the two thumbs just touching. Sit upright
in the correct posture. Do not lean to the left, or tilt to the right,
or bend forward, or arch backwards. The ears should be in line with the
shoulders, and the nose in line with the navel. Rest your tongue on the
roof of your mouth, with the lips and teeth closed. The eyes should
always stay open. Breathe faintly through the nose.

Once the body is settled, take one breath out, sway left and right, and
then sit immovably, like a mountain.

Think of the thing that does not think. How do you think of the thing
that does not think? Beyond thinking. This is the essential art of
sitting.

The sitting I am talking about is not learning meditation. It is simply
the dharma-gate of ease and joy. It is the practice-and-realisation of
a fully accomplished awakening. The koan is realised in the present.
Traps and cages have never reached it. If you get hold of this meaning,
you are a dragon that has got to water, a tiger that has got to its
mountain. Know that the true dharma is appearing of itself, and
dullness and distraction have already been knocked away.

When you get up from sitting, move the body slowly and stand up calmly.
Do not be abrupt about it.

I have seen it: going beyond the ordinary and beyond the holy, dying
sitting or dying standing — these depend entirely on this power. And
more than that: the pivotal moments that turn on a finger, a pole, a
needle, a mallet; the confirmations that come with a whisk, a fist, a
staff, a shout — none of these have ever been graspable by thinking and
discrimination. How could they be known by supernormal powers, or by
practice and realisation? They may be conduct beyond sound and form.
Are they not the standard that comes before knowing and seeing?

That being so, it makes no difference whether you are clever or stupid,
and there is no sorting people into the sharp and the dull.
Single-minded effort is itself the practice of the way.
Practice-and-realisation is by nature undefiled. Going towards it is
simply an ordinary matter.

In general, in this world and in other quarters, in India and in the
East alike, all hold the buddha-seal equally and each gives free rein
to its own style; but they devote themselves solely to sitting, and are
held fast by the immovable. Though we speak of ten thousand
distinctions and a thousand differences, they simply take up Zen and
practise the way.

Why abandon the sitting platform of your own house and go wandering
uselessly in the dusty regions of other countries? Put one foot wrong
and you go straight past what is right in front of you.

You have already got hold of the pivot of a human body. Do not spend
your time for nothing. You are holding and maintaining the essential
working of the buddha way. Who would idly enjoy a spark struck off a
flint? On top of which: form and substance are like a drop of dew on
the grass, and fate is like a flash of lightning. In a moment they are
empty; in an instant they are lost.

I beg of you, distinguished friends in the study of this: do not, from
long familiarity with feeling the elephant, be suspicious of the real
dragon. Go forward on the road that points straight at the thing
itself. Honour the person who is beyond study and beyond doing. Fall
into step with the awakening of buddha after buddha. Take up in
succession the samadhi of patriarch after patriarch. Behave like this
for a long time and you will be like this. The treasure house will open
of itself, and you will use it as you please.
