Fukanzazengi: Universal Recommendation for Zazen (1227)

Look into it and you find this. The way is already whole, and it runs
through everything. So what is there to practise, and what is there to
attain? The truth carries itself. Why would it need your effort? The
whole of it stands clear of the dust and noise of the world. Who really
believes you can polish it clean, like a mirror? It is never anywhere
but where you are standing right now. What good would it do to go
somewhere else to look for it?

And yet. Let there be a hair's breadth of difference between you and
it, and that gap is as wide as the sky is from the ground. Let the
smallest liking or disliking start up, and you have lost it in the
confusion.

Suppose you are proud of what you understand. Suppose you are rich in
insight and have caught a glimpse of the truth. Suppose you have found
the way and cleared your mind, and your ambition reaches up to knock at
the sky. You may be strolling round and round at the edge of the
entrance. You are still missing, more or less, the living road that
leads out.

Look at the Buddha in the Jeta Grove. He was born knowing, and you can
still see the marks of the six years he sat. Look at Bodhidharma at
Shaolin. He carried the seal of the mind from India to China, and the
story of his nine years facing a wall is still told. The old saints
were like that. Why will people now not do the work?

So stop chasing after words and running down phrases. Learn the step
backwards: the one that turns your light around and lets it shine on
you. Body and mind will drop away of themselves, and your original face
will be there in front of you. If you want that, get on with that.

For sitting, a quiet room is best. Eat and drink moderately. Put down
everything you are involved in. Let the ten thousand things rest. Do
not think about good or bad. Do not take up right or wrong. Stop the
wheels of mind and intellect and consciousness turning. Stop measuring
things with memory and imagination and contemplation. Have no plan to
become a buddha. None of this has anything to do with sitting or lying
down.

At your usual sitting place, spread a thick mat and put a round cushion
on top of it. Sit in the full lotus or the half lotus. For the full
lotus, first put your right foot on your left thigh, then your left
foot on your right thigh. For the half lotus, just press your left foot
onto your right thigh. Loosen your robe and belt and arrange them
neatly.

Then put your right hand on your left foot, and your left hand in your
right palm, with the tips of your thumbs just touching. Sit up
straight. Do not lean to the left or tilt to the right. Do not bend
forward or arch back. The ears should be in line with the shoulders,
and the nose in line with the navel. Rest your tongue on the roof of
your mouth, with your lips and teeth closed. Keep your eyes open.
Breathe gently through your nose.

Once your body is settled, breathe out once, rock left and right, and
then sit immovably, like a mountain.

Think of the thing that does not think. How do you think of the thing
that does not think? Beyond thinking. This is the essential art of
sitting.

The sitting I am talking about is not learning to meditate. It is
simply the gate to ease and joy. It is practice and realisation in one,
a fully accomplished awakening. The koan is realised in the present. No
trap and no cage has ever reached it. If you get hold of this, you are
a dragon that has got to water, a tiger that has got to its mountain.
Know that the true dharma is showing itself, and that dullness and
distraction have already been knocked away.

When you get up from sitting, move slowly and stand up calmly. Do not
be abrupt about it.

I have seen it myself. Going beyond the ordinary and beyond the holy;
dying while sitting, or dying standing up: all of it depends on this
power. And more than that. Think of the great turning points in Zen,
when a master raised a finger, or a pole, or a needle, or a wooden
mallet, and a student woke up. Think of the moments when a whisk, a
fist, a staff or a shout confirmed it. None of these was ever grasped
by thinking things over. How could they be understood by supernatural
powers? How could they be understood by practice and realisation? They
are conduct beyond sound and form. Are they not the standard that comes
before knowing and seeing?

So it makes no difference whether you are clever or stupid. There is no
sorting people into the sharp and the dull. Single-minded effort is
itself the practice of the way. Practice and realisation are naturally
undefiled. Going towards it is an ordinary, everyday matter.

In this world and in other worlds, in India and here in the East, all
hold the Buddha's seal equally, and each has its own style. But they
give themselves to sitting, and are held fast by stillness. There may
be ten thousand distinctions and a thousand differences. They simply
take up Zen and practise the way.

Why leave the sitting place of your own house and go wandering
uselessly in the dusty regions of other countries? Put one foot wrong
and you go straight past what is right in front of you.

You have already got hold of the essential thing: a human body. Do not
spend your time for nothing. You are holding the essential working of
the Buddha's way. Who would fool about with a spark struck off a flint?
And besides, your form and substance are like a drop of dew on the
grass, and your fate is like a flash of lightning. In a moment they are
empty. In an instant they are gone.

I beg you, distinguished friends in the study of Zen. Do not be like
the blind men in the old story, each feeling one part of the elephant
and arguing about what it was, so used to the piece in your hands that
you doubt the real dragon when it comes. Go forward on the road that
points straight at the thing itself. Honour the person who is beyond
study and beyond doing. Fall into step with the awakening of buddha
after buddha. Take up the samadhi, the settled concentration, of
ancestor after ancestor. Behave like this for a long time and you will
be like this. The treasure house will open of itself, and you will use
it as you please.
