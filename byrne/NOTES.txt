BYRNE'S EUCLID — status notes (2026-09-20), work paused

Alex's ruling: build on Sergey Slyusarev's recreation
(github.com/jemmybutton/byrne-euclid; book text CC BY-SA 4.0, byrne.mp
GPLv3). The edition must carry CC BY-SA 4.0 and credit it.

WHAT WORKS
- Standalone MetaPost: one mpost job per \defineNewPicture reproduces the
  diagrams exactly as SVG (prototype _src/proto/p1.mp: Prop. I.1's margin
  figure and the inline black line both correct). Needs:
    u:=1cm;  (byrnebook.cls \mpPre)
    input metafun; input byrne;  prologues:=3; outputformat:="svg";
    MPINPUTS=_src/texmf/metapost/context/mpii:.../common:.../base:
  metafun (ConTeXt mpii/common/mpiv) was fetched from
  github.com/contextgarden/context; MetaPost base (plain.mp) and luamplib
  from the TeX Live archive on mirrors.mit.edu (the Utah CTAN mirror fails
  certificate verification through the proxy). TeX Live here has the byrne
  package but not luamplib, metafun or plain.mp.
- Every inline macro in byrne.sty reduces to \drawFromCurrentPicture
  [placement][name]{MP code}: an image() in the current instance, reused by
  name until the next \defineNewPicture resets the undefine list. So the
  pictures can be generated in source order without LaTeX.

WHAT IS HARD, and why it is paused
- ~5,000 inline pictures, many INSIDE math: $\drawUnitLine{AB} =
  \drawUnitLine{CA}$, \dfrac of pictures (Books V-VI), \halign proportion
  tables, \vcenter/\hbox stacks. A text converter must rebuild all of that
  layout, and the renderers need a new inline-image feature (glyphs inside
  running text, fractions of images) in both assemble.py and build_ebook.
- Compiling the real book with lualatex (to typeset those blocks by TeX)
  half-works: with _src/texmf as TEXMFHOME it runs, but EB Garamond is not
  installed (fontspec errors) and the pictures did not appear in the test
  PDF (luamplib here is the 2026 archive version against TeX Live 2023's
  LuaTeX; unresolved).
- Realistic estimate: several days of engineering for a faithful
  reflowable edition, which is roughly what the web recreations took.
