Proposition I.48 (Theorem)

[Figure acx: The diagram for Proposition I.48, in Byrne’s colours.]

If *the square of one side (⟦g:00bc⟧) of a triangle is equal to the squares of the other two sides (⟦g:0086⟧ and ⟦g:004a⟧), the angle (⟦g:0205⟧) subtended by that side is a right angle.*

Draw ⟦g:0087⟧ ⊥ ⟦g:0086⟧ and = ⟦g:004a⟧ (prop. I.3 and I.11)

and draw ⟦g:0206⟧ also.

Since ⟦g:0087⟧ = ⟦g:004a⟧ (const.)

⟦g:0087⟧ ² = ⟦g:004a⟧ ²;

∴ ⟦g:0087⟧ ² + ⟦g:0086⟧ ² = ⟦g:004a⟧ ² + ⟦g:0086⟧ ²

but ⟦g:0087⟧ ² + ⟦g:0086⟧ ² = ⟦g:0206⟧ ² (prop. I.47),

and ⟦g:004a⟧ ² + ⟦g:0086⟧ ² = ⟦g:00bc⟧ ² (hyp.)

∴ ⟦g:0206⟧ ² = ⟦g:00bc⟧ ²,

∴ ⟦g:0206⟧ = ⟦g:00bc⟧ ;

and ∴ ⟦g:0207⟧ = ⟦g:0205⟧ (prop. I.8),

consequently ⟦g:0205⟧ is a right angle.

Q. E. D.
