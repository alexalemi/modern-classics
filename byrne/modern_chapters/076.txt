Proposition III.7 (Theorem)

[Figure aed: The diagram for Proposition III.7, in Byrne’s colours.]

If *from any point within a circle ⟦g:0306⟧ which is not the centre, lines* ⟬{⟦g:0307⟧‖⟦g:0308⟧‖⟦g:0309⟧}⟭*are drawn to the circumference; the greatest of those lines is that (⟦g:0307⟧) which passes through the centre, and the least is the remaining part (⟦g:00bb⟧) of the diameter.Of the others, that (⟦g:0308⟧) which is nearer to the line passing through the centre, is greater than that (⟦g:0309⟧) which is more remote.Fig. 2. The two lines (⟦g:030a⟧ and ⟦g:0067⟧) which make equal angles with that passing through the centre, on opposite sides of it, are equal to each other; and there cannot be drawn a third line equal to them, from the same point to the circumference.*
