Proposition I.4 (Theorem)

[Figure abe: The diagram for Proposition I.4, in Byrne’s colours.]

If *two triangles have two sides of the one respectively equal to two sides of the other, (⟦g:0008⟧ to ⟦g:0059⟧ and ⟦g:005a⟧ to ⟦g:005b⟧) and the angles (⟦g:005c⟧ and ⟦g:005d⟧) contained by those equal sides also equal; then their bases or their sides (⟦g:004a⟧ and ⟦g:005e⟧) are also equal: and the remaining angles opposite to equal sides are respectively equal (* ⟦g:005f⟧ = ⟦g:0060⟧ *and* ⟦g:0061⟧ = ⟦g:0062⟧ *): and the triangles are equal in every respect.*

Let two triangles be conceived, to be so placed, that the vertex of the one of the equal angles, ⟦g:005c⟧ or ⟦g:005d⟧; shall fall upon that of the other, and ⟦g:0008⟧ to coincide with ⟦g:0059⟧, then will ⟦g:005a⟧ coincide with ⟦g:005b⟧ if applied: consequently ⟦g:004a⟧ will coincide with ⟦g:005e⟧, or two straight lines will enclose a space, which is impossible (ax. I.10), therefore ⟦g:004a⟧ = ⟦g:005e⟧ , ⟦g:005f⟧ = ⟦g:0060⟧ and ⟦g:0061⟧ = ⟦g:0062⟧ , and as the triangles ⟦g:0063⟧ and ⟦g:0064⟧ coincide, when applied, they are equal in every respect.

Q. E. D.
