Proposition I.24 (Theorem)

[Figure aby: The diagram for Proposition I.24, in Byrne’s colours.]

If *two triangles have two sides of the one respectively equal to two sides of the other (⟦g:011d⟧ to ⟦g:011e⟧ and ⟦g:011f⟧ to ⟦g:008f⟧), and if one of the angles (⟦g:0120⟧) contained by the equal sides be greater than the other (⟦g:0121⟧), the side (⟦g:0122⟧) which is opposite to the greater angle is greater than the side (⟦g:0123⟧) which is opposite to the less angle.*

Make ⟦g:0124⟧ = ⟦g:0121⟧ (prop. I.23),

and ⟦g:0046⟧ = ⟦g:008f⟧ (prop. I.3),

draw ⟦g:0125⟧ and ⟦g:0087⟧.

∵ ⟦g:0046⟧ = ⟦g:011f⟧ (ax. I.1, hyp., and const.)

∴ ⟦g:0126⟧ = ⟦g:0127⟧ (prop. I.5)

but ⟦g:0128⟧ < ⟦g:0127⟧ ,

and ∴ ⟦g:0128⟧ < ⟦g:0129⟧ ,

∴ ⟦g:0122⟧ > ⟦g:0087⟧ (prop. I.19)

but ⟦g:0087⟧ = ⟦g:0123⟧ (prop. I.4)

∴ ⟦g:0122⟧ > ⟦g:0123⟧ .

Q. E. D.
