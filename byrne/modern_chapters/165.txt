Proposition VI.5 (Theorem)

[Figure agx: The diagram for Proposition VI.5, in Byrne’s colours.]

If *two triangles have their sides proportional (* ⟦g:059b⟧ : ⟦g:01b2⟧ :: ⟦g:011d⟧ : ⟦g:0174⟧ *) and (* ⟦g:01b2⟧ : ⟦g:038b⟧ :: ⟦g:0174⟧ : ⟦g:059c⟧ *) they are equiangular, and the equal angles are subtended by the homologous sides.*

From the extremities of ⟦g:0174⟧, draw ⟦g:059d⟧ and ⟦g:00f5⟧, making

⟦g:059e⟧ = ⟦g:00e0⟧ , ⟦g:059f⟧ = ⟦g:05a0⟧ (prop. I.23);

and consequently ⟦g:05a1⟧ = ⟦g:05a2⟧ (prop. I.32),

and since the triangles are equiangular,

⟦g:038b⟧ : ⟦g:01b2⟧ :: ⟦g:00f5⟧ : ⟦g:0174⟧ (prop. VI.4);

but ⟦g:038b⟧ : ⟦g:01b2⟧ :: ⟦g:059c⟧ : ⟦g:0174⟧ (hyp.);

∴ ⟦g:059c⟧ : ⟦g:0174⟧ :: ⟦g:00f5⟧ : ⟦g:0174⟧ , and consequently ⟦g:059c⟧ = ⟦g:00f5⟧ (prop. V.9). In like manner it may be shown that

⟦g:011d⟧ = ⟦g:059d⟧ .

Therefore, the two triangles having a common base ⟦g:0174⟧, and their sides equal, have also equal angles opposite to equal sides, i. e.

⟦g:05a3⟧ = ⟦g:059e⟧ and ⟦g:05a4⟧ = ⟦g:059f⟧ (prop. I.8).

but ⟦g:059e⟧ = ⟦g:00e0⟧ (const.) and ∴ ⟦g:05a3⟧ = ⟦g:00e0⟧ ;

for the same reason ⟦g:05a4⟧ = ⟦g:05a0⟧ ,

and consequently ⟦g:05a5⟧ = ⟦g:05a2⟧ (prop. I.32);

and therefore the triangles are equiangular, and it is evident that the homologous sides subtended by the equal angles.

Q. E. D.
