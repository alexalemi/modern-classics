Proposition V.9 (Theorem)

Magnitudes *which have the same ratio to the same magnitude are equal to one another; and those to which the same magnitude has the same ratio are equal to one another.*

Let ⟦g:0529⟧ : ⟦g:052d⟧ :: ⟦g:052c⟧ : ⟦g:052d⟧ , then ⟦g:0529⟧ = ⟦g:052c⟧ .

For, if not, let ⟦g:0529⟧ > ⟦g:052c⟧ , then will

⟦g:0529⟧ : ⟦g:052d⟧ > ⟦g:052c⟧ : ⟦g:052d⟧ (prop. V.8), which is absurd according to the hypothesis.

∴ ⟦g:0529⟧ is ≯ ⟦g:052c⟧ .

In the same manner it may be shown, that

⟦g:052c⟧ is ≯ ⟦g:0529⟧ ,

∴ ⟦g:0529⟧ = ⟦g:052c⟧ .

Again, let ⟦g:052d⟧ : ⟦g:0529⟧ :: ⟦g:052d⟧ : ⟦g:052c⟧ ,

then will ⟦g:0529⟧ = ⟦g:052c⟧ .

For (invert.) ⟦g:0529⟧ : ⟦g:052d⟧ :: ⟦g:052c⟧ : ⟦g:052d⟧ ,

therefore, by the first case, ⟦g:0529⟧ = ⟦g:052c⟧ .

∴ Magnitudes which have the same ratio, &c.

This may be shown otherwise, as follows :—

Let A : B = A : C, then B = C, for, as the fraction ⟪A‖B⟫ = the fraction ⟪A‖C⟫, and the numerator of one equal to the numerator of the other, therefore, denominators of these fractions are equal, that is B = C.

Again, if B : A = C : A, B = C. For, as ⟪B‖A⟫ = ⟪C‖A⟫, B must = C.
