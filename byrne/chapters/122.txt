Proposition IV.15 (Problem)

[Figure agp]

To *inscribe an equilateral and equiangular hexagon in a given circle ⟦g:050f⟧.*

From any point in the circumference of the given circle describe ⟦g:0510⟧ passing through its centre, and draw the diameters ⟦g:0042⟧, ⟦g:005a⟧ and ⟦g:02f0⟧; draw ⟦g:02fb⟧, ⟦g:0511⟧, ⟦g:03e3⟧, &c. and the required hexagon is inscribed in the given circle.

Since ⟦g:0042⟧ passes through the centres of the circles, ⟦g:0512⟧ and ⟦g:0513⟧ are equilateral triangles, hence ⟦g:0514⟧ = ⟦g:0515⟧ = one-third of ⟦g:0023⟧ (prop. I.32) but ⟦g:0516⟧ = ⟦g:0023⟧ (prop. I.13);

∴ ⟦g:0514⟧ = ⟦g:0515⟧ = ⟦g:0517⟧ = one-third of ⟦g:0023⟧ (prop. I.32), and the angles vertically opposite to these are all equal to one another (prop. I.15), and stand on equal arches (prop. III.26), which are subtended by equal chords (prop. III.29); and since each of the angles of the hexagon is double of the angle of an equilateral triangle, it is also equiangular.

Q. E. D.
