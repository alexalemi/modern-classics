Proposition IV.13 (Problem)

[Figure agn]

To *inscribe a circle in a given equiangular and equilateral pentagon.*

Let ⟦g:04f2⟧ be a given equiangular and equilateral pentagon; it is required to inscribe a circle in it.

Make ⟦g:04f3⟧ = ⟦g:04f4⟧ , and ⟦g:04f5⟧ = ⟦g:04f6⟧ (prop. I.9)

Draw ⟦g:033a⟧, ⟦g:0080⟧, ⟦g:0084⟧, ⟦g:032a⟧, &c.

∵ ⟦g:04f7⟧ = ⟦g:04f8⟧ , ⟦g:04f3⟧ = ⟦g:04f4⟧ , and ⟦g:0080⟧ common to the two triangles

⟦g:04f9⟧ and ⟦g:04fa⟧;

∴ ⟦g:0084⟧ = ⟦g:033a⟧ and ⟦g:04fb⟧ = ⟦g:04f6⟧ (prop. I.4)

And ∵ ⟦g:04fc⟧ = ⟦g:04fd⟧ = twice ⟦g:04f6⟧

∴= twice ⟦g:04fb⟧ , hence ⟦g:04fc⟧ is bisected by ⟦g:0084⟧.

In like manner it may be demonstrated that ⟦g:04fe⟧ is bisected by ⟦g:032a⟧, and that the remaining angle of the polygon is bisected in a similar manner.

Draw ⟦g:00c3⟧, ⟦g:031c⟧, &c. perpendicular to the sides of the pentagon.

Then in the two triangles ⟦g:04ff⟧ and ⟦g:0500⟧

we have ⟦g:04f3⟧ = ⟦g:04f4⟧ (const.), ⟦g:0080⟧ common,

and ⟦g:0501⟧ = ⟦g:0502⟧ = a right angle ;

∴ ⟦g:00c3⟧ = ⟦g:031c⟧ (prop. I.26)

In the same way it may be shown that the five perpendiculars on the sides of the pentagon are equal to one another.

Describe ⟦g:0503⟧ with any one of the perpendiculars as radius, and it will be the inscribed circle required. For if it does not touch the sides of the pentagon, but cut them, then a line drawn from the extremity at right angles to the diameter of a circle will fall within the circle, which has been shown to be absurd (prop. III.16).

Q. E. D.
