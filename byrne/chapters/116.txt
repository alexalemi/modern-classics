Proposition IV.9 (Problem)

[Figure agj]

To *describe a circle about a given square ⟦g:04b0⟧.*

Draw the diagonals ⟦g:04b1⟧ and ⟦g:04b2⟧ intersecting each other;

then, because ⟦g:04b3⟧ and ⟦g:04b4⟧ have their sides equal, and the base ⟦g:04b1⟧ common to both,

⟦g:04b5⟧ = ⟦g:04b6⟧ (prop. I.8),

or ⟦g:04b7⟧ is bisected:

in like manner it can be shown that ⟦g:04b8⟧ is bisected;

but ⟦g:04b7⟧ = ⟦g:04b8⟧ ,

hence ⟦g:04b6⟧ = ⟦g:04b9⟧ their halves,

∴ ⟦g:0174⟧ = ⟦g:0138⟧ (prop. I.6);

and in like manner it can be proved that

⟦g:0138⟧ = ⟦g:0174⟧ = ⟦g:04ba⟧ = ⟦g:01b2⟧ .

If from the confluence of these lines with any one of them as radius, a circle be described, it will circumscribe the given square.

Q. E. D.
