Canto Twenty-Nine

	Singing like a woman in love,
	she went on, at the end of her words:
	"Beati quorum tecta sunt peccata!", blessed are they whose sins are covered.

	And like nymphs who used to walk alone
	through the wild shadows, one wanting
	to see the sun and another to escape it,

	she then moved against the stream, going
	along the bank; and I level with her,
	following her small step with a small one.

	Between her steps and mine there were not a hundred
	when the banks turned equally,
	in such a way that I was facing east again.

	Nor had our road gone far like that
	when the lady turned right round to me,
	saying: "My brother, look and listen."

	And then a brightness suddenly ran
	on all sides through the great forest,
	so that I wondered whether it was lightning.

	But because lightning stops as soon as it comes,
	and this, lasting, shone brighter and brighter,
	I said in my thought: "What is this thing?"

	And a sweet melody was running
	through the shining air; at which good zeal
	made me reprove the boldness of Eve,

	who, there where earth and heaven were obedient,
	a woman, alone and only just formed,
	would not bear to stay under any veil;

	under which, if she had been dutiful,
	I would have felt those unspeakable delights
	earlier, and for a longer while.

	While I went on among so many first fruits
	of the eternal pleasure, wholly suspended,
	and still longing for more gladness,

	the air under the green branches became
	before us like a kindled fire;
	and the sweet sound was now understood as singing.

	Oh most holy Virgins, Muses, if hunger,
	cold or sleepless nights I ever suffered for you,
	the case spurs me to call on you for payment.

	Now your spring on Helicon must pour out for me,
	and Urania, muse of the heavens, help me with her choir,
	to put into verse things hard to think of.

	A little further on, the long stretch of air
	still between us and them
	made seven golden trees of what was there;

	but when I had come so near them
	that the common object, which deceives the sense,
	no longer lost any of its features by distance,

	the faculty that hands what it sees to reason
	understood that they were candlesticks,
	and understood "Hosanna" in the voices of the singing.

	Above, the beautiful equipment was flaming
	far brighter than the moon in a clear
	midnight sky in the middle of her month.

	I turned round full of wonder
	to the good Virgil, and he answered me
	with a look loaded with no less astonishment.

	Then I gave my face back to the high things,
	which were moving towards us so slowly
	that new brides would have outrun them.

	The lady scolded me: "Why do you burn so
	with love only for the living lights,
	and do not look at what comes behind them?"

	Then I saw people coming after them,
	as if after their leaders, dressed in white;
	and there was never such whiteness here.

	The water shone on my left side,
	and gave me back my own left side, if I looked
	into it, as a mirror does.

	When I had taken up such a station on my bank
	that only the river kept me at a distance,
	I stopped my steps to see better,

	and saw the little flames going in front,
	leaving the air painted behind them,
	so that they looked like trailed brushes;

	so that above them there stayed marked out
	seven bands, all in those colours
	the Sun makes his rainbow of, and the moon her halo.

	These streamers went back further than my sight,
	and, by my reckoning, the outer ones
	were ten paces apart.

	Under so beautiful a sky as I describe,
	twenty-four elders, two by two,
	came crowned with fleurs-de-lis.

	They were all singing: "Blessed are you
	among the daughters of Adam, and blessed
	be your beauties for ever!"

	When the flowers and the other fresh grass
	on the other bank, opposite me,
	were free of those chosen people,

	as light follows light in heaven,
	four living creatures came after them,
	each crowned with green leaves.

	Each of them was feathered with six wings;
	the feathers full of eyes; and the hundred eyes of Argus,
	if they were alive, would be like these.

	I do not spread more rhymes on describing
	their shapes, reader; for another cost presses me,
	so that I cannot be generous with this one;

	but read Ezekiel, who paints them
	as he saw them come from the cold quarter
	with wind and with cloud and with fire;

	and just as you will find them in his pages,
	so they were here, except that as to the wings
	John is with me, and differs from him.

	The space inside the four of them held
	a triumphal chariot on two wheels,
	which came drawn at the neck of a griffin.

	He stretched up one wing and the other
	between the middle band and the three and three,
	so that in splitting them he did no harm to any.

	They rose so high that they were out of sight.
	His limbs were of gold where he was bird,
	and the rest white, mixed with red.

	Not only did Rome never make Scipio Africanus glad
	with so fine a chariot, or Augustus either,
	but the Sun's own would be poor beside it —

	the Sun's, which, going off its road with Phaethon driving, was burnt
	at the prayer of the devout Earth,
	when Jove, in his hidden way, was just.

	Three ladies came dancing in a circle
	at the right wheel; one of them so red
	that she would hardly have been noticed inside a fire;

	the second was as if her flesh and bones
	had been made of emerald;
	the third seemed snow just fallen;

	and now they seemed led by the white one,
	now by the red; and from her singing
	the others took their step, slow and quick.

	On the left four kept festival,
	dressed in purple, following the way
	of one of them who had three eyes in her head.

	After the whole company I have described
	I saw two old men in unlike clothing,
	but alike in bearing, dignified and solid.

	One showed himself to be a physician, one of the household
	of that great Hippocrates whom nature
	made for the creatures she holds dearest;

	the other showed the contrary care,
	with a sword shining and sharp,
	such that it frightened me on this side of the river.

	Then I saw four in humble bearing;
	and behind all of them a single old man
	coming, asleep, with a keen face.

	And these seven were dressed like the first troop,
	but they did not make a garland
	of lilies about their heads,

	but of roses and other red flowers;
	a look from a little distance would have sworn
	that they were all on fire above their eyebrows.

	And when the chariot was opposite me,
	a thunderclap was heard, and those worthy people
	seemed to be forbidden to go further,

	stopping there with the leading standards.
