THE FORM, AS IN THE INFERNO

33 cantos, 4,755 lines, tercet for tercet and line for line, tabbed,
unrhymed. check.py compares the shape exactly against chapters/ and
requires every line to keep its tab, because ONE LOST TAB TURNS A
TERCET INTO A PARAGRAPH: assemble.py joins the lines into running
prose, and the word ratio does not move at all.

Ratio landed at 1.18 against the Inferno's 1.19 (verify --min-ratio
1.05 --max-ratio 1.35). A canto much under 1.05 is compressing.


ARNAUT DANIEL'S PROVENÇAL (XXVI.140-147), AND IT IS THE ONE PLACE
WHERE THIS EDITION DEPARTS FROM THE LATIN RULE

Latin stays Latin everywhere in this cantica, because it is liturgy
sung by souls who are singing it and the reader recognizes it as
liturgy. The eight Provençal lines are a different case: they are a
SPEECH WITH CONTENT — Arnaut naming himself, repenting, and asking to
be remembered — and leaving them untranslated makes the emotional
close of the terrace a blank page for an English reader.

THE BURKE RULE DECIDES IT: an untranslated block is a hole in the work
rather than a piece of scholarship. Burke could keep the French and
then gloss it, because prose has room; here the tercet count and the
line count are fixed and there is no room to gloss, so the choice is
between a hole and a rendering, and the rendering wins.

AND THE EFFECT IS BETTER SERVED. Dante's own readers could read
Provençal — it was the literary language of love poetry in Italy — so
what they met was a different but comprehensible language. Keeping it
today reproduces the surface and destroys the effect.

WHAT CARRIES THE FACT OF THE OTHER LANGUAGE is the line that
introduces him. "El cominciò liberamente a dire" becomes "He began,
freely, in his own tongue, to say", which tells the reader exactly
what happened, is a fair rendering of liberamente in context, and
costs no line.

The same reasoning governs the two Italian songs quoted by their
first lines: Casella's "Amor che ne la mente mi ragiona" (II.112) and
"Donne ch'avete intelletto d'amore" (XXIV.51) are Dante's own canzoni,
and are given in English. The point of both passages is that the
reader hears what the poem is quoting.


THE ACROSTIC OF CANTO XII IS NOT REPRODUCED, AND THE ANAPHORA IS

Lines 25-63 are thirteen tercets whose initial letters spell VOM
(uomo, "man"): four beginning Vedea, four O, four Mostrava, and a
last one that gathers all three. An English acrostic would have to be
paid for in distorted lines, which is the one thing this edition
refuses (it is the terza rima decision again, in miniature).

WHAT THE DEVICE IS ACTUALLY DOING — hammering the same three
openings, so that the reader hears the pattern — survives intact: the
four tercets open "I saw", the next four "Oh", the next four "It
showed", and the thirteenth carries all three in order. Nothing is
lost that a reader of the English could have seen anyway.


LOCKED VOCABULARY (beyond the Inferno's)

  cornice          "terrace" — the seven ledges, never "cornice".
  girone           "circle", only for the seven.
  balzo, scoglio   "bank", "ledge".
  P                the seven P's cut in Dante's forehead stay "P".
  duca / maestro / dolce padre / segnore
                   "guide" / "master" / "sweet father" / "my lord",
                   kept distinct, because Dante varies them on purpose
                   and the variation is the temperature of the scene.
  ombra            "shade".
  disio / voglia / talento
                   "longing" / "will" / "desire", kept distinct: the
                   whole cantica is an argument about wanting the
                   right thing, so these are technical words.
  libero arbitrio  "free will" (XVI, XVIII), an argument and not a
                   phrase.
  antipurgatorio   not Dante's word and it is not used in the text.
  contrapasso      not named here; not imported from the Inferno.


TIME MARKERS ARE KEPT, ALL OF THEM

This is the only cantica with a clock. Every "it was the hour when",
every position of the sun, every reckoning by Jerusalem, the Ganges
and the Ebro (II.1-9, III.25, IV.137, XV.1-6, XXVII.1-6) is rendered
in full and not summarised into "at dawn". They are the spine of the
narrative: they are why the reader feels a mountain being climbed
rather than described.


NO NOTES ANYWHERE IN THE VOLUME, and the reference cases decided:
  - "quel da Esti" (V.77) stays "the man of Este"; the line does not
    need a name it does not give.
  - Manfred is identified inside his own speech, as Dante does it.
  - "il buon Barbarossa" (XVIII.119) keeps the epithet.
  - The DXV prophecy (XXXIII.43) is rendered as the riddle it is:
    "a five hundred, ten and five". Do not solve it for the reader.
  - "Delectasti" (XXVIII.80) and the other psalm-tags stay as Dante
    gives them, unexpanded.


THE VERNE RULE, no note anywhere:
  - Manfred, excommunicate, saved, and the Church's curse declared
    powerless (III).
  - The terrace of lust holds both kinds of sinners and Guinizelli
    explains the difference without extra condemnation (XXVI).
  - The whore and the giant, the eagle, the fox and the dragon of
    XXXII, and Beatrice's prophecy against the Church in XXXIII.
  - Statius made a secret Christian by reading Virgil's Fourth
    Eclogue (XXII), which is Dante's claim and not history.

THE READABILITY PASS (2026-09-02)
The same pass as paradiso/ (see its running_notes for the rule in full),
applied to all 33 cantos. Ratio moved 1.18 -> 1.22; tercet count, lines
per tercet and tabs unchanged (check.py compares against chapters/).
What changed, and only this:
- Periphrasis resolved to the name where Dante is not setting a puzzle
  (Thaumas's daughter -> the rainbow; the bird of Jove -> the eagle;
  "that Greek whom the Muses suckled" -> Homer; the Fieschi, Averroes,
  Scipio, Phaethon, Charles of Anjou / Valois / Naples, Philip, Boniface).
  Where the periphrasis IS the point it stays and the name rides
  alongside it ("the emeralds, her eyes"; "the griffin, the beast that
  is one person only in two natures").
- Every Latin line glossed inline in the same line, at zero line cost
  (Qui lugent, Adhaesit pavimento, Neque nubent, sitiunt, Labia mea,
  Summae Deus clementiae, Virum non cognosco, Beati mundo corde, Venite
  benedicti, Beati quorum, Veni sponsa, ad vocem tanti senis, Benedictus
  qui venis, Manibus date lilia, In te Domine speravi, Asperges me, Deus
  venerunt gentes, Modicum et non videbitis). The must_contain pins keep
  the Latin phrase itself, which is what they test.
- Plain astronomy: the time-markers are rendered as times of day in the
  same line ("it was about two in the afternoon"; "it was between ten and
  eleven"), and the star-lore that gives them is kept, not cut.
- The riddles Dante leaves unsolved stay unsolved: the DXV, the whore and
  the giant, the pageant's three and four ladies (given their colours,
  not their names). Beatrice's rebuke and Virgil's leaving untouched.
- One handhold for a Florentine custom that the line depends on: the sop
  eaten on a victim's grave (XXXIII), which Beatrice's "God's vengeance
  is not afraid of sops" is meaningless without.
