Chapter 3: How Brilliant His Fortunes Once Were

"If Fortune argued against you like that, you would certainly have nothing to say in reply; or, if you can find some justification for your complaints, you must show me what it is. I will give you room to speak."

Then I said: "Your arguments are plausible, certainly — soaked in the honeyed sweetness of music and rhetoric. But their charm lasts only while they are sounding in the ear; a wretched man's sense of his own misfortunes lies deeper than that, in the heart. So when the sound has stopped vibrating in the air, the grief that lives in the heart is felt again, and more bitterly."

Then she said: "That is exactly right, because we have not yet come to the cure of your illness. So far these are only sedatives, working towards the treatment of a complaint that has been stubborn up to now. The remedies that go deep I shall apply when the time comes.

"All the same, to talk you out of your determination to be thought wretched: have you forgotten how far your happiness reached and where its limits were? I say nothing of how, when you were orphaned and alone, you were taken into the care of distinguished men; how you were chosen to be allied with the highest in the state, and were already dear to them before marriage tied you to their house — which is the most precious of all bonds. Did everybody not call you supremely happy in your wife's virtues, in the splendid offices her father held, and in the blessing of sons? I pass over the distinctions you had in your youth which are often denied to old age, because I do not care to talk about blessings other people have shared.

"I would rather come to the unequalled peak of your good fortune. If the enjoyment of any earthly success weighs anything at all in the scale of happiness, can the memory of that splendid day be washed away by any rising flood of troubles? That day when you watched your two sons ride out from your house as joint consuls, with a train of senators behind them and the goodwill of the people to welcome them; when the two of them sat in the curule chairs in the senate house, and you, by your speech in praise of the king, earned a reputation for eloquence and ability; when in the Circus, seated between the two consuls, you satisfied the crowd packed around you with the triumphal handouts they were waiting for — I think that day you cheated Fortune while she was making a fuss of you and treating you as her favourite. You carried off a prize she had never granted to a private citizen before.

"So do you want to settle accounts with Fortune? This is the first time she has ever looked at you with envy. If you compare the extent and the limits of your blessings and your misfortunes, you cannot deny that you are fortunate still. Or, if you do not count yourself favoured by Fortune because what then looked like prosperity has gone, then do not count yourself wretched either, since what you now believe to be a disaster is also passing.

"What — have you only just arrived in this life, a sudden stranger to the scene? Do you think there is any stability in human affairs, when man himself vanishes in the swift running of time? It is true that there is little reason to trust that the gifts of chance will stay; and yet the last day of a life is in a manner the death of whatever Fortune is left. So what difference do you think there is between your leaving her by dying and her leaving you by running away?"

Song III: All Passes

	When the sun in his rose-coloured chariot
	begins to light the dawn,
	his flaming beams come at the stars
	and every glimmering one of them goes pale.
	When the wood, fed by the west wind,
	blushes red with rose-blossom,
	let the rough south wind breathe on it
	and it stands bare, its glory gone.
	The deep lies smooth and quiet
	while the winds are hushed asleep;
	then angry storms whip it up
	and the waves run wild and high.
	So if the changing face of nature
	does not hold still for a moment,
	count a man's fortunes fleeting,
	count happiness as brief as a dream.
	One law only stands fast:
	created things do not last.
