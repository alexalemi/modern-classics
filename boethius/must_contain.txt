# Every pin below was copied out of boethius/chapters/ and then rendered
# into the modern form the translation is REQUIRED to use. The pins are
# repeated verbatim in running_notes.txt so nothing has to be guessed at.
# Matching is case- and whitespace-insensitive but NOT punctuation-
# insensitive, so the pins avoid internal commas wherever possible.
#
# The lesson these encode, from epictetus/ and re-learned in augustine/:
# PIN FROM THE TEXT, then decide the modern form deliberately. Never
# write a pin from a recollection of how the famous quotation goes.

# Book Five, chapter VI. The definition of eternity, and the single
# most-quoted sentence Boethius wrote -- Aquinas takes it over whole,
# and the resolution of the whole book depends on it. Note what it does
# NOT say: not "everlasting", not "for ever". All at once.
# Source: "possession of endless life whole and perfect at a single
# moment."
the complete and perfect possession of endless life all at once

# Book Four, chapter VII. The conclusion of the argument about why the
# wicked prosper, and the sentence the whole book has been walking
# towards. Already plain; pinned unchanged.
every fortune is good fortune

# Book Two, chapter I. Philosophy on the wheel -- the image this book
# gave to the Middle Ages and the reason Fortune has a wheel at all.
# Source: "if it takes to standing still, it ceases to be the wheel of
# Fortune."
it would stop being Fortune's wheel

# Book Two, chapter II. Fortune speaks in her own defence, the great
# set-piece of the book. Already perfect; pinned unchanged.
I turn the wheel that spins

# Book Three, Song IX -- the hymn to the maker of the world, the most
# famous poem in the Consolation and the one Chaucer and Dante both
# knew. Pinned on the invocation it opens with, which must survive as
# an address and not be flattened into a description.
Maker of earth and sky

# Book Three, Song XII. The Orpheus poem, whose whole point is the
# backward look -- Philosophy tells it against Boethius, who is himself
# looking back. Both names must survive.
Orpheus
Eurydice

# NOT PINNED, deliberately: "Philosophy" and "Fortune", which cannot
# fail and would read as coverage without being any. The grimm rule.
