BOETHIUS, THE CONSOLATION OF PHILOSOPHY — TRANSLATION STRATEGY
==============================================================

THE WORK

Written in 524, in prison at Pavia, by a man awaiting execution for a
treason he did not commit. Boethius had been consul, and his two sons
consuls together; he was the last Roman who could read Greek philosophy
in Greek and had set out to translate the whole of Plato and Aristotle
into Latin. Accused of conspiring against Theodoric, he was condemned
without a hearing and put to death — by report, horribly.

The book is what he wrote in the cell. Philosophy appears to him as a
woman, listens to his self-pity, and then argues him out of it over five
books: on Fortune, on what happiness actually is, on why the wicked
prosper, and finally on whether God's foreknowledge leaves anything for
a human being to decide. It ends with the last question answered and no
mention of the sentence.

Source: Standard Ebooks' H. R. James (1897). 39 prose chapters, 39
verse songs, 39,360 words.

WHY THIS BOOK

The only candidate in the 2026-08-17 screening high on BOTH axes:
archaism 23.7 AND calque 22.9. Every other candidate is a one-axis
case. James is thoroughly Victorian — thou, hast, erstwhile, haply,
whither, "ofttimes" — and underneath that sits a technical vocabulary
that is doing real work and cannot simply be smoothed away.

For a thousand years this was the book educated Europe actually read.
Alfred the Great translated it into Old English, Chaucer into Middle
English, Elizabeth I into her own hand. It is the bridge between
antiquity and the Middle Ages, and the natural companion to the
Confessions.


THE VERSE IS THE CENTRAL DECISION, AND THE RHYME IS THE TRANSLATOR'S

Thirty-nine metra alternate with the prose. They are not decoration:
they are where Boethius rests the argument and restates it as feeling,
and Philosophy uses them as medicine — she says so outright.

BOETHIUS WROTE THEM UNRHYMED, in some twenty-eight different classical
quantitative metres. RHYME IS JAMES'S VICTORIAN ADDITION. So dropping
it is not a loss but the removal of a costume the TRANSLATOR put on —
exactly the argument that governs Pusey's "Thou" in augustine/ and
Burton's invented archaism in nights/. Ask of every feature: is this
Boethius', or James's?

THE RULE: render the songs as VERSE — never as prose, which is this
book's silent summarisation — in clear modern English with a strong,
audible rhythm, and WITHOUT forcing rhyme. Where a rhyme arrives
without costing sense, keep it. Never contort a line to reach one;
contortion is the very thing we are removing. Keep the line breaks and
keep the source's alternating indentation, which marks the shorter line
of each couplet: it renders as a single indented block, and the block
must stay one block (the fleming rule).

A song must still sound like a song when read aloud. If a rendering
could be reflowed into a paragraph without loss, it has failed.


THE PROSE

Philosophy talks like a clinician who is also fond of the patient. She
diagnoses, prescribes, checks whether the medicine is working, and
escalates from "gentler remedies" to "stronger" as the books go on —
that medical frame is explicit and must survive. She is brisk, a little
severe, and occasionally very funny at Boethius' expense.

Boethius' own part is short and mostly interjection: he objects, he
concedes, he admits he has lost the thread. Keep him distinct from
Philosophy. In Book One he is entirely self-pity; by Book Five he is
asking the hard questions himself, and that progression is the plot.

KEEP THE PERSONIFICATIONS. Philosophy is "she" throughout — the whole
frame depends on it. Fortune is "she", and in Book Two chapter II she
speaks in her own voice, in her own defence, which is one of the great
set-pieces. Do not neutralise either.


VOCABULARY

MODERNISE SILENTLY (James's Victorian surface, not Boethius'):
  thou/thee/thy/hast/art/doth  -> you/your/have/are/does
  erstwhile, ofttimes          -> once, often
  haply                        -> perhaps
  whither/whence/thither       -> where/from where/there
  aught/naught                 -> anything/nothing
  nay, verily, yea             -> cut, or "no"/"truly"
  wot, ween, list (verb)       -> know, think, choose
  vouchsafe                    -> grant
  FALSE FRIENDS, all present: want (= lack), still (= always),
    prevent (= come before), sensible (= perceptible), several
    (= separate), presently (= at once), estate (= condition),
    conversation (= way of life), suffer (= allow), curious (= careful)

KEEP AND GLOSS ONCE — these carry the argument:
  Fortune            the book's antagonist; never "luck" or "chance"
  the highest good   summum bonum, the hinge of Book Three
  Providence / Fate  DISTINGUISHED in Book Four chapter VI and the
                     distinction is the whole answer to the problem of
                     evil. Providence is the plan seen whole; Fate is
                     the same plan worked out in time. Never blur them.
  necessity          Book Five chapter VI turns on ABSOLUTE necessity
                     versus necessity CONDITIONAL on knowledge. If that
                     distinction blurs, the book's conclusion collapses.
  eternity           NOT everlastingness. Book Five defines it as
                     possessing endless life all at once, and the whole
                     resolution depends on the difference.
  substance/accident, form/matter — kept, glossed at first use.


THE VERNE RULE, AND ITS STRANGEST APPLICATION

Boethius was a Christian — he wrote theological tractates — and there
is NOT ONE WORD of Christianity in this book. No Christ, no Scripture,
no church. Philosophy consoles him with philosophy alone. That silence
is the most discussed fact about the Consolation and it is entirely
deliberate.

SO: DO NOT CHRISTIANISE AND DO NOT DE-CHRISTIANISE. Where he says God,
say God. Where he says the gods, or Fate, or the divine mind, say that.
Do not import a scriptural cadence, and do not add a note pointing out
the absence. James is already slightly more devotional than the Latin;
where his phrasing tips that way, bring it back to neutral.

Likewise: the Platonism (recollection, the pre-existent soul, the world
soul in the Book Three hymn) stands as written. So does the astronomy,
the geography of the known world in Book Two chapter VII, and the
account of the wicked as beasts in Book Four chapter III.


STRUCTURE

Five books, 39 numbered prose chapters, 39 songs. Chapter numbers
restart in each book, which is correct and matches how the book is
cited (Cons. III.9); assemble.unique_ids handles the repeated anchors.

Each chapter file is: the chapter heading, the prose, then the song
heading and the song as one indented block. Song I of Book One has no
prose chapter and stands alone at the head of the book — it is
Boethius' opening lament, and it is what Philosophy interrupts.

CHAPTER TITLES ARE NEW WRITING (the source gives lone Roman numerals),
written from each book's own argument. THE ARGUMENTS THEMSELVES ARE NOT
PUBLISHED — see prep.py — but they are kept in arguments.txt as the
independent census witness.

RATIO: EN->EN modernisation of a Victorian translator, so target
1.00-1.15, as augustine/ landed at 1.05. Verify --min-ratio 0.85
--max-ratio 1.3. Under 1.00 on a chapter with a long song means the
verse has been compressed; verse is where the loss hides, because a
dropped line is invisible to the word ratio at this scale.
