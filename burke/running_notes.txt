BURKE — RUNNING CONSISTENCY LEDGER
==================================
Read text_analysis.txt first.

FILE / PART DISCIPLINE
----------------------
Thirteen sections; 13 of the 22 files are section parts. Check the
manifest for "of" > 1 BEFORE writing any file. The shape is exactly:

    Section Ten: The Church Establishment, and the Seizure of Its Property
    (Part 2 of 4)

    <body>

Parts 2 and later NEVER re-introduce the section; non-final parts NEVER
conclude it. assemble.strip_front drops the first non-blank line of
EVERY file in a group and takes the heading from part 1 only, so a
wrong heading in a later part vanishes silently and a missing part
marker is invisible to verify.py.

The thirteen headings are fixed in prep.py's TITLES and must be
reproduced EXACTLY. Cross-references in the text use the same word form
("as I said at the beginning of this letter"), never a file number.

MARKUP
------
  - _x_ and *x* render as <em>; everything else is convention.
  - Gutenberg's _italics_ are already correct and mostly need only to be
    carried across onto the equivalent English word.
  - A tab indent is a quotation block. Six of them: the Latin verse
    tags and the long French quotations near the end. Keep them
    indented and keep their line breaks.
  - NEVER write an all-caps line: assemble.py reads one as a heading.
  - The opening delimiter may not follow a word character, so write
    "can *not*", never "can*not*".

SENSITIVE PASSAGES — DECIDED IN ADVANCE
---------------------------------------
THE VERNE RULE, IN FULL, AND NO EDITOR'S NOTE ANYWHERE IN THE VOLUME.
Kept entire and unsoftened:
  - "a swinish multitude". The most notorious phrase he ever wrote,
    thrown back at him for a century -- radicals published journals
    called Pig's Meat in reply -- and never retracted. Softening it
    would delete the reception history of the book.
  - The contempt for the National Assembly as country attorneys and
    obscure practitioners raised above their station.
  - The Marie Antoinette passage entire, ten thousand swords and all,
    which even sympathetic contemporaries thought absurd.
  - His partisan and in places inaccurate account of 5-6 October, as
    HIS account.
  - The defence of prejudice as a positive good, of prescription, of
    hereditary rank and of an established church.
  - His hostility to Dissenters, to "political men of letters", and the
    references to Jewish financiers in the assignat passages. This is
    the ugliest thing in the book, it is his, and it stands.
A note saying that some of this has aged badly would be the wink the
Verne rule forbids. A reader in 2026 does not need the help.

SOURCE MISPRINTS CORRECTED, EACH LOGGED HERE
--------------------------------------------
A source oddity is either KEPT or corrected WITH A REASON WRITTEN DOWN;
it is never quietly fixed while translating. Two so far:
  - Section Two prints Horace as "Condo et compono quae mox depromere
    PASSIM". The line is "possim", and I corrected it in draft without
    saying so, which is the defect and not the fix. RESTORED to the
    source reading. Burke's printer is not ours to correct in passing.
  - Section Three prints "he charged him with having broken the
    _original CONTRAST_ between king and people". Two paragraphs above,
    the footnote quotes the same statute with "_original contract_",
    which is also what the Declaration of Right says. This is a
    transmission error and not a claim, so the Verne rule does not
    protect it, and it is CORRECTED to "contract" -- the hume precedent,
    where "necessary and evitable" for "inevitable" reversed the very
    argument it concluded.

Target 0.90-1.05. EN->EN modernisation runs about 1:1: unstacking a
200-word period costs roughly what cutting the throat-clearing saves.
Verify with --min-ratio 0.85 --max-ratio 1.15.

FOREIGN-LANGUAGE QUOTATIONS: KEEP VERBATIM, THEN GLOSS
------------------------------------------------------
Decided at file 009 and applied backwards to 008. Burke quotes at
length in French and Latin because his correspondent read both; his
modern reader does not, and an untranslated block is a hole in the
argument rather than a piece of scholarship. So the ORIGINAL STANDS
VERBATIM -- it is a document, and Burke's own italics inside it are
part of what he is pointing at -- and a plain English rendering
FOLLOWS it, carrying no emphasis markers of its own so that emphasis
parity against the source is undisturbed.
  - 008, Lally Tollendal's letter (~350 words of French, the eyewitness
    Burke rests his account of the sixth of October on). Glossed. This
    is why 008's ratio is 1.15 against the book's 1.00: 570 words of
    the file are the gloss, which is new writing in the soap-bubbles
    caption sense. Do not "correct" it downwards.
  - 009, the Cicero de Legibus footnote. Glossed.
  - 008's French closes "ce ne sera alors moi qui auroit eu tort",
    which is garbled (for "ce ne sera pas moi qui aurai eu tort"). The
    FRENCH IS NOT TOUCHED -- it is quoted matter -- and the gloss simply
    renders the evident sense. Logged here rather than fixed, per the
    rule above.

LORD GEORGE GORDON (009), AND WHY THERE IS STILL NO NOTE
--------------------------------------------------------
The ugliest page in the book after the swinish multitude: Burke jeers
at Gordon's conversion to Judaism, offers to swap "our Protestant
rabbi" for the Archbishop of Paris, and prices the ransom in the
compound interest on the thirty pieces of silver. It is of a piece
with the Jewish-financier references in the assignat sections, which
the ledger above already rules stand as his. TRANSLATED IN FULL AND
UNSOFTENED, with no note, on the same reasoning: it is Burke's own
voice inside his own argument, the reader loses no information and
gains no false one, and a note would be the wink the Verne rule
forbids. "the vulgar, in their blunt, homely style" in the same file
goes to "ordinary people" -- the hume precedent, a dated WORD and not
a dated CLAIM.

MORE SOURCE MISPRINTS, FILE 011 (each corrected, each logged)
-------------------------------------------------------------
  - "If, as I said, any persons are to MATE good deficiencies to the
    public creditor". Plainly "make". A transmission error and not a
    claim, so the Verne rule does not protect it.
  - The Denham quotation prints "May no such storm / Fall on our times,
    where RAIN must reform!" Cooper's Hill reads "where ruin must
    reform", which is also the only reading that means anything in a
    passage about Henry VIII sacking the abbeys. Corrected.
NOT misprints, and changed anyway as dated WORDS rather than dated
claims (the mill/ Mahomedan -> Muslim precedent), listed so that the
next reader does not take them for silent emendations:
  - "Encyclopædia" -> "Encyclopédie", which is what the work is called
    in English now.
  - "Duke de Rochefoucault" / "Cardinal de Rochefoucault" -> "de la
    Rochefoucauld", the standard modern spelling of the family name.
    Burke's own footnote about the relationship is untouched.
  - "2,200,000 _l._ sterling" -> "£2,200,000 sterling". The italic "l."
    is the printer's pound sign, not emphasis on a word; see
    check.EMPH_DROPPED, which subtracts exactly these four spans from
    the parity count for this file and says why.

FILE 012: A COMPUTED TABLE THAT DOES NOT COMPUTE (the fleming rule)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Burke's footnote on the National Assembly's charity spending gives each
item in livres AND in sterling, and prints a total for both columns.
CHECK EVERY TABLE THAT CLAIMS TO BE COMPUTED -- fleming/ found 495x3
printed as 1475 exactly this way. Here:
  - the livres column adds to 51,082,034, which is the printed total;
  - every sterling row is its livres figure divided by exactly 24,
    EXCEPT the grain-premium row, printed 235,329 9s 2d where
    5,671,907/24 = 236,329.458 = 236,329 9s 2d;
  - and the sterling column only reaches its own printed total of
    2,128,418 1 8 with 236,329 in it. As printed it comes to
    2,127,418 1 8, exactly £1,000 short.
Two independent relations agree on which cell is wrong, which is what
makes this a correction and not a guess. Corrected, with an editor's
note on the spot -- the one note of its kind in the volume, and it is a
statement about the EDITION (what was changed and why), not a comment on
a claim of Burke's, so it stays inside the Verne rule.
The table is also RE-SET as a tab-indented block instead of the source's
run-on prose paragraph, on the candle/ precedent for garbled ASCII
tables. Both renderers take a tab-indented run as preformatted.

FILE 013: FIVE MORE OCR-CLASS CORRUPTIONS IN THE SOURCE
--------------------------------------------------------
All five are transmission errors, not claims, so the Verne rule does not
protect them; all five corrected. Listing them because the SHAPE is the
lesson -- every one reads past the eye at speed, and three of them are
real English words in the wrong place:
  - "Ho spent the income of his prerogative nobly"      -> He
  - "If there had been any just cause for this new
     RELIGIONS persecution"                            -> religious
  - "PROM the general style of your late publications"  -> From
  - "those who US TEN with docility to their doctrines" -> listen
  - "and WORE endowed with qualities which made them
     useful in the Church and State"                    -> were
This source (Gutenberg #15679, the Works vol. III) is a scan, and these

SCAN ERRORS INSIDE A QUOTATION ARE STILL SCAN ERRORS
-----------------------------------------------------
The French and Latin quotations carry the same OCR damage as the English
around them, and repairing it is RESTORING the quotation, not altering
it -- the opposite of the rule that Burke's printer is not ours to
correct. Repaired so far, all of them n/u or o/u confusions:
  014  "qui OUT déterminé"      -> qui ont déterminé
  014  "ON plutôt qui devoit"   -> ou plutôt
  015  "puisque tout est à RÉCRÉER" -> recréer (to create again, not
       to amuse oneself -- the accent inverts the sense of the sentence
       that is the whole point of the footnote)
  015  "_Quinze-Vingt_"         -> Quinze-Vingts, the Paris hospital
The test is the one used throughout: is this the AUTHOR's, or the
TRANSMISSION's? A quotation is protected from us, not from its scanner.

FILE 018: THE SLAVE-REVOLT SENTENCE, AND WHY IT IS NOT THE VERNE RULE
----------------------------------------------------------------------
"As the colonists rise on you, the negroes rise on them." Rendered "the
people they hold enslaved rise against them."
This is the mill/ "planters and negroes" precedent exactly, and the
reasoning is the same. The Verne rule protects a dated CLAIM that is
part of the author's subject. Burke's subject in this passage is the
logic of the rights of men running past the men who declared it -- the
colonists invoke it against France, the enslaved invoke it against the
colonists, and the answer in both cases is troops. The claim survives
ENTIRE when the noun is replaced by the relationship, and the rendering
is MORE explicit rather than less (the ball/ Leonid precedent): a modern
reader learns from it that these were enslaved people, which the noun
alone does not say. Nothing else in the sentence moves, and "massacre,
torture, hanging! These are your rights of men!" stands as he wrote it.
Contrast the Gordon passage in 009, which STANDS: there the jeering IS
Burke's argument about who is applauding the Revolution.
