DECIDED AT THE VOICE PILOT (Knot One)

- THE ARCHAISM IN THE KNOTS IS A JOKE AND MUST SURVIVE. Knot One is a
  mock-medieval pastiche: "I trow", "clomb", "perchance", "an' thy guess
  be within one poor half-hour". Carroll is parodying chivalric romance,
  and modernising the diction would destroy the gag exactly as
  modernising Jabberwocky would. Keep the knight-speak; make only the
  genuinely opaque bits clear. THE SAME PRINCIPLE THAT KEEPS US AWAY
  FROM ALICE APPLIES INSIDE THIS BOOK: deliberate style is not a
  barrier to be removed.
  Expect the same in other Knots — Carroll changes costume constantly
  (the Oriental court of Her Radiancy, the seaside lodgings, the
  omnibus). Follow the costume.
- THE PUNS ARE THE POINT AND THEY ARE KEPT: "his answer will be tart"
  (a sharp reply / a tart to eat) and "we shall but get our deserts"
  (what we deserve / desserts). Both survive intact.
- CARROLL'S KNOT ONE ARITHMETIC CHECKS OUT. Level 4 mph, up 3, down 6;
  an out-and-back mile takes half an hour whichever it is (1/4+1/4 =
  1/3+1/6), so six hours gives 12 miles out and 24 in all. Twelve miles
  all level would take 3 hours and all uphill 4, so the summit falls at
  3.5 hours after three o'clock, within the half-hour he allows: half
  past six. Both his published answers are right.
- The money gloss is placed once, in the Preface, as a bracketed note.
- The stray "A TANGLED TALE." line that ends the source Preface is the
  half-title of the next page and is dropped.
