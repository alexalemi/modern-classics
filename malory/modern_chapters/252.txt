Chapter 13: How Sir Palomides by chance met King Mark in flight, and how he overthrew Dagonet and other knights

So by chance King Mark rode past a spring, on the road where a knight-errant stood on horseback, armed at all points, with a great spear in his hand. And when he saw King Mark coming in flight, he said, "Knight, turn back, for shame, and stand with me, and I will be your protection."

"Ah, fair knight," said King Mark, "let me pass, for behind me comes the best knight in the world, with the shield banded in black."

"Shame on you," said the knight. "He is none of the great knights. And if he were Sir Lancelot or Sir Tristram, I would not be afraid to meet the better of the two."

When King Mark heard him say that, he turned his horse and stayed by him. And then that strong knight levelled a spear at Dagonet, and struck him so hard that he carried him over his horse's tail, and nearly broke his neck. And right after him came Sir Brandiles, and when he saw Dagonet take that fall he was exceedingly angry, and shouted, "Defend yourself, knight!" And so they crashed together wondrously hard. But the knight struck Sir Brandiles so hard that he went to the ground, horse and man. Sir Uwain came after and saw all this.

"Jesus," he said, "that is a strong knight."

And then they set their spears in rest, and this knight came on so fiercely that he struck down Sir Uwain. Then came Ozanna with the hardy heart, and he was struck down.

"Now," said Sir Griflet, "if you take my advice, let us send to that knight-errant and find out whether he is of Arthur's court, for I believe it is Sir Lamorak de Galis."

So they sent to him, and begged the strange knight to tell his name, and whether he was of Arthur's court or not.

"As for my name, they shall not know it. But tell them I am a knight-errant as they are, and let them know that I am no knight of King Arthur's court."

And so the squire rode back to them and told them his answer.

"By my head," said Sir Agravain, "he is one of the strongest knights I ever saw, for he has overthrown three noble knights, and we must take him on, for shame."

So Sir Agravain set his spear in rest, and the other was ready, and struck him down over his horse to the ground. And in the same way he struck down Sir Uwain les Avoutres, and Sir Griflet too. Then he had served them all except Sir Dinadan, for he was behind, and Sir Mordred was unarmed, and Dagonet had his armour.

So when this was done, this strong knight rode on his way at a gentle pace, and King Mark rode after him, praising him greatly; but he would answer not a word, but sighed wondrously deeply, hanging his head, and paying no attention to what he said. So they rode a good three English miles, and then this knight called a servant to him, and told him to ride to the fine manor over there: "and commend me to the lady of that castle and place, and ask her to send me refreshment of good food and drink. And if she asks you who I am, tell her that I am the knight who follows the Glatisant Beast," that is to say, in English, the Questing Beast; for wherever that beast went, it gave tongue in its belly with a noise as if it were thirty couple of hounds.

Then the servant went his way and came to the manor, and greeted the lady, and told her where he came from. And when she understood that he came from the knight who followed the Questing Beast, "O sweet Lord Jesus," she said, "when shall I see that noble knight, my dear son Palomides? Alas, will he not stay with me?" And with that she fainted and wept, and grieved exceedingly. And then, as soon as she could, she gave the servant all that he asked. And the servant returned to Sir Palomides, for he was a servant of King Mark's. And as soon as he came, he told them the knight's name was Sir Palomides.

"I am well pleased," said King Mark, "but keep quiet and say nothing."

Then they dismounted and sat down and rested for a while. And with that King Mark at once fell asleep. When Sir Palomides saw him sound asleep, he took his horse and rode his way, and said to them, "I will not be in the company of a sleeping knight." And so he rode off at a great pace.
