Chapter 4: How Merlin told the story of this damsel

At that time there was a knight who was the son of the king of Ireland, and his name was Lanceor. He was a proud knight, and counted himself one of the best of the court; and he felt great spite against Balin for achieving the sword, that anyone should be counted bolder, or of more prowess. And he asked King Arthur if he would give him leave to ride after Balin and avenge the insult he had done.

"Do your best," said Arthur. "I am very angry with Balin. I wish he were paid back for the insult he has done to me and to my court."

Then this Lanceor went to his lodging to make himself ready. In the meantime Merlin came to King Arthur's court, and there he was told the adventure of the sword, and of the death of the Lady of the Lake.

"Now I will tell you," said Merlin. "This same damsel who stands here, who brought the sword to your court, I will tell you the reason for her coming: she is the falsest damsel alive."

"Do not say so," they said.

"She has a brother, an exceedingly good knight of prowess and a thoroughly true man. And this damsel loved another knight, who kept her as his lover; and this good knight her brother met the knight who kept her as his lover, and killed him by force of his hands. When this false damsel learned of this, she went to the Lady Lile of Avalon, and begged her for help, to be avenged on her own brother."
