Chapter 8: How each of them recognised the other, and of their great courtesy, and how Sir Lancelot's brother Sir Ector came to him, and of their joy

And with that Sir Lancelot knelt down on his knees, and threw his shield and his sword away from him. When Sir Percival saw him do this he wondered what he meant. And then he said this: "Sir knight, whoever you are, I charge you by the high order of knighthood, tell me your true name."

Then he said, "So help me God, my name is Sir Lancelot du Lake, son of King Ban of Benwick."

"Alas," said Sir Percival, "what have I done? I was sent by the queen to look for you, and I have been looking for you for nearly two years now, and over there is Sir Ector de Maris, your brother, waiting for me on the far side of that water. Now for God's sake," said Sir Percival, "forgive me the offences I have done you here."

"It is soon forgiven," said Sir Lancelot.

Then Sir Percival sent for Sir Ector de Maris, and when Sir Lancelot caught sight of him, he ran to him and took him in his arms; and then Sir Ector knelt down, and they wept on each other, so that it moved everyone to pity to see them. Then Dame Elaine came, and she gave them as warm a welcome as lay in her power; and there she told Sir Ector and Sir Percival how and in what way Sir Lancelot had come into that country, and how he was healed. And there it came out how long Sir Lancelot had been with Sir Bliant and with Sir Selivant, and how he first met them, and how he left them because of a boar; and how the hermit healed Sir Lancelot of his great wound, and how he came to Corbin.
