Chapter 19: How Sir Marhaus, Sir Gawain, and Sir Uwain met three damsels, and each of them took one

"Now each of us shall choose a damsel."

"I will tell you," said Sir Uwain, "I am the youngest and the weakest of us, so I will have the eldest damsel, for she has seen much, and can best help me when I am in need, for I have more need of help than either of you."

"Now," said Sir Marhaus, "I will have the damsel of thirty winters, for she suits me best."

"Well," said Sir Gawain, "I thank you, for you have left me the youngest and the fairest, and she is the one I like best."

Then each damsel took her knight by the reins of his bridle, and brought him to where the three roads parted, and there they swore their oath to meet at the spring that day twelve months, if they were alive. And so they kissed and parted, and each knight set his lady up behind him. And Sir Uwain took the road that lay west, and Sir Marhaus took the road that lay south, and Sir Gawain took the road that lay north.

Now we will begin with Sir Gawain, who kept to that road until he came to a fair manor where there lived an old knight and a good householder. And there Sir Gawain asked the knight if he knew of any adventures in that country.

"I will show you some tomorrow," said the old knight, "and marvellous ones."

So in the morning they rode into the forest of adventures, to a clearing, and there they found a cross. And as they stood and waited there, there came by them the fairest knight and the handsomest man they ever saw, grieving more than any man ever grieved. And then he saw Sir Gawain, and greeted him, and prayed God to send him much honour.

"As for that," said Sir Gawain, "many thanks. And I also pray God to send you honour and renown."

"Ah," said the knight, "I may set that aside, for sorrow and shame come to me after honour."
