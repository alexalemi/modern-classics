Chapter 6: How King Arthur gathered all the knights to joust in the meadow beside Camelot before they left

"Now," said the king, "I am sure that all of you of the Round Table will depart on this quest of the Holy Grail, and I shall never see you all together again; so I want to see you all together in the meadow of Camelot, to joust and to fight in a tournament, so that after your deaths men may say of it that such good knights were all together on such a day."

They all agreed to that plan, and at the king's request, and put on the armour that belonged to jousting. But the king's whole purpose in all this was to see Galahad put to the test; for the king thought he would not easily come back to the court after he left. So they were assembled in the meadow, rich and poor alike. Then Sir Galahad, at the request of the king and the queen, put on a fine short coat of double mail, and he also put on his helmet, but he would take no shield, for all the king's asking. And then Sir Gawain and other knights begged him to take a spear. He did so; and the queen was in a tower with all her ladies, to watch that tournament. Then Sir Galahad took his place in the middle of the meadow, and began to break spears marvellously, so that everyone wondered at him; for there he outdid all the other knights, for within a short while he had overthrown many good knights of the Round Table, except two, and those were Sir Lancelot and Sir Percival.
