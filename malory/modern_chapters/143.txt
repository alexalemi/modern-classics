Chapter 24: How King Arthur pardoned them, and asked them where Sir Gareth was

Then they all said graciously at once, "God forgive you, and so do we. And we beg you to tell us where we may find Sir Beaumains."

"Fair lords," said Sir Ironside, "I cannot tell you, for he is very hard to find. For young knights such as he is, when they are on their adventures, never stay in any one place."

But as for the praise that the Red Knight of the Red Launds, and Sir Persant and his brother, gave Beaumains, it was a marvel to hear.

"Well, my fair lords," said King Arthur, "you may be sure I shall do you honour for the love of Sir Beaumains, and as soon as ever I meet him I shall make you all knights of the Round Table on one day. And as for you, Sir Persant of Inde, you have always been called a very noble knight, and so your three brothers have always been called. But I wonder," said the king, "that I hear nothing of the Black Knight, your brother. He was a very noble knight."

"Sir," said Pertolepe, the Green Knight, "Sir Beaumains killed him in an encounter with his spear. His name was Sir Percard."

"That was a great pity," said the king, and so said many knights. For these four brothers were very well known in King Arthur's court as noble knights, for they had long made war on the knights of the Round Table.

Then Pertolepe, the Green Knight, said to the king, "At a crossing of the water of Mortaise Sir Beaumains met two brothers who for the most part always kept that crossing, and they were two deadly knights. And there he killed the eldest brother in the water: he struck him on the head such a blow that he fell down in the water, and there he was drowned, and his name was Sir Gherard le Breusse. And afterwards he killed the other brother on the land, and his name was Sir Arnold le Breusse."
