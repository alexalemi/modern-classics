Chapter 13: How Morgan tried to kill Sir Uriens her husband, and how Sir Uwain her son saved him

Meanwhile Morgan le Fay believed King Arthur was dead. So one day she saw King Uriens lying asleep in his bed. Then she called to her a maiden who was in her confidence, and said, "Go and fetch me my lord's sword, for I never saw a better time to kill him than now."

"O madam," said the damsel, "if you kill my lord you can never escape."

"Never you mind," said Morgan le Fay, "for now I see that this is the best time to do it. So hurry, quickly, and fetch me the sword."

Then the damsel left, and found Sir Uwain sleeping on a bed in another room. So she went to Sir Uwain, and woke him, and told him, "Get up, and keep watch on my lady your mother, for she means to kill the king your father as he sleeps in his bed, and I am going to fetch his sword."

"Well," said Sir Uwain, "go on your way, and leave this to me."

At once the damsel brought Morgan the sword with trembling hands, and she took the sword swiftly, and drew it, and went boldly to the bedside, and watched for how and where she could best kill him. And as she lifted up the sword to strike, Sir Uwain leapt at his mother, and caught her by the hand, and said, "Ah, fiend, what are you doing? If you were not my mother, I would strike off your head with this sword. Ah," said Sir Uwain, "men say that Merlin was fathered by a devil, but I can say an earthly devil bore me."

"O fair son, Uwain, have mercy on me. I was tempted by a devil, and so I beg your pardon. I will never do such a thing again. Save my honour and do not give me away."

"On this condition," said Sir Uwain, "I will forgive you: that you will never set about such deeds again."

"No, son," she said, "and I give you my word on it."
