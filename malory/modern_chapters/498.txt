Chapter 7: Of what some men believe about the death of King Arthur, and how Queen Guinevere became a nun at Almesbury

Yet some men say in many parts of England that King Arthur is not dead, but was taken by the will of our Lord Jesus into another place; and men say that he will come again, and he will win the Holy Cross. I will not say that it shall be so, but rather I will say: here in this world he changed his life. But many men say that there is written upon his tomb this verse: Hic jacet Arthurus, rex quondam, rexque futurus. Here lies Arthur, the once and future king.

So I leave Sir Bedivere here with the hermit, who lived at that time in a chapel beside Glastonbury, and there was his hermitage. And so they lived in their prayers, and fastings, and great abstinence. And when Queen Guinevere understood that King Arthur was killed, and all the noble knights, Sir Mordred and all the rest, then the queen stole away, with five ladies, and so she went to Almesbury; and there she had herself made a nun, and wore white clothes and black, and she took on as great a penance as ever any sinful lady did in this land, and no one could ever make her merry; but she lived in fasting, prayer and works of charity, so that all kinds of people marvelled at how virtuously she was changed. Now we leave Queen Guinevere in Almesbury, a nun in white clothes and black, and there she was abbess and ruler, as was only right; and we turn from her, and speak of Sir Lancelot du Lake.
