Chapter 6: How Sir Tristram landed on the island to fight his battle with Sir Marhaus

So, to cut this tale short, when Sir Tristram had landed on the island he looked to the far side, and there he saw six ships at anchor near the shore; and in the shadow of the ships, on the land, the noble knight Sir Marhaus of Ireland was waiting. Then Sir Tristram commanded his servant Gouvernail to bring his horse ashore and set his armour in order in every respect. And then, when he had done so, he mounted his horse; and when he was in his saddle, well equipped, and his shield set on his shoulder, Tristram asked Gouvernail, "Where is this knight I am to fight?"

"Sir," said Gouvernail, "don't you see him? I thought you had seen him. He is waiting over there in the shadow of his ships, on horseback, with his spear in his hand and his shield on his shoulder."

"That's true," said the noble knight Sir Tristram. "Now I see him well enough."

Then he commanded his servant Gouvernail to go back to his boat: "And commend me to my uncle King Mark, and ask him, if I am killed in this battle, to bury my body as he thinks best. And as for me, let him know that I will never yield out of cowardice; and if I am killed and do not flee, then they have lost no tribute because of me. And if it happens that I flee, or yield and beg for my life, tell my uncle never to bury me in Christian ground. And on your life," said Sir Tristram to Gouvernail, "do not come near this island until you see me beaten or killed, or else see me win against that knight over there."

So they parted from each other, weeping bitterly.
