FORWARD REFERENCES TO HONOUR

- Mordred is Arthur's son by his half-sister Morgause (I.19-20) and is
  saved from the May-Day ship (I.27). Malory does not mention it again
  for a long time; when it returns in XX-XXI, "his son" must land.
- Balin's sword (II.19) is set in a floating stone by Merlin and drawn by
  Galahad (XIII.2-5). The two passages must name it the same way.
- The Siege Perilous (III.2, XIII.4): Merlin's empty seat at the Round
  Table, death to anyone but the destined knight. Same name, same gloss.
- The Dolorous Stroke (II.15): Balin's wounding of King Pellam lays waste
  three kingdoms; the Maimed King is healed only by Galahad (XVII).
- Lamorak's murder by Gawain's brothers (X) and the Orkney-Pellinore feud
  are paid off in XX-XXI. Gareth, who loved Lancelot best, is killed by
  him unknowing in the rescue of Guinevere (XX.8); Gawain's grief and
  hatred drive the end. Agravain and Mordred expose the lovers (XX.1-3).
- Excalibur and its SCABBARD: Merlin tells Arthur the scabbard is worth
  ten swords (I.25); Morgan steals it (IV.14). Keep "scabbard" as the word.
- Elaine of Astolat's barge and letter (XVIII.20) is the source of
  Tennyson's Lady of Shalott. Her letter stays a letter, in quotes.
