SPINOZA, ETHICS — RUNNING NOTES
================================
The shared consistency ledger. Read before translating any file.

THE GOVERNING RULE, AND IT IS THE OPPOSITE OF THIS PROJECT'S USUAL ONE
----------------------------------------------------------------------
IF A SENTENCE IS ALREADY CLEAR, LEAVE IT ALONE (the hume rule).
Spinoza's mean sentence is 17.9 words and only 16% run over 35. There
is nothing to unstack. A translator who arrives expecting Burke's
200-word periods will find nothing to do and will start rewriting prose
that was already fine, and a file in which every sentence has been
recast has been damaged however defensible each recasting was.

What actually needs doing, in order:
  1. the technical vocabulary, locked below;
  2. Elwes's thin Victorian layer -- "whatsoever", "in so far as" where
     it is doing nothing, "appertain", "owns God as its cause";
  3. the connective throat-clearing that opens proofs.

THE LOCKED GLOSSARY
-------------------
These are terms, not words. Getting one wrong does not make a sentence
obscure, it makes it mean something Spinoza spent a Part disproving.
Caps spent once, then used plainly.

  substance      KEEP. That which is in itself and is conceived through
                 itself. NEVER "stuff", "material", "matter".
  mode           KEEP. A modification of substance; a particular thing.
  attribute      KEEP.
  affection      -> "state" or "modification" where Elwes means
                 affectio (Elwes footnotes this himself at I.Def.V).
                 NEVER "fondness".
  emotion        KEEP for affectus. It is the title of Parts III and IV
                 and the whole subject; do not vary it to "feeling",
                 "passion" or "affect".
  passion        KEEP where Spinoza opposes it to activity.
  idea           KEEP. Not "notion", not "opinion", not "thought".
  adequate /     KEEP both. Technical properties of an idea.
  inadequate
  perfection     KEEP, and keep his identification of it with reality.
                 He says outright they are the same thing.
  reality        KEEP.
  virtue         KEEP, and it means POWER TO ACT (virtus), not moral
                 goodness. Part IV turns on this. Where a modern reader
                 would certainly misread it, the surrounding sentence
                 may carry the sense, but the word stays.
  endeavour      KEEP for conatus. "Everything, in so far as it is in
                 itself, endeavours to persist in its own being."
                 Pinned in must_contain. Do NOT vary to "strives",
                 "tends", "seeks" -- it is the hinge of Parts III-V and
                 a reader has to be able to follow it by ear.
  blessedness    KEEP for beatitudo. The last word of the book.
  bondage        KEEP. The title of Part IV.
  acquiescentia  Elwes has "acquiescence" / "self-approval". Keep
                 "self-approval" where it is the emotion, and
                 "contentment" where he means acquiescentia in se ipso
                 loosely -- but never "complacency" in the modern
                 sense, which has inverted since 1883.
  God or Nature  LOCKED, no comma. Elwes writes it three times and it
                 is the most famous phrase in the book.
  under the form KEEP for sub specie aeternitatis. Not "aspect", not
  of eternity    "standpoint". Pinned.

STRUCTURE AND CONVENTIONS
-------------------------
Every file is already laid out by prep.py and the layout is FIXED:

  Proposition 7            <- a line on its own, title case, no period.
                              assemble.py renders it as a subheading.
  <the proposition>        <- its own paragraph.
  Proof. ...               <- paragraph opening with the word.
  Corollary 1. ...
  Note. ...
  Explanation. ...

DO NOT renumber, merge or reorder these, and do not write an all-caps
line anywhere -- assemble.py reads one as a heading. Book titles and
emphasis: the pipeline is markup-free except for _x_ / *x*, and Spinoza
needs almost none.

THE REFERENCES ARE ALREADY DONE. All 1,075 were resolved by prep.py
into one form:
    (by Proposition 4 of this Part)
    (by Part 2, Proposition 7, Corollary)
    (by Definition 13 of the Emotions)
COPY THEM THROUGH EXACTLY. Do not renumber, re-abbreviate, or "tidy"
them, and do not convert them back to roman. check.py compares the set
of citations in each modern file against its source file and a drift is
a defect: a citation is the only thing in this book that a reader
cannot repair for themselves.

VOICE
-----
Spinoza is the least rhetorical writer in this collection and the most
absolute. He is not persuading, he is demonstrating, and the flatness
IS the argument -- he says in the Preface to Part III that he will
treat human passions exactly as if he were dealing with lines, planes
and solids. Do not warm him up. Do not add connective throat-clearing.
Do not soften "hence it follows" into "so you can see that". Keep the
Q.E.D.

BUT THE NOTES ARE A DIFFERENT VOICE and this is where the book opens
up: the Appendix to Part I on final causes, the Preface to Part III,
the notes on free will and on the man who thinks he chooses, the
Preface to Part IV, the whole of Part V's second half. There he is
polemical, occasionally sardonic, and writing for a reader rather than
for a proof. Keep the two registers apart. The Appendix to Part I is
the best prose in the book and the reason many people read it at all.

SENSITIVE CONTENT — THE VERNE RULE, no note anywhere in the volume.
The Appendix to Part I is a sustained attack on providence, miracles
and scripture read as prophecy; it is why the Ethics could not be
published in his lifetime and why he was expelled from his synagogue at
twenty-three. The identification of God with Nature stands as he wrote
it. Part IV's remarks on the weak-minded, on the drunkard and on women
stand as his. Nothing is softened, nothing is annotated.
