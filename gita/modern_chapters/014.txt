Chapter 15: The Supreme Person

Krishna said:

There is a tree, they say, that never perishes: the ashvattha fig,
with its roots above and its branches below. Its leaves are the hymns of
the Vedas. Whoever knows this tree knows the Vedas.

Its branches spread out below and above, fed by the gunas, and the
things of the senses are its buds. And down below, its roots stretch on
and on into the world of human beings, binding them to action. Here
in this world no one can see its true shape — not its end, not its
beginning, not what it stands on. This ashvattha has grown deep, strong
roots. Take the hard axe of detachment and cut it down. Then you
must search out that place from which those who reach it never come
back, saying, "I take refuge in that first Person, from whom the
ancient stream of all activity flowed out." Those who are free of
pride and delusion, who have conquered the fault of attachment, who
live always in the self, whose desires have died away, who are freed
from the pairs of opposites called pleasure and pain, go undeluded to
that place that never perishes.

The sun does not light it, nor the moon, nor fire. Those who go
there do not come back. That is my highest home.

An eternal fragment of me becomes a living soul in the world of
living things, and it draws to itself the senses, with the mind as the
sixth, which rest in nature. When the self, the lord of the body,
takes on a body, and when it leaves one, it takes these senses along
with it as it goes, the way the wind carries scents away from the
flowers. Presiding over the ear and the eye, touch and taste and
smell, and the mind as well, it enjoys the things of the senses.

Whether it is leaving the body or staying in it, enjoying the
world or caught up in the gunas, the deluded do not see it. Those who
have the eye of knowledge see it. Yogis who strive see it too,
present within themselves; but those whose selves are unformed, who
have no insight, do not see it, however hard they strive.

The brilliance in the sun that lights up the whole world, the
brilliance in the moon and the brilliance in fire: know that this
brilliance is mine. I enter the earth and hold all beings up with
my strength. I become Soma, the moon, which is made of the sap of life,
and I nourish all the plants. I become the fire of digestion and
live in the bodies of all that breathe, and joined with the breath that
goes in and the breath that goes out, I digest the four kinds of food.

I am seated in the heart of every being. From me come memory and
knowledge, and their loss. I am the one thing all the Vedas are there to
make known. I am the maker of the Vedanta, the end of the Vedas, and I
am the one who knows the Vedas.

There are two persons in this world, the perishable and the
imperishable. All living beings are the perishable; the one who stands
unchanging is called the imperishable. But there is another, the
highest Person, called the supreme self: the Lord who never perishes,
who enters the three worlds and holds them up. Because I am beyond
the perishable, and higher even than the imperishable, I am known in the
world and in the Vedas as the Supreme Person.

Whoever is free of delusion and knows me in this way as the Supreme
Person knows everything, Arjuna, and worships me with his whole being.
So I have told you this most secret teaching, Arjuna, you who are
without fault. Whoever understands it will be wise, and will have done
everything there is to do.
