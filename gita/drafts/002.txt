Chapter 3: The Way of Action

Arjuna said:

{1} Krishna, if you think understanding is better than action, then why
are you pushing me into this terrible action? {2} You keep mixing your
words, and you seem to be confusing my understanding. Tell me one thing,
and tell me for certain: what will bring me to the highest good?

Krishna said:

{3} Long ago, Arjuna, I taught that there are two ways to stand firm in
this world: for the followers of Sankhya, the yoga of knowledge; for the
yogis, the yoga of action. {4} No one reaches freedom from action by
simply not starting anything, and no one reaches perfection just by
giving things up. {5} For nobody can stay without acting even for a
moment. Everyone, whether they like it or not, is made to act by the
gunas, which are born of nature.

{6} Someone who holds back his hands and feet and tongue but sits there
with his mind dwelling on the things of the senses fools himself, and he
is called a hypocrite. {7} But someone who reins in his senses with his
mind, Arjuna, and without attachment sets his body to work in the yoga
of action — that person stands out. {8} Do the work that is yours to do,
since action is better than doing nothing. Without action you could not
even keep your body alive.

{9} This whole world is bound by action, except for action done as a
sacrifice. So do your work as a sacrifice, Arjuna, and let go of
attachment.

{10} In the beginning, when Prajapati, the Lord of Creatures, made all
beings, he made sacrifice along with them, and he told them: "Grow and
multiply by this. Let it be your wishing cow, the cow that gives
whatever you wish for. {11} With it, nourish the gods, and let the gods
nourish you. By nourishing one another, you will reach the highest good.
{12} For the gods, nourished by sacrifice, will give you the pleasures
you want." Whoever enjoys what they give and gives nothing back to them
is nothing but a thief. {13} Good people, who eat what is left over from
the sacrifice, are freed from every fault. But the wicked, who cook only
for themselves, are eating their own sin.

{14} Living beings come from food; food comes from rain; rain comes from
sacrifice; and sacrifice comes from action. {15} Know that action comes
from Brahman, and Brahman comes from the imperishable. So Brahman, which
reaches everywhere, is always present in sacrifice. {16} This is the
wheel that has been set turning, Arjuna. Whoever does not help keep it
turning here lives a sinful life, delighting only in his senses, and he
lives in vain.

{17} But a person who delights only in the self, who is satisfied with
the self and finds his whole contentment in the self — for him there is
nothing he has to do. {18} He has nothing to gain here by acting and
nothing to gain by not acting, and he does not depend on any being for
anything. {19} So do the work you have to do, always, without
attachment. A person who acts without attachment reaches the highest.

{20} It was by action that King Janaka and others like him reached
perfection. You should act too, if only with an eye to holding the world
together. {21} Whatever the best person does, everyone else does too.
Whatever standard he sets, the world follows.

{22} There is nothing in the three worlds, Arjuna, that I have to do.
There is nothing I lack and nothing I have to get. And yet I keep on
working. {23} For if I ever stopped working, ever let up for a moment,
people everywhere would follow my path, Arjuna. {24} These worlds would
fall into ruin if I did not act. I would be the cause of the mixing of
the classes, and I would destroy all these creatures.

{25} The ignorant act because they are attached to their work, Arjuna.
The wise should act just as hard, but without attachment, wanting to
hold the world together. {26} A wise man should not unsettle the
understanding of ignorant people who are attached to their work. He
should do all his own work in a disciplined way, and so get them to
enjoy theirs.

{27} All actions, everywhere, are done by the gunas of nature. But a
person whose self is confused by ego thinks, "I am the one doing this."
{28} But the person who knows the truth about how the gunas and their
workings are divided up, Arjuna, understands that it is only the gunas
acting on the gunas, and he does not get attached. {29} People confused
by the gunas of nature get attached to the workings of the gunas.
Someone who knows the whole should not unsettle these slow people who
know only part of it.

{30} Lay all your actions on me, with your mind fixed on the self
within. Be free of hope and of any thought of "mine," and fight, with
this fever gone from you.

{31} People who always follow this teaching of mine, full of faith and
without finding fault with it, are set free from the bonds of action as
well. {32} But those who find fault with it and do not follow my
teaching — know that they are confused about everything there is to
know, that they are mindless, and that they are lost.

{33} Even a wise man behaves according to his own nature. All beings
follow their nature. What good will holding it back do? {34} Longing and
loathing lie in wait in every sense, fixed on its object. Don't fall
under their power. They are the robbers waiting by your road.

{35} Better your own duty done imperfectly than another's duty done
well. Better to die doing your own duty; another's duty is full of
danger.

Arjuna said:

{36} Then what is it, Krishna, that drives a person to sin, even against
his will, as if he were being forced?

Krishna said:

{37} It is desire. It is anger. It is born of the guna of passion. It is
a great devourer and a great evil. Know that this is the enemy here.
{38} As fire is covered by smoke, as a mirror is covered by dust, as an
unborn child is wrapped in the womb, so all this is wrapped in desire.
{39} Knowledge is covered over by it, Arjuna: by this constant enemy of
the wise, which takes the form of desire, never satisfied, like a fire.

{40} The senses, the mind and the understanding are said to be its home.
Through them it covers knowledge and leads the one who lives in the body
astray. {41} So first rein in your senses, Arjuna, and then kill this
evil thing that destroys knowledge and insight.

{42} The senses, they say, are high. Higher than the senses is the mind.
Higher than the mind is the understanding. And higher than the
understanding is he — the self. {43} Know, then, what is higher than the
understanding. Steady yourself by your self, Arjuna, and kill this enemy
that takes the form of desire, so hard to get at.
