Chapter 16: The Divine and the Demonic

Krishna said:

{1} Fearlessness, purity of heart, standing firm in the yoga of
knowledge; giving, self-control and sacrifice, studying the Vedas,
austerity and honesty; {2} doing no harm, truthfulness, freedom from
anger, giving things up, peace, never speaking ill of others, kindness
to every living thing, freedom from greed, gentleness, modesty, and
steadiness; {3} energy, patience, endurance, purity, freedom from
malice, and freedom from too much pride: these, Arjuna, belong to
someone born with the divine nature.

{4} Hypocrisy, arrogance and conceit, anger and harshness, and
ignorance: these, Arjuna, belong to someone born with the demonic
nature.

{5} The divine nature leads to freedom, and the demonic nature, it is
held, to bondage. Don't grieve, Arjuna. You were born with the divine
nature.

{6} There are two kinds of beings created in this world, the divine and
the demonic. I have described the divine at length. Now, Arjuna, hear
from me about the demonic.

{7} Demonic people do not know when to act and when to hold back. There
is no purity in them, no right conduct, and no truth. {8} They say the
world has no truth in it, no foundation and no Lord; that it was not
brought into being by one thing following from another — what else
could it be? — and that desire is its only cause.

{9} Holding on to this view, these lost souls of small understanding
and cruel deeds rise up as enemies of the world, to destroy it. {10} They
give themselves over to desire that can never be satisfied. They are
full of hypocrisy, pride and arrogance; in their delusion they seize on
false ideas, and they set to work with unclean purposes. {11} They are
gripped by endless worry that ends only with death. The enjoyment of
their desires is their highest aim, and they are sure that this is all
there is. {12} Bound by a hundred cords of hope, given over to desire
and anger, they work to pile up wealth by unjust means, all to satisfy
their desires.

{13} "This is what I have gained today, and this is what I want, and I
will get it. This much is mine already, and this wealth will be mine
too. {14} I have killed that enemy, and I will kill others as well. I am
a lord. I enjoy what I like. I am successful, strong and happy. {15} I am
rich, and I come from a great family. Who else is there like me? I will
make sacrifices, I will give gifts, I will enjoy myself." So they talk,
deluded by ignorance.

{16} Led astray by their many thoughts, caught in a net of delusion,
addicted to the enjoyment of their desires, they fall into a foul hell.
{17} Full of their own importance, stubborn, drunk with the pride of
wealth, they make sacrifices in name only, out of hypocrisy and against
the rules. {18} Given over to ego, force, arrogance, desire and anger,
these jealous people hate me, who live in their own bodies and in the
bodies of others.

{19} These cruel, hateful people, the lowest of men, I hurl again and
again into demonic wombs, as they go around in the round of birth and
death. {20} Fallen into demonic wombs, deluded birth after birth, they
never reach me, Arjuna, and from there they sink to the lowest state of
all.

{21} There are three gates to this hell, and they destroy the self:
desire, anger and greed. So a person should give up these three.
{22} The man who is freed from these three gates to darkness, Arjuna,
does what is truly good for himself, and so he reaches the highest goal.

{23} Whoever throws away the rules the scriptures lay down and does
whatever his desires tell him does not reach perfection, or happiness,
or the highest goal. {24} So let the scriptures be your authority in
deciding what you should do and what you should not. Know what the rules
of scripture say, and then do your work here in the world.
