EUCLID AND HIS MODERN RIVALS — SOURCE ASSESSMENT
Lewis Carroll (C. L. Dodgson), 1879; second edition 1885
Scouted 2026-08-06, before any prep was written.

The lesson of thompson/ was that four fifths of the work on an OCR book
is making a source worth translating. This book is worse than thompson/
in one specific way and better in another, and both are worth knowing
before a line of prep.py is written.


1. THERE IS NO GUTENBERG EDITION

Checked, with a control: the same search finds Pillow Problems at #79080,
so the search works and the book genuinely is not there. Archive.org has
two scans and nothing else does.

  euclidhismodernr00carr      1885, 2nd ed.   322pp  scribe2 scan
  euclidandhismode000469mbp   1879, 1st ed.   338pp  Million Books scan

Both about 74,000 words.


2. USE BOTH. THEIR OCR ERRORS ARE INDEPENDENT.

This is the single biggest lever on the whole job, and it is not obvious
from either scan alone. Take the opening stage direction:

  1885: "[Scene, a College dudy. Time, midvigJtf. Mixos discovered
         seated between tivo gigantic j)iles of manuscripts. Ever and
         anon lie takes a joaper from one Tieap^ reads it ... with a
         ivearij sigh ... running of fiugers through it"

  1879: "[Scene, a College study. Time, midnight. Mrsros discovered
         seated between two gigantic piles of manuscripts. Ever and
         anon he takes a paper from one heap> reads it ... with a weary
         sigh ... running of fingers through it>"

THE ITALICS ARE THE PROBLEM, and they are where the play keeps its stage
directions. The 1885 scan renders italic text as debris; the 1879 scan
renders it almost cleanly. Neither scan is reliable on its own and
neither is reliably better — in the SAME two sentences:

  - 1885 has "the second Corollary of Euc. I. 32" and 1879 has "I. 33".
    I.32's second corollary is the one about exterior angles summing to
    four right angles, which is the joke (Minos's hair "radiates in all
    directions"), so 1885 is right.
  - 1885 has "the paper had I. 20 in it" and 1879 has "I. 30". The
    sentence two lines earlier is "Assuming I. 20?", so 1885 is right
    again — but only the context says so, and a digit is exactly what no
    checker can see.

PLAN: the 1885 is COPY TEXT, because it is the edition Carroll revised.
The 1879 is the CORRECTOR, especially for every italic passage. They
differ in content as well as in OCR, so a difference is a question, not
an answer — but a difference is where to look.


3. THE SPEAKER TAGS ARE DAMAGED, AND THAT IS THE DANGEROUS PART

The book is a play. Minos, a college examiner, is visited at midnight by
the ghost of Euclid and then by Herr Niemand, the phantom of a German
professor who speaks for each modern textbook in turn. Every speech is
tagged with an abbreviation, and the OCR mangles them:

  1885   Min 380  Nie 294  Euc 95  Mill 46  Nos 28  Mln 12  Eiic 11
         Hue 9  Wie 4
  1879   Min 295  Nie 241  Mm 129  Euc 54  Hue 49  Nos 20  Mln 8
         Nle 6  Hin 5  JSuc 4  Sue 4  HUG 3  Uuc 3

This is the bunyan/ trap — "Christ." is Christiana and not Christ — but
noisy instead of systematic, and it matters more: attribute a speech to
the wrong ghost and Carroll's argument changes hands. Read as OCR
soup it produces fluent, confident, entirely wrong dialogue.

THE GOOD NEWS, and the reason this is tractable: THE SPEAKER VOCABULARY
IS TINY AND CLOSED. Min, Euc, Nie, Nos and a few walk-ons. So a
nearest-match against a fixed list is safe, the two scans can VOTE, and
prep can RAISE on any tag it cannot resolve rather than guess. Do that;
do not let an unresolved tag through.

GALILEO'S RULE APPLIES: speaker tags must be Title-Case, never ALL-CAPS.
assemble.py reserves all-caps lines for section headings, and an all-caps
tag renders as a run-on paragraph.


4. WHAT ELSE IS IN THERE

From the 1885 ABBYY XML: 653 Text blocks, **29 Picture** and **19
Table**. So the full thompson/ apparatus is needed —
  - 29 geometrical diagrams, to be cut from the page scans (replate.py:
    grow each side until it reaches whitespace, then map darkness to
    alpha so the paper goes and the ink survives);
  - 19 tables, EVERY ONE of which must be checked against the page image.
    normalise()'s "drop paragraphs under three words" ate three rows out
    of a table in thompson/ and moved the word ratio by nothing.

Structure: four Acts, about 27 Scenes, and five Appendices. The
Appendices are dense tabular matter — lists of propositions cross-
referenced to a dozen rival textbooks — and want a decision of their own
before prep, in the way bunyan/'s 1,010 commentator notes did.

Page furniture to strip, all of it INSIDE the text: running heads of the
shape "MINOS AND EUCLID. [ACT I.", printer's signature marks ("'^ B"),
an ERRATUM slip bound between the contents and Act I whose text the OCR
has interleaved with the first page, and page numbers. Remember the
thompson/ mend rule: stripping a running head leaves a blank line, a
blank line is a paragraph break, and paragraphs then split at every page
turn. No English paragraph starts in lower case; iterate to a fixed
point.


5. WHY THE BOOK IS WORTH IT

It is a very funny book that almost nobody has read. Carroll puts the
ghost of Euclid on trial, has him defend the Elements against thirteen
Victorian rivals one at a time, and lets Minos demolish them with
perfectly straight-faced courtesy. It is also the best surviving account
of what a Victorian mathematician thought a proof was FOR — and the
whole argument is conducted as farce, at midnight, by a man who cannot
sleep for marking.
