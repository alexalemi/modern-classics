I read in the Preface (p. 10) 'The present volume follows substantially the order of Blanchet's Legendre, while the form of the demonstrations is modeled after the more logical method of Euclid/ He has not, however, adopted the method of infinite series, which constitutes the crucial distinction between that writer and Euclid.

The Propositions are pretty nearly in Euclid's order: with a few changes in order and numeration, the book would be a modernised Euclid, the only important differ- ences being the adoption of Play fair's Axiom and the omission of the diagonals in Euc.
