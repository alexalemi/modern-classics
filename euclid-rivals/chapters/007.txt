The sum total of the chief defects which I have noticed is as follows :— fourteen of Euclid's Theorems in Book I. omitted; seven unaxiomatic Axioms; six instances of ' Petitio Principii.^

The abundant specimens of logical inaccuracy, and of loose writing generally, which I have here collected would, I feel sure, in a mere popular treatise be discreditable— in a scientific treatise, however modestly put forth, de- plorable—but in a treatise avowedly put forth as a model of logical precision, and intended to supersede Euclid, they are simply monstrous.

My ultimate conclusion on your Manual is that it has no claim whatever to be adopted as the Manual for pur- poses of teaching and examination.
