Even in 1762, this^ system could be characterised by d'Alembert as that commonly used in France in opposition to the equal temperament which Rameau subsequently proposed. Marpurg X has collected a long series of other systems of tuning. Since players found tliemselves compelled by the use of only 12 digitals to the octave, to put up with a series of false intervals, and to let their ears become accustomed to them, it was certainly better to make up their minds to give up their few perfect major Thirds still remaining in the scale, and to make all the major Thii'ds equally erroneous. It necessarily produces more disturbance to hear very falsely tuned Thirds amidst correct intervals, than to hear intervals which are all equally out of tune and are not contrasted with others in perfect intonation. Hence as long as it is necessarv practically to limit the number of separate tones within the octave to 12, there can be no question at all as to the superiority of the equal temperament with its 12 equal Semitones, over all others, and, as a natural consequence, this has become the sole ¶ acknowledged method of tuning.
