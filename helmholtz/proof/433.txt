After long continued action of the force \(p\), however, the exponential functions vanish, and \(H\) receives the constant value \[H_\infty = \frac{p}{\beta mk}.\] On the other hand, for very small values of \(\tau\) the initial maxima for \(\beta\tau = \pi\), may attain the value \[H_0 = \frac{p}{\beta mk} (1 + e^{-\alpha\tau}).\] If the pressure \(p\) changes its sign whenever \(\cos \beta\tau\) does so, the amplitude \(H\) after ¶ \(n\) such changes of sign will be \[H = \frac{p}{\beta mk} (1 + e^{-\alpha\tau}) \cdot (1 + e^{-\alpha\tau} + e^{-2\alpha\tau} + \dots + e^{-n\alpha\tau})\] or \[H = \frac{p}{\beta mk} \cdot \frac{1 + e^{-\alpha\tau}}{1 - e^{-\alpha\tau}} \cdot (1 - e^{-(n+1)\alpha\tau}).\]

This expression shews the reinforcement, increasing with every change of sign, which ensues upon the coincidence of the period of change of pressure with the period of the proper tone. The denominator \((1 - e^{-\alpha\tau})\) gives the amount of damping during half the periodic time of vibration. Finally, when this is very small \(H\) will be very large, and at last, after an infinite number of repetitions, ¶ \[H = \frac{H_0}{1 - e^{-\alpha\tau}}.\]
