10 Table III. is constructed to shew how often each principal interval, not exceeding a Tritone, is contained in the Octave. The first column gives the cyclic cents in the interval for easy reference to Table I. The second column contains the names of the intervals. The third contains, up to one place of decimals, the number of times that the interval is contained in the Octave, found by dividing log 2 by the logarithm of the interval as given in Table I. This is therefore not always the same as the number arrived at by dividing 1200 by the number of *cyclic* cents, but only by the number of precise cents, as given in Table I. Thus, taking the skhisma of 2 cyclic and 1·953 ex. cents, 1200 ÷ 2 = 600, too small, but 1200 ÷ 1·953 = 614·4 as in Table III.
