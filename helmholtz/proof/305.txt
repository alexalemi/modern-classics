A major triad will therefore be written thus:

	*C* + *E₁* − *G*

+ and a minor triad

	*A₁* − *C* + *E₁* or *C₁* − *E*♭ + *G₁*

+ Now if we lay it down as a rule that as every inferior figure, 1, 2, 3, &c., *depresses* its tone by the 1, 2, 3, &c., comma, every superior figure, 1, 2, 3, &c., shall *raise* its tone by the same 1, 2, 3, &c., commas, we may write the major triad as

	*c* + *e₁* − *g* or *c¹* + *e* − *g¹*

+ and the minor triad as

	*c* − *e¹*♭ + *g* or *c₁* − *e*♭ + *g₁*,

+ or even

	¶ *c¹* − *e²*♭ − *g¹* or *c₂* − *e₁*♭ − *g₂*.⁎

The three series of Tones directly related to *C* are consequently to be written thus:

	*C* − − *E₁* − *F* − *G* − *A₁* − − *c*
	*C* − − *E¹*♭ − *F* − *G* − *A₁* − − *c*
	*C* − − *E¹*♭ − *F* − *G* − *A¹*♭ − − *c*

+ and the intercalary tones are—

	Between the tonic and Third, *D*, *D₁*, or *D¹*♭.
	Between the Sixth and Octave, *B₁* and *B*♭ or *B¹*♭.

Footnote: ⁎ In the 1st [German] edition of this book, as in Hauptmann's, the small letters were supposed to be a comma lower than the capital letters, and a stroke above or below the letters was only occasionally used for raising or depressing the pitch by two commas. Hence a major triad was written *C* − *e* − *G* or *c̄* − *E* − *ḡ*; a minor triad *a* − *C* − *e*, or *A̲* − *c* − *E̲*, &c. The notation used here [in the 3rd and 4th German and the 1st English editions] and also in the French translation is due to Herr A. v. Oettingen, and is much more readily comprehended. [Herr v. Oettingen's notation of lines above and below, which was at Prof. Helmholtz's request retained in the 1st English edition of this translation, was found extremely inconvenient for the printer, and actually delayed the work three months in passing through the press. Another notation which I had used formerly, and into which I translated Herr v. The spirit of Herr v. Oettingen's notation is therefore retained, while its use has been rendered typographically convenient.—*Translator.*]
