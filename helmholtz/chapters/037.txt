Appendix XIII: Description of the Mechanism Employed for Opening the Several Series of Holes in the Polyphonic Siren

(See p. 162, note ⁎.)

Fig. 65 (p. {@414|414a}, {@414b|b}) shews the vertical section of the upper box of the double siren, in order to display its internal construction. E is the wind pipe which is prolonged into the interior of the box, and firmly fixed in the cross beam AA of the support of the apparatus. The prolongation of the wind pipe into the box B has {¶413d}conical surfaces at its upper and lower ends, on which slide corresponding hollow surfaces in the bottom and top surfaces of the box, so that this box can revolve freely about the wind pipe as an axis. At \(\alpha\) may be seen a section of the toothed wheel fastened to the cover of the box. At \(\beta\) is the driving wheel which is turned by the handle \(\gamma\); and \(\delta\) is a pointer which is directed to the graduation on the edge of the disc \(\epsilon\epsilon\).

D is the upper extremity of the axis of the movable discs, of which only the upper one CC is here shewn. The axis turns on fine points in conical cups. The upper cup is introduced into the lower end of the screw \(\eta\), which can be more or less tightened by the milled screw head introduced above, so that any required degree of ease and steadiness in the motion of the axis may be attained.

Inside the box are seen the sections of four pierced rings \(\kappa\lambda\), \(\lambda\mu\), \(\mu\nu\), and \(\nu o\), which fit on to one another with oblique, tile-shaped edges, and thus mutually hold each other steady. Each of these rings lies beneath a series of holes in the cover, and contains precisely the same number of holes as the corresponding series of the cover and of the rotating disc. By means of studs, shewn at i i in fig. 56 (p. 162), {¶414}these four rings can be slightly displaced, so as either to make the holes of the ring coincide with the holes of the box, and thus give free passage to the air and produce the corresponding tone; or else to close the holes of the cover by the {¶414b}interspaces between the holes of the ring, and thus cut off their corresponding tone altogether.

[Figure gl]

In this way it is possible to sound the various tones of this siren in succession or simultaneously, and hence obtain separate or combined tones at pleasure.
