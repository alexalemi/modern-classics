Appendix XIX: Plan of Mr. Bosanquet's Manual

{¶429}(See p. {@328c|328c}.)

The accompanying figure 66 [taken by permission from p. 23 of Mr. R. H. M. Bosanquet's *Elementary Treatise on Musical Intervals and Temperament*, 1876] {¶429b}{¶429c}{¶429d}shews the arrangement of a part of this manual for 53 equal divisions of the octave. The upper division gives a longitudinal section of two digitals standing {¶430}one above the other. All the digitals are of the same form and differ only in colour. The middle part of the figure presents an elevation of the front ends of these digitals. In the lower part there is a view as seen from above [plan]. Proceeding from one of the tones, as *c*, upwards and backwards, leaping over an intermediate digital in the way, we pass to *d* and *e* [on white digitals], and then continuing by major Seconds we pass to *f*♯, *g*♯, *a*♯ [on black digitals], and finally *b*♯ or / *c* [on a white digital again]. The sign / means, as has been explained in the text (p. {@329b|329b}), sharpening by a comma \(\frac{77}{76}\) [or one of the 53 degrees = 22·642 cents] and is very nearly equivalent to our superior ¹ [which means sharpening by a comma of Didymus \(\frac{81}{80}\) = 21·5 cents, for which 22 cents is usually employed]. Between the members of this series are inserted, on the digitals leapt over, those of another series proceeding by major Seconds, *d*♭, *e*♭ [both black], *f*, *g*, *a*, *b*, *c*♯ [all five white].

[Figure gm: Fig. 66. — Bosanquet's manual for 53 equal divisions of the octave, in three views: a longitudinal section of two digitals, an elevation of the stepped rows of white and black digitals marked with note names, and the plan from above.]

The series which lie just above one another differ from each other by a comma {¶430b}of the same kind, the upper being the sharper.

In playing the scale of *c* major ⁎ as *c*, *d*, \ *e*, *f*, *g*, \ *a*, \ *b*, *c′*, observe that a horizontal line drawn through the points where *d* and *g* are printed in the figure will just pass through all the required keys. At \ *e*, \ *a*, \ *b*, we thus come on a deeper intermediate series.† Every major scale is fingered in precisely the same way no matter with what note it begins.

Footnote: ⁎ [In the German edition a cross was placed on the digitals of the plan which were played in this key, but the German copy could not be used here because it followed German musical notation. The text has been altered accordingly.—*Translator*.]

Footnote: † [A horizontal line through *b* in the figure will pass through *c d e f g a b c*, and thus give the Pythagorean major scale.—*Translator*.]

The harmonium constructed by Mr. Bosanquet distributes the 53 tones over 84 digitals, some of those at the upper part of the manual being identical with some of those at the lower part, in order to avoid having frequently to jump from upper to lower digitals. In the system of 53 divisions / / / *b* = \ \ *c*, since five smallest degrees represent a diatonic Semitone. [For a further account of Mr. Bosanquet's notation see App. XX. sect. A. art. 27. For a more detailed plan of his generalised fingerboard see *ibid.* sect. F. No. 8, and for his methods of tuning see *ibid.* sect. G. art. 16.]
