Chapter 13: How Kriemhild and Her Husband Rode to the Festival

{778} Let us leave all their bustle now and tell how Lady Kriemhild
and her maidens traveled from the Nibelung land toward the Rhine. Never
did horses carry so much rich clothing. {779} A great many traveling
chests were loaded onto the wagons. Then Siegfried the warrior rode off
with his kinsmen, and the queen with him, toward the place where they
hoped for joy. Later it turned to great sorrow for them all.

{780} They left Siegfried's little boy, Kriemhild's son, at home. It
had to be so. Great grief would come to him from their journey to
court: the child never saw his father or his mother again. {781} Lord
Siegmund rode away with them too. Had he known for certain how the
festival would turn out, he would never have seen any of it. No worse
grief could ever have come to him through the people he loved.

{782} Messengers were sent ahead to carry the news. Then many of Ute's
kinsmen and Gunther's vassals rode out to meet them in a splendid
company, and the host began to busy himself greatly for his guests.
{783} He went to Brunhild where he found her sitting. "How did my
sister receive you when you came into my land? You must receive
Siegfried's wife just the same way."

"I will do that gladly, and with good reason," she said. "I am fond of
her."

{784} Then the mighty king said: "They arrive early tomorrow morning.
If you mean to receive them, set to work quickly, so that we are not
left waiting for them here in the castle. In all my life, guests so
dear to me have never come."

{785} At once she told her maids and her ladies to look out good
clothes, the best to be found, for her household to wear in front of
the guests. They did it gladly, I can easily tell you that.
{786} Gunther's vassals hurried to serve them too, and the host gathered all
his warriors around him. Then the queen rode out in great state, and
the dear guests were given a mighty welcome.

{787} With what joy they received those guests! It seemed to them that
Lady Kriemhild had never received Lady Brunhild in Burgundy half so
well. Those who had never seen her before found their spirits lifted
by the sight. {788} Now Siegfried had arrived too, with his vassals.
The heroes were seen wheeling back and forth across the whole field in
huge companies; no one could escape the crush and the dust.

{789} When the ruler of the land saw Siegfried and Siegmund, how warmly
he spoke to them! "Be heartily welcome to me and to all my kinsmen. We
shall be in high spirits because of this journey of yours to our
court."

{790} "God reward you," said Siegmund, that honor-seeking man. "Ever
since my son Siegfried won you as a kinsman, my heart has told me that
I ought to come and see you."

King Gunther said: "It is a joy to me that you did."

{791} Siegfried was received, as was only right, with the greatest
honor; no one there bore him any ill will. Giselher and Gernot helped
in this with great courtesy. I believe guests were never offered such
kindness before.

{792} Now the two kings' wives drew near each other. Many a saddle was
emptied as beautiful women were lifted down onto the grass by heroes'
hands; those who liked to serve the ladies had plenty to do. {793} The
lovely women went up to each other, and many a knight rejoiced that
the two of them greeted each other so beautifully. Warriors in crowds
were seen standing beside their young ladies. {794} The splendid
company took one another by the hand. There was a great deal of
courteous bowing, and loving kisses from beautiful women: a happy sight
for Gunther's and Siegfried's men.

{795} They waited no longer but rode into the town. The host asked
that his guests be shown clearly how glad everyone in Burgundy was to
see them, and many a splendid jousting charge was run before the young
ladies. {796} Hagen of Tronje and Ortwin made it very clear that they
were in command there: whatever they ordered, no one dared leave
undone, and the dear guests were given great service by both of them.
{797} At the castle gate you could hear shields ringing from thrusts and
blows; the host and his guests held back out there a long time before
they went in. Truly, the hours went by for them in great sport.

{798} Then they rode before the great hall with the ladies. Many a
fine silk cloth, good and well cut, was seen hanging over the beautiful
women's saddles on every side. Then Gunther's men came up, {799} and
the guests were told to be led quickly to their lodgings. Now and then
Brunhild was seen glancing at Lady Kriemhild, who was beautiful enough:
her color shone gloriously against the gold.

{800} All through the town of Worms the household could be heard
shouting. Gunther told Dankwart, his marshal, to take charge of them,
and he began to lodge the company very comfortably. {801} They were
fed outside and inside; truly, foreign guests were never better cared
for. Whatever they wanted was ready for them: the king was so rich that
no one was refused anything. {802} They were served in friendship and
without a trace of ill will. Then the host sat down at table with his
guests. Siegfried was asked to sit where he had sat before, and many a
handsome man went with him to his seat. {803} A good twelve hundred
warriors sat at table in his circle. Brunhild the queen thought that no
bondsman could be richer than this. Yet she was still so well disposed
to him that she gladly let him live.

{804} One evening, as the king sat at table, many rich clothes were
soaked with wine where the cupbearers had to walk between the tables;
full service was given with great care. {805} As has long been the
custom at festivals, the ladies and the young girls were given fine
lodgings. Wherever they had come from, the host wished them well, and
all of them were given plenty, with kindness and honor.

{806} When the night ended and the day appeared, many a precious stone
set in good clothing flashed out of the traveling chests as the ladies'
hands touched them, and many a splendid garment was picked out.
{807} Before it was fully light, many knights and squires came out before
the hall, and the noise rose again, before the early Mass that was sung
for the king. The young heroes rode so well that the king thanked them
for it. {808} Many a trumpet blared with tremendous force, and from the
drums and flutes the noise grew so great that the whole wide town of
Worms rang with it. The high-spirited heroes were on horseback
everywhere.

{809} Then a very high game began in that land, with many a good
warrior; you could see plenty of them there, their young hearts full of
high spirits, and many a fine good knight under his shield. {810} In
the windows sat the glorious ladies and many beautiful girls, all
richly adorned; they watched the sport of many a bold man. The host
himself began to ride with his kinsmen.

{811} So they passed the time, and it did not seem long. Then the
ringing of many bells was heard from the cathedral. The horses came,
and the ladies rode off, and many a bold man followed the noble queens.
{812} They dismounted onto the grass in front of the cathedral.
Brunhild was still well disposed to her guests. They went into the
wide cathedral wearing their crowns. Later that affection was broken,
and great hatred brought it about.

{813} When they had heard Mass, they rode back again with many honors,
and afterward they were seen going happily to table. Their joy never
flagged at that festival until the eleventh day.
