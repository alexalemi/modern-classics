Chapter 10: How Gunther Married Brunhild at Worms

{579} On the far side of the Rhine, the king and his guests could be
seen riding down to the shore in company after company, and many a
girl was led to meet them by the bridle, for everyone who was to
receive them stood ready. {580} When the people from Isenland and
Siegfried's Nibelung men came across in the ships, they hurried to
land, and their hands were anything but idle, there where the king's
kinsmen were found waiting on the other bank.

{581} Now hear this too, of Queen Ute, the mighty lady: how she
brought her girls down from the castle and rode out with them herself.
Many a knight and many a girl made new acquaintances that day.
{582} Duke Gere led Kriemhild's horse by the bridle, but only as far
as the castle gate; from there bold Siegfried had to serve her the
rest of the way. She was a beautiful girl, and the young lady repaid
him for it richly later on. {583} Bold Ortwin rode beside Lady Ute,
and knight and girl rode along together, two by two. At such a grand
reception, we can safely say, so many ladies had never before been
seen together.

{584} Many a splendid buhurt was ridden by praiseworthy heroes on the
way down to the ships before Kriemhild the beautiful; it would not
have done to leave that out. And there many a lovely lady was lifted
down from her horse. {585} The king had come across now, and many a
worthy guest with him. Ah, what stout lance-shafts splintered before
the ladies' eyes! You could hear shield crash against shield as they
charged, and ah, how loudly the costly shield-bosses rang in the
press!

{586} The lovely women stood at the landing as Gunther and his guests
came down from the ships, and he led Brunhild by the hand himself.
Bright jewels and bright clothes shone against each other. {587} Lady
Kriemhild went forward with perfect courtesy to receive Lady Brunhild
and her household. White hands were seen pushing back the garlands
from their brows as the two of them kissed; that was done out of
courtesy. {588} Then the princess Kriemhild said, well-bred as she
was: "You are welcome in these lands, to me and to my mother and to
all the loyal kinsmen we have." Bows were made all round. {589} The
ladies embraced each other again and again. No one ever heard of so
loving a welcome as the two ladies, Lady Ute and her daughter, gave
the bride; they kissed her sweet mouth over and over.

{590} When all Brunhild's women had come up onto the sand, many a
lovely woman was taken warmly by the hand by handsome warriors, and
the beautiful girls were seen standing before Lady Brunhild. {591} It
was a long while before all the greeting was done, and truly, many a
rose-red mouth was kissed. The two noble princesses still stood side
by side, and that was a sight many a fine warrior was glad to see.
{592} Those who had heard it claimed before that nothing so beautiful
as these two ladies had ever been seen now looked with their own eyes,
and had to admit it was no lie. Nor could anyone find a trace of
artifice on either of them. {593} Those who knew how to judge women
and lovely looks praised Gunther's wife for her beauty; but the wise
ones, who had looked more carefully, said that Kriemhild must be
ranked above Lady Brunhild.

{594} Girls and women now went to meet one another, and many a
beautiful figure was seen there in full array. Silken tents and many a
rich pavilion stood all about; the whole field before Worms was full
of them. {595} There was crowding and pushing around the king's
kinsmen. Then Brunhild and Kriemhild were asked to go, and all the
ladies with them, to where there was shade, and the warriors of
Burgundy escorted them there.

{596} By now the guests had all got onto their horses, and many a
splendid joust was ridden, lance through shield. The field began to
throw up dust as if the whole land had burst into flame, and there
heroes made their names. {597} Many a girl watched what the warriors
were doing. It seems to me that Lord Siegfried and his men rode many
a charge back and forth before the tents; he led a thousand handsome
Nibelungs. {598} Then Hagen of Tronje came, as the host had told him
to, and the hero broke up the buhurt gently, so that the beautiful
girls would not be left covered in dust; and the guests obeyed him
with good grace.

{599} Then Lord Gernot said: "Let the horses stand until it starts to
cool. Then we will set about serving the beautiful women before the
great hall, and when the king wants to ride, see that you are all
ready."

{600} When the buhurt had died away over the whole field, the knights
went to the ladies under the many pavilions to pass the time, with
high hopes of joy, and there they whiled away the hours until they
were ready to ride on. {601} Toward evening, as the sun went down and
it began to grow cool, they delayed no longer: many a man and woman
set off for the castle, and many a beautiful lady was caressed with
the eyes. {602} Plenty of clothes were ridden to tatters by good
heroes, by those high-spirited men, after the custom of the land,
until the king dismounted in front of the great hall. Then the ladies
were helped down, as high-hearted heroes do. {603} Now the two mighty
queens parted. Lady Ute and her daughter went with their household
into a very spacious room, and on every side you could hear the great
din of rejoicing.

{604} The seats were made ready, for the king wanted to go to table
with his guests. Beside him stood beautiful Brunhild, and she wore a
crown in the king's land: truly she was powerful enough for that.
{605} Many a fine seat and good table was set with food, so we are
told, with everything they could need and hardly anything lacking, and
many a lordly guest was seen beside the king. {606} The host's
chamberlains carried the water around in basins of red gold. If anyone
ever told you that men were served better at any prince's feast, there
would be little point in it; I would not believe it.

{607} Before the lord of the Rhine took the water to wash his hands,
Lord Siegfried did what was his right: he reminded him, on his
loyalty, of what he had promised him before he ever saw Brunhild at
home in Isenland. {608} He said: "You must remember what your hand
swore to me: that when Lady Brunhild came to this land, you would give
me your sister. What has become of those oaths? I went through great
hardship on your voyage."

{609} The king said to his guest: "You are right to remind me. My hand
shall not be forsworn, truly. I will arrange it as best I possibly
can." Then Kriemhild was sent for, to come to court before the king.
{610} She came with her beautiful girls to the front of the hall, and
young Giselher sprang down the stairs. "Now tell these girls to turn
back. No one but my sister alone is to go before the king."

{611} So Kriemhild was brought to where the king was. Noble knights
from many princes' lands stood in the wide hall, and they were told to
stand still. By now Lady Brunhild had walked as far as the table.
{612} Then King Gunther said: "Sister, dear heart, by your own
goodness, release me from my oath. I swore you to a warrior, and if he
becomes your husband, you will have done my will most loyally."

{613} The noble girl said: "Dearest brother, you need not plead with
me. Truly, I will always be as you wish; whatever you command me, that
shall be done. I will gladly promise myself to the man you give me,
my lord, for a husband."

{614} Siegfried's color rose at that loving glance, and the warrior
offered Lady Kriemhild his service. They were told to step together
into a ring, and she was asked whether she would take this handsome
man. {615} In girlish modesty she was shy about it, a little; but such
was Siegfried's luck, and his happiness, that she had no wish to
refuse him there and then, and the noble king of the Netherlands
promised to take her for his wife. {616} When he had pledged himself
to her and the girl to him, Siegfried's arms were quick to give the
lovely child a tender embrace, and then beautiful Kriemhild was kissed
before the heroes.

{617} When that had happened the company divided, and Siegfried was
seen sitting with Kriemhild on the bench across from the king, with
many a man to serve him, and the Nibelungs were seen going over to
Siegfried's side. {618} The king had taken his seat with Brunhild the
bride when she saw Kriemhild sitting beside Siegfried, and nothing had
ever grieved her so much. She began to weep, and hot tears ran down her
bright cheeks.

{619} The lord of the land said: "My dearest lady, why do you let the
light of those bright eyes grow dim? You have every reason to be
happy: my land and my castles are yours, and many a handsome man."

{620} "I have good reason to weep," said the beautiful girl. "My heart
aches for your sister. I see her sitting there beside your bondsman,
and I will weep forever if she is to be ruined like that."

{621} King Gunther said: "You would do well to say nothing now. I will
tell you the whole story another time, why I gave my sister to
Siegfried. Truly, she can live happily with that warrior all her life."

{622} She said: "I will always grieve for her beauty and her breeding.
If I knew where to go, I would gladly run away, and I will never lie
close by your side until you tell me why Kriemhild is Siegfried's
wife."

{623} The noble king said: "Then I will tell you. He has castles, just
as I do, and wide lands. Be sure of this: he is a mighty king. That is
why I gave him the beautiful, praiseworthy girl to love." {624} But
whatever the king told her, she stayed downcast.

Now many a good knight hurried from the tables to the horses, and
their buhurt grew so hard that the whole castle rang with it. But the
host was heartily tired of his guests. {625} He thought he would lie
more softly beside the beautiful lady. He had not given up the hope
in his heart that much joy was to come to him through her, and he
began to look at Lady Brunhild lovingly. {626} The guests were asked
to leave off their knightly games, for the king wanted to go to bed
with his wife. At the stairs of the hall Kriemhild and Brunhild met;
there was no hatred between the two of them yet. {627} Then their
households came, wasting no time, and their noble chamberlains brought
them lights. The warriors, the two kings' men, parted into two
companies, and many a fighter was seen going off with Siegfried.

{628} The two lords came to where they were to lie, and each of them
thought of conquering his lovely lady with love. That put them both
in a gentle mood. Siegfried's pleasure that night was very great
indeed. {629} When Lord Siegfried lay beside Kriemhild and caressed
the young lady so tenderly with his noble love, she became as dear to
him as his own life; he would not have taken a thousand other women in
exchange for her alone. {630} I will tell you no more of how he loved
his lady. Hear instead how Gunther lay beside Lady Brunhild. That
handsome warrior had often lain more comfortably beside other women.

{631} The people had withdrawn, the ladies and the men, and the
chamber was quickly shut. He thought he was about to caress her lovely
body; but truly, it was a long way off yet before she became his wife.
{632} She went to the bed in a shift of white linen, and the noble
knight thought: "Now I have here everything I ever wanted in all my
days." Her beauty had every right to please him. {633} The noble
king's hand hid the lights away, and then the bold warrior went to
where the lady lay. He lay down close to her, and his joy was great as
the hero wrapped his arms around the lovely woman. {634} He could have
begun many a loving caress, if the noble lady had let him. But she
grew so angry that it troubled him: he had expected a lover and found
an enemy's hatred instead.

{635} She said: "Noble knight, you will let it be. What you are hoping
for cannot happen. I mean to stay a virgin — you had better understand
that — until I have found out the truth." Then Gunther turned on her
in anger. {636} He wrestled for her love and tore her clothes, and the
lordly girl reached for a belt, a strong woven band she wore around
her waist, and with it she did the king harm enough. {637} She tied
his feet and his hands together, carried him to a nail, and hung him
on the wall. Since he had disturbed her sleep, she denied him her
love. Truly, her strength very nearly cost him his life.

{638} Then the one who had thought himself the master began to beg.
"Untie my bonds, most noble queen. I will never try to overpower you
again, beautiful lady, and I shall hardly ever lie so close to you
again."

{639} She did not care how he felt, for she lay very comfortably. He
had to hang there all night long until the day, until the bright
morning shone through the windows. If he had ever had any strength,
there was little of it left in him now.

{640} "Now tell me, Lord Gunther," said the beautiful girl, "would it
trouble you at all if your chamberlains found you tied up by a woman's
hand?"

The noble knight said: "That would turn out badly for you, {641} and I
would have little honor from it either," said the quick-witted man.
"By your own goodness, let me come to you now. Since my love is so
hateful to you, I will never lay a finger on your clothes."

{642} Then she untied him quickly and let him up. He went back to the
bed, to the lady, and lay down so far away that after that he hardly
ever touched her fine shift; and she wanted no more of it either.

{643} Then their household came in and brought them new clothes, and
plenty of them were ready for the morning. However cheerful everyone
else was, the lord of the land was sad enough, even though he wore a
crown that day. {644} Following the custom they kept, and that was
right to keep, Gunther and Brunhild delayed no longer but went to the
cathedral, where Mass was sung. Lord Siegfried came there too, and a
great crowd pressed in. {645} Everything they needed stood ready for
them, as suited their royal honor, their crowns and their robes. Then
they were consecrated, and when that was done all four of them were
seen standing joyfully under their crowns.

{646} Many young men took the sword there, six hundred or more, in
honor of the kings, I want you to know that. Great rejoicing rose in
the land of Burgundy, and you could hear the shafts ringing in the
hands of the new-made knights. {647} The beautiful girls sat at the
windows and watched many a bright shield flashing before them. But the
king had drawn apart from his men; whatever anyone else was doing, the
king was seen walking about in sorrow.

{648} His mood and Siegfried's were not alike, and the noble knight
had a good idea what was wrong with him. He went to the king and
asked: "How did you get on last night? You must tell me."

{649} The host said to his guest: "I have won myself shame and harm.
I have brought the very devil home into my house. When I thought I was
going to love her, she tied me up tight, carried me to a nail, and
hung me high on the wall. {650} There I hung in misery all night until
day, before she untied me. And how comfortably she lay there! I
complain of this to you as a friend, trusting in your good will."

Strong Siegfried said: "Truly, I am sorry to hear it. {651} I will
prove that to you, if you will let me without resenting it: I will see
to it that tonight she lies so close beside you that she never keeps
her love from you again." After all his hardship Gunther liked that
talk well. {652} Lord Siegfried said: "You can take heart. I think we
fared very differently last night. Your sister Kriemhild is dearer to
me than my life. Lady Brunhild must become your wife tonight.

{653} "I will come to your chamber tonight," he said, "so secretly,
in my cloak of invisibility, that no one will see through my trick.
Send the chamberlains out to their quarters, {654} and I will put out
the lights in the pages' hands; by that you will know I am inside, and
that I am glad to serve you. Then I will tame your wife for you, so
that you can love her tonight, or I will lose my life doing it."

{655} "So long as you do not make love to my dear lady yourself," the
king said, "I am content. Otherwise do whatever you like with her,
even if you took her life. I could put up with that; she is a
terrible woman."

{656} "I swear it on my loyalty," said Siegfried, "that I will not
make love to her. Your beautiful sister is dearer to me than any woman
I ever saw." And King Gunther fully believed what Siegfried said.

{657} The pastimes had brought both joy and distress. Now all the
buhurts and the uproar were called off, because the ladies were to go
up to the hall, and the chamberlains told the people to clear the way.
{658} The courtyard was emptied of horses and people. A bishop led
each of the ladies as they went to table before the kings, and many a
handsome man followed them to the seats. {659} The king sat there in
good cheer and high hopes; he was thinking of what Siegfried had
promised him. That one day seemed to him as long as thirty, for all
his thoughts were fixed on his lady's love. {660} He could hardly wait
for the tables to be cleared. Then beautiful Brunhild was allowed to
go to her rest, and Lady Kriemhild too, each to her own chamber. Ah,
what quick warriors were seen going before the queens!

{661} Lord Siegfried sat most lovingly beside his wife, in joy and
without a care. She was stroking his hands with her white hand when he
vanished before her eyes; she could not tell when. {662} When she was
playing with him like that and suddenly could not see him any more,
the queen said to his household: "I wonder very much where the king
has gone. Who has taken his hands out of mine?"

{663} She let the matter drop. He had gone to where he found the
chamberlains standing with the lights, and he began to put them out in
the pages' hands; and by that Gunther knew it was Siegfried. {664} He
knew well what he wanted, and he told the girls and the ladies to
leave. When that was done the mighty king himself barred the door and
quickly shot two strong bolts across it. {665} He hid the lights
quickly under the bedclothes. Then a game began that there was no way
around, between strong Siegfried and the beautiful girl, and to King
Gunther it was joy and sorrow both.

{666} Siegfried lay down close to the young lady. She said: "Now stop
that, Gunther, if I mean anything to you, or you will suffer the same
hardship as before." Later the lady made bold Siegfried pay for that.

{667} He kept his voice hidden and said nothing at all. Gunther could
hear well enough, though he could not see them, that nothing intimate
was happening between those two; they had very little comfort on that
bed. {668} Siegfried behaved as if he were Gunther the mighty king and
took the praiseworthy girl in his arms. She threw him out of the bed
onto a bench nearby, so that his head cracked loudly against a stool.
{669} The bold man sprang up again with all his strength. He meant to
do better this time; but when he set about forcing her, it went hard
with him. I think no woman will ever again put up such a defense.

{670} When he would not give up, the girl sprang up. "You are not to
tear my white shift. You are a rough brute, and you will be sorry for
it; I will see to that," said the handsome girl. {671} She locked her
arms around the splendid warrior and meant to tie him up as she had
the king, so that she could have her peace in the bed. The lady took a
hard revenge for the tearing of her clothes. {672} What good were his
great strength and his great power now? She showed the warrior what
mastery her body had: she carried him by main force — there was no
helping it — and jammed him hard between the wall and a chest.

{673} "Oh no," the warrior thought. "If I lose my life now at a
girl's hands, then every wife from this day on will be free to hold
her head high against her husband, who would never have dared it
otherwise."

{674} The king heard it all, and feared for the man. Siegfried was
furious, and shame came over him. With tremendous strength he set
himself against her, and now, in desperation, he tried it again with
Lady Brunhild. {675} It seemed a long time to the king before he
subdued her. She crushed his hands until the blood spurted out from
under the nails, so great was her strength, and the hero did not like
that. But in the end he forced the lordly girl to give up {676} the
unruly will she had asserted before. The king heard all of it, though
he said nothing. Siegfried pressed her down onto the bed so that she
screamed out loud; his strength hurt her terribly. {677} She reached
for her side, where she found the belt, and would have tied him with
it, but his hand stopped her so hard that her limbs cracked, and her
whole body with them. That decided the fight; and so she became
Gunther's wife.

{678} She said: "Noble king, let me live. Whatever I have done to you
will be made good. I will never again resist your noble love. I have
found out well enough that you know how to be a woman's master."

{679} Siegfried stood back and left the girl lying there, as if he
meant to take off all his clothes. He drew a golden ring from her
hand, and the noble queen never noticed. {680} He took her belt as
well, a good strong band. I do not know whether he did that out of
pride. He gave the two of them to his wife; later it cost him dearly.
Then Gunther and the beautiful girl lay down together. {681} He loved
her as was his right, and she had to give up her anger and her shame.
From his embrace she grew a little pale; and ah, how that great
strength of hers drained away with her love! {682} Now she was no
stronger than any other woman. He caressed her lovely body tenderly,
and if she had tried her strength again, what good would it have done
her? Gunther had done all that to her with his love. {683} How
lovingly she lay beside him then, in tender affection, until the
bright day.

Lord Siegfried, meanwhile, had gone out again, to where a lovely woman
gave him a warm welcome. {684} He headed off the question she had
meant to ask, and for a long time he kept from her what he had
brought, until she went crowned in his own land. But whatever he owed
to give her, how little of it he ever withheld!

{685} In the morning the host was in far better spirits than he had
been the day before, and that made for good cheer all over his land,
among many a noble man he had invited to his house; they were served
to their hearts' content. {686} The wedding feast lasted until the
fourteenth day, and in all that time the noise of every kind of joy
that anyone could devise never stopped, and the king's expense was
reckoned very high. {687} The noble host's kinsmen, as the king
ordered, gave in his honor clothes and red gold, horses and silver
too, to many a man from foreign lands; and those who asked for gifts
went away happy. {688} Lord Siegfried of the Netherlands and his
thousand men gave away every garment they had brought to the Rhine,
and the horses and saddles too; they knew how to live like lords.
{689} Before all the rich gifts had been handed out, those who wanted
to go home thought it was taking too long. Never were guests better
looked after. So the wedding feast ended, as Gunther the warrior
wished.
