Chapter 12: How Gunther Invited Siegfried to a Festival

{724} Now Gunther's wife was thinking, all the time: "How grandly Lady
Kriemhild carries herself! And yet her husband Siegfried is our
bondsman, and for a long while now he has done us very little service."
{725} She carried this in her heart and kept it well hidden. That they
stayed such strangers to her, that so little service ever came to her
from Siegfried's land, grieved her deeply, and she would have given a
great deal to know the reason.

{726} So she tried the king, to see whether it might be arranged for her
to see Kriemhild again. She said in private what she had in mind, and
the lord did not think much of the idea. {727} "How could we bring them
here to this land?" said the mighty king. "It would be impossible. They
live too far from us, and I would not dare to ask it of them." Lady
Brunhild answered him with a cunning turn: {728} "However high and
mighty a king's vassal may be, whatever his lord commands him, he still
must not leave it undone." Gunther smiled when she said that. However
often he saw Siegfried, he never counted it as a vassal's service.

{729} "Dearest lord," she said, "for my sake, help me bring Siegfried
and your sister to this land, so that we can see them here. Truly,
nothing could ever make me happier. {730} Your sister's good breeding
and her gracious spirit: whenever I think of them, how it warms me, how
we sat together when I first became your wife. She may love bold
Siegfried with honor."

{731} She begged him so long that at last the king said: "I tell you,
there are no guests I would be gladder to see. You need not press me
hard. I will send my messengers for the two of them, so that they come
to us on the Rhine."

{732} Then the queen said: "Then you must tell me when you mean to send
for them, and in how many days our dear kinsmen are to come into the
land. And the men you mean to send, let me know who they are."

{733} "I will," said the prince. "I will let thirty of my men ride
there." He had them come before him, and by them he sent his message to
Siegfried's land. To their delight, Brunhild gave them very splendid
clothes.

{734} Then King Gunther said: "You warriors shall say from me, and hold
back nothing that I send, to strong Siegfried and to my sister, that no
one in the world could be fonder of them than I am. {735} And ask them
both to come to us on the Rhine; for that, I and my lady will be forever
at their service. Before this midsummer he and his men shall see many
here who wish him great honor. {736} To King Siegmund tell my service,
that I and my people will always be his friends. And tell my sister,
too, that she must not fail to ride to her kinsmen. Never did a festival
suit her better."

{737} Brunhild and Ute and every lady to be found at court all sent
their service to Siegfried's land, to the lovely women there and to many
a bold man. With the counsel of the king's kinsmen the messengers set
out. {738} They traveled fitted out for the road; their horses and their
clothing had all been brought to them, and then they left the land. They
were eager to reach the place they were going, and the king had the
messengers carefully guarded with an escort. {739} In three weeks they
came riding into the land, to Nibelung's castle in the border country of
Norway, where they had been sent, and there they found the warriors. The
messengers' horses were very weary from the long roads.

{740} Siegfried and Kriemhild were both told that knights had arrived
wearing clothes of the kind that was the fashion in Burgundy. She sprang
from a couch where she lay resting {741} and asked a maid to go to a
window. The girl saw bold Gere standing in the courtyard, him and the
companions who had been sent there with him. Against her heartache at
being so far from home, what joyful news she found!

{742} She said to the king: "Now look, there they stand, the men walking
in the courtyard with strong Gere, the ones my brother Gunther has sent
us down the Rhine." Then strong Siegfried said: "They shall be welcome
to us."

{743} The whole household ran to wherever the messengers could be seen,
and each of them in turn spoke to the envoys very kindly, as well as he
knew how. Lord Siegmund was very glad of their coming. {744} Then Gere
and his men were given lodgings, and the horses were ordered looked
after. The messengers went to where Lord Siegfried sat beside Kriemhild;
they had been given leave to come to court, so that is what they did.
{745} The host and his wife stood up at once. Gere of Burgundy was
warmly received, with his companions, Gunther's men, and mighty Gere was
asked to go and sit down.

{746} "Allow us our message before we go and sit. Let us travel-weary
guests stand for now. We are to tell you news of what Gunther and
Brunhild have sent you; their affairs stand very high. {747} And also
what Lady Ute, your mother, sends. Young Giselher and Lord Gernot too
and your best kinsmen have sent us here. They send you their service
from Gunther's land."

{748} "Now God reward them," said Siegfried. "I trust them fully, for
loyalty and good will, as one should trust kinsmen; and so does their
sister. You must tell us more: are our dear kinsmen at home in good
spirits? {749} Since we parted from them, has anyone done anything to my
wife's kinsmen? You must let me know. I will always help them bear it in
loyalty, until their enemies have reason to curse my service."

{750} Then Margrave Gere, a very good warrior, said: "They are in every
virtue so high of heart. They invite you to the Rhine for a festival.
They would be very glad to see you, you may be sure of that. {751} And
they ask my lady to come there with you, once the winter has come to an
end. Before this midsummer, they would like to see you." Then strong
Siegfried said: "That could hardly happen."

{752} Then Gere of Burgundy spoke again: "Your mother Ute, Gernot and
Giselher have urged you: you must not refuse them. That you live so far
from them, I hear lamented every day. {753} Brunhild my lady and all her
maidens rejoice at the news, and if it could be that they saw you again,
it would lift their hearts high." That news sounded good to beautiful
Kriemhild.

{754} Gere was her kinsman. The host told him to sit, and he ordered
drink poured for the guests; there was no more delay about that. Now
Siegmund too had come to where he saw the messengers, and the lord spoke
to the Burgundians in a friendly way: {755} "Welcome, you warriors, you
men of Gunther. Since my son Siegfried took Kriemhild as his wife, you
ought to be seen more often here in this land, if you would show us any
kinship."

{756} They said they would gladly come whenever he wished. Their great
weariness was lifted from them with good cheer. The messengers were
asked to sit, food was brought to them, and Siegfried had his guests
given plenty of it. {757} They had to stay there a full nine days, and
in the end the swift knights complained that they could not ride home to
their own land. Meanwhile King Siegfried had sent for his kinsmen.

{758} He asked them what they advised: should they go to the Rhine? "My
kinsman Gunther has sent for me, he and his kinsmen, for a festival. I
would gladly go to him, except that his land lies too far away.
{759} And they ask Kriemhild to travel with me. Now advise me, dear
kinsmen:
how am I to get there? If I had to go to war for them through thirty
lands, Siegfried's hand would gladly serve them."

{760} Then his warriors said: "If you have a mind to make the journey to
the festival, we will advise you what to do. You should ride to the
Rhine with a thousand warriors; then you can stand with honor there
among the Burgundians."

{761} Then Lord Siegmund of the Netherlands said: "If you want to go to
the festival, why do you not tell me so? If you do not scorn it, I will
ride there with you, and I will bring a hundred warriors to make your
company larger."

{762} "If you will ride with us, dearest father," said bold Siegfried,
"I shall be very glad of it. Within twelve days I will leave my lands."
All who asked for them were given horses and clothes. {763} Since the
noble king had a mind for the journey, the good swift messengers were
told to ride home again. To his wife's kinsmen on the Rhine he sent word
that he would gladly be at their festival.

{764} Siegfried and Kriemhild, as we hear it told, gave the messengers
so much that their horses could not carry it home. He was a rich man.
Their strong pack-horses were driven off in good cheer. {765} Siegfried
and Siegmund clothed their people, and Count Eckewart at once ordered
ladies' clothes sought out, the best to be found, or that could be
bought anywhere in all Siegfried's land. {766} The saddles and the
shields began to be made ready. The knights and ladies who were to go
with them were given whatever they wanted, so that they lacked nothing.
And so he brought his kinsmen many a magnificent guest.

{767} The messengers hurried home along the roads. Then Gere the warrior
came to Burgundy and was warmly received. They got down from their
warhorses and their mounts in front of Giselher's hall. {768} Young and
old came, as people do, asking for news. The good knight said: "When I
tell it to the king, you will hear it then and there." And he went with
his companions to where he found Gunther.

{769} For sheer joy the king sprang up from his seat, and beautiful
Brunhild thanked them for coming back so quickly. Gunther said to the
messenger: "How is Siegfried, who has given me so much good?"

{770} Bold Gere said: "He turned red with joy, he and your sister. No
man ever sent his kinsmen such loyal greetings as Lord Siegfried and his
father have sent you."

{771} Then the noble king's wife said to the margrave: "Now tell me, is
Kriemhild coming to us? Has that beautiful woman kept any of the good
manners she used to show so well?"

"She is coming to you, be sure of it," said Gere the warrior.

{772} Ute quickly asked the messengers to come before her, and anyone
could tell from her questions how glad she was to hear it: was Kriemhild
still well? He told her how he had found her, and that she would be
coming very soon. {773} Nor were the gifts kept hidden at court, the
gold and the clothes Lord Siegfried had given them. These were brought
out for the men of the three kings to see, and he was thanked for his
great generosity.

{774} "He can easily afford to give," said Hagen then. "He could not
squander it if he lived forever. The Nibelung treasure is locked in his
hand. Ah, if only it ever came into the land of the Burgundians!"

{775} The whole household rejoiced that the guests were coming. Early
and late the men of the three kings were hard at work, and many seats
were set up for the company. {776} Bold Hunold and Sindold the warrior
had plenty to do; all that while they had to serve as stewards and
cupbearers and set up many a bench. Lord Ortwin helped them with it, and
Gunther thanked them for it. {777} Rumold the master of the kitchen, how
well he ruled his underlings in the days that followed! Broad kettles,
pots and pans by the dozen; what a lot of them were found there! And
they made the food ready for the people who were coming into the land.
